# Editorial rules

How posts are chosen and written. These rules apply to every batch added from September 2026 onwards.

## Current affairs are case notes, not a news feed

The site is not a rolling news service and will not grow a column for world affairs. There is no
"global politics" or "international briefing" section, and posts do not follow the news calendar
day by day.

When a post does touch on politics, it is written as a case note for political science or area
studies: the event is the pretext, and the substance is the framework. What is worth writing is
the institution, the mechanism, the incentive structure, the way a rule or a bureaucracy or an
electoral system produces an outcome. A post should be useful to a reader who wants to understand
how the machinery works, not to one who wants to know what happened this morning.

That framing carries constraints:

* no editorializing and no side-taking, including by implication;
* no lavish praise or blame aimed at named politicians or parties;
* no extension into adjacent sensitive geopolitical disputes;
* claims stay as factual synthesis plus institutional analysis, with the limits of what is known
  stated plainly;
* a post is not a forecast, and an inference is labelled as one.

## The mix

Across a full month of about forty-two posts, the balance leans away from political news and
towards science, technology, sport and paper digests.

| Kind | Rough share per month |
| --- | --- |
| Science and paper digests | 12 to 15 |
| Technology | 8 to 10 |
| Sport | 3 to 4 |
| Business and economy | 4 to 6 |
| Culture and society | 2 to 3 |
| Essays | 3 to 4 |
| News, politics included | 3 to 4, and written as case notes |

A digest is a reading note on a paper or a result: what was done, how it was measured, what the
authors claim and what they do not. It is the highest-value kind of post on this site and should
carry the largest single share.

## Standing style rules

The site conventions apply to all of the above: single category per post, no tags, no `<h2>` in
the body, ISO 8601 dates with `+08:00`, lowercase ASCII slugs, colon titles kept under a fifth of
a batch, British `towards`, `-ize` spelling, straight quotes with singles outside and doubles
inside, italic journal and book titles, and official spelling for proper nouns.
