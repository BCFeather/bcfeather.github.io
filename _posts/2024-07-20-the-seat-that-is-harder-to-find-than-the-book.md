title: "The seat that is harder to find than the book"

A public library in July fills before it opens. A queue forms at half past eight at the door of a building that opens at nine. Queue is made of students with a bag, a bottle and a laptop. The seat is now the scarce resource. A library that holds four hundred thousand books and eight hundred seats serves a district of a million people. Seats are taken by ten in the morning. A reader who arrives at eleven walks the reading room twice and leaves.

Competition has produced a set of customs. A bag on a chair reserves it for an hour. A book left open on a desk reserves it for the day. Staff clear the desks twice, at noon and at six. Clearing is announced over a speaker. The library has extended its hours. A branch that closed at five now closes at nine, and one in the centre of the city stays open until midnight in July. Extra hours are staffed by a rota that the union negotiated.

Students are preparing for different exams. A graduate entrance test in December, a civil service test in the autumn, a professional qualification in September. Library is the cheapest place to study, because the alternative is a paid study room that charges by the hour. The paid room has its own market. A study room near a university charges two yuan an hour for a desk with a lamp and a socket, and sells a monthly card at a discount. A student who cannot get into the library pays for the card out of a part-time wage.

Cooling centre is a second function. A reading room at twenty-six degrees is the only air-conditioned space that a pensioner can enter without buying anything. Staff have noticed that the morning queue includes people who read a newspaper and leave at noon. The book is still there and it is borrowed less. A reader who comes for a seat takes a volume off the shelf to hold the place. A librarian who returns the unshelved books at closing time finds the same ten titles on the desks every evening.

More have been built. Three branches opened in the past two years, each with a reading room and a set of desks. Branches are in the outer districts where the rent is cheap. Students who use them travel for an hour to reach a desk. The library's budget is spent on the building rather than on the stock. A branch that buys ten thousand books a year spends most of its money on staff and electricity. Short is the acquisition list, and the readers who come for a seat rarely complain about it. A reader who finds no seat sits on the steps outside, which the staff tolerate until the fire inspector visits.

Civic infrastructure used differently from its purpose is what the building has become. Reading room was designed for a population that read at home. It now serves a population that has nowhere else to sit.
