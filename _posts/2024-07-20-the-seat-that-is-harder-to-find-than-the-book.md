A public library in July fills before it opens. A queue forms at half past eight at the door of a building that opens at nine. The queue is made of students with a bag, a bottle and a laptop.

The seat is now the scarce resource. A library that holds four hundred thousand books and eight hundred seats serves a district of a million people. The seats are taken by ten in the morning. A reader who arrives at eleven walks the reading room twice and leaves.

The competition has produced a set of customs. A bag on a chair reserves it for an hour. A book left open on a desk reserves it for the day. The staff clear the desks twice, at noon and at six. The clearing is announced over a speaker.

The library has extended its hours. A branch that closed at five now closes at nine, and one in the centre of the city stays open until midnight in July. The extra hours are staffed by a rota that the union negotiated.

The students are preparing for different exams. A graduate entrance test in December, a civil service test in the autumn, a professional qualification in September. The library is the cheapest place to study, because the alternative is a paid自习室 that charges by the hour.

The paid room has its own market. A study room near a university charges two yuan an hour for a desk with a lamp and a socket, and sells a monthly card at a discount. A student who cannot get into the library pays for the card out of a part-time wage.

The library has become a cooling centre as well. A reading room at twenty-six degrees is the only air-conditioned space that a pensioner can enter without buying anything. The staff have noticed that the morning queue includes people who read a newspaper and leave at noon.

The book is still there and it is borrowed less. A reader who comes for a seat takes a volume off the shelf to hold the place. The librarian who returns the unshelved books at closing time finds the same ten titles on the desks every evening.

The building is a piece of civic infrastructure that is used differently from what it was built for. The reading room was designed for a population that read at home. It now serves a population that has nowhere else to sit.

The city has built more of them. Three branches opened in the past two years, each with a reading room and a set of desks. The branches are in the outer districts where the rent is cheap. The students who use them travel for an hour to reach a desk.

The library's budget is spent on the building rather than on the stock. A branch that buys ten thousand books a year spends most of its money on staff and electricity. The acquisition list is short, and the readers who come for a seat rarely complain about it. A reader who finds no seat sits on the steps outside, which the staff tolerate until the fire inspector visits.
