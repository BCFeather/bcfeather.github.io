---
title: "Finland raises its flag at NATO headquarters"
date: 2023-04-04 21:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Finland became the thirty-first member of NATO on 4 April 2023, completing a process that took eleven months and doubling the alliance's land border with Russia to about 2,600 kilometres."
---

Finland's flag was raised at NATO headquarters in Brussels on 4 April, the seventy-fourth anniversary of the treaty, and the country became the thirty-first member of the alliance. The foreign minister signed the accession document in the presence of the secretary general, and the ceremony took eleven minutes. It ended a process that had begun in May 2022, when the government in Helsinki decided within a week of the invasion of Ukraine that neutrality had stopped being a policy.

The ratifications took longer than the decision. Turkey held out for ten months, demanding that Finland and Sweden extradite people it accused of terrorism and lift arms restrictions, and it approved the Finnish application on 30 March 2023 after a series of meetings that produced a memorandum rather than a treaty change. Hungary ratified on 27 March, having spent a year linking the question to its own disputes with the European Union. Budapest said it would not be rushed, and it ratified Sweden's application only in 2024, a year after the Finnish one.

The military effect is geography. Finland shares about 1,340 kilometres of border with Russia, which doubles the alliance's land frontier with that country, and it maintains a conscript army of 280,000 with mobilization reserves of 900,000. It has artillery in numbers no other European state can match, air defences built for a threat that the rest of Europe has only begun to imagine, and a defence budget that is rising towards two per cent of output, and towards three in the next parliament. Its wartime experience explains the habit: the country kept a reserve of equipment and of trained men that most members let lapse after 1991.

Russia's answer was a promise to reinforce. The foreign ministry said it would take additional measures in the north-west and that the length of the border would require new deployments, and the government announced the creation of a new military district. Whether the deployments will be real is the question analysts have asked about every Russian announcement since February 2022, because the forces that would be needed are the forces that are committed elsewhere.

Sweden is still outside. Its application was filed on the same day as Finland's, and Turkey has continued to hold it over the question of Kurdish groups and over a series of demonstrations in Stockholm. Hungary has delayed the Swedish ratification as well, and the two holdouts have turned a defence decision into a bargaining chip in domestic politics, which is the cost of an alliance that requires unanimity.

For Finland the ceremony settled a debate that had run for decades and ended it in a year. The country had fought two wars against the Soviet Union, lost a tenth of its territory, and built a foreign policy on the argument that neutrality was the only way to survive next to a larger neighbour. Membership means that the argument is now about what the alliance owes Finland rather than about whether Finland can stand alone.
