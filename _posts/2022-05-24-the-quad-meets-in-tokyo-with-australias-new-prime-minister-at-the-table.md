---
title: "The Quad meets in Tokyo with Australia's new prime minister at the table"
date: 2022-05-24 22:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Four leaders met in Tokyo on 24 May, one of them sworn in the day before, and produced a joint statement on maritime domain awareness, undersea cables and infrastructure that said nothing about Ukraine and everything about China."
---

The leaders of Australia, India, Japan and the United States met in Tokyo on 24 May, the fourth gathering of the grouping known as the Quad and the second held in person. Anthony Albanese arrived having been sworn in as prime minister of Australia on the day before, and Fumio Kishida hosted Joe Biden and Narendra Modi in a city that had been chosen for the meeting long before the Australian election result was known. The joint statement they issued ran to several pages and mentioned the war in Ukraine once, in a sentence about the United Nations charter.

Substance was about the Indo-Pacific, and the wording was careful. The four committed to a maritime domain awareness initiative that would share satellite and ship-tracking data across the region, to undersea cable projects and to infrastructure financing that offers an alternative to Chinese loans, and to a vaccine partnership and a humanitarian and disaster relief network. Each of these had been discussed for years; what May produced was a schedule and a set of working groups with dates attached.

A grouping with no treaty, no secretariat and no obligation to defend anyone survives precisely because it promises nothing to its members. India has refused to join any arrangement that looks like an alliance, and it buys Russian weapons and oil while the other three sanction Russia, so the joint statement was drafted around what all four could sign. Japanese officials described the meeting as a signal about the rules of the region; Indian officials described it as a platform for practical cooperation; American officials described it as the centrepiece of a strategy that had been announced in the previous year.

Timing gave the meeting extra weight. Biden had launched an economic framework for the region the day before in Tokyo, thirteen countries signing on to supply-chain and clean-energy commitments without market access, and he had come from a summit in Seoul where he toured a Samsung plant and pressed for more semiconductor production in allied countries. The four leaders were meeting while the war in Ukraine was reshaping assumptions about energy, food and the security of supply chains across Asia, which is the frame in which Pacific capitals read the statement.

China's response was a statement from the foreign ministry that the grouping was a small circle designed to contain it, and an editorial in the party's newspaper that compared it to a return of the cold war's alliances. The Quad's members reject that description, and the group's own weakness is the reason they can: with no obligations, it can also deliver very little that a member does not want to deliver.

What emerged was an agenda of things that can be done without a treaty: cables that need financing, ships that need tracking, vaccines that need distributing, and a statement that repeats the phrase free and open Indo-Pacific as the definition of what all four want. The grouping met for the fourth time and, as on the previous three, left no mechanism behind it except the next meeting.
