---
title: "China-ASEAN Expo opens in Nanning, focusing on RCEP opportunities for regional trade"
date: 2022-09-16 10:25:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The China-ASEAN Expo opened in Nanning, centred on RCEP implementation and plans to upgrade the free trade partnership between China and Southeast Asian nations."
---

The 19th China‑ASEAN Expo and accompanying Business and Investment Summit opened in Nanning, with the theme of sharing RCEP opportunities and advancing the China‑ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0. The annual gathering brought together government officials, business representatives and industry delegates from across Southeast Asia and China.

The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership had entered force earlier that year, cutting tariffs, simplifying rules of origin and creating a unified framework for trade across the region. Expo organisers said the fair served as a practical platform for firms to understand new RCEP provisions, connect supply chains and explore cross‑border investment.

Exhibition zones covered agricultural products, green energy, digital trade, advanced manufacturing and cross‑border logistics. Small and medium‑sized enterprises from ASEAN countries displayed coffee, fruit, handicrafts and processed food, while Chinese firms showcased new energy equipment, smart agricultural tools and digital service solutions. Many exhibitors signed framework agreements on the opening day.

Senior officials at the opening ceremony stressed that China‑ASEAN trade ties had remained resilient through pandemic disruptions. Bilateral trade volume had kept growing, and the two sides continued to deepen cooperation on industrial chains, infrastructure and people‑to‑people exchanges. Negotiations on the upgraded free trade framework aimed to cover digital economy, environmental standards and intellectual property.

Observers noted that the expo carries wider regional meaning. At a time of global slowdown and trade fragmentation, ASEAN and China have kept supply links open. Cross‑border railway routes, port cooperation and customs facilitation projects supported by the partnership help reduce delivery times and lower costs for small traders.

Challenges remain. Inflation, shipping delays and uneven post‑pandemic recovery affect different ASEAN economies in different ways. Participants agreed that further coordination on customs clearance, e‑commerce rules and green transition would help RCEP deliver its full potential. The expo will continue to act as a regular bridge for dialogue and commercial collaboration.
