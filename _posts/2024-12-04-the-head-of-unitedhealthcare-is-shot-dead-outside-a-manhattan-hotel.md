title: "The head of UnitedHealthcare is shot dead outside a Manhattan hotel"
date: 2024-12-04 22:10:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Brian Thompson, the chief executive of UnitedHealthcare, was shot outside the New York Hilton Midtown on the morning of 4 December, and a suspect was arrested five days later in Pennsylvania after a nationwide search."
---

Brian Thompson, chief executive of UnitedHealthcare, was shot and killed outside the New York Hilton Midtown on the morning of the fourth of December as he walked towards an investor conference his company was holding at the hotel. He was fifty. Police said the gunman approached from behind, fired several times and fled on foot before riding a bicycle into the park. Witnesses were few, the morning was dark, and the first images released by the police showed a man in a hooded jacket carrying a backpack.

Search that followed lasted five days and became the most watched criminal investigation in the country. Detectives released photographs from a hostel on the Upper West Side where the suspect had stayed, and from a coffee shop where he had been caught on camera smiling. Shell casings at the scene carried writing on them, and police said the words referred to insurance claims and to the industry's practice of denying coverage, which shifted the case from an ordinary homicide into an argument about the American health system.

Arrest came on the ninth of December in Altoona, Pennsylvania, where a man was recognised at a McDonald's by a customer and then by an employee. Luigi Mangione, twenty-six, was carrying a handgun with a printed receiver, a suppressor and a false identification card that matched the one used at the hostel. He was charged in New York with murder and with weapons offences, and the federal government filed its own charges, which set up a contest over which jurisdiction would try him first.

Background of the accused was unusual and was reported within days. Mangione came from a wealthy Baltimore family, had degrees from the University of Pennsylvania, and had worked in technology in the city before leaving his job and travelling. Writings found with him described the health insurance industry as a parasite and called the killing a response to its practices, and his social media accounts showed an interest in back pain, surgery and the difficulty of getting care.

Public reaction was the part that surprised the company and the police. Thousands of people posted comments that treated the killing as an act of protest, and insurers began removing photographs of their executives from their websites. Companies that run health plans announced they would restrict access to their offices, and the words on the shell casings were quoted more often than the name of the dead man. Argument that ran through the weeks afterwards was about whether the anger was justified, and almost nobody defended the industry's record on denied claims.

Case has continued long past December. Mangione was indicted on state murder charges in the first degree, which in New York requires a finding of terrorism as the motive, and his lawyers have contested the evidence and the conduct of the police. Federal charges carry the possibility of a death sentence, and the dispute over the order of the trials has gone through the courts. Insurance company, meanwhile, has had to explain in public what it does when a claim is refused, which is a conversation the industry had avoided for years.
