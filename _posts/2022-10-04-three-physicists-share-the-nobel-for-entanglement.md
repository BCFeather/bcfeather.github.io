---
title: "Three physicists share the Nobel for proving that entanglement survives the laboratory"
date: 2022-10-04 23:10:00 +0800
categories: [science]
description: "Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger tested Bell's inequality with entangled photons and turned a thought experiment into quantum information."
---

Three physicists share this year's prize for experiments with entangled photons, which established that the world cannot be described by the kind of hidden variables that Albert Einstein preferred. Alain Aspect, who is seventy-five and works at Paris-Saclay, John Clauser, who is seventy-nine and works from California, and Anton Zeilinger, who is seventy-seven and works in Vienna, were named on Tuesday morning. It is the first prize given for quantum information and the third for the family of tests that Bell's paper made possible.

A question open since 1935 was settled by their work. Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen had argued that quantum mechanics must be incomplete, and John Bell turned the argument into an inequality in 1964. The inequality says that no theory of local hidden variables can reproduce the correlations that entangled particles show. Clauser, with Stuart Freedman, measured those correlations in 1972 using photons from a lamp and a calcium source. Aspect's group repeated the test in 1982 with a switched apparatus that closed the obvious loophole.

Entanglement is the property that two particles can share a state which has no separate description for either of them. Measure one, and the other responds at a distance, though no signal passes and nothing travels faster than light. What the experiments showed is that the response cannot be explained by each particle carrying a hidden instruction from the moment they separated. The early tests worked across a few metres of table, and the later ones across hundreds of metres of cable and open air. Zeilinger's group used the property to teleport the state of a photon in 1997, and the technique became the basis of quantum cryptography.

The prize had been predicted for a decade, and the delay was partly a matter of taste. The committee has often waited for an application to appear before it rewards a result, and quantum information became an application when governments began to fund quantum computers and encrypted links. China launched a satellite for quantum key distribution in 2016, and several banks now run trials of the technology.

The three men took different roads to the same question. Clauser worked on the experiment as a graduate student, later left academic physics for a while and returned when the field began to pay attention. Aspect built the apparatus twice, the second time with fast switches that chose the measurement settings after the photons had left the source. Zeilinger came from neutron interferometry and turned to photons because they are easy to send through air and glass. All three have said that the argument among theorists mattered less to them than the apparatus on the bench.

This award does not end the argument about what quantum mechanics means. The experiments rule out a class of theories, and they leave the interpretation of the formalism exactly where it was. Aspect has said that the plain reading of the theory requires giving up either locality or the idea that a measured value exists before it is measured. What no experiment can do is send a message by entanglement alone, and every scheme that claimed to has collapsed under the same arithmetic. The prize settles the experiment and leaves the philosophy open, which is how the field has always been.
