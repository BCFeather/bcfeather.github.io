---
title: "War arrives in Khartoum"
date: 2023-04-15 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Fighting broke out in Khartoum on 15 April 2023 between the Sudanese army and the Rapid Support Forces, turning a dispute between two generals into a war that would push millions from their homes and leave the capital a battlefield."
---

Fighting began in Khartoum before dawn on 15 April, and by midday the capital had become a front line. The army and the Rapid Support Forces, two organizations that had shared power since the coup of 2021, fought over the presidential palace, the airport, the state broadcaster and the bridges across the Nile. Residents described gunfire in every district and aircraft over the city, and the airport closed with aircraft burning on the apron.

At the root of it was integration. A framework agreement signed in December 2022 had promised a civilian transition and required the two forces to merge within two years, and the question of who would command whom had not been settled. The army wanted the paramilitary force absorbed under its officers and the Rapid Support Forces wanted a slower timetable and a separate chain of command, and each side read the other's position as a plan for its own removal.

Casualty figures rose faster than the reporting could follow. The Sudanese doctors' committee said 56 civilians had been killed and nearly 600 people wounded by the second day, and the United Nations put the dead at 427 with more than 3,700 injured by 23 April. Hospitals were hit, several closed, and the teaching hospital in Khartoum ran out of blood and oxygen within a week, which the doctors' committee reported in daily statements. Water was cut to parts of Omdurman, and families carried the wounded to private clinics that had no fuel for their generators.

Both generals were products of the same state. Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, the army commander, and Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as Hemedti, had worked together to remove the civilian government in 2021 and had spent the following eighteen months dividing the security services between them. Hemedti's force grew out of the militias that fought in Darfur, and its leaders were sanctioned by the United States in 2022 for their role in the violence there. The force had also built a business empire in gold and in livestock that the merger would have put under the army's auditors.

Outside powers spent the first week moving their own people. The United States evacuated its embassy staff on 22 April in a convoy escorted by helicopters, Britain airlifted diplomats from an airfield north of the capital, and France, Germany and China followed. The corridor that made the evacuations possible was negotiated with both generals, and the negotiations revealed that neither was short of weapons or of the ability to receive a phone call.

Truces came and went within hours. The army and the Rapid Support Forces announced ceasefires for Eid, for the passage of aid, and for the evacuation of foreigners, and each was broken by shooting in the same districts. Mediators tried Jeddah in May, and the talks produced a declaration of commitments rather than a mechanism to enforce them, which is the shape that Sudan's diplomacy would take for the rest of the year, and the mediators never found a way to verify a ceasefire that both sides could deny breaking.
