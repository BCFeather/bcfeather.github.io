---
title: "McCarthy loses the speakership to eight of his own members"
date: 2023-10-03 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The House voted 216 to 210 on 3 October 2023 to remove Kevin McCarthy as Speaker, the first removal of its kind in American history, after eight Republicans joined the Democrats."
---

For the first time in the history of the House of Representatives, the Speaker was removed from office by a vote of the chamber. The motion came from Matt Gaetz of Florida, who had threatened it for weeks, and the tally was 216 to 210, with every Democrat and eight Republicans supporting the removal. McCarthy had survived a similar threat in January only by agreeing that a single member could force such a vote, a concession he made to win the gavel after fifteen ballots. The vote itself took less than an hour, and the clerk read the result to a chamber that had spent days being told it would fail.

Colleagues accused him of relying on Democratic votes to avert a government shutdown at the end of September. The continuing resolution that passed on 30 September dropped the deep spending cuts his right flank demanded and left out new money for Ukraine, and McCarthy chose to keep the government open rather than let it close. Gaetz and his allies called that a betrayal of the deal that had put McCarthy in the chair, and they were joined by members who wanted the border provisions that the Senate would never accept. The demand for those provisions signalled what the right flank wanted rather than a plan to get it.

Patrick McHenry of North Carolina became speaker pro tempore, a temporary role created after the attacks of 2001, and the House stopped doing legislative business. The chamber cannot pass bills without a Speaker, and the calendar for November includes the appropriations that fund the government after 17 November, the farm bill and the annual defence authorization. The Senate continued to work, which left the two chambers further apart than usual.

It also froze aid for Ukraine at a moment when the administration was assembling a new request. Support for Kyiv had already become conditional inside the Republican conference, and the Speaker who had promised to keep the money flowing was replaced by a caretaker with no authority to schedule a vote. Allies in Europe read the vote as a signal that American commitments were becoming unreliable.

Three candidates tried and failed to win a majority over the following weeks, and the House sat without a Speaker for twenty-two days, the longest such gap in the chamber's history. Republicans who had voted to remove McCarthy found that the same rule now worked against their own nominees, and each failed ballot hardened the split between the factions. Each ballot narrowed the field of acceptable names, and the conference settled on a member who had never mounted a leadership campaign.

McCarthy told reporters that he would not run again and that he had kept his word on the shutdown, which he treated as the reason for his fall. Eight members of a majority of 221 ended the career of the man who led it, and the rule that allowed them to do so had been written by McCarthy himself in January. The chamber has a Speaker again, and the rule that removed the last one remains in force.
