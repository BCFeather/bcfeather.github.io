---
title: "Police clear a Columbia University building occupied by protesters"
date: 2024-04-30 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Officers from the New York police entered Hamilton Hall at Columbia University on the night of 30 April and arrested more than a hundred people, ending an occupation that had begun hours earlier and a protest camp that had stood for two weeks."
---

Officers from the New York police department entered the campus of Columbia University on the night of the thirtieth of April and cleared Hamilton Hall, which had been occupied by protesters earlier in the day, along with the encampment on the lawn that had stood since the eighteenth of April. The university said more than a hundred people had been arrested, and it said the decision to call the police had been taken after the occupation of the building blocked access to a facility used by staff and students.

Protest that produced the confrontation began as a solidarity camp for Gaza. Students set up tents on the main lawn on the eighteenth of April, demanding that the university divest from companies that supply Israel and that it end its investments in weapons manufacturers. The camp grew within days, and the university's president, Minouche Shafik, authorized the police to clear it on the eighteenth, which resulted in more than a hundred arrests and which she later said had been a mistake.

Occupation of Hamilton Hall was a change in the character of the protest. The building was taken in the early hours of the thirtieth, and the protesters renamed it Hind's Hall after a child killed in Gaza. University officials said the occupation was led by people from outside the campus as well as by students, and they said it had destroyed property and prevented staff from working.

Decision to use force was criticized from both directions. Faculty members said the university had escalated a situation that could have been negotiated, and the university senate, an elected body of staff, voted to censure the president over her handling of the protests. The mayor of New York said the police had acted with restraint, and the governor said the state would not allow occupations of public institutions.

National context of the clearing was a wave of similar camps on campuses across the country. Protesters at universities in California, Texas, Michigan and elsewhere set up encampments, and more than two thousand people were arrested in April and May, according to counts by news organizations. Several universities negotiated agreements with their students to end the camps, and others cleared them with police, which produced the confrontations that dominated the news.

Consequence of the thirtieth of April is a dispute about who decides what a university is for. The protests were about the war in Gaza, and they became a contest over the limits of protest on a campus that prides itself on dissent. Commencement ceremonies were moved or cancelled at several universities, and donors said they would withhold gifts.

What the clearing established was that the institutions would defend their operations, and what it did not establish was whether the protest would change the policies the students were protesting against. Classes were moved online for the last week of the semester, and the university said it would hold its commencement exercises at a sports stadium off campus. The university's senate and several faculty bodies asked for an inquiry into the decision, and the president said she would answer questions at a public meeting in the autumn.
