---
title: "Shinzo Abe is shot while campaigning in Nara and dies in hospital"
date: 2022-07-08 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A man fires twice from behind at close range with a homemade weapon, and the former prime minister dies five hours later."
---

Shinzo Abe was shot on Friday morning in the city of Nara while he was speaking in support of a candidate in the upper house election, and he died in hospital in the early evening. The former prime minister was sixty-seven and had left office in 2020. He was shot twice from behind at a distance of a few metres, and the wounds were in the neck and the chest. The doctors said he had lost too much blood before he arrived.

A homemade weapon was used. The police said the suspect had built it from parts that were wrapped in tape, and that it fired two projectiles that were found at the scene. The man was arrested immediately and he was identified as a forty-one-year-old former employee of the maritime defence force. He told the investigators that he had a grievance against an organization that he believed Abe had supported, and he had attended a similar speech the day before.

Security arrangements are now the subject of an inquiry. Former prime ministers travel with a police detail, and the detail on Friday was smaller than the one that would accompany a serving leader. The speech was held in an open square beside a road, and the audience was standing on both sides of the speaker. The police said the officers had been positioned in front, and the suspect approached from behind. The national police agency has promised a review of the rules.

Sunday's election, in which half the upper house is chosen, is two days away and the campaign stopped for a day. The parties suspended their broadcasts and their rallies, and the prime minister said the vote would go ahead as planned. The upper house is half elected every three years, and the governing coalition was expected to keep its majority. The assassination has changed the tone of the last two days of the campaign, and the broadcasters replaced their political programmes with retrospectives.

Messages from abroad arrived immediately. The American president said he was shocked and angry, and the leaders of the European Union and of India and Australia sent condolences. The Chinese and South Korean governments also sent messages, which is remarkable for a man whose visits to a shrine for the war dead had been criticized in both countries. The Japanese stock market fell on the news and the yen, which was already weak, moved little.

What the killing does is end the career of the politician who defined the last decade of Japanese conservatism. He was the longest-serving prime minister in the country's history and the author of the economic programme that carries his name, and he remained a faction leader after he left office. The man accused of killing him had no connection to any organization that the police have named, and the country is now asking how a former leader could be approached in the open with a weapon that was built at home. The answer will shape the election that is held two days after his death.
