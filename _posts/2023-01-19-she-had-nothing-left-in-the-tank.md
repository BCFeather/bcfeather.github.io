Jacinda Ardern told her party on 19 January that she would resign as prime minister and as leader of the Labour Party, and the sentence that was quoted everywhere was that she no longer had enough in the tank. She was forty-two, had been in office for five and a half years, and had led the party through two election victories. She said she would leave by the start of February, and that she would spend the time between the announcement and the departure handing over the files.

Her record had begun with a surprise. She became prime minister in October 2017 at thirty-seven, the youngest in the country's history at the time, and within a year she was the second elected head of government in the world to give birth while in office. The images of her holding her daughter at the United Nations became part of how the country saw itself. The interviews she gave afterwards described a job in which the days were scheduled in fifteen-minute blocks, and a person who had stopped sleeping properly.

Crisis defined the years that followed. The volcanic eruption on Whakaari in December 2019 killed twenty-one. The pandemic closed the borders for two years and made the country a case study in both success and fatigue.

The political cost accumulated. Her party's support had fallen from the extraordinary level of 2020, the opposition had changed leaders, and the election due in October was widely expected to be close. She said the decision had nothing to do with the polls, and her colleagues said the same thing with less conviction. Two of her ministers had already said they would leave at the election, and the party's organization had been built around her name for three campaigns.

The caucus met on 22 January and elected Chris Hipkins, the education minister who had run the pandemic response, as the new leader. He was forty-four, had spent his career in public administration, and took office on 25 January, with Carmel Sepuloni as deputy prime minister, the first person of Pacific descent to hold the post. He had run the country's Covid response and had a reputation for competence rather than for communication, which was the opposite of his predecessor's.

Leaders resign for many reasons and few of them say the true one. The sentence about the tank was unusual because it was probably accurate, and because it was spoken by a politician who had built her reputation on stamina. The sentence about the tank was repeated so often that it became the summary of her tenure, and the summary left out the fifty-one people in Christchurch. Her party's fortunes after her departure were less kind than her own.
