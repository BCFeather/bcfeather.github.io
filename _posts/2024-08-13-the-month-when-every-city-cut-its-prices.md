---
title: "The month when every city cut its prices"
date: 2024-08-13 21:20:00 +0800
tags: [housing, china, prices, cities]
categories: [business]
description: "New home prices fell in every month of the year and second-hand prices fell in all seventy of the cities that the statistics office tracks. The measures that were supposed to stop the fall have not stopped it."
---

Seventy cities get a monthly price index from the statistics office. July figures were released in the middle of August. New home prices fell on a yearly basis in every one of them. The second-hand market is worse. A resale flat is priced by its owner rather than by a developer. An owner who needs to sell cuts until a buyer appears. Second-hand prices fell in all seventy cities for the month, a first.

Measures taken in May were designed to break the cycle. Cities were allowed to remove the floor on mortgage rates, to lower the down payment for a first home, and to buy unsold stock with public money. Effect lasted about six weeks. The stock purchase is the measure with the clearest arithmetic. A local government buys completed but unsold flats and converts them into subsidised rental housing. Purchase removes inventory from the market and adds a liability to a budget already short.

Developers are the constraint. A company that cannot sell cannot repay the loan on the land it bought in 2021. A company that cannot repay does not finish the building. A buyer who is offered a discount on an unfinished project asks about the completion date first. The buyers who remain are the ones with cash. A household that needs a mortgage is waiting for the rate to fall further. A household that has the money is waiting for the price to fall further. Both are waiting for the same thing, which is why the waiting does not end.

Rentals have absorbed part of the demand. A family that sold its flat and is renting has put a unit back into the rental stock. Rents in the large cities have risen by more than the index fell. The land sales tell the same story in a different unit. A plot that a city expected to sell for a large sum receives a bid from a state-owned platform at the reserve price, which is a sale on paper and a loss in the budget.

Demand gave way to supply in policy. The measures that are being discussed in the autumn are about completing unfinished buildings rather than about persuading people to buy them. Money for that is larger and slower. The completion obligation is the mechanism that the government has tightened. A developer must finish the building before the buyer's money is released from an escrow account. A developer who depended on that money to build cannot start.

No two cities are in the same position. A coastal city with a growing population has held its price better than an inland city that built for a boom that did not arrive, and the national index averages the two into one number. The index will be read again in September. A month of falls is a trend when it repeats, and the statisticians will publish the same seventy cities with the same method, which is the value of an index that nobody can adjust.
