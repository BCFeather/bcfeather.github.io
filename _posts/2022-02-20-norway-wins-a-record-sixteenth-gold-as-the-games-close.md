title: "Norway wins a record sixteenth gold as the Beijing Games close"
date: 2022-02-20 23:40:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "The Norwegian team finishes with 37 medals, China takes nine golds and third place, and the fortnight ends without a public in the stands."
---

The Beijing Winter Olympics closed on the evening of 20 February with Norway at the top of the table and a record in the books. The Norwegian team won 16 golds, eight silvers and thirteen bronzes, 37 medals in all, and the gold count is the most any country has taken at a single Winter Games. Germany finished second with 12 golds, and the host country took third with nine golds, four silvers and two bronzes, its best result at a Winter Games.

China's nine golds came from both ice and snow, which had not happened before. Short-track speed skating produced two, freestyle skiing two, snowboard one, figure skating one, and speed skating one, where Gao Tingyu won the 500 metres in 34.32 seconds and broke the Olympic record. Su Yiming, at seventeen, won the snowboard big air and became the country's first Olympic champion in that sport, and Gu Eileen won two golds and a silver across three freestyle events. The team sent 177 athletes, its largest Winter delegation, and competed in more than 95 per cent of the events.

The Russian team finished with 32 medals, and the figure would have been one higher had the team figure skating gold not been held back pending the case around a sample taken from one of its skaters. The Court of Arbitration for Sport had allowed the skater to continue competing in the individual event, and the federation's medal ceremony was postponed. Canada took 26 medals, the United States 25, and 29 delegations won at least one medal, against 30 four years earlier.

Five venues did the work of seven sports, and the sliding centre at Yanqing will host world championships later, and the ski area at Zhangjiakou was built for a public that had little access to snow before. The organizers said the Games were held with 2,846 athletes from 91 delegations, 109 events in fifteen disciplines, and a participation rate for women of 45.4 per cent, the highest of any Winter Games. The cost of the construction never appeared in full, and the estimate that circulated in the Chinese press was above 4 billion dollars for the venues alone.

Pandemic rules shaped the fortnight from beginning to end. No tickets were sold to the public, the stands were filled with invited spectators who were tested each day, and the athletes lived inside a loop that separated them from the city. The organizers reported a small number of positive cases among athletes and officials, and the events went ahead without postponement. The experiment answered a question that the next organizers had been asking, which was whether a Games could be held at all under those conditions.

Milan and Cortina will hold the next Winter Games in 2026, across a much wider geography and with a budget that assumes existing venues. The lesson that the organizers of 2022 leave behind is that a Games can be staged inside a bubble, that the bill for doing so is paid in atmosphere rather than in money, and that the medal table is still the part that people remember.
