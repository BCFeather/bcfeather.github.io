---
title: "Four hundred and twenty-five basis points in a year: the Fed slows to fifty"
date: 2022-12-14 22:00:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The Federal Reserve raises rates by half a point, taking the policy rate to 4.25-4.5 per cent, and signals more to come."
---
Half a percentage point was announced at two in the afternoon, and it was the seventh increase of the year. The target range moved to four and a quarter to four and a half per cent, up from near zero in March. The four previous meetings had produced increases of three quarters of a point, so the smaller step was itself the news. Officials described it in the statement as a way of allowing the effects of earlier moves to work through the economy.

Arithmetic of the year is startling when it is written in one line. Seven increases, four hundred and twenty-five basis points, and the fastest sequence of rises since the early 1980s. Mortgage rates doubled, the dollar reached parity with the euro, and the cost of borrowing for a company with a middling credit rating rose by more than the models had allowed. None of it had yet shown up in the unemployment rate, which remained near a fifty-year low.

Projections published with the decision suggested that the peak would be higher than the markets had assumed. Most officials expected the rate to reach a little above five per cent during the following year, and none of them expected a cut before 2024. That forecast was more hawkish than the one published in September, which is unusual at a meeting where the increase itself was smaller. The nineteen participants also raised their estimate of how much unemployment the campaign would require.

The chair, speaking at his press conference, refused to describe the step as a pause. He said that the committee would need to keep raising rates and then hold them for some time, and that the mistake to avoid was an early easing that allowed inflation to return. Reporters asked about the risk of going too far, and he said that the committee was watching the data rather than a calendar. He also said that no one on the committee expected a rate cut in the following year, and the sentence moved the market as much as the decision had.

Bond yields fell during the press conference, and the reason was the sentence about the pace rather than the sentence about the level. Traders had spent the previous month arguing about whether the committee would slow down, and the answer removed one uncertainty while leaving the larger one in place. By the close the two-year yield had dropped below the level it held before the meeting. Equity indices gave up their early gains in the last hour of trading, which is what happens when a rally meets a forecast.

The economy that the rate will act upon is a strange one. Growth had been positive for two quarters, consumer spending had held up, and the labour market was still adding jobs at a rate that would have been considered healthy in any other year. Inflation was coming down slowly from a peak of more than nine per cent, and the last mile, as the chair called it, is the part that costs the most. Nobody in the room expected that mile to be covered quickly, and nobody in the room could say how long it was.
