---
title: "Trump's fourth indictment arrives in Georgia"
date: 2023-08-15 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A grand jury in Fulton County, Georgia, indicted Donald Trump and eighteen others on 14 August 2023 on charges of racketeering and conspiracy over attempts to overturn the state's 2020 result, the fourth criminal case against the former president."
---

Fulton County's grand jury returned an indictment on the evening of 14 August that named nineteen defendants and forty-one counts, and the first name on it was Donald Trump. The charges against him run to thirteen, led by a count under Georgia's racketeering statute, the law written for mob prosecutions, which allows prosecutors to describe a set of acts by a group of people as a single enterprise. The district attorney, Fani Willis, said the defendants had worked as a criminal organization to change the result of an election that Georgia had already counted three times.

The racketeering charge is the structural innovation, and it changes the politics of the case. Georgia's law carries a minimum sentence of five years and cannot be pardoned by a president, because state convictions lie outside federal pardon power. It also lets Willis tell a story that federal prosecutors have told in narrower pieces, connecting the pressure calls to the secretary of state, the false electors, the breach of voting machines in Coffee County and the harassment of an election worker.

The cast is wider than any previous indictment. It includes the former mayor of New York, the former White House chief of staff, several lawyers who filed the suits, and a justice department official who drafted a letter telling Georgia's legislature to convene and appoint electors. It also includes people who had been treated as witnesses in other investigations, which is the part of the filing that has caused the most alarm among the defendants' lawyers. Several of them had given statements to federal prosecutors in Washington earlier in the year.

Surrender was arranged quickly. Willis gave the defendants until noon on 25 August to appear at the county jail, and she said she would seek a trial date within six months. Trump's bond was set at two hundred thousand dollars, the highest of the four cases he faces, with conditions that bar him from commenting on witnesses, co-defendants and court staff, and he was photographed and fingerprinted at the jail before his release. The mugshot was published within the hour and appeared on merchandise within the day.

Four cases now run in parallel, in Washington, Florida, New York and Georgia, with different judges and different calendars. Trump's lawyers have moved to have the federal election case dismissed and to remove the judge, and they have said they will seek to move the Georgia case to federal court. Each motion buys time, and time is the resource that a campaign has more of than a prosecutor's office. The four cases also compete for witnesses, for evidence and for the public's attention, and each team of lawyers knows it.

A state prosecutor in a county courthouse has done what two federal investigations and a special counsel had not, by treating the attempt to overturn an election as a conspiracy with named participants. Whether a jury agrees will be decided in a courtroom in Atlanta, and the date will be set by a judge who is standing for election next year.
