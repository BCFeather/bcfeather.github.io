Beijing hosted the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation from the fourth to the sixth of September. Fifty-three delegations attended. Meeting is held every three years in a capital that alternates between the two partners. The pledge was counted in yuan. A figure of three hundred and sixty billion yuan over three years was announced, which is a little under the sum that was pledged at the previous meeting.

Money is divided into categories. A part is lent at concessional rates, a part is granted. A part is credit for trade. Three categories have different terms. Communique does not separate them in the headline number. The projects are counted in kilometres. A railway that links a port to a capital is the kind of project that the forum is known for. Lines signed this year include one in a landlocked country and a corridor that serves a mining region.

Debt is the subject handled carefully. A country that borrowed for a railway and cannot service the loan asks for a restructuring. Answer involves a bank, a finance ministry and a set of terms that are renegotiated rather than forgiven. The critical minerals are the new item on the agenda. A country with lithium or cobalt is being asked to sign a supply agreement alongside the infrastructure. Agreement is about the export of ore rather than about the building of a road.

African position has changed. A decade ago the delegations came for the finance. This year several came with a list of conditions about local processing and local employment. A refinery that is built in the country that mines the ore is the demand that the visitors make. The security item is smaller and it is not new. A peacekeeping contribution and a police training programme are part of the package. Recipients are the countries where a Chinese company has a mine.

Summit is also a schedule. A head of state who attends has four bilateral meetings in two days. Meetings that matter are held in the hotel rather than in the hall. The forum was founded in 2000 and it has met every three years since. Early meetings were about tariffs and about a preferential trade arrangement. Later ones have been about infrastructure and about debt.

Trade is the measure published quarterly. Two-way trade runs to hundreds of billions of dollars a year. Balance has moved from a surplus for one side to a surplus for the other within a decade. Agriculture is the item that the smaller delegations care about. A country that exports cocoa or coffee wants a processing plant and a buyer, and an agreement that guarantees a price for three years is worth more to a farmer than a loan for a road.

Students are the part not counted in the pledge. A scholarship programme brings a few thousand African students to Chinese universities each year, and the graduates are the people who negotiate the next agreement. The follow-up is the test. A pledge that is announced in September is disbursed over three years, and the projects that are finished are counted by the ministry that signed them. Next meeting will publish that count.
