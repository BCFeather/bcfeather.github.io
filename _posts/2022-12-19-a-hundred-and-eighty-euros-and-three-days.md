---
title: "A hundred and eighty euros and three days"
date: 2022-12-19 21:00:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "European Union energy ministers agree a gas price cap of 180 euros per megawatt hour, with a trigger that few expect to be hit."
---
The cap was agreed in Brussels on 19 December, after a meeting that ran into the evening and a series of drafts that had been rewritten for weeks. It sets a ceiling of 180 euros per megawatt hour for month-ahead contracts on the Dutch exchange, which is the reference price for gas across the continent. The mechanism is called a market correction, and it is designed to switch on rather than to stay on. Fifteen member states voted for the text, and the ones that opposed it were outnumbered in a qualified majority. Ministers had been arguing since October, and the text had been rewritten four times on the day itself, and two earlier meetings had ended without a text at all.

Its trigger was the part that mattered most, and it was loosened twice before it passed. The cap applies when the front-month price stays above 180 euros for three working days and, at the same time, sits more than thirty-five euros above a reference price for liquefied natural gas. Once it is triggered, trades are limited for twenty working days, and the mechanism ends if the conditions stop being met. That second condition was added because several governments feared a cap that would leave Europe unable to attract cargoes.

Opposition came from the north and from the market. Germany, the Netherlands and Denmark had argued that a ceiling would frighten suppliers away and leave the continent short in a cold winter. They accepted the final text after the trigger was tightened, and their finance ministers said in public that the mechanism remained a risk. Traders said the same thing in fewer words, and one of them described the scheme as a ceiling nobody would ever hit. Traders pointed out that the reference price for liquefied natural gas is itself set in a market that the mechanism does not touch.

What the mechanism is meant to address is the price that filled storage and emptied wallets. Gas had traded above three hundred euros in August, and it fell back through the autumn as storage filled and the weather stayed mild. Households were still paying the bills from the summer, and governments had spent hundreds of billions of euros on subsidies that the cap is meant to make unnecessary.

Smaller economies had pushed hardest, and their argument was about the single market. A country that buys gas on the Dutch exchange pays the price set there, even if its own supply comes from a pipeline that costs far less. That transmission is the reason the mechanism exists, and it is also the reason the countries with pipelines opposed it. The compromise satisfied neither camp completely, which is what compromises do, and the commission promised a report to the member states before the review.

It applies from February, and it will be reviewed in the spring. By then the winter will have shown whether the ceiling was needed, and the storage figures will have shown whether it frightened anyone away. Ministers left Brussels with a number and a set of conditions, and with an agreement that both camps described as better than nothing.
