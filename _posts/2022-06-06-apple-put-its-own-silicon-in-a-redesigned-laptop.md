---
title: "Apple put its own silicon in a redesigned laptop"
date: 2022-06-06 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [technology]
description: "At its developers' conference the company announced a second generation of its own processor, a thinner MacBook Air built around it, and a set of software features that assume an iPhone is always within reach."
---

Apple opened its annual developers' conference on 6 June with a chip. The M2 is the second generation of the processor the company began designing after it decided to leave Intel behind, and the first machine built around it is a redesigned MacBook Air that weighs a little over a kilogram. The keynote ran for nearly two hours and spent most of its time on software, which is the pattern the conference has followed since the pandemic pushed it online. The chip was announced before any machine using it had reached reviewers, which is the order Apple has followed since it began designing its own processors.

Specifications released afterwards describe a chip with twenty billion transistors, manufactured on a refined five-nanometre process, with memory bandwidth of a hundred gigabytes a second. Apple claims an eighteen per cent improvement in processor performance over the M1 and a thirty-five per cent improvement in graphics, which are the kinds of figures that arrive with every generation and that reviewers test within a week.

The laptop is the part most people will notice. The wedge shape is gone, replaced by a flat case with a notch in the screen, a MagSafe charging port and a fan that does not exist, because the chip does not need one. Apple quotes eighteen hours of video playback, which is roughly twice what the Intel version of the same machine managed two years ago. Colours were added, including a dark blue that the company's photographs placed against a white background.

Software announced at the conference assumes that the phone is the centre of the system. A Mac can now use an iPhone as its camera without a cable, a feature called Continuity Camera that turns a phone into a webcam, and the password system moves towards keys stored on devices rather than strings typed into boxes. The next version of the desktop system, Ventura, adds a window manager that groups applications into stacks, and the next version of the mobile system rebuilds the lock screen around widgets.

Strategically, the announcement marks the point where Apple's consumer line no longer depends on anyone else's processors. The transition was promised in June 2020 and took two years, and the machines that shipped in the interval converted a large share of the installed base. Intel's share of the personal computer market has been falling for a decade, and the company's own road map slipped twice during the period Apple spent designing its replacement. Microsoft and Qualcomm have made the same bet on processors designed for thin machines, and their results have been mixed.

The redesign shows how much of a computer's shape follows from its chip. Removing the fan, shrinking the battery and thinning the case all became possible once the processor stopped generating the heat that Intel's parts did, and the result is a machine that looks like the Air did in 2010 rather than like the one it replaces. The machine was priced at eleven hundred and ninety-nine dollars and ships in July.
