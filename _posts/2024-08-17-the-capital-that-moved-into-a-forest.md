Indonesia marked eighty years of independence on the seventeenth of August with a ceremony in Jakarta and a second ceremony in a place that has been a capital for less than a year on paper.

Jakarta's problem is water in two directions. The rain that falls on the city has nowhere to go. The sea that surrounds it is rising. A sea wall is being built along the northern shore at a cost that exceeds the new capital's first phase.

The toll road to the site took two years. A road that crosses a forest needs a permit for every kilometre of cleared land. The permits were issued faster than the environmental review that normally precedes them.

The new capital is called Nusantara. It sits in East Kalimantan on land that was rainforest until the diggers arrived. The first phase covers a government district of offices and housing. The rest of the plan runs to 2045.

The move was announced in 2019 with two arguments. Jakarta is sinking, at a rate that reaches ten centimetres a year in the north of the city. The island of Java holds more than half the country's population on seven per cent of its land.

The construction has been paid for by the state budget and by investors who were promised a role. The foreign money has been slower than the plan assumed. The project has been financed mostly from domestic sources so far.

The ceremony was the first test of the site. A few thousand guests were flown in, a road was finished ahead of schedule. The hotels that do not exist yet were replaced by tents and by ships moored in the bay.

The forest is the part that the environmentalists watch. The site was chosen partly because it was degraded land. The surveys found that a large share of it had been logged before the project began. The new city has displaced fewer people than a site near a village would have.

The people who will move are the civil servants. A ministry that relocates has to move its staff and their families. A family that has lived in Jakarta for a generation is being asked to move to a province where the schools are new.

The cost of living is the argument against it. A city that is being built from nothing has prices that are set by the construction, and a civil servant on a fixed salary in Jakarta will pay more for a meal in Nusantara than in the neighbourhood he left.

The water is the open question. A city in a forest needs a supply that does not depend on a river that the mining upstream has already silted. The reservoir was built, and the catchment that feeds it is still being cleared.

The next phase is scheduled for the next president. The project was launched by one administration and will be finished or slowed by the next, and the ceremony on the seventeenth was the last one that the outgoing president will attend as the head of state.
