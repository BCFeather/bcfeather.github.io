---
title: "COP27 ends with a fund for climate losses and a quarrel about the cause"
date: 2022-11-20 20:00:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "COP27 closes in Sharm el-Sheikh with a fund for climate losses, and with very little agreed on the cause of those losses."
---

The conference ran into overtime, as these conferences do, and the final text was adopted in the small hours of 20 November. Delegates from nearly two hundred countries had been meeting for two weeks in a resort on the Red Sea, and the negotiation had gone past its deadline by more than a day. The decision that mattered most created a fund for losses and damages, money for the countries that are hit hardest by a climate they did little to cause. Officials from island states had arrived with the same demand they had carried to every conference since 1992.

The idea had been on the table for thirty years. Small island states had asked for compensation when the treaty was signed in 1992, and the rich countries had refused for most of the time since. In Sharm el-Sheikh the refusal finally gave way, partly because the presidency of the conference belonged to Egypt and the pressure from the African group was steady, and partly because the physical evidence of the past year had been hard to argue with. Floods in Pakistan and drought in the Horn of Africa had both been described in the opening speeches as invoices.

That fund exists on paper rather than in cash. The text asked for a transitional committee of twenty-four members, and set the next conference, a year later in Dubai, as the deadline for finishing the design. Contributors, eligible recipients and the scale of the money were all left to that committee, and the delegates who were most pleased with the outcome were the ones most careful to say that nothing had yet been paid. A fund without a figure is a promise, and promises of this kind have been made before.

Other parts of the package were weaker. The text referred to a phase-down of unabated coal and repeated the language of Glasgow, and several countries had pushed for a commitment to phase out all fossil fuels. That sentence did not survive, and the oil producers who objected won a reference to low-emission energy instead. The phrase has been read in different ways since, which is the point of phrases of that kind. Delegates from the European Union said publicly that the outcome was insufficient, and signed it anyway.

Adaptation money fell short as well. The pledge made in Glasgow to double adaptation finance by 2025 remained a pledge, and the total committed in Sharm el-Sheikh fell well short of what the affected countries had asked for. Several delegations said that the new fund, whatever it eventually held, could not replace the steady finance that was promised years ago and delivered slowly. A finance minister from a Pacific state made the point in one sentence: his country needed money for sea walls before it needed money for funerals.

By the time the plenary ended, the sun was coming up over the water, and the officials walked out into a resort that had been booked for a week it no longer needed. The fund exists now as a paragraph with a deadline, which is how most things in this process begin.
