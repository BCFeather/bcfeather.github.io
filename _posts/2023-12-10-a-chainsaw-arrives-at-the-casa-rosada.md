title: "A chainsaw arrives at the Casa Rosada"
date: 2023-12-10 22:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Javier Milei was sworn in as Argentina's president on 10 December 2023, promising a shock programme of spending cuts, a devalued currency and the closure of ministries, in a country with inflation above 160 per cent and reserves below zero."
---

Javier Milei took the oath of office on 10 December in the chamber of the Argentine congress, and walked to the Casa Rosada through a crowd that had brought chainsaws to the square outside. The libertarian economist, who had been a member of congress for two years and a television panellist for a decade, won the November runoff by twelve points on a promise to shrink the state to a size it had not had since the 1950s. He carried a chainsaw in the campaign and did not leave it at the door.

The economic inheritance was set out by his own team in the days before the handover. Annual inflation had passed 160 per cent, the central bank's net reserves were negative, the fiscal deficit stood at about five per cent of output, and price controls had produced shortages of fuel, medicine and milk. Forty per cent of the population was below the poverty line, and the parallel exchange rate was roughly twice the official one, which meant that every import was a negotiation and every export was a subsidy.

His first decisions were taken within hours. He signed a decree cutting the number of ministries from eighteen to nine, devalued the official peso by more than half and let it float in a narrow band, cut transport and energy subsidies, and announced that the central bank would stop financing the treasury. The measures were presented as a shock that would be felt immediately and would hurt, a sentence his ministers repeated in every interview of the first week.

Politics is where the plan meets its limit. His party holds seven of 257 seats in the lower house and none of the provincial governorships, which means that legislation requires allies he does not have. He governs by decree where the constitution lets him, and by the emergency powers that congress grants in a crisis, and the first test will be whether the provinces accept an adjustment that removes transfers they have depended on for decades.

Society was divided in the way the vote suggested. The young and the self-employed voted for him in numbers that surprised pollsters, and the public sector unions, the teachers and the pensioners who lost most in the first round of cuts were already on the streets by the second week. The unions called a general strike for January, the first in five years, and the government answered that it would not negotiate with organizations it described as part of the caste.

The plan's arithmetic is the part that no decree can settle. Removing the fiscal deficit in a year without touching the largest items of spending means cutting pensions, subsidies and provincial transfers at once, which is a political operation rather than an economic one. Milei's argument is that the alternative is hyperinflation, and that a country which has changed its currency six times in four decades, and defaulted on its debts nine times, has run out of gentle options. The first quarter of 2024 will show whether the country agrees.
