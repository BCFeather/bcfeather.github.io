---
title: "A majority assembled in five days"
date: 2022-11-24 23:00:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Anwar Ibrahim is sworn in as Malaysia's tenth prime minister, twenty-five years after he was dismissed as deputy prime minister."
---
The royal palace announced the decision at midday, and the ceremony followed at five in the afternoon. Anwar Ibrahim took the oath in front of the king, in a hall that had seen four prime ministers in four years. He was seventy-five years old, and the appointment came twenty-five years after he had been removed from the deputy prime ministership and jailed on charges he always said were political.

Election day on 19 November had produced no majority, and for five days the country had no government. The largest bloc was led by Anwar's coalition, and the second was led by the party of the former prime minister, and neither could reach one hundred and twelve seats on its own. The king then exercised a discretion that the constitution gives him, and he called the party leaders to the palace one after another. The palace had asked for calm while the negotiations continued, and the streets of Kuala Lumpur stayed quiet through the weekend.

A unity arrangement of a kind the country had never tried was the result. Two deputy prime ministers were appointed, one from each of the two main partners, and the cabinet had to hold together parties that had spent the campaign attacking one another. Smaller parties from Borneo joined as well, and their support was worth the price of a few ministries.

Anwar's programme, as he described it in his first press conference, was short and domestic. He said his priorities were the cost of living, the stability of the currency, and the fight against corruption that had been the theme of his campaigns for thirty years. He also promised to review the laws that had been used against him, and to bring the country's prosecutors under closer scrutiny.

The economy he inherits is in reasonable shape and under pressure at the same time. Growth had been recovering after the pandemic, and inflation was hurting households that had spent their savings during the lockdowns. The ringgit had weakened against the dollar through the year, and the budget deficit was larger than the government had promised. Officials in the finance ministry had already begun to prepare a revised budget, because the one passed in October assumed a government that no longer existed.

Foreign policy will be watched closely, because Malaysia sits between two large trading partners and had been drifting towards one of them. Anwar said the country would remain non-aligned and would keep its relations with both, and he used the word balance more than once. The markets took the appointment calmly, and the stock exchange closed slightly higher. By the evening the new prime minister had been photographed with the king, and the palace had issued a statement asking the country to accept the outcome; five days is a short time in the history of a country that spent decades under one party, and the coalition that emerged was assembled in that time. Business groups said in public that they welcomed the appointment, and their statements mentioned stability more often than any policy.
