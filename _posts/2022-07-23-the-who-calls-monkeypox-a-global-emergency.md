---
title: "The WHO calls monkeypox a global emergency as the cases pass sixteen thousand"
date: 2022-07-23 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The director general overrules his own committee for the second time in three years, and the outbreak is concentrated among men who have sex with men."
---

The director general of the World Health Organization declared the monkeypox outbreak a public health emergency of international concern on Saturday, and he did so against the advice of the committee that he had convened. The committee had met on Thursday and had failed to reach a consensus, and the director general said that the decision was his to make. It is the second time in three years that he has used the power, the first being for the pandemic in January 2020. The declaration is the highest alert that the organization can issue.

Numbers at the time of the decision were about sixteen thousand cases in more than seventy countries. The outbreak began in May among people who had travelled to and from countries where the virus is endemic, and it has spread through close contact in Europe and in the Americas. Most of the cases have been in men who have sex with men, and the health agencies have said that the pattern is a feature of the network through which it spread rather than of the virus itself. Close skin contact passes the virus, and so do bedding and clothing, which is why the sexual networks of the outbreak carry it so efficiently.

Disease is usually mild and it has killed a small number of people. The symptoms are a fever, a swelling of the lymph nodes and a rash that turns into blisters, and the illness lasts two to four weeks. The cases in this outbreak have presented with lesions in places that the older descriptions did not emphasize, and the doctors have said that the presentation is unusual. The vaccines that were stockpiled for smallpox are being offered to the contacts of the cases in several countries.

Declaration changes the obligations of the member states. The organization can now issue recommendations on surveillance, on the isolation of the cases and on the sharing of the vaccines, and the states are asked to report their measures. The funds for the response are limited and the organization has asked the donors for more. The vaccines are made by a single company in Denmark, and the supplies are smaller than the demand.

Criticism of the organization is that it acted slowly. The committee had met twice before and had declined to make the declaration, and the director general said that the decision was finely balanced. The activists who had been pressing for the alert said the delay had cost the chance to contain the outbreak, and the officials said that the outbreak had already spread beyond the point where containment was possible. The two arguments are the ones that were made in 2020 as well.

What the declaration does is give the response a legal frame and a budget line. It does not produce vaccines or tests by itself, and it does not make the governments act. The outbreak is concentrated in a group that has been stigmatized before, and the health agencies have been careful about the language they use. The next month will show whether the alert changes the behaviour of the governments, and the answer will be visible in the numbers that are reported.
