---
title: "Sadr withdraws from politics and his followers fight the army in Baghdad"
date: 2022-08-30 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The cleric announces that he is leaving public life, the green zone is stormed, and thirty people are killed before he tells his supporters to go home."
---

The cleric Muqtada al-Sadr announced on Monday that he was withdrawing from political life, and within an hour his supporters had entered the green zone in Baghdad and reached the palace that the government uses for ceremonies. The militias that are loyal to him fought the army and the units of the popular mobilization in the streets around the zone, and the exchanges of fire continued through the night. The health ministry said thirty people had been killed and several hundred wounded. The curfew was imposed at seven in the evening and it was lifted the following day.

The immediate cause was the resignation of the parliament. Sadr had won the largest bloc in the election of October, and he had been unable to form a government with the parties that he was willing to work with. He told his members to resign in June, and the chamber has been unable to elect a speaker or a president since. The parties that are close to Iran have been trying to form a government without him, and their candidate for the prime minister has been rejected by the parties that are close to him.

At the same time, the difficult part is the militia. Sadr's movement has a political wing and an armed wing, and the two have been intertwined since the Americans invaded in 2003. He has used street protests to block his opponents before, and he has used the threat of violence to win concessions. On Monday his statement was read as a signal to his supporters rather than as an instruction to his armed men, and the crowds that moved towards the zone had no plan beyond the gate.

Meanwhile, the calm came from the same man who had caused the trouble. On Tuesday afternoon he appeared in a video and told his supporters to leave the streets within an hour, and they did. The army withdrew from the zone and the curfew was lifted, and the government's offices reopened. The analysts said the episode had shown both the strength and the weakness of the movement, which can mobilize a crowd in an hour and cannot control what the crowd does.

Beyond the violence, the context is the formation of a government that has been stalled for ten months. The country has a caretaker cabinet and a budget that has not been passed, and the oil revenue that pays for everything is being spent without a vote. The parties that are close to Iran want a prime minister who will protect their interests, and Sadr wants one who will not. The two blocs have been negotiating through the speaker of the parliament and through the militias, and neither has accepted the other's candidate.

What the week shows is a political system that has no way to resolve a tie. The constitution was written to share power among the communities, and the election produced a result that the sharing cannot accommodate. The street has become the instrument that the parties use when the chamber fails, and the street has its own logic. The next government will be formed by the parties that stayed in the chamber, and it will be formed without the man who has just left it.
