description: "Two explosions near the grave of Qasem Soleimani in Kerman on 3 January killed nearly a hundred people during a memorial procession, and Islamic State claimed the attack a day later, the deadliest strike inside Iran since the revolution of 1979."
---

On the afternoon of the third of January, a procession of pilgrims was walking along the road to the cemetery in Kerman where Qasem Soleimani is buried, on the fourth anniversary of the drone strike that killed him, when two explosions went off about twenty minutes apart. The first blast was near the gate of the cemetery, the second a few hundred metres further along the route, placed so that people running from the first would meet the second. Iranian officials put the death toll at close to a hundred, with more than two hundred wounded, and described it as the worst attack of its kind inside the country since the revolution. The bodies were counted through the night in Kerman's hospitals, and the state television that broadcast the funerals the next day did not need to explain why the coffins were so small.

Islamic State claimed the attack on the following day, through a statement on one of its channels, and named the two men it said had carried it out. That claim was awkward for a government that had spent the first hours pointing at other enemies, and Iranian officials responded by saying the bombers had been directed by foreign intelligence, a formulation that keeps the blame where the political system wants it. What actually happened is probably simpler and harder. The group that calls itself Islamic State Khorasan has recruited across the border in Afghanistan for a decade, has cells inside Iran, and has struck Iranian targets before, including a shooting at a shrine in Shiraz in 2022 and a bombing at a memorial in the same city. Kerman fits that pattern closely, and the choice of a crowd gathered around a martyr's grave fits it even more closely.

Timing explains the anxiety that spread through the region within hours. The attack came during a war in Gaza, with Houthi missiles falling on shipping in the Red Sea and exchanges of fire across the Israel-Lebanon border, and any bombing inside Iran raises the question of whether Iran itself would retaliate and against whom. Several governments in the region spent the first day trying to work out whether the target had been chosen by Tehran's enemies or by its own jihadist opponents, because the two answers lead in opposite directions. When Islamic State claimed it, the temperature dropped, and the risk of a wider war receded a little. That is a small mercy measured in the currency of geopolitics, and it is the kind of mercy that leaves the families in Kerman with nothing.

There is a domestic dimension that the official response will try to bury. Soleimani was a national figure whose funeral in 2020 drew millions into the streets, and the crowds that gather at his grave each January are a test of how much of that loyalty the system can still mobilise. A bombing at that gathering strikes at the ritual itself, which is why the authorities moved so quickly to blame outsiders and to close the sites of the memorial. The security services that failed to stop two men with explosives on a route they could predict will be examined behind closed doors, and the answer will probably be that the route was too long, the crowd too large, and the checkpoints too thin to make the gathering safe against an attacker who was willing to die.

What happens next is the part that always happens. Funerals, an investigation, arrests announced within weeks, and a public argument about whether the state's security apparatus is strong or merely visible. For the region, the attack will be remembered as the moment when the war that had been fought through proxies arrived, for one afternoon, inside a country that had been spared the worst of it. For the people of Kerman, it will be remembered as the anniversary when the road to the cemetery became a place where something exploded, which is a change in the meaning of a place that no investigation can undo.
