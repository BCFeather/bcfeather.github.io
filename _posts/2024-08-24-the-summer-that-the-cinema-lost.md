---
title: "The summer that the cinema lost"
date: 2024-08-24 21:50:00 +0800
tags: [film, china, retail]
categories: [culture]
description: "The summer season closed below last year's takings by a fifth. The tickets were cheaper, the screens were the same, and the audience that used to come twice came once."
---

The summer season closed in the last week of August with a total below last year's by about a fifth. The season runs from the first of June to the end of August. It is the two months that a distributor plans the year around.

The tickets were cheaper this year. A promotion that cut the average price by a few yuan ran through July. The takings fell anyway, which means that the number of tickets fell by more than the price did.

The films were a mixed set. Two large releases carried the season, one in the first week of July and one in the middle of August. The weeks between them were filled with titles that opened and closed within a fortnight.

The audience has changed shape. A family with two children buys four tickets and a bucket of popcorn. A family that is watching its spending waits for the streaming release. The wait is now ninety days rather than a year.

The screens are the industry's fixed cost. A country with twelve thousand screens needs a film that fills them. A film that opens on four thousand screens and earns nothing in the second week leaves the exhibitor with the rent.

The marketing has moved to the short video. A trailer that runs thirty seconds is cut for a phone screen. The campaign is measured in views rather than in posters. A film that trends for a day sells tickets for a week.

The cinema is also a property business. A multiplex in a shopping mall pays a rent that is calculated on the square metre. A mall that loses its cinema loses the footfall that the restaurants depend on.

The staff are paid by the hour and they are the first cost that is cut. A cinema that runs eight screens with four staff on a weekday closes two of them in the afternoon. The projectionist who used to be a technician is now a button on a screen.

The releases that were postponed are the part that the exhibitors complain about. Three titles moved to the national holiday in October, which is a stronger week. The summer lost the films that would have filled the gap.

The exhibitor is the party that carries the loss. A distributor takes a share of the ticket that falls as the weeks pass. A cinema takes the rest, which means that a film that opens badly is a film that the cinema paid for.

The subsidy came back in the middle of the season. A programme that pays a few yuan of every ticket ran in two provinces, and the effect was visible in the second week of July and gone by August.

The second-tier cities are the ones that fell most. A market that grew by building multiplexes in smaller cities has more screens than the films can fill, and the occupancy in a county town on a Tuesday evening is eight per cent.

The season ended with a total that the industry will describe as a correction. A market that grew for twenty years has stopped growing, and the correction is the word that is used before the numbers are revised down again.
