date: 2022-01-12 23:35:00 +0800

description: "The prime minister apologized for a gathering in May 2020 that he said he had believed was a work event, hours before the civil servant investigating the parties published her terms of reference."
---

Boris Johnson apologized in the Commons on 12 January for attending a gathering in the garden of Downing Street on 20 May 2020, at a time when his own rules forbade meeting more than one person outdoors. He said he had believed at the time that the event was a work meeting, that he had stayed for about twenty-five minutes, and that he offered his sincere apologies for the anger it had caused.

Details are unhelpful to that account. There were bottles of wine, a suitcase of beer and a swing in the garden decorated for a birthday, according to witnesses whose accounts reached the press that week, and the invitation had asked guests to bring their own alcohol. The prime minister's staff had circulated emails about the event that the investigating team later obtained, and one of them included a reference to a social gathering.

The political damage comes from the contrast rather than from the party. Britain had spent 2020 and 2021 under restrictions that kept families from visiting dying relatives, and the government had repeatedly told the public that the rules applied to everyone. A prime minister who attended a party and then explained it as a work event was inviting the country to decide whether he had been fooled or had lied, and neither answer is survivable for long. Labour's attack line was simpler: the rules had been written by the same people who broke them, and the prime minister's authority rested on his own guidance.

Timing compounded the problem. Johnson had spent the week defending the government over other gatherings, including one the night before the Duke of Edinburgh's funeral, and several Conservative members of parliament had begun to brief against him. The civil service investigator, Sue Gray, published her terms of reference that afternoon, which meant that every further explanation would be tested against documents she could read.

Rebellion did not materialise in January. Conservative members who disliked the prime minister counted the alternative leaders and found none they preferred, and the opposition benches could threaten a vote only if the police opened investigations, which they soon did. The immediate effect was to make the prime minister's authority a matter of daily speculation rather than of policy. He would survive the police questionnaires and a fine, and the civil servant's report would describe a culture in which the rules did not apply to the people who wrote them. By the summer the police had issued more than a hundred fines to staff, the prime minister among them, and the affair had cost him the backing of several newspapers and the ability to set the agenda for weeks.

A lockdown party is a small event in a large government, and the reaction was large because the rule was the whole point. The prime minister had told the country that it could not see its families, and his staff had bought wine for a party in the garden; the explanation he offered asked the public to believe that he did not know what he was attending.
