title: "Benedict XVI dies at ninety-five in the monastery his resignation built"
date: 2022-12-31 22:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Benedict XVI dies at ninety-five in the monastery where he had lived since he became the first pope to resign in six centuries."
---
Vatican announcement of the death came at 9.34 in the morning on 31 December, in a statement that was shorter than the obituaries that followed. He died in the monastery inside the Vatican gardens where he had lived since 2013, and he was ninety-five. Pope Francis had visited him days earlier and had spoken about him at a general audience. The funeral was set for 5 January, and Francis said he would preside. Flowers were laid at the gate of the monastery within an hour of the announcement, and the square outside was full by noon.

His resignation in February 2013 was the act that defined him. No pope had resigned since 1415, and the decision took the church and the world by surprise. He announced it in Latin, in a consistory that had been called to discuss something else, and he said that his strength was no longer equal to the task. He was eighty-five at the time, and the last years of his pontificate had been marked by a scandal over documents and by a sense that the machinery of the Vatican had outgrown him.

It created an office that had no rules. A pope emeritus had never existed, and the church had to invent his title, his clothing and his place in protocol. He lived in a converted monastery and promised to remain hidden, and he kept that promise for most of the decade. He wrote books, gave a few interviews, and said in one of them that the church had to confront the abuse crisis that was consuming it. The title he chose was pope emeritus, and the ring he wore was returned to the archive in a ceremony that the Vatican filmed.

His theology was the work of a German professor, and it never stopped being that. He wrote about liturgy, about the relationship between faith and reason, and about the Church as a community that had to be corrected by its own tradition. As the prefect of the doctrine office he had been the enforcer of the previous pontificate, and as pope he continued that work with a lighter hand. The people who disagreed with him described a church turned inwards, and the people who agreed described a church that had remembered what it was.

The abuse crisis was the hardest part of his legacy. A report published in Germany in 2022 examined the handling of cases in his home archdiocese, and it found failures that his defenders said he had not known about and his critics said he had. He asked for forgiveness in a letter, and the letter was received as too late by some of the people it addressed. The report had been commissioned by the archdiocese itself, and its publication was the first time a German diocese had opened its files to outside researchers.

Tributes came from the officials who had worked with him and from the theologians who had argued with him for fifty years. The tributes from his successor were warmer than the ones they had exchanged in public while both of them held office. A resignation that lasted nine years ends differently from a death, and the church that watched it will have to work out what a retired pope leaves behind.
