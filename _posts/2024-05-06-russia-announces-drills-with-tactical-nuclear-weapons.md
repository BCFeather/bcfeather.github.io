---
title: "Russia announces drills with tactical nuclear weapons"
date: 2024-05-06 22:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The Russian defence ministry said on 6 May that it would hold exercises simulating the use of non-strategic nuclear weapons in the southern military district, and attributed the decision to statements by Western officials about sending troops to Ukraine."
---

Russian defence ministry announced on the sixth of May that it would hold exercises in the coming weeks to practise the preparation and use of non-strategic nuclear weapons, and it said the drills would involve missile units of the southern military district, aviation and the navy. Statement attributed the decision to what it described as provocative statements by Western officials, including a suggestion by the French president that troops from NATO countries might be sent to Ukraine.

Scale of the exercise was left vague. Ministry said it would practise the delivery of special ammunition, which is the term used for nuclear warheads, and that the purpose was to confirm the readiness of units and equipment. Analysts noted that announcements of this kind have generally preceded movements of short-range missile units rather than the actual use of weapons, and that the value lies in the signal rather than in the training.

Weapons involved in such drills are the ones that have worried planners in NATO for two years. Russia has a large stock of warheads for short-range missiles, artillery and air-launched weapons, and it has said it moved some of them to Belarus. These are distinct from the strategic forces that are governed by the New START treaty, and their use would cross a threshold that has been intact since 1945 without producing the global catastrophe that is associated with the larger weapons.

Timing was linked to the calendar of the war. Announcement came a week before the inauguration of Vladimir Putin for a fifth term, and it followed a period in which Russian forces had been pressing along the front in eastern Ukraine. It also came as Western governments debated whether to allow Ukraine to use donated weapons against targets inside Russia, which is the question that the exercise was most directly intended to influence.

Response from NATO was measured. Spokesman said the alliance was monitoring the announcement and that it had not seen a change in Russia's nuclear posture, and several governments repeated that any use of nuclear weapons would have severe consequences. Officials in Washington said the exercise was an attempt at coercion rather than a preparation for use, and they noted that similar language had appeared before and had not been followed by movement of weapons.

Ministry did not say how many units would take part or where the exercise would be held, and it did not invite observers, which leaves foreign governments to judge the drill from satellite images and from the movement of launchers.

Meaning of the announcement is likely to be found in the domestic audience as much as in the foreign one. Nuclear rhetoric has become a regular instrument of Russian policy since 2022, used to raise the cost of Western decisions without committing to anything that could be verified. Drill of the sixth of May adds a new level of detail, since it involves practice rather than words. Whether it changes Western calculations is doubtful, and whether it signals a harder line inside the Kremlin is a question that only the next few months will answer.
