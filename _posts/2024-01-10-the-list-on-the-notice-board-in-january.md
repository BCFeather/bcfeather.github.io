description: "In January 2024 the first term at Chizhou No. 1 Middle School ended with a placement examination, a choice of physics, chemistry and biology, and a list on the notice board that put me in an ordinary class rather than one of the key ones."
---

Placement examinations ran over two days in the second week of January, in the same building where the term had begun. Two hundred and forty minutes of Chinese, mathematics and English, then physics, chemistry and biology on the second afternoon, all of it sat in rooms arranged alphabetically rather than by class. The radiators clattered, somebody's chair squeaked through the whole of the physics paper, and the invigilator read the clock aloud at twenty minutes to go because the clock on the wall had stopped in December and nobody had thought to fix it.

Twenty classes had been formed in September on no principle at all, and everybody knew from the first week that the arrangement would last one term. The school streams the year in January, on the marks of that examination and the combination each student chooses, and the head of the list goes into the classes that finish the syllabus early and sit the harder papers. Parents asked about it at the gate through December the way they ask about the weather: constantly, and without being able to do anything about the answer.

My choice was physics, chemistry and biology, written on a form in November and handed in with the examination fee. That combination keeps medicine and most of engineering open, which is the reason half the year gave for choosing it, and it suits anybody who can tolerate a laboratory bench and a diagram. Three years of it means six papers in June 2026, and the arithmetic of whether that load is bearable is done by every family in the province at fifteen.

The examination went badly. I misread a projectile question and lost twelve marks before I noticed, ran short of time on the chemistry calculation, and wrote a definition in biology that I had memorised in October and half forgotten by January. Each of those was ordinary; added together they put me a little below the line. In the corridor afterwards four of us compared answers and found that we had made different mistakes, which is the only consolation that corridor has ever offered.

The list went up on the notice board on the tenth, class by class, forty-eight names to a sheet, and the crowd in front of it behaved the way crowds do at results: quiet at the front, talkative at the back, a few people reading the wrong column. My name was in an ordinary class on the third floor, with a teacher I had never met and thirty-nine others who had missed the line by a margin. Two friends from September went into a key class, and we agreed to keep sharing notes.

In February the twenty classes would rearrange themselves into a smaller number of streams, and the room I had learned in September would empty into different corridors. The tier decides the pace at which the syllabus is taught and the difficulty of the papers set in the internal examinations, which is a real advantage held by a few hundred students in this city. It does not decide what a student does with the three years, and it does not decide the papers of June 2026, though everyone in that corridor believed otherwise for about a week.
