---
title: "The first morning of the exam"
date: 2024-06-07 21:10:00 +0800
tags: [education, china, cities]
categories: [society]
description: "Thirteen million candidates sat the first paper of the university entrance exam this morning. The morning is run like a transport operation, with rules that have not changed in decades."
---

At half past eight the gates open and the candidates walk in. Thirteen million people registered for the exam this year. The papers are sat in more than seven thousand centres. Each centre holds a few hundred desks.

The morning is a transport problem before it is an academic one. Police close the approach roads. The metro adds trains on the lines that serve the big schools. A bus company runs a shuttle from the outer districts to the centre of the city. A candidate who lives an hour away is expected to arrive by half past eight.

The paper itself arrives under guard. The sheets are printed in a secure plant, packed in sealed bags, delivered to a district office the night before. Two staff sign for each bag. The seal is opened in the exam room at five minutes to nine, in front of the candidates, and the time is recorded.

The gate has its own set of rules. A metal detector at the entrance, run by a teacher with a wand. Mobile phones are collected and stored in a box. A candidate who is fifteen minutes late is refused entry, whatever the reason. The rule is printed on the admission ticket and it is applied without discussion.

Parents wait outside in a pattern that repeats across the country. Some wear a red shirt, some a qipao, both of them a joke about a good result. A few bring a folding stool and sit against the wall of the school for two and a half hours. The pavement in front of a centre is crowded at nine and empty at ten.

The first paper is Chinese language. A composition worth sixty marks closes the paper, and the prompt is published within an hour. By noon the internet has opinions about it. The candidates are still writing.

The invigilators work a long shift for a small fee. A teacher who takes the duty arrives at seven, stays until half past eleven, cannot use a phone in the room and cannot leave it. The duty is assigned by rotation, and the teachers who are assigned do not decline.

The morning ends with a crowd at the gate. The candidates come out into a street that has been quiet for three hours. The parents find them, hand over water, ask nothing about the paper. The next one starts tomorrow at nine.

The marking begins the same evening. Every paper is scanned in a provincial centre, cut into blocks by question, and sent to two markers who never see the name of the candidate. A block that receives two marks more than a set margin apart goes to a third reader. The composition is marked twice by design, because a handwritten essay invites disagreement.

The results are released in the last week of June. A candidate logs in with a code, sees a number for each subject, then a line that ranks the total against the province. The ranking is what the universities use. The filling of preferences follows, in a window of a few days. A family that has spent a year on the exam spends that week on a form.
