---
title: "Ecuador raids a Mexican embassy and Mexico breaks off relations"
date: 2024-04-12 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Ecuadorian police entered the Mexican embassy in Quito on 5 April to arrest a former vice president who had been granted asylum, and Mexico suspended diplomatic relations in response, a rupture that regional organizations condemned."
---

Ecuadorian police entered the Mexican embassy in Quito on the night of the fifth of April and arrested Jorge Glas, a former vice president of Ecuador who had been convicted of corruption and who had been granted asylum by Mexico. Officers climbed over the wall of the compound and took Glas out by force, and the Mexican ambassador was injured when he tried to stop them. Mexico announced the suspension of diplomatic relations the same night.

Legal question at the centre of the dispute is the status of asylum in diplomatic premises. The convention on diplomatic relations of 1961 says that the premises of a mission are inviolable and that agents of the receiving state may not enter them without the consent of the head of mission. Mexico said the raid was an unprecedented violation, and Ecuador said Mexico had granted asylum to a person convicted by its courts, which it described as an abuse of the institution.

Person at the centre of the case has a long legal history. Glas was vice president under Rafael Correa and was convicted in 2017 of receiving bribes in connection with a construction contract, and he was released in 2022 after a habeas petition. He entered the Mexican embassy in December 2023 and was granted asylum, and Ecuador said the asylum was granted after he had been convicted, which under its reading of the rules makes it invalid.

Reaction from regional organizations was swift. The Organization of American States called a meeting and issued a statement that criticized the raid and called for dialogue. The left-leaning governments in the region, including Brazil, Colombia and Chile, condemned the violation of the embassy while urging both sides to seek a settlement, and the Mexican president said his government would take the case to international courts.

Consequence for Ecuador is isolation at a moment when it is fighting organized crime and seeking international support. President Daniel Noboa defended the arrest as a defence of national sovereignty and said no criminal would be allowed to use a diplomatic mission as a refuge. Several governments recalled their ambassadors, and the affair complicated the government's negotiations with the United States and with the European Union over security cooperation.

The case also went to the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, where Mexico asked for provisional measures, and a decision is expected to take months. Ecuador's courts remanded Glas in custody while he served the sentence that had been imposed on him.

Meaning of the fifth of April is a rule of international law that had held for sixty years being broken by a small state in a hurry. The convention on diplomatic relations exists so that governments can talk to each other even when they are in conflict, and the raid made the embassy of every country in Quito less secure. What happens next depends on the courts, and on whether the countries of the region prefer to defend the principle or to defend a government that is fighting gangs.
