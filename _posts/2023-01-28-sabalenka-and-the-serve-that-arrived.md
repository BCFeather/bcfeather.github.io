Aryna Sabalenka won the Australian Open on 28 January, beating Elena Rybakina 4-6, 6-3, 6-4 in a final that turned on the shot that had nearly ended her career. She was twenty-four, seeded fifth, and playing under a neutral flag because of the war in her country. It was her first Grand Slam title and her first appearance in a final of one. The final lasted a little under two and a half hours, and it was played in the evening heat of a Melbourne summer.

The serve had been her problem for two years. She had led the tour in double faults, and the number had become a joke that followed her from tournament to tournament, until the joke stopped being funny and became the thing she could not do. She had changed her service motion in the off-season, shortening the toss and simplifying the rhythm, and the change had held through six rounds in Melbourne. Her coach had rebuilt the motion with a shorter toss and a higher contact point, and the change had been tested in practice for six weeks before the season began.

Rybakina was the Wimbledon champion and had beaten her in the previous season. She took the first set with the clean, flat hitting that had won her the title in London, and the final looked as if it would follow the script that the rankings suggested. Sabalenka broke early in the second set and did not lose her serve again. Rybakina had won Wimbledon at twenty-three, which made her the sort of champion the tour produces and does not always keep.

She had not dropped a set all season before the final, which is a statistic that says more about the level of her opponents in the first weeks of the year than about her own form. In the final she dropped the first, and then won the next two with the sort of hitting that makes the women's game look like a different sport from the men's. The statistic was accurate and misleading, because the opponents she had beaten in those six matches were ranked mostly outside the top twenty.

It ended on a double fault by Rybakina, which was the small irony of the afternoon, and Sabalenka put her hands over her face before she walked to the net. The victory moved her to second in the world, and the press conference that followed was mostly about the serve. She had saved three break points in the fifth game of the third set, and two of them with second serves that she would have double-faulted a year earlier. The crowd stood for the last game and stayed standing afterwards.

The shot she could not trust a year earlier was the one that ended the match. Sport's arithmetic is occasionally that neat, and it is rarely that quick. The first title is the one that changes the questions, and the questions that followed were about whether she could win a second.
