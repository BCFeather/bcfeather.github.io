title: "Shanghai reopens two months after it shut"
date: 2022-06-01 22:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Barriers along the roads out of the city came down at midnight, and twenty-five million people who had been confined to their neighbourhoods since April found a place that no longer worked the way they remembered."
---

Barriers came down along the roads out of Shanghai after midnight on 1 June, and a city of twenty-five million began to move again. Low fences of blue metal that had divided neighbourhoods for weeks were lifted, buses resumed their routes, and the first ferries crossed the Huangpu before dawn. Residents who had been confined to their compounds since early April walked to offices, to markets and to the river, many of them carrying the results of a test taken within the previous seventy-two hours.

Costs of the shutdown are still being counted, and the early figures are large. Shanghai accounts for about a tenth of China's industrial output and handles the largest container traffic of any city in the world, and the port ran at a fraction of its capacity for weeks while lorries waited at provincial borders. The city's economy contracted by nearly fourteen per cent in the second quarter, and the national growth rate for the same period fell to four-tenths of one per cent, which is the weakest quarter since the first wave of the pandemic.

Conditions for reopening were set out in advance and enforced by software. A negative test from the previous three days became the price of entering an office, a shop or a metro carriage, and the health code on every phone changed colour according to where its owner had been. The city promised a testing point within a fifteen-minute walk of every home, staffed by the same volunteers who had delivered vegetables to doorways in April.

What the lockdown took from daily life is documented in thousands of posts that circulated inside China and were removed from public view. Residents described running out of insulin and infant formula, bargaining with neighbours over rice, and shouting from balconies at night. Two months of that changed how people in the city regard the machinery of epidemic control, and the change is visible in the queues at testing booths that remained long after the barriers were gone.

Policy in Beijing did not change with the reopening. The guiding formula remains to find and isolate every case, and the same machinery was applied in other cities through the spring and summer, including in Danyang, in Xian and in the border towns of Yunnan. Officials argued that the alternative was the kind of mortality seen in Hong Kong in February, where the elderly death toll ran into the thousands.

Behind the reopened streets sits a city with a damaged service sector and a thinner population of foreign staff. Small restaurants on Anfu Road that had paid rent through two months of closure reopened with fewer tables and shorter hours, and several multinational firms have moved regional functions to Singapore. Traffic returned within a week, and the testing queues shortened within a month, but the confidence that a business can plan beyond the next outbreak has not come back with either. Departure boards at Hongqiao filled again within a fortnight, though the airport was still handling a fraction of the traffic it had before April.
