---
title: "Putin suspends the last arms treaty"
date: 2023-02-21 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "In his address to the Federal Assembly on 21 February 2023, President Putin said Russia would suspend its participation in the New START treaty, the last remaining limit on the two largest nuclear arsenals."
---

In an address to the Federal Assembly on 21 February, the day before the anniversary of the invasion, President Putin said that Russia would suspend its participation in New START. The treaty, signed in 2010 and extended in 2021, caps deployed strategic warheads at 1,550 on each side and allows inspections of the other side's facilities. Suspension is short of withdrawal, and Putin described it as a response to what he called the West's attempts to inflict a strategic defeat on Russia.

Announcement came in the middle of a speech that was mostly about the war. He described the campaign as a defensive operation, accused the West of starting it, and said that Russia would continue to fight until its objectives were met. He also announced a new programme of weapons development and said that the country's nuclear forces would be modernized further, without giving figures. He also said that the suspended treaty could be revived, without saying what would have to change for that to happen.

Difficulties had accumulated before the speech. Inspections had been suspended since the pandemic, and Russia had refused to allow them to resume in 2022, citing the sanctions and the restrictions on its aircraft. The State Department had described that refusal as a breach of the treaty's terms, and the two sides had spent the winter arguing about procedure rather than about weapons.

Experts described the suspension as a signal rather than a step towards use. Without inspections and without data exchanges, each side loses the ability to verify what the other has deployed, and the uncertainty that follows is its own kind of risk. The arsenals are still limited by the treaty's caps for the moment, and the caps are only as good as the confidence that each side has in the other's compliance. A treaty without inspections is a set of promises that neither side can check, and the checking was the part that had kept the competition stable.

The response from the alliance was measured and immediate. The secretary general of NATO described the decision as a step in the wrong direction, the American secretary of state said that the United States remained ready to talk, and the Russian foreign ministry said that the suspension would be reversed only if the West changed its behaviour. Both sides said they wanted a new agreement, and neither side proposed a forum in which to write one. NATO's own statement called the treaty a cornerstone of stability, which was the language it had used when the treaty was extended in 2021.

What the speech reflected was a decision about the audience. Putin was speaking to a domestic audience on the eve of a difficult anniversary, and the arms control language was a way of describing the war as a confrontation between equals rather than as an invasion of a neighbour. The treaty survived the speech in form, and the confidence that had made it useful did not. The speech was broadcast in full and repeated on the evening news without commentary.
