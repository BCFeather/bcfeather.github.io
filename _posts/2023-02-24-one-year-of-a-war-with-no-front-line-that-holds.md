---
title: "One year of a war with no front line that holds"
date: 2023-02-24 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A year after the invasion of 24 February 2022, the war in Ukraine had settled into a line of trenches and artillery, with about eight million people displaced abroad and cities in the east reduced to ruins."
---

The first anniversary of the invasion fell on a Friday, and both capitals marked it with speeches rather than with offensives. Ukraine's president stood in Kyiv and described the year as the proof that the country could not be conquered, and Russia's president had made his address three days earlier. The front line ran through the same provinces it had run through in the autumn, and the changes of the previous six months had been measured in kilometres rather than in provinces.

Two armies that had both failed to break through defined the military picture. Russia's autumn mobilization had produced several hundred thousand soldiers, and its winter offensive had been concentrated on a handful of towns in the Donbas. Ukraine's counter-offensives in the north-east and the south had recovered thousands of square kilometres in the autumn, and the winter had ended them. Artillery, drones and electronic warfare had made movement visible, and visibility had made manoeuvre expensive. Both sides had spent the winter preparing offensives that the ground and the drones made impossible to conceal.

People and buildings measured the humanitarian picture. About eight million Ukrainians were living abroad, and about five million were displaced inside the country, and the two numbers had barely changed since the summer. The World Bank and the European Commission put the cost of reconstruction at well over a hundred billion dollars, and the estimates of the damage to housing, hospitals and schools were revised upward every quarter. Excess mortality in the occupied areas was recorded by historians rather than by ministries, and the recording was slow.

The energy system had become a target. Russia had begun attacking the grid in October, and the winter had been survived with imports, repairs and rationing, and with a great deal of improvisation. Ukrainian engineers described the repairs as temporary, and the municipalities described the generators as permanent, and both descriptions were accurate for the season they were used in.

Diplomacy had become a stalemate with more participants. Ukraine's ten-point peace formula was supported by its partners and rejected by Russia, Russia's proposals were rejected by Ukraine, and the mediators who had offered themselves, including Turkey and the United Nations, had produced the grain deal and the prisoner exchanges and nothing else. The war had become one in which the parties could agree on corridors and could not agree on borders.

What the anniversary produced was a set of numbers that the year had been spent collecting. Tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians had been killed, and the true figures were known only to the general staffs and to the morgues. Weapons deliveries that had been unthinkable a year earlier had become routine, and the debates about them had shifted from whether to send tanks to how many and how quickly. A year that had begun with an expectation of collapse ended with an expectation of a longer war. The figures were estimates, and the estimates were the only ones that both sides would accept.
