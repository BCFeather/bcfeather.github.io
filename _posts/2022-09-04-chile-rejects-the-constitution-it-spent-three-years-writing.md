---
title: "Chile rejects the constitution it spent three years writing"
date: 2022-09-04 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Sixty-two per cent vote against a text drafted by an elected convention, and the president says the process will begin again."
---

Chileans rejected the new constitution on Sunday by sixty-two per cent to thirty-eight, on a turnout of about eighty-five per cent. The text had been written by a convention of a hundred and fifty-four members, most of them elected outside the parties, over a year of sessions. It would have replaced the charter written in 1980 under the dictatorship, which the protests of 2019 had made the symbol of everything the country wanted to leave behind. The result was announced shortly after nine in the evening, and the streets of Santiago filled within minutes.

Supporters of the text had argued that it answered the demands of the uprising, with rights for indigenous peoples, a new system of regional government and a list of social guarantees. The draft ran to a hundred and seventy-eight pages and included provisions that the campaign could not explain in a sentence. Its opponents, who included parts of the left as well as the right, said it would create a state that could not pay for itself and would replace a senate with a chamber whose powers were unclear. Neither side claimed that the campaign had been about the details.

The rejection is a defeat for the president, who had campaigned for the text and had said that a new constitution was the answer to the crisis of legitimacy. Gabriel Boric, who took office in March at thirty-six, said on Sunday night that the process would go on and that a new convention would be convened. He asked the parties to agree on the terms before the end of the year, and he promised that the government would continue its programme in the meantime. His approval had fallen to about forty per cent before the vote, and it fell again in the polls afterwards.

Parties of the right, which had opposed the draft almost unanimously, said the result was a verdict on a text written by people who did not represent the country. Their argument during the campaign was about order and about the economy, and it worked with voters who had supported the protests of 2019 but had grown tired of the uncertainty that followed. Turnout in the poorer districts of Santiago was high and the no vote carried them, which contradicted the assumption that the uprising had been a mandate for a new founding document.

The question now is what a second convention could produce. Congress has to pass a law that sets the rules, the ballot and the timetable, and it has to do it with a chamber that is split. Some of the parties that voted no have said they want a smaller body with appointed experts; others say the constitution should be amended rather than replaced. The government's proposal is for another elected convention, and the arithmetic for that is hard to see.

What the vote shows is that a mandate to change can expire before the change is written. Chile's constitution of 1980 survived the transition, fifteen years of centre-left governments and a social explosion, and it will survive this attempt as well. The country has now voted twice on the same question in three years and given opposite answers to the two parts of it. The next text will be written by people who know that the public is reading the draft rather than the promise.
