Three hundred and twenty-nine events now fill the programme. A handful of them are contested by a few dozen athletes in sports that have no professional circuit. Medal table counts them the same as a hundred metres final. The additions follow a rule. A sport that wants a place submits a case that includes its global audience, its youth appeal and its cost. Host city proposes a set of extra events for its own Games. List is approved four years ahead.

Breaking made the list this year. Sixteen dancers, a DJ, a panel of judges and a scoring system that weighs technique, vocabulary and originality. Event sold out its venue and the judges' decisions were argued about on the same evening. Sport climbing was split into two disciplines. A speed climb is a race up a wall of fifteen metres, decided in five seconds. A boulder event is four problems solved in four minutes each. The combined format was unpopular with the athletes, which is why the split was made.

Athletes in these sports are not wealthy. A climber who trains six days a week supports the training with a part-time job or a sponsor who sells equipment. Prize money at a World Cup is small and the Olympic medal is the only currency that a sponsor recognises. The skateboarding is the youngest field. A competitor of fourteen won a gold in the park event. Average age of the final was under twenty. Sports that a teenager can reach the top of are the ones that the programme wants for its audience.

Cost of the venue is the objection. A temporary park built for twelve days costs as much as a permanent one and leaves nothing behind. A city that builds a permanent one has to find a use for it afterwards, and the use is usually a public facility. The medal table is read differently by different countries. A country that counts golds first ranks a small delegation high. A country that counts the total ranks a large one high. Two tables are published side by side and neither settles the argument.

Qualification is the hard part. A climber who wants a place has to earn it at a continental event, and the quota is allocated to the country rather than to the person. A national federation with one good athlete and no depth may not be able to use the place it was given. The television contract covers the main stadium and the pool. A sport staged in a temporary park is filmed by a crew that the broadcaster hires separately, and the rate for the coverage is a fraction of the main one. Exposure follows the money, which is why the new sports fight for a place on the schedule. Next host has already proposed its own additions, and the list will be read by the same committee that read this one.

Events that nobody practises still produce a champion. A person who has spent ten years on a discipline that few people watch stands on a podium in front of a crowd that has come for the ceremony. It is the same size as a medal from the pool.
