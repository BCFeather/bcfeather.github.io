Autumn recruiting opens in September, a year before the students who apply will graduate. A firm that hires in the autumn wants the cohort it can train. Those hired now start next summer. The order of the calendar is the reason. A large employer opens its application in September, runs a test in October, interviews in November and sends the offers in December. A student who applies in March finds the places gone. Funnel is narrow and the numbers are published. A firm that hires twenty graduates receives three thousand applications, tests eight hundred, interviews two hundred and makes thirty offers.

Interview is the second filter, and it is behavioural. A paper of forty questions in an hour is the same for a bank and for a manufacturer. A student who has not practised the format loses ten minutes to the instructions. The interview is the second filter and it is behavioural. A candidate is asked to describe a problem that they solved. Interviewer is trained to listen for the structure rather than for the outcome.

Internship is the route that works best. A student who spent the previous summer in the firm is known to a manager. A known candidate is compared with a file rather than with a stranger. Target schools are the part firms do not publish. A bank recruits from a list of fifteen universities. A student at a school that is not on the list applies through a portal that is read once.

Salary is advertised and fixed by a grade. A graduate scheme pays the same to everyone who enters at the same level. Negotiation is about the posting rather than about the number. Civil service is the alternative the same students take. A national exam in November selects for a set of posts. A candidate who passes it trades a salary for a post that is secure.

Resume is a document of one page. A recruiter who reads two hundred in an afternoon spends seven seconds on each. Seven seconds are spent on the school and on the first line of the experience. The pressure is concentrated in fourteen days. A student who has applications due in the same week writes four essays in a weekend, and the ones that are written last are the ones that are read last.

Referrals are the informal channel. A student whose supervisor knows a manager sends a name rather than a file, and the name is read in a week rather than in a month. Firms that do not come to campus are the majority. A company of two hundred people recruits through a job board and hires three graduates a year, and the ones it hires are the ones who applied twice. The offers arrive in December and the season closes. A student who has none applies again in the spring, when the places are fewer and the competition includes everyone who waited. By January the season is a line on a form, and the students who were hired in December are the ones who will tell the year below which of the four filters mattered.
