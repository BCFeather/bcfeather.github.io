---
title: "The half year that closed at five per cent"
date: 2024-06-30 21:30:00 +0800
tags: [economy, china, statistics]
categories: [business]
description: "The first half of the year grew by five per cent, on a mix of exports, consumption and a shrinking property sector. The number is the target and the composition is the question."
---

The statistics office published the half-year figures at the end of June. The economy grew by five per cent in the first six months, which is the lower end of the range that the government had set for the year.

The growth came from three places. Exports rose, helped by electronics and by electric vehicles. Household consumption rose, helped by travel and by eating out. Investment in factories rose, helped by a scheme that subsidises equipment.

The property sector shrank again. Investment in residential construction fell by about a tenth. Sales of new flats fell further, by a fifth in the largest cities. The sector used to contribute a fifth of the economy through construction and through the land sales that funded local budgets.

The local budgets are the part that the number does not show. A county that sold land for a large share of its revenue three years ago sold very little this year, and it has cut spending on roads and on maintenance. The cut is visible in the streets.

Prices stayed flat. Consumer prices rose by a fraction of a per cent. Producer prices fell for most of the half year. A flat price with a growing output is unusual and it suggests that demand is weaker than the headline figure implies.

Employment was steady in the survey and weak among the young. The rate among people aged sixteen to twenty-four was in double figures for most of the half year. The ministry changed the way it counts that figure halfway through, which broke the comparison with earlier months.

The policy response was gradual. Interest rates were cut twice. A scheme to trade old appliances for new ones was extended. The cities that had restricted home purchases removed the restrictions, and the sales did not follow.

The second half will be compared with a stronger base. The figures of last year's autumn were high. Holding five per cent for the year therefore asks more of the next six months than of the last six.

The half year ended with a number that matched the plan. The composition behind the number is the part that the plan did not fix.

The exports were carried by two categories. Electronic components rose, because the factories that make them added capacity. Vehicles rose, because the electric models found buyers in Europe and in South-East Asia. The two categories account for most of the increase and the rest of the basket was flat.

The local governments borrowed to replace the land revenue. A financing vehicle attached to a city raises money against a road or a water plant, and the interest is paid from the budget. The half-year figures do not include the debt and the debt is what the next budget has to carry.

The survey of small firms told a similar story. Orders were steady and the payment for them arrived later. A workshop that used to be paid in thirty days was paid in ninety, and the difference was financed by a loan that the owner described as the cost of staying open.
