---
title: "Lysychansk falls and Russia claims the whole of Luhansk"
date: 2022-07-03 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Ukrainian forces withdraw from the last city they held in the province, and the army says the line has moved to a shorter defensive position."
---

Ukrainian forces withdrew from Lysychansk over the weekend, and the general staff confirmed on Sunday that the city had been given up. The defence ministry said the decision had been taken to save the lives of the soldiers and to move to a line that could be held. Russia's defence minister told the president that the whole of the Luhansk region was now under Russian control, which is a claim that the Ukrainian side has not disputed. The city had been the last part of the province that the government held.

Fighting in Lysychansk followed the pattern of Sievierodonetsk, which fell a week earlier after a battle that lasted a month. Russian forces approached from the south and the east and cut the roads that supplied the garrison, and the Ukrainians held the high ground until the shelling made it untenable. The withdrawal was described by the soldiers who took part as orderly and by the Russian commentators as a rout. The truth is that a city of a hundred thousand people had been reduced to rubble before either side claimed it.

Its military value is smaller than the political value. Luhansk is one of the two regions that the separatists declared independent in 2014, and the Russian government has promised since February to take the whole of the Donbas. Capturing the last city allows it to say that one of the two is finished. The cost has been a month of artillery and thousands of casualties on both sides, and the Ukrainian army has said that the ratio has been in its favour.

Attention has moved to the south. The Ukrainian officials have been talking for weeks about a counteroffensive in the Kherson region, and the strikes on the bridges behind the front have begun to cut the Russian supply lines. The forces that were withdrawn from Lysychansk are being moved to that direction, according to the general staff. The Russians have been moving equipment and troops towards the same area, which suggests that they expect the attack.

Weapons from the west are the reason the south is possible. The long-range rockets that arrived in June have allowed the Ukrainians to hit ammunition depots and command posts far behind the line, and the HIMARS launchers have been used with care to avoid the counter-battery fire. The American and British governments have promised more, and the training of the new brigades has been taking place in the west of the country. The next phase will be decided by how much of that arrives before the autumn.

What the fall of Lysychansk shows is that the war has become a contest of attrition with a shifting front. Russia can take a city when it concentrates its artillery, and it cannot hold the ground cheaply once it has taken it. Ukraine can trade space for time, and it cannot do so forever without the weapons and the people. The line has moved and the argument about who is winning will be settled by the next month, not by the last one.
