---
title: "The Fed raises again and talks about banks"
date: 2023-03-22 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point on 22 March 2023, to a range of 4.75 to 5 per cent, while saying that tighter credit conditions would weigh on the economy."
---

Ten days after the second bank failure, the committee raised rates by a quarter point and kept its promise to reduce its balance sheet. The decision was unanimous, and the statement added a sentence about the banking system being sound and resilient, which was the sentence the market read first. It also added a sentence about tighter credit conditions, and the two new sentences pulled in opposite directions. The statement was released at two o'clock and the press conference began half an hour later, and the questions were about banks before they were about inflation.

The dot plot was the part that carried the signal. The median projection showed a policy rate above 5 per cent at the end of the year, which implied one more increase, and the market had priced cuts instead. Powell was asked about the gap in the press conference and said that the committee had not changed its view because the outlook for inflation had not changed enough, and that the banking stress would do some of the work that rates would otherwise have to do. The committee had raised rates nine times in a year, and this one was the smallest, and the smallest one arrived in the middle of a crisis.

Projections were revised in the direction of resilience and in the direction of weakness at the same time. Growth for the year was revised up, unemployment was revised down, and inflation was revised up slightly, while the statement warned that credit would tighten. The forecast was for a soft landing produced by a banking problem, which is a combination that the committee's models had no experience with.

Markets had expected the pause and got the increase, and the reaction was small and confused. The two-year yield fell on the day, the ten-year fell more, and equities ended lower, and the curve inverted further. The price of insurance against bank defaults kept rising while the central bank was announcing that the system was sound, and the divergence was the measure of how much the market trusted the sentence about resilience. The facility created on 12 March lent more in a week than the discount window had lent in a year.

The international dimension was visible in the following week. The swap lines with other central banks were used more than they had been since 2020, and the dollar funding that European banks needed became more expensive, and the Bank of England and the European Central Bank raised rates in the same fortnight. The tightening was coordinated in the way that it had been for a year, and the coordination was now being conducted in the shadow of a credit event.

What the decision revealed was a committee that had decided to separate its two problems. Inflation was the one it could measure, and the banking system was the one it could not, and it chose to keep fighting the first while promising to lend against the second. The promise was the facility it had created on 12 March, and its existence was the reason the second increase in a fortnight was possible at all.
