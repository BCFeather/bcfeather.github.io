---
title: "Sam Bankman-Fried is arrested in the Bahamas and charged with fraud in New York"
date: 2022-12-13 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "A month after FTX collapsed, the founder is taken into custody at his apartment and accused of using customer money to prop up his trading firm."
---

Sam Bankman-Fried was arrested at his apartment in Nassau on Monday evening at the request of American prosecutors, and the indictment was unsealed in New York on Tuesday. The charges include wire fraud, securities fraud, money laundering and violations of the campaign finance laws, and the maximum on the longest count is decades in prison. The prosecutors say he used money that belonged to customers of FTX to cover losses at Alameda Research, his trading firm, and that he lied to investors and lenders about it. He appeared in a magistrate's court in the Bahamas and was remanded in custody.

FTX had collapsed in the second week of November, nine days after a run on deposits began and four days after a balance sheet leaked. The exchange had been valued at thirty-two billion dollars in January, and its customers had been told that their deposits were held in accounts that could be withdrawn at any time. The bankruptcy filing put the shortfall at about eight billion dollars, and the new chief executive described the company's records as a mess of interlocking entities with no proper accounting.

The evidence in the indictment is largely made of the company's own messages. Prosecutors quote instructions to move customer funds, a spreadsheet that tracked the transfers and conversations in which the shortfall was discussed. Two of his closest associates, Caroline Ellison and Gary Wang, had been cooperating with the investigation since the previous week, and their plea agreements were unsealed on the same day. Both of them had run parts of the group that the prosecutors describe as central to the fraud.

He had spent the month after the collapse giving interviews in which he said he had made mistakes and had not understood the scale of the problem. He blamed the bankruptcy lawyers, the exchange's rivals and the collapse in asset prices, and he insisted that he had acted in good faith. On the day before the arrest he was still posting about the failure and about the funds that he said he wanted to recover. The prosecutors said his statements were part of the fraud rather than an explanation of it.

The political dimension is the campaign finance charge. Prosecutors say he made donations in his own name and in the names of others to candidates and committees, and that the money came from the business rather than from him. He had been the second largest donor to the Democratic candidates in the midterm elections, and the filings now describe a scheme to hide the source. Members of Congress who had received the money began returning it within days of the collapse, and several said they would give the amounts to charity.

What the case leaves is a question about how a company of that size could be regulated so lightly for so long. FTX had a licence in the Bahamas, an American affiliate with a small presence and a network of investors who were told that the books had been audited. The bankruptcy examiner will spend months on those questions, and the criminal case will answer a narrower one. The answer to that appeared in the indictment in the form of a routine instruction to move money from one entity to another, which is what the government says the customers never saw.
