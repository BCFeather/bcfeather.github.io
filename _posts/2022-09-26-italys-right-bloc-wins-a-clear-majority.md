---
title: "Italy's right bloc wins a clear majority and Meloni is set to become prime minister"
date: 2022-09-26 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Brothers of Italy takes about a quarter of the vote, the coalition wins both chambers, and turnout falls to a record low."
---

The election on Sunday gave the coalition of the right a majority in both chambers, and the largest party inside it was Brothers of Italy, which took about twenty-six per cent of the vote. The party had been at four per cent four years earlier, and it spent the last parliament in opposition while every other party joined a government of national unity. Its allies took about nine per cent each, and the three together are expected to command more than half of the seats in the chamber and in the senate. The result was clear by midnight.

Its leader is expected to become prime minister, and she would be the first woman to hold the office. She campaigned on a promise of tax cuts, a naval blockade against migration and a review of the European Union's rules, and she softened the last of those in the final week. Her party's origins are in the neo-fascist movement of the post-war period, and its opponents used that history throughout the campaign. She described the result as a mandate to govern and said she would speak to all Italians rather than to her voters.

Turnout was about sixty-four per cent, the lowest in the history of the republic. The abstention was heaviest in the south, where the parties of the right did well, and in the poor suburbs of the northern cities. Analysts said the fall was a verdict on a parliament that had changed prime ministers three times without an election, and on a campaign that the voters found hard to follow. The Five Star Movement, which had been the largest party in 2018, fell to about fifteen per cent and held its vote in the south.

Outgoing prime minister Mario Draghi had been in office since February 2021 and had never been elected. Mario Draghi was brought in by the president to spend the European recovery money and to keep the country inside the euro's rules, and he did both. His government fell in July when three of the parties in it withdrew, and the election was called within days. He said on Sunday that the country had a government and that the institutions would do their work.

Reaction from Brussels was formal and cautious. Officials said they would wait for the programme and the budget, which is due at the end of the year. The spread between Italian and German bonds widened in the week before the vote and narrowed afterwards, which suggests that the market had priced the outcome. The recovery fund, which is the largest in the union, comes with conditions that a government of the right will have to accept if it wants the money.

What the vote shows is a country that has stopped punishing its governments and started rewarding the one party that never joined them. The coalition has a majority that the polls did not predict a year ago, and it has a programme that its own members have described in different ways. The first test will be the budget in December, and the second will be whether the three parties can govern together for a full term. Nobody in Italy remembers the last time that happened.
