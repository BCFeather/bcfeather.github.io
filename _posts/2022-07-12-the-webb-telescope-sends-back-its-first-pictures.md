---
title: "The Webb telescope sends back its first pictures and they are already a surprise"
date: 2022-07-12 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [science]
description: "Five images released at once show galaxies at the edge of the observable universe, a dying star and the water in an exoplanet's atmosphere."
---

The first scientific images from the James Webb Space Telescope were released on Tuesday, and they arrived five at a time in a broadcast from the space agency's headquarters. The telescope was launched on Christmas Day in 2021 and it has spent six months unfolding and cooling and calibrating. The pictures were taken in the first weeks of operation and they were chosen to show what the instrument can do rather than to answer a single question. Astronomers had been waiting for them since the launch.

A patch of sky the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length contains thousands of galaxies, and it is the deepest image yet taken. Some of them are so distant that their light left them within a billion years of the beginning, and the telescope's mirror resolves details that the older ones could not. The picture was built from more than a hundred exposures and it was processed in days rather than weeks. The astronomers said the number of galaxies in it was larger than the models had predicted.

Other images show a star that is dying and the gas it has thrown off, a group of five galaxies that are interacting, and the spectrum of an exoplanet's atmosphere. The spectrum is the one that matters for the search for life, because it shows the absorption lines of water and of clouds in the light that passes through the atmosphere. The planet is a gas giant that orbits a star in the constellation of Sagittarius, and the measurement was made in a few hours of observing time.

Two differences from the older telescope matter. Its mirror is six and a half metres across and it is made of eighteen segments that were aligned in space, and it observes in the infrared, which is the part of the spectrum that carries the light of the oldest galaxies. The telescope can also measure the composition of an atmosphere from a spectrum, which the older one could do only for a few objects. The cost was about ten billion dollars and the mission is planned to last at least ten years.

Astronomers reacted as they always do to a new instrument. The teams that had been allocated time in the first year said the data were better than the simulations, and the ones who had not been allocated time began to write proposals for the next rounds. The images were released to the public without a restriction, and the raw data will follow in the coming days. The first papers are expected within weeks, and the first surprises are expected before that.

What the release shows is an instrument that will change several fields at once. It will look at the atmospheres of planets around other stars, at the formation of galaxies and at the dust that hides the birth of stars in the Milky Way. The first pictures are a demonstration and the science will follow, and the questions that it was built to answer are the ones that the last thirty years of astronomy have been unable to. The next decade of the field will be shaped by what it finds in the first year.
