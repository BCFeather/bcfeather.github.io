---
title: "Chinese carmakers ship five million vehicles abroad in the first half"
date: 2026-07-15 23:10:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "Exports rose by nearly two thirds to 5.1 million vehicles, with electric and plug-in models accounting for almost half of them, a pace that has outrun the ports and the ships that carry them."
---

Chinese carmakers exported 509.6 thousand vehicles in June and 5.1 million in the first half of the year, an increase of 65.3 per cent on the same period of 2025, according to figures the manufacturers' association published on 15 July. New energy vehicles accounted for 2.36 million of the total, more than double their level a year earlier, and they made up roughly 46 per cent of everything shipped. The association said the pace had exceeded its own forecast for the year. The association had forecast growth of about a fifth for the year.

Destinations have shifted with the politics. Shipments to Russia, to the Middle East, to Latin America and to Southeast Asia grew fastest, while exports to the European Union slowed after the tariffs that Brussels imposed on electric cars. Chinese manufacturers responded by building assembly plants in countries that face lower duties and by shipping partially assembled kits, which customs classify differently from finished vehicles. Mexico and Brazil took the largest volumes in Latin America, and the Gulf states took the largest in the Middle East.

Ports and ships are the physical constraint. Car carriers were scarce for three years, and Chinese operators ordered new vessels that are only now entering service. Terminals in Shanghai and in Yantai have been running at capacity, and some manufacturers have chartered entire ships for their own exports rather than wait for space on a shared service. Chinese ports handled about four million car exports in the first half, and several terminals have begun to build dedicated berths.

Domestic market is the reason exporters are so aggressive. China's own car market has been in a price war for two years, with discounts that have pushed several brands into losses and forced others out of the industry. A factory that sells at home below cost needs volume abroad to keep its lines running, which is why the export price has fallen even faster than the export volume has risen. Subsidies for trade-ins lifted sales in the spring and pulled demand forward from the summer.

Profits show the cost of that strategy. Several listed manufacturers reported margins below three per cent in the first quarter, and the industry's total profit fell despite record output. Dealers have complained that they are selling cars at a loss to meet targets, and the government has begun to discuss measures against what it calls disorderly competition. Battery makers, which supply the cars, have been running at lower utilisation as well.

Five million cars is a number that changes the industry's arithmetic. It is larger than the total sales of several established manufacturers, and it is being achieved in a year when the importing countries are raising barriers and when the shipping capacity is tight. The next constraint will be the one nobody in the industry can control, which is how many of those cars the world's roads can absorb. The association expects the full-year total to approach ten million, a figure that would put China ahead of Japan and Germany combined.
