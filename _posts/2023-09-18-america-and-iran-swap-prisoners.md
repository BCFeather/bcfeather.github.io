---
title: "America and Iran swap prisoners"
date: 2023-09-18 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Five American detainees left Iran on 18 September 2023 in exchange for five Iranians held in the United States, as six billion dollars of frozen Iranian oil revenue was moved to accounts in Qatar."
---

The exchange took place in Doha on 18 September, with the American citizens flying out on a Qatari aircraft and the five Iranians, none of whom had been charged with violent crimes, released from American custody. The group included Siamak Namazi, an Iranian-American businessman detained in 2015, and his father, who had been arrested when he travelled to visit him. The deal was mediated by Qatar, with Switzerland and Oman involved in the mechanics, and it took more than a year of quiet talks that neither government would acknowledge in public.

Money was the mechanism. Six billion dollars of Iranian oil revenue had been stranded in South Korean banks since 2018, when American sanctions blocked the transfers, and the funds were moved to accounts in Qatar that Iran can use to buy food, medicine and other humanitarian goods. American officials said the money would be monitored and that Iran would not be able to use it for anything else, and Iranian officials said the restriction was an insult that they had accepted for practical reasons, while their foreign minister described the transfer as a first step.

Agreement is narrow, and deliberately so. It does not touch the nuclear programme, under which Iran enriches uranium to sixty per cent purity and has barred some inspectors, and it does not address Iran's support for armed groups in the region or its supply of drones to Russia. The American administration has said the deal is about citizens rather than about the relationship, and its critics have said the money will free other funds for the things the deal does not mention, which is the argument that will be made in every future negotiation of this kind.

Domestic politics in both countries attacked it. Republicans in Congress said the payment was a ransom that would encourage more detentions, and several of them have tried to block the transfer. Iranian hardliners said the deal showed that negotiation with the United States produces nothing, and the country's president, who was elected on a promise of relief from sanctions, has been unable to deliver any.

Wider context is a stalemate that both sides have stopped trying to break. Talks in Vienna to restore the 2015 nuclear agreement ended in 2022, and the two countries have since exchanged messages through Oman and Qatar without meeting. Iran's oil exports have risen anyway, mostly to China, and its stock of enriched uranium has grown to the point where weapons-grade material could be produced within weeks. The International Atomic Energy Agency has said it cannot verify the programme fully. The American administration has been negotiating with Iran while sanctioning it, which is the position that every administration since 1979 has ended up in.

Five people are free who had been held for years, some of them in cells that their families could not visit, and a sum of money has moved from one bank to another. Whether this becomes a channel or a one-off depends on whether either government wants to talk about the things that the deal left out.
