Across the country this week, pupils sat the school entrance exam that sorts them at fourteen. A pupil of fifteen sits papers in Chinese, mathematics, a foreign language, physics, chemistry, history and politics. A total decides which high school the pupil may attend. The share that goes to an academic high school is set by the province. National guidance asks for roughly half, and the provinces interpret it within a range. A city that builds more high schools admits more pupils, which is why the ratio differs between neighbouring prefectures.

Lines are published on a portal. A family enters a number and receives a score, then a list of schools with the lowest score that was admitted last year. Most families have that list and nothing else, and it is a year out of date. The vocational schools recruit in the same week. A programme in nursing, in cooking, in numerical control or in vehicle repair takes pupils for three years and then places them in a job. Placement rates are published and they are high for the programmes that a factory sponsors.

The argument about the split is old. A parent who wanted an academic place for a child who scored two marks under the line will say that the exam decided a life at fifteen. A head teacher will say that the alternative was an exam at twelve. Both statements are true and neither settles the question. The tutoring industry has changed shape. Regulation closed the large classes three years ago and the small ones moved into apartments. A group of six pupils and a teacher who used to work at a school is the common arrangement now. Price per hour has risen.

County schools are the ones that lose. A strong pupil from a county town is recruited by a school in the provincial capital, which offers a dormitory and a fee waiver. A county keeps the building and loses the cohort, and its results decline in the following year. The physical test is sat in May. A run, a jump, a throw, each worth a few points that are added to the total. A pupil who is good at sport can gain enough to cross the line, which is the reason the gyms are full in April and empty in June.

Results were released on the same evening. Each script is scanned and cut into blocks, and each block is marked twice. A school that produces a sudden jump in results attracts a moderation visit, which is the mechanism that keeps the scores comparable between counties. The results were released on the same evening. Portals crashed for twenty minutes. A family that had prepared a celebration postponed it. A family that had prepared nothing found a place in a programme it had not considered.

Pupils who reach a vocational programme will sit a different examination in three years. The line that sorted them will not appear on any record they carry. Behind the scores sits a sorting machine that no family can read from the outside, which is the part of the system that the reform has left alone.
