---
title: "The American economy shrinks for a second quarter and the word recession is argued over"
date: 2022-07-28 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "Output falls at an annual rate of nought point nine per cent, and the definition that the economists use is not the one that the politicians use."
---

American output fell at an annual rate of nought point nine per cent in the second quarter, according to the first estimate published on Thursday, and it is the second quarter in a row in which it has fallen. The first quarter was revised to a decline of one point six per cent, and the two together meet the rule of thumb that the textbooks use for a recession. The officials in the administration said the economy does not meet the definition, and the definition that the statisticians use is different. The details in the report are mixed.

Fall was driven by the decline in business inventories and by the trade deficit. The inventories fell because the shops that had over-ordered in the spring stopped ordering, and the imports were larger than the exports as the dollar rose. The consumer spending grew, but more slowly than in the previous quarter, and the investment in housing fell as the mortgage rates rose. The final sales to the domestic buyers, which is the measure that strips out the inventories, grew slightly.

Definitional argument is the one that the commentators had all week. The National Bureau of Economic Research, which is the private body that dates the cycles, uses a broader set of indicators that includes employment and income and production, and it has not declared a recession. The administration pointed to the labour market, which added a million jobs in the quarter, and to the unemployment rate at three and a half per cent. The opposition pointed to the two quarters and to the fall in real wages.

Political effect is the one that matters in an election year. The president said the figures showed a transition to a more stable growth, and he mentioned the fall in the price of petrol. The Republicans said the two quarters were a recession and that the administration was redefining the word. The voters have been telling the pollsters for months that they think the economy is already in one, and the price of food and of rent is the reason.

Federal Reserve is in the middle of the argument. The chairman said on Wednesday that the labour market is too strong for a recession, and the data published on the next day made that harder to say. The committee has raised rates by two and a quarter points this year and it plans more, and the lags mean that the effect on the real economy will arrive in the autumn. The next estimate will be published at the end of August and it will be revised twice.

What the report does is show an economy that is slowing without collapsing. The consumers are still spending and the firms are still hiring, and the housing market and the inventories are subtracting from the total. The word that the country uses for this will be decided by the body that dates the cycles and by the campaign, and the two will not agree. The autumn will bring the answer from the payrolls and from the shops, and the argument will be settled by the numbers rather than by the speeches.
