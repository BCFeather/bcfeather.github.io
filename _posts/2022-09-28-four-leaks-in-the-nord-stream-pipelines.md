---
title: "Four leaks in the Nord Stream pipelines and a seismograph that recorded explosions"
date: 2022-09-28 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Pressure falls in both lines within hours, the seabed is disturbed at three points, and the operators say the damage is beyond repair for now."
---

Pressure in the Nord Stream 1 pipeline began to fall on Monday evening, and within a few hours the same had happened in Nord Stream 2, which had never carried gas. The operators detected the drop from land and sent vessels to the Baltic, and the first reports described bubbles on the surface above three separate points between Denmark and Sweden. A fourth leak was reported on Thursday. Neither line was in service, and both were still full of gas, which is why the loss of pressure was the first sign.

Seismologists in Denmark and Sweden recorded what they described as explosions rather than earthquakes. The signals arrived at stations on Bornholm and in southern Sweden, and the readings were consistent with a charge detonated underwater rather than with a tremor. The Swedish national network published the traces, and the Danish authorities said the events had occurred within a few hours of each other. The explosions were small by the standards of the science and large by the standards of a pipeline.

Nobody has claimed the attack. The European Union's foreign policy chief said the damage was the result of sabotage and promised a response, and the governments of Denmark and Sweden opened criminal investigations. Poland and Ukraine blamed Russia, and the Kremlin said the accusations were predictable and that the leaks were a loss for everyone. The American administration said it could not confirm who was responsible, and it noted that both lines had been taken out of service before the leaks appeared.

The timing is what the analysts have fastened on. The leaks happened two days after the Baltic Pipe, which carries Norwegian gas to Poland through Denmark, was opened, and in the week that the referendums in the occupied regions were being held. The pipelines had been built to bypass Ukraine and Poland, and their value had always been political as much as commercial. A pipeline that cannot be used by anyone is a different asset from one that can be used by someone.

The immediate effect on supply was small because the lines carried nothing. The effect on the market was larger, and the price of gas in Europe rose on the news even though the volume did not change. The operators said the damaged sections were in international waters and that repair would require a survey of the seabed and a decision about the environment. Each leak released methane into the atmosphere, and researchers estimated the total at a few hundred thousand tonnes.

What the incident has done is remove an option. Any future settlement that involved resuming Russian gas through the Baltic would now require new pipe, which is a project of years and billions. The infrastructure that was built to bind the two economies together has become the symbol of their separation, and it was destroyed by an act that nobody has admitted. The investigation will take months, and the pipes will stay full of seawater until somebody decides what to do with them. The companies that built the lines have said they will not repair them while the war continues, and the insurance policies do not cover an act of war.
