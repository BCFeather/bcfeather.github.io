The new students arrive in the second week of September, which is a month after the admission letter and a fortnight after the registration deadline on the letter. The first day is a queue that forms before the hall opens at eight. A hall with a hundred tables processes a thousand students in a morning. The tables are for the medical check, the dormitory key, the card that pays for the canteen and the fee that was already paid.

The dormitory is assigned by department rather than by choice. A room of four in a building from 1998 has two bunk beds, a desk for each and a shared kettle. The students who arrive first take the beds near the window.

The military training begins within three days. A fortnight of drill in the sun is run by officers from a local unit. The uniform that is issued for it is the first piece of clothing that a student wears on campus.

The parents stay for two days and then leave. A hotel near the campus sells its rooms at three times the normal rate in the week of registration. The ones that are booked are booked in July.

The adjustment is the part that nobody prepares for. A student who was the best in a county school meets thirty people who were the best in theirs. The first exam in October is the one that settles it.

The clubs recruit on the same week. A hundred societies set up tables along the main road. The ones that hand out a form on the first day have the largest intake.

The money is the constraint that is discussed quietly. A student from a county household receives a grant that covers the fee and part of the living cost. The ones who do not qualify take the work that the library advertises.

The course selection is a scramble on a website. A general education module that is popular fills in four minutes. A student who misses it takes a course at eight in the morning on a Saturday.

The campus is a small city for the four years. A market that sells everything from a bicycle to a notebook sits outside the gate. The shops that serve the students close in the summer and reopen in September.

The teachers meet the cohort in the first week and not again until the exam. A lecture of two hundred students is delivered from a podium, and the seminar of twenty is where the work is actually discussed.

The homesickness arrives in the third week. A student who has never lived away from home calls every evening for a fortnight and then stops, and the ones who do not call are usually the ones who are fine.

The term settles by October. The lines are gone, the uniforms are folded into a cupboard for the next year, and the campus is the place it will be for the next eight months, until the graduation photographs in June.
