---
title: "American inflation reached a forty-year high"
date: 2022-06-10 22:30:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "Consumer prices rose eight point six per cent in the year to May, the fastest since December 1981, with energy and shelter doing most of the work and core prices still climbing."
---

Consumer prices in the United States rose by eight point six per cent in the year to May, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 10 June, which is the fastest annual rate since December 1981. The monthly increase was one per cent, and the figure surprised forecasters who had expected inflation to have peaked in March. Markets fell on the release, and the yield on the two-year Treasury rose above three per cent for the first time since 2018. The release came five days before the Federal Reserve's scheduled meeting, and it removed the option of a smaller increase.

Composition of the increase explains why it has lasted. Energy prices were up by more than a third over the year, with petrol, electricity and natural gas all contributing, and food prices rose by about ten per cent, led by eggs, dairy and meat. Shelter, which is measured with a lag because rents are collected over time, rose at the fastest rate since 1986 and is now the largest single contributor to the core index. It was the third month in a row in which the annual rate had risen.

Petrol prices crossed five dollars a gallon in most states during the week. Diesel was higher still, and the difference matters because almost everything sold in a shop travels by lorry. Airlines, which had cut capacity during the pandemic and were struggling to restore it, raised fares by a quarter over the year, and used cars, which had driven the first wave of inflation in 2021, finally began to fall. Rents in the largest cities rose at double-digit rates for new leases, and the official measure will reflect those increases for the rest of the year.

Wages have not kept pace. Average hourly earnings rose by about five per cent over the year, which means that a typical worker has taken a real cut of roughly three per cent, and surveys of consumer sentiment fell to the lowest level recorded since the series began in the 1950s. The political consequence followed within hours, as the White House blamed oil producers and shipping costs while Republicans blamed the spending bills passed in 2021.

Implications for interest rates were immediate. The Federal Reserve had been signaling a half-point increase at its meeting on 15 June, and the release made a three-quarter-point move the expected outcome, which is what the committee then did. Officials had spent the spring hoping that inflation would fade as supply chains reopened, and the May figures removed that hope for the summer. The president's approval rating had already fallen below forty per cent, and the number gave his opponents a single statistic to repeat.

The number measures a year of accumulated price rises, and what it cannot show is how quickly they stop. Supply chains have improved, freight rates have fallen by a third from their peak, and the labour market remains tight enough to keep services inflation high. The combination leaves the central bank with the choice it has been avoiding: slow the economy deliberately, or accept that prices keep rising.
