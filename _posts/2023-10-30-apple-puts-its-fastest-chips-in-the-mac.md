---
title: "Apple puts its fastest chips in the Mac"
date: 2023-10-30 22:40:00 +0800
categories: [technology]
description: "Apple announced the M3, M3 Pro and M3 Max on 30 October 2023, the first personal computer chips built on a 3-nanometre process, alongside new 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pros and a 24-inch iMac starting at $1,299."
---

Apple announced three chips on 30 October at a short evening event that the company billed as scary and that turned out to be a hardware refresh. The M3, M3 Pro and M3 Max are the first personal computer processors built on a 3-nanometre process, and the company said the family brings a redesigned graphics core with dynamic caching, hardware-accelerated ray tracing and a mesh-shading pipeline that its laptop GPUs had lacked. The presentation ran about thirty minutes, shorter than the company's usual product events.

The design follows the pattern Apple has used since 2020, with a shared architecture scaled across tiers. The base M3 has eight CPU cores and up to ten GPU cores, the Pro adds performance cores and memory bandwidth, and the Max doubles the graphics again and supports up to 128 gigabytes of unified memory. The comparison the company chose throughout the presentation was against the M1, which flatters the numbers because three generations have passed since the laptops that shipped with it.

The products are the 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro and the 24-inch iMac. The 14-inch model with the base M3 starts at $1,599, the Pro configurations at $1,999, and the 16-inch with the Max at $3,499, while the iMac keeps its colours, gains the M3 and starts at $1,299. The Touch Bar disappeared from the lineup entirely, and the Space Black finish became an option on the high-end laptops. The machines keep the same displays and ports, and the changes are internal.

What the event did not include is as telling as what it did. The MacBook Air, the iPad line and the Mac Pro were left alone, and the desktop studio machine still runs on the previous generation at its top configuration. Apple has now completed the transition from Intel across the portable range, and the gaps in the desktop lineup suggest that the next refresh depends on whether the Max can be scaled into a workstation part. The iMac also keeps its 4.5K panel and chassis, and this is its first update since 2021.

Competitive positions have shifted. Qualcomm has announced laptop chips designed to rival the M series, Intel's latest mobile processors have closed some of the efficiency gap, and the software advantage that Apple enjoyed in 2020 has narrowed as developers optimized for both architectures. The 3-nanometre process is a manufacturing lead rather than a permanent one, and the foundry that builds these chips also builds for Apple's competitors.

For buyers the practical question is whether the base M3 is enough. Most of the workloads that the event demonstrated, from ray-traced games to video timelines with many layers, run comfortably on the entry configuration, and the premium for the Pro and Max buys memory bandwidth and screen size rather than a different class of machine. The chips are faster than the ones they replace, and the lineup now has a clear ladder from the Air to the Max.
