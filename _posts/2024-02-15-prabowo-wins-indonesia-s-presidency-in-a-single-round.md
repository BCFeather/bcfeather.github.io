---
title: "Prabowo wins Indonesia's presidency in a single round"
date: 2024-02-15 21:05:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Quick counts from several polling firms put Prabowo Subianto and his running mate Gibran Rakabuming Raka near 58 per cent in the election of 14 February, enough to avoid a runoff in a country voting for a third time in a decade."
---

Prabowo Subianto won Indonesia's presidential election on the fourteenth of February by a margin that made a second round unnecessary, according to quick counts published by several polling organizations within hours of the polls closing. Those counts, which sample a large share of polling stations and have been reliable in previous elections, put Prabowo and his running mate Gibran Rakabuming Raka at about 58 per cent. Anies Baswedan and Muhaimin Iskandar were on about 25 per cent, and Ganjar Pranowo and Mahfud MD on about 17. Official result from the election commission is not due until March, and no candidate disputed the counts on the night.

Largest single-day vote in the world was held that day. Around two hundred million people were registered, more than half of them under forty, and they voted for a president, a vice-president and the national and regional legislatures in a six-hour window spread across three time zones. Turnout was reported at more than eighty per cent. Logistics involved about a million polling station staff, and the count began the same evening with public tally sheets photographed and posted at each station, a system that makes large-scale fraud difficult and slow.

Victory is the end of a long road for Prabowo. He stood for the presidency in 2014 and 2019 and lost both times to Joko Widodo, and in 2019 he refused to accept the result for weeks before being brought into the government as defence minister. Campaign this time was built on continuity. He promised to carry on the infrastructure programme and the commodity-processing policy of the outgoing administration, and he chose the president's eldest son as his running mate, a decision made possible by a Constitutional Court ruling in October that changed the age requirement for candidates. Chief justice of that court was the president's brother-in-law, and he was later removed by a judicial commission for ethics violations.

Gibran's presence on the ticket is the part that will be argued about. At thirty-six he is the youngest vice-president in the country's history, and the dynastic element is hard to avoid. Outgoing president's son will serve under a man who was once his father's opponent, an arrangement supporters describe as reconciliation and critics as a deal. More substantive question is whether it constrains the new government's room to break with the previous one, and the answer is probably yes.

Losers accepted the counts without calling them fraudulent, which is a change from 2019, when the streets of Jakarta filled with contested-result protests that turned violent. Anies said the result was not what his campaign had hoped for and asked supporters to wait for the official count. Ganjar said the same. Composition of the legislature will take longer to establish, and the parties backing the president-elect were leading in the early tallies. On the night of the count the country had a result, and a transition that runs from March to October, when the new president takes office.

Agenda that Prabowo will inherit is a large one. The economy has been growing at about five per cent, the commodity boom that filled the budget during the previous decade is fading, and the new capital city being built in Borneo has consumed money without producing much yet. He has promised free school meals and a child nutrition programme that will cost tens of trillions of rupiah a year, and he has said he will fund it by improving tax collection. Whether the coalition he assembles in the coming months will support that is the first test of the mandate the count gave him.
