---
title: "Apple briefly becomes the first company worth three trillion dollars"
date: 2022-01-03 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "The shares touched a price that put the market value above thirteen figures for the first time, seventeen months after the company crossed two trillion, and the close left it just short."
---

Apple's shares touched 182 dollars and 86 cents on 3 January, a price that gave the company a market value above three trillion dollars and made it the first public company to reach that level. The stock closed slightly below the mark at 182 dollars and one cent, leaving the valuation a few billion short of the figure that headlines reported, and the intraday high was enough for the record books. The mark was passed around midday in New York, and the shares had risen about a third over the previous twelve months.

The move capped a year in which the company's shares rose about a third while the rest of the technology sector wobbled. Apple had crossed two trillion dollars in August 2020, so the third trillion took roughly seventeen months, and most of the gain came from a services business that grew faster than the hardware that carries it. Buybacks reduced the share count, which lifted the value of each remaining share without adding to the company's operations.

A comparison with its peers is what makes the number interesting. Microsoft and Amazon together were worth less than one Apple at the peak of the autumn, and the sector's smaller companies had fallen as investors moved from growth stories to balance sheets. Apple had the balance sheet: about 200 billion dollars of cash and securities, a hardware franchise with margins in the forties, and a customer base that upgrades on a schedule the company controls.

Valuation also depends on a bet about the next product. Analysts who defended the price argued that services would keep compounding and that the installed base of more than a billion devices gave Apple a distribution advantage no rival could match. Sceptics pointed out that a company worth three trillion dollars is valued at about thirty times its earnings, that iPhone growth had flattened, and that regulators in Brussels and Washington were beginning to treat the App Store as a toll road rather than a product.

Regulation is the variable the price does not capture well. The Digital Markets Act, agreed in Europe that spring, would require sideloading and alternative payment systems, and the antitrust cases against the store had begun to produce discovery documents that embarrassed the company. A services business built on a closed platform is worth less if the platform opens, and nobody could say in January how much less. The European rules were agreed in March and would apply from 2024, which gave the company time to litigate and to redesign its fees.

Three trillion dollars is an arithmetic fact and not a judgment. It says that the market expected Apple's cash flows to keep growing for a very long time, at a moment when the cost of money was about to rise and when the company's own growth was slowing. The record lasted a few hours, which is the right measure of what such records are worth, and the price that made it says more about the cost of money in 2022 than about the design of a phone.
