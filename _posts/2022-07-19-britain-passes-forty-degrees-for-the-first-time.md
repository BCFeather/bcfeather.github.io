---
title: "Britain passes forty degrees for the first time and the fires follow"
date: 2022-07-19 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Coningsby records forty point three, the trains slow to a crawl and the fire services in London have their busiest day since 1940."
---

The temperature at Coningsby in Lincolnshire reached forty point three degrees on Tuesday afternoon, which is the highest that has been recorded in Britain. The previous record was thirty-eight point seven, set in Cambridge in 2019. The heat had been building since the weekend and it covered most of the country, with the south-east and the east of England the hottest. The meteorological office issued its first red warning for extreme heat, which is the level it uses when the risk is to life rather than to comfort.

Trains and buses kept running, but slowly. The railways imposed speed limits because the rails expand in the heat and can buckle, and the overhead cables sag. The airports reported delays and a runway at Luton was closed for repairs after the surface softened. The trains that did run were crowded and the ones that were cancelled left the passengers at stations that had no shade. The roads were treated with a material that is usually used in the winter.

The fire services will remember the fires. The brigade in London had its busiest day since the second world war, and it attended more than a hundred incidents in a single afternoon. A grass fire beside a railway line closed a line into the city, and a fire in a field near a housing estate in Essex destroyed several homes. The army was asked for help in one county, and the fire services in several others said they had run out of water carriers.

Counting came later, in the statistics for the following weeks. The office for national statistics later reported that the deaths in the week of the heat were above the average for the season, and the excess was largest among the old and the very young. The hospitals cancelled outpatient appointments and the ambulance service took more calls than usual. The government advised people to stay indoors, and the schools in some counties closed early.

For years the climate models have described summers like this one. The models said that a summer as hot as this would arrive once in a decade in the middle of the century, and the observed record has now moved four degrees above the one that was set three years ago. The heat in Britain came from a plume of air that had been over Spain and France, where the temperatures were higher still. The jet stream had split and the high pressure sat over the country for three days.

Built for a cooler island, the country met a hotter one this week. The houses are designed to keep heat in, the railways are laid for a narrower range of temperature, and the water systems assume a summer that is wet. The record will be broken again, and the question is whether the next summer finds the country better prepared. The answer will be visible in the number of deaths and in the state of the rails, and neither of those is a matter of opinion.
