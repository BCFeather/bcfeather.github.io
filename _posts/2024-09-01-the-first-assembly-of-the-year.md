---

The school year opened on the first of September. A primary school holds an assembly in the yard at eight. The children stand in lines that are arranged by class and by height.

The assembly is short and it is rehearsed. A head teacher speaks for six minutes, a flag is raised. A pupil reads a paragraph about the summer. The whole ceremony takes twenty minutes and it is the same every year.

The textbooks are new for three subjects. The editions that a province adopts are decided by a national committee. A school that receives a new edition in September has to teach from it in the same week.

The class size is the number that parents ask about. A school in a city district runs forty-five pupils to a room. A school in a county runs thirty. The difference is visible in the number of desks and invisible in the exam results.

The canteen is the first contract of the year. A district office signs it for the schools it runs. The menu is fixed for a term. A parent who wants a change writes to the office and receives a reply in October.

The homework limit is the rule that is hardest to enforce. A policy that caps the written work at ninety minutes a day is measured by a survey of parents. A parent who wants more homework for a child in the final year is not a rare case.

The after-school care is the part of the day that has grown. A school that offers a supervised session until half past five charges a small fee and pays the teachers who run it. The session is the reason that a working parent can hold a job.

The teachers who are missing are in the counties. A rural school that advertises three posts in the summer fills one. The two that are empty are covered by a teacher who travels between schools.

The traffic at the gate is a morning event of its own. A street that carries two lanes of cars becomes a car park at half past seven. The police who direct it are the same officers every year.

The photograph is taken by every family. A child in a new uniform stands in front of the gate with a bag that is larger than the child. The picture is sent to the grandparents before the first lesson begins.

The first week sets the timetable for the year. A subject that is scheduled on a Friday afternoon stays there, and a class that loses its Wednesday music lesson in September does not get it back in March.

The classroom that was refurbished in the summer has a new board. A school that replaced its blackboards with screens spent the money on the hardware and not on the training, and the teachers use the screen as a blackboard.

The year ends in the same yard. The assembly in September and the assembly in June are the same ceremony with different words, and the children who stand in the lines in the first week have grown by the second.
