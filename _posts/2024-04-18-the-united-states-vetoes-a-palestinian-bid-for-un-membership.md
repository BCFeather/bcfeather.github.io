---
title: "The United States vetoes a Palestinian bid for UN membership"
date: 2024-04-18 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The Security Council voted twelve to one with two abstentions on 18 April to recommend Palestinian membership of the United Nations, and the American veto blocked the resolution despite broad support in the chamber."
---

Security Council voted on the eighteenth of April on a resolution that would have recommended that the General Assembly admit Palestine as a member state, and the vote was twelve in favour, one against and two abstentions. The single vote against was cast by the United States, which used its veto for the fifth time since the war in Gaza began. Britain and Switzerland abstained, and France, Russia, China and the elected members voted in favour.

Resolution that was blocked was a procedural step rather than a decision about statehood. Admission requires a recommendation from the council and then a two thirds vote in the General Assembly, where Palestine has had observer status since 2012. The application had been revived in early April, when the Palestinian representative asked the council to reconsider a request that had been dormant since 2011, and the council's experts had examined whether Palestine met the criteria of the charter.

American explanation for the veto was that statehood should follow a negotiated settlement. The deputy ambassador said the United States supported a two-state solution and that the vote did not reflect a lack of support for the Palestinians, and she said the application should be decided through talks between the parties rather than through the United Nations. She also said the Palestinian Authority had not reformed itself enough to govern a state.

Reaction from the Palestinian side was anger and a vow to try again. The representative said the veto would encourage Israel to continue the war and that it had shown the council to be unable to act on the question that matters most to his people. He said the leadership would return to the General Assembly, where the veto does not apply, to seek expanded rights for the observer mission.

Support in the chamber was broad and included countries that have close ties with the United States. France and Japan voted in favour, and so did the elected members from Asia, Africa and Latin America. Several governments said the veto had isolated the United States and had damaged the council's credibility at a moment when it was already unable to stop the war in Gaza.

Alternative route that opened afterwards was in the General Assembly, where a resolution in May upgraded the rights of the Palestinian mission and asked the council to reconsider the application favourably. Palestine cannot vote in the assembly and cannot be a party to treaties in the way that a state can, and the upgrade gave it the right to be seated among the member states and to speak at meetings.

Consequence of the eighteenth of April is a question that will be decided by a body that cannot decide it. The Security Council cannot admit a state over the objection of a permanent member, and the General Assembly cannot admit one without the council. What the vote established was that most governments were prepared to recognize Palestine in the one forum where recognition is collective, and that the American veto could delay the outcome without settling the argument about statehood.
