title: "Musk buys Twitter for forty-four billion dollars and fires the executives"
date: 2022-10-27 23:55:00 +0800

description: "The deal closes after a Delaware court forced it, and the chief executive, the finance chief and the policy chief leave."
---

A deal closed on Thursday evening, and Elon Musk became the owner of Twitter for forty-four billion dollars, a price he had agreed to pay in April and spent the summer trying to escape. He posted a line about the bird being freed, and he changed his description on the service to Chief Twit. Within hours the chief executive, the finance chief, the policy chief and the general counsel had been dismissed. Trading in the shares was halted on the New York exchange, and the delisting took effect on Friday.

He had arrived at the company's headquarters on Wednesday carrying a sink, a joke that he explained in a video as letting it sink in. He spent the day in meetings with staff and posted photographs of himself in the lobby. Employees described a week of rumour, with managers telling them to wait for news that could not be shared. Some of them learned that they had lost their jobs from messages that stopped working on their phones.

Litigation around the purchase is short and humiliating for the buyer. He made a bid of fifty-four dollars and twenty cents a share in April and waived the due diligence that a purchase of that size normally involves. In July he told the board that the number of fake accounts had been misrepresented and that he was walking away, and the company sued him in Delaware. The judge ordered the transaction to close by the end of October, and the appeal he filed was given no time.

Money is the part that worries the banks. Seven lenders, among them Morgan Stanley, Bank of America and Mitsubishi UFJ, provided thirteen billion dollars of debt, and the interest comes to about a billion dollars a year against a company that has rarely been profitable. He sold about fifteen billion dollars of Tesla shares during the year to fund his part of the equity. The borrowing sits on the company rather than on him, which makes finding the cash to service it the first problem.

What he bought earns about five billion dollars a year and has lost money in most recent quarters. Advertising is three quarters of the revenue, and advertisers had already been trimming budgets as the economy slowed. Several agencies said publicly that they would wait to see how the new owner behaved before committing holiday spending. A private company does not have to publish quarterly accounts, which removes some of the pressure and most of the information. The bond documents that the banks sold to investors carry covenants tied to earnings, and those will be tested twice a year.

He has promised to turn the service into a town square for the world and to cut back the moderation that he says has made it dull. He also said he would set up a council to advise on content policy before any suspended account was restored. Nobody has explained how the company pays its debts if the advertisers leave. The staff who stayed were told to expect changes, and the first ones arrived before the week was over.
