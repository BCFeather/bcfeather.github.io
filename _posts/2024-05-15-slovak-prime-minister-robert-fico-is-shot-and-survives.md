---
title: "Slovak prime minister Robert Fico is shot and survives"
date: 2024-05-15 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Robert Fico, the Slovak prime minister, was shot five times at close range after a government meeting in Handlová on 15 May, and survived surgery for wounds to his abdomen and shoulder; a seventy-one-year-old man was detained."
---

Prime minister of Slovakia, Robert Fico, was shot five times at close range in the town of Handlová on the fifteenth of May, shortly after a government meeting held outside the capital. Attack happened as he greeted supporters in front of the town's cultural centre, and the gunman was detained by police within minutes. Fico was taken to a local hospital and then flown to Banská Bystrica, where surgeons operated on wounds to his abdomen and shoulder into the evening.

Deputy prime minister said after the operation that Fico was stable but that his condition remained serious, and the government described the attack as politically motivated. Minister of the interior identified the suspect as a seventy-one-year-old man who had legally owned a firearm, and officials said he had attended an anti-government protest earlier in the month. By the end of the month Fico had been discharged and was recovering at home in Bratislava.

Police and prosecutors treated the case as an attempt at premeditated murder and later reclassified it as a terrorist attack, a step that carries a heavier sentence and that the government said reflected the political character of the act. Suspect was charged and remanded, and investigators said he had acted alone. There was no evidence of a wider plot, and the government said the security services had found no organised group behind the shooting.

Reaction across the political spectrum was unusually uniform for a country that has been divided for a decade. President-elect Peter Pellegrini called the attack an assault on democracy, and opposition leaders suspended criticism of the government for a week. European leaders condemned it, and the president of the European Commission said violence had no place in politics. Several politicians received additional security, and parliament passed a package of measures restricting access to the building.

Context in which the attack happened is a polarised country. Fico returned to power in October 2023 after a campaign in which he promised to stop military aid to Ukraine and to challenge the consensus on foreign policy, and his government has since taken control of public broadcasting and prosecuted several officials from the previous administration. Political language had become harsher for a year, and commentators had warned that the rhetoric could produce violence before it did.

Hospital in Banská Bystrica said the prime minister had been treated for damage to his digestive tract as well as to his shoulder, and that he would need weeks of recovery before returning to work.

Consequence of the shooting is a harder line from a government that was already confrontational. Within weeks Fico's coalition moved to restrict the right to assemble and to increase sentences for offences against officials, and critics described the measures as an attempt to profit from the attack. Election results in June gave a party allied with the government a plurality in the European Parliament vote, which the prime minister presented as vindication. Whether the shooting changes the country's direction or accelerates it is a question that Slovaks will answer at the next election.
