---
title: "Mortgages run to forty years as the central bank rewrites the credit rules"
date: 2026-08-28 23:35:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The People's Bank of China and the financial regulator issued a joint opinion on 28 August reforming real estate credit, raising the maximum term on personal housing loans from thirty years to forty and establishing a main-bank system for project loans."
---

The People's Bank of China and the National Financial Regulatory Administration issued a joint opinion on 28 August on reforming real estate credit, and one clause of it will be quoted for years. The maximum term on personal housing loans rises from thirty years to forty, which lowers the monthly payment without lowering the price. The opinion was issued jointly with the National Financial Regulatory Administration, which means the banks that will apply it have already agreed to the terms. The document runs to a dozen sections, and the mortgage term is the clause that will be quoted in every sales office this autumn.

The arithmetic is simple and uncomfortable. Stretching a mortgage from thirty years to forty cuts the monthly instalment by roughly a tenth at current rates, and it adds a decade of interest. A buyer who cares about affordability this month and a bank that cares about repayment capacity over four decades are optimizing for different things, and the policy resolves the disagreement in favour of the buyer. A buyer who takes the longer term pays more in total and less each month, and the difference accumulates into a sum that few borrowers calculate before signing. Banks, for their part, hold the loan for a shorter part of its life than the borrower, which means the risk that matters to a lender and the risk that matters to a household differ from each other.

The rest of the document deals with the developer side. It establishes a main-bank system under which one bank supervises a project's accounts and disbursements, an attempt to stop the practice of one apartment being sold to several creditors. It also asks banks to distinguish between projects that can be finished and developers that cannot be saved. The main-bank system is intended to end the practice of a project borrowing from several institutions at once, which left creditors unable to see what the others had lent. It also gives the supervising bank a say in how sale proceeds are used, a control that developers have resisted because it limits their ability to move money between projects.

The timing follows a long slump. Sales and prices have fallen for several years, developers have defaulted in sequence, and local governments have lost the land revenue that funded their budgets. Credit policy is the instrument that can be changed without a new law, and officials have been changing it in small steps all year. Local governments have lost a large share of their revenue as land sales have fallen, and the credit rules are one of the few levers that does not require them to raise taxes. Officials have been careful to describe the measures as supporting reasonable demand, a phrase that avoids the word stimulus and preserves the policy's political framing.

The risk is familiar to anyone who watched a similar policy in other markets. Long terms support prices by making payments look smaller, and they transfer risk from the borrower's monthly budget to the borrower's working life. A forty-year loan taken at thirty-five ends at seventy-five, which is a retirement plan as much as a mortgage. Long terms have supported prices in other markets too, and the experience there suggests that the support fades once buyers begin to expect falling prices. A forty-year loan also changes the resale calculation, because the remaining term and the outstanding balance become part of what a buyer must accept.

Officials will describe the measure as support for reasonable demand, and it will work as that for some months. Whether it changes the direction of the market depends on the thing no lending rule can supply: a household's confidence that the flat it buys will still be worth what it paid. The measure will work as support for some months and as a puzzle for longer, since it makes payments smaller without changing the amount borrowed. Confidence is the one input that no lending rule supplies, and the market will not turn until households believe that the flat they buy will hold its value.
