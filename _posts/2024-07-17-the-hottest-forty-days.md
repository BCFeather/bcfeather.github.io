The period that begins in the middle of July is the hottest of the year. It runs for forty days in three blocks, and the calendar marks the first of them in the middle of the month. The temperature in the river valleys stays above thirty-five through most of it.

The rules for outdoor work are written at the provincial level. A site stops between noon and four when the temperature passes forty. A site stops for three hours when it passes thirty-seven. The employer is required to pay a daily allowance when the temperature passes thirty-five, whatever the work.

The allowance is small. It is worth a few tens of yuan per day in most provinces, and it is paid by the employer rather than by the state. A construction company pays it to its own crew and does not pay it to the crew of a subcontractor.

The enforcement is the weak part. An inspector who visits a site in the afternoon finds it empty and concludes that the rule was obeyed. A site that runs a night shift does the work after the inspection and reports it as scheduled maintenance.

The delivery platforms have a different problem. A courier is classified as an individual contractor, so the allowance does not apply by law. Some platforms pay a summer subsidy and call it an incentive, which is a payment that can be withdrawn at any time.

The hospitals see the result in the second week. Heat exhaustion is a patient who is dizzy and sweating and who recovers in a cool room. Heat stroke is a patient who has stopped sweating and who needs ice and an intravenous line. The second case is rare and it is the one that kills.

The drinks market responds. A bottle of water that costs two yuan at a kiosk costs four at the gate of a park. A shop near a construction site sells a case of bottled water a day and keeps the empty bottles in a crate for the collector.

The elderly are the group that the rules do not reach. A person who lives alone and who has an air conditioner may not turn it on, because the bill arrives at the end of the month and the pension is fixed. The community office that visits such households does so in the afternoon, which is the hour when the risk is highest.

The period ends at the end of August. The calendar calls the next one the beginning of autumn, and the temperature does not read the calendar.

The construction schedule is rewritten around the heat. A site that poured concrete at noon in April pours at six in the morning in July. The change adds an hour to the day and it removes the worst of the exposure, which the project manager counts as a saving on insurance.

The air conditioner is the household's answer and it is also the grid's problem. A block of flats where every window unit runs at once draws more than the transformer was built for, and the meter room is the first place to fail.
