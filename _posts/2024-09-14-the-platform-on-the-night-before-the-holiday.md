The mid-autumn holiday runs from the fifteenth to the seventeenth this year. The festival itself falls on the seventeenth. A three-day break that begins on a Sunday produces a travel peak on the Saturday before it.

The station on the fourteenth is the busiest place in the city. A hall that handles a hundred thousand passengers on a normal day handles a hundred and sixty thousand on the night before a holiday. The queue for the security check reaches the car park.

The tickets sold out in eleven minutes. A booking window that opens thirty days ahead releases the seats at eight in the morning. A route to a provincial capital was gone before the page had finished loading.

The high-speed line is the one that people want. A journey of four hours by train is a journey of eight by road. The road on a holiday weekend is a car park between two toll gates.

The airline is the alternative for the long routes. A flight to a city two thousand kilometres away costs twice the train fare in the peak and less than it in the week after. The fare curve is the reason that a family travels on the fifteenth and returns on the nineteenth.

The hotel price is set by the occupancy. A county town that has one good hotel sells its rooms at four hundred yuan on the night of the festival. The same room is a hundred and eighty on the Tuesday after.

The short trip is the pattern that has grown. A household that has three days does not go far. A town within two hours of a city receives more visitors than a resort that is a flight away.

The moon is the point of the holiday. A family that cannot travel gathers in a courtyard and eats a meal that is the same every year. The ones who travel take a box of cakes to the relatives they visit.

The station at night is a different building. A hall that is full at seven is empty at eleven. The cleaning crew that comes in at midnight works until the first train at five.

The return is the harder direction. A train that leaves the coast on the seventeenth is full. A family that cannot get a seat on it takes the slow train and arrives at two in the morning.

The holiday costs the traveller a premium and the railway a loss. A line that runs at full capacity for three days runs half empty for the other three hundred and sixty. The fare that covers the peak does not cover the year.

The ticket scalper was pushed out and the queue moved online. A system that requires a real name on the ticket has removed the resale at the station, and the queue that used to form at the window now forms on a screen at eight in the morning.

The station staff work the peak on overtime. A worker who directs a queue for six hours receives a payment for the day and a day off in the following week, and the roster for the holiday is written in August.

The platform empties after the last train. A station that was a crowd at nine is a lit hall at midnight, and the staff who direct the crowd go home when the last one has boarded.
