title: "The summer rush begins at the station"

description: "The sixty-two day summer travel season opens on the first of July. The railways add trains, the stations add staff, and the peak falls in the third week of the month."
---

The summer travel season opens on the first of July. It runs for sixty-two days, until the end of August, and it covers the school holidays, the Olympic fortnight and the return to term. The railways add several hundred pairs of trains to the timetable for the period.

The traffic is not evenly spread. The first days carry students going home. The middle of the month carries families going to a coast or a mountain. The last week of August carries everyone back at once, which is the tightest part of the schedule.

A station handles the crowd with a set of moves that it rehearses every year. The waiting hall is divided into zones by train number. The escalators are switched to run upward in the morning and downward in the evening. A temporary fence is put up to separate the queue for the security check from the queue for the ticket office.

The ticket allocation is the constraint. A line that runs eight trains a day in March runs fourteen in July. The extra paths come from shortening the turnaround at each end. A train that arrives at ten past the hour leaves at twenty past, which gives the cleaners eight minutes.

The student ticket is a discount rather than a reservation. A student buys a half-price seat and the seat is taken from the same pool as everyone else's. The pool is what runs out first, which is why a family booking in June finds the morning trains full.

The station at night is a different place. A train that leaves at one in the morning carries people who have worked a full day and who will sleep through the journey. The shops are shuttered. The lights are dimmed. The platform is quiet enough to hear the announcement twice.

The luggage is the visible sign of the season. A family of four with three suitcases, a folded pram and a bag of fruit moves through the hall at the speed of its slowest member. The staff at the gate have learned to let the group through in one block.

The heat inside the hall is the part that the timetable does not address. A waiting room with ten thousand people in it is warmer than the street. The air conditioning runs at full capacity and the temperature still climbs through the afternoon.

The station staff work the season as overtime. A ticket inspector who normally checks two trains a shift checks five. A cleaner who has a fixed round is moved to the platform that the late train uses. The payments are made in August, and the staff who take them plan a holiday in September.

The station also sells the journey. A shop that sells a neck pillow, a cup of instant noodles and a phone charger earns more in July than in the rest of the year, because the passenger who is about to sit for six hours buys all three.

The season ends when the last student train arrives. The extra paths are withdrawn. The temporary fence comes down, and the timetable returns to the shape it had in June.
