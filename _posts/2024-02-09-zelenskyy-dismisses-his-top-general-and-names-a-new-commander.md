---
title: "Zelenskyy dismisses his top general and names a new commander"
date: 2024-02-09 21:26:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "President Volodymyr Zelenskyy removed General Valerii Zaluzhnyi as commander-in-chief on 8 February after months of friction, and appointed the ground forces commander Oleksandr Syrskyi in his place."
---

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy removed General Valerii Zaluzhnyi from the post of commander-in-chief of the armed forces on the eighth of February, ending weeks of public speculation about a rift between the two most visible figures in the war effort. Oleksandr Syrskyi, who had commanded the ground forces since 2019, was appointed in his place. The decree was published in the evening, and both men issued statements within an hour. Zaluzhnyi said the tasks of 2022 had changed for 2024, and Zelenskyy said the army needed a renewed approach and a new command.

Friction had been visible since the autumn. In November, Zaluzhnyi published an essay in The Economist saying the war had reached a stalemate of positional fighting and that a technological leap was needed to break it. Zelenskyy's office publicly disagreed with the word stalemate, and the disagreement became a story about civil-military relations rather than about strategy. Recruiting problems, the failure of the summer counter-offensive and a political argument about mobilization added to it. Rumours of the dismissal circulated for a week before it happened, which is a sign of how leaky the system had become.

Zaluzhnyi leaves with a reputation that is difficult to argue against. He was appointed in July 2021, and in February 2022 he commanded a defence that most Western intelligence assessments expected to collapse in days. Defence of Kyiv, the Kharkiv counter-offensive in September 2022 and the recovery of Kherson in November are all credited to him. He is also the officer most identified with a war that has settled into attrition, and his popularity in Ukrainian polls at times exceeded the president's, which is the kind of fact that becomes a political problem whether or not anyone makes it one.

Syrskyi is a different kind of officer. Born in Russia in 1965 and serving in Ukraine since the 1980s, he commanded the defence of Kyiv in 2022 before moving to the eastern front. Defence of Bakhmut is credited to him, costly in casualties and effective in buying time, and he is known as a detail officer who works close to the front. Whether that translates into a different strategy is the open question. Any new commander inherits the same shortage of shells, the same problem of mobilizing enough people and the same long front line.

Political effect in Kyiv was quieter than the military one. The dismissal removes a potential rival from the scene and concentrates authority in the president's office, which is what Zelenskyy's critics said he wanted. It also removes the officer most trusted by the soldiers, and the reaction in the ranks over the following weeks was muted without being warm. A replacement is always a bet, and this one will be judged by whether the line holds through the spring, when the Russian army is expected to press the advantages it has.

What the change means for the war's direction is now a matter for the new command. Syrskyi has said publicly that the priority is to hold the line and to preserve manpower, which is a defensive programme in a war that Ukraine had been fighting on the offensive for a year. Whether that is a strategy or a description of the situation will become clear during the summer, when the ground dries and the Russian army moves again. Until then, the appointment is a decision about who answers for the war rather than about how it will be fought.
