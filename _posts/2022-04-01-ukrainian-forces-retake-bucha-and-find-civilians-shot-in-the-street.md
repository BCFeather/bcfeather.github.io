---
title: "Ukrainian forces retake Bucha and find civilians shot in the street"
date: 2022-04-01 21:10:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A town north-west of Kyiv changed hands twice in five weeks, and when Ukrainian troops walked back in they found bodies in civilian clothes lying along a residential road, some with their hands tied, which turned a military withdrawal into the most documented atrocity of the war so far."
---

Ukrainian troops entered Bucha on 1 April, three days after Russian forces pulled out of the northern axis around Kyiv, and found the town's main road lined with dead civilians. Photographs and video from the scene showed bodies in winter coats and tracksuits, several face down, some with their wrists bound behind them, and residents described men being taken from apartment blocks and shot in the street. A trench behind a church held more, and neighbours had begun to fill it in before the army arrived.

The town had been held since the first week of March, when Russian armour moved down the road from the Belarusian border towards Kyiv and stalled at Irpin. Bucha sat inside that pocket, close enough to the capital that artillery could reach it and far enough that no camera covered it. Fighting destroyed the main street, and those who stayed spent five weeks in basements without water, electricity or medical care, drawing water from a well and burying neighbours in gardens.

Moscow's account arrived within hours. The defence ministry called the footage staged and said the bodies had been placed after Russian troops left, and Vladimir Putin repeated the claim at a press conference on 12 April, describing Bucha as a provocation prepared in advance to justify a new sanctions package. Journalists who reached the town in the following days described bodies that had been in the open for weeks, and satellite photographs taken while Russian forces were still in the area showed shapes on the road in the same positions.

Evidence collection began immediately, and it will take years. The prosecutor general's office reported more than 1,200 civilian bodies recovered across the Kyiv region by the middle of April, some from mass graves, and forensic teams worked through identification, cause of death and chain of custody, taking DNA samples from relatives who had come to a schoolyard to give them. Families who had buried a father or a son in a garden before the withdrawal asked for the graves to be opened, and the regional prosecutor opened a case file for each street. A Reuters photographer, Maks Levin, who had gone missing near the village of Huta Mezhyhirska, was found dead with two gunshot wounds, the sixth journalist killed in the country since the invasion.

The discovery reshaped the diplomacy. Talks that had produced a tentative framework in Istanbul at the end of March lost momentum, and Putin said Ukraine had walked away from the understandings reached there. The European Union adopted a fifth package of sanctions on 8 April that included a ban on coal imports, and the Group of Seven governments pledged to pursue war crimes cases, while the International Criminal Court's prosecutor, who had opened an investigation on 2 March, sent a team to the region.

Legal process will run far behind politics, and the two will not converge neatly. Mass casualty events in this war have a way of becoming symbols before they become case files, and Bucha is now the reference point against which later discoveries in Borodianka, Irpin and the south are measured, which is both a form of accountability and a risk to it.
