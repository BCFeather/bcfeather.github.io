The second day of the lunar year is the day on which a married daughter returns to her parents' home, and in the parts of the country where the custom is strongest the morning is spent on a road. The car is loaded the night before, the gifts are counted, the child is dressed in the red coat that was bought for the occasion, and the route is planned around the traffic that the navigation app predicts for the hour after breakfast.

The custom has an old explanation and a current one. The old one is about the household that a woman left when she married, and the visit was the annual return to it. The current one is about the calendar. The first day belongs to the household she lives in, the second belongs to the household she came from, and the two are balanced by a rule that everyone accepts.

The gift is the part of the visit that carries the most information. A box of fruit, a bottle of oil, a package of the tea that the father prefers, a toy for a nephew. The gift is not a payment and it is not a formality, and the household that receives it reads the relationship in what was chosen. The value has a range, the range is narrow, and the calculation is done by both families every year.

The meal is the centre of the day and it is usually cooked by the mother. The dishes are the ones that the daughter remembers from childhood, the soup that takes four hours, the fish that is fried whole, and the table is set with the plates that are kept for the occasion. The meal is a piece of memory served hot, and the daughter who has moved to a city a thousand kilometres away eats it once a year.

The visit also has a duration that is negotiated. A family that has to drive back for work on the fourth day stays one night, and a family that has a longer holiday stays three. The duration is a compromise between the two households and the employer, and the compromise is renegotiated every January.

What the day shows is that a festival is a structure of obligations. The obligations are distributed across the days of the holiday, and each day has a household that it belongs to. The second day is the day of the return, and the return is the part of the festival that the migration is organized around.

For the family that observes the custom loosely, the day still has a use. It is the day on which the year's first long drive happens, the day on which the cousins see each other, and the day on which the photographs that will be looked at in December are taken. The custom has a shape and the shape survives the belief, which is why a family that has never explained the rule to its children still follows it.
