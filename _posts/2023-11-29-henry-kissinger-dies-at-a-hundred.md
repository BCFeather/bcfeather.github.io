title: "Henry Kissinger dies at a hundred"
date: 2023-11-29 23:45:00 +0800

description: "Henry Kissinger, who died on 29 November 2023 at the age of a hundred, shaped American foreign policy through detente, the opening to China and the end of the Vietnam war, and remained a sought-after consultant long after the policies he designed had been dismantled."
---

Henry Kissinger died at his home in Connecticut on 29 November at the age of a hundred. He had been national security adviser and then secretary of state under Richard Nixon and Gerald Ford, and he remained a consultant, author and speaker for four decades after leaving office. His firm advised governments and companies, and he travelled to Beijing as recently as July, when he met the Chinese leadership at a moment of strained relations.

His record is a list of the century's diplomatic turning points. He negotiated the strategic arms limitation talks with the Soviet Union, opened the channel to China that produced Nixon's visit in 1972, and shared the Nobel peace prize in 1973 for the Paris agreement that ended American combat in Vietnam. He also ordered the bombing of Cambodia, supported the overthrow of Salvador Allende in Chile, and approved the surveillance of his own staff, decisions that followed him for the rest of his life. He received the presidential medal of freedom in 1977, and his memoir of the Nixon years sold more copies than any other book by a secretary of state.

He was born in Furth, in Germany, in 1923, and his family fled the Nazis in 1938. He served in the American army in the second world war, studied at Harvard, and built an academic career on the study of Metternich and Bismarck before entering politics as an adviser to Nelson Rockefeller. His book on the Congress of Vienna is still read, and its argument, that a stable order depends on the acceptance of legitimate boundaries, was the intellectual frame for everything he did.

His method was personal and secretive. He preferred a small channel to a formal negotiation, and he treated the appearance of agreement as a tool in itself. Critics said the method produced deals that ignored the people whose countries were being divided, and that his willingness to separate morality from strategy made him complicit in the killing that followed his decisions. Supporters said he understood that a statesman works with the world as it is.

His later career was a study in the persistence of influence. He wrote books on diplomacy, on Chinese foreign policy and on world order, and he was consulted by presidents of both parties. He was also the subject of sustained criticism, including attempts in several countries to question him about disappearances and deaths, and a lawsuit in Chile over the killing of a general in Washington in 1970.

His death removes the last senior figure of the cold war's opening to China. The relationship he helped to build has become the central strategic question of this decade, and the men who now manage it have inherited his framework without his access. In Beijing, the foreign ministry called him an old friend of the Chinese people and credited him with the opening that began in 1971, a phrase that says as much about the value of the channel he kept open as about the man himself.
