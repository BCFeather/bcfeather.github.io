---
title: "Biden goes to Israel and the Security Council stalls"
date: 2023-10-18 23:35:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "President Biden visited Tel Aviv on 18 October 2023 to pledge support and press for humanitarian aid, while in New York the United States vetoed a Brazilian resolution on Gaza and a summit in Amman was called off."
---

President Biden landed in Tel Aviv on 18 October for a visit that lasted less than eight hours, and he came with a message that combined support with limits. He told the Israeli prime minister that America would stand with the country after the attack of 7 October, and he said in the same breath that the way the war is fought matters, that humanitarian aid had to move into Gaza, and that the anger felt across the region had to be taken into account. He also met families of hostages and repeated that the United States was working on their release. He flew in and out on the same day, and the visit was announced less than forty-eight hours before it began.

The trip came after a blast at a hospital in Gaza City killed several hundred people, an event that had already produced rival accounts of responsibility and street protests in several Arab capitals. Arab leaders declined to meet him in Amman, and a planned summit with the king of Jordan, the Egyptian president and the Palestinian president was cancelled. The cancellation denied the administration a venue in which it could present its insistence on aid and on civilian protection to the governments that matter most in the region.

In New York the same day, the Security Council failed again. A draft resolution written by Brazil, which called for humanitarian pauses and condemned violence against civilians on both sides, drew twelve votes in favour, with Russia and Britain abstaining and the United States vetoing. The American ambassador said the text did not mention Israel's right to self-defence, and other members said the council was being prevented from doing its job. A Russian draft had failed two days earlier for lack of support.

Aid became the practical test. The Rafah crossing between Gaza and Egypt was damaged and its operation depends on agreement among three governments and armed groups on the ground, and trucks waiting on the Egyptian side could not move for days. The United Nations said more than a hundred trucks a day would be needed to replace the commercial supplies that had stopped, and the first convoys that eventually crossed carried a fraction of that.

American policy is constrained by domestic politics. The administration has asked Congress for more than a hundred billion dollars in supplemental spending that includes military aid for Israel and for Ukraine, and the request has to pass a House that has no Speaker. It has also warned Iran and Hezbollah against opening a wider front, and it has moved two carrier groups and additional air defence assets into the region as a signal that the warning carries weight.

The visit produced statements rather than outcomes. Biden said Israel had the right and the duty to respond, and he said the response should follow the laws of war. Those two commitments pull in different directions in a densely populated strip, and the diplomacy of the following weeks will be about how much of the second the first can accommodate.
