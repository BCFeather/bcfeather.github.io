---
title: "Serena Williams says she will stop playing and the US Open becomes a farewell"
date: 2022-08-09 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [sport]
description: "In an essay for a fashion magazine she describes moving away from tennis, and she leaves the number of tournaments open."
---

Serena Williams announced on Tuesday that she would stop competing, and she chose a fashion magazine to say it rather than a press conference. In the essay she described the last year as a transition and said she would be evolving away from the sport rather than retiring from it. She is forty next month and she has won twenty-three singles titles at the grand slams, one short of the record. The announcement did not say when the last match would be, which turned the American Open into the occasion.

The timing was her own and it reflected the shape of the season. She had returned in the summer after a year away, and she had played three tournaments without reaching the later rounds. Her body had been the problem for most of the last five years, and she wrote that she had been pregnant during the last Australian Open and had not told anyone. The essay also described the difficulty of choosing between the sport and the family she wants to grow.

The record she did not reach is the one that will be discussed. Margaret Court won twenty-four singles titles, most of them before the open era, and the comparison has followed Williams for a decade. She reached four finals after her return in 2018 and lost all four, two of them to players who were ranked outside the top ten. Her supporters say the number is a formality and her critics say it is the measure, and neither argument will be settled now.

At the same time, the money she earned from the sport changed the sport. She and her sister came from a public court in Compton and they were paid the same as the men at the slams after a campaign that the two of them led. Her prize money is the largest in the history of women's tennis, and her investment in venture funds and in a fashion label has made her one of the wealthiest athletes. She has also been the reason that a generation of Black players from the American public courts took up the game.

Meanwhile, the farewell will be played in New York, where she has won six times and where the crowd has treated her as a local. The tickets for the first week sold out within hours, and the tournament said it would not schedule her matches at the same time as the other attractions. The players she will face have said they grew up watching her, which is what the players of every generation say about the one before. The draw was made on Thursday, and her first opponent was a woman ranked outside the top fifty.

What the announcement does is close the career of the player who defined the last two decades of the women's game. She won her first major in 1999 and her last in 2017, and the gap between them is the longest in the open era. The sport will now be without the person who filled its stadiums and its arguments, and it will take a while to notice how much of both she carried.
