---
title: "Two anchors leave in the same afternoon"
date: 2023-04-24 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "Fox News and CNN announced the departures of Tucker Carlson and Don Lemon on 24 April 2023 within an hour of each other, ending the two most discussed evening careers in American cable news on the same day."
---

Fox announced on the morning of 24 April that it had agreed to part ways with Tucker Carlson, and CNN announced an hour later that it had parted with Don Lemon. Neither network gave a reason beyond a statement, and neither host appeared on air again. Carlson's programme had been the highest-rated show on cable news, and Lemon had been a fixture of the morning since 2014 and of the evening before that. Neither network named a permanent replacement, and both filled the hour with a rotation of existing hosts.

The timing put both departures inside the week that followed the Dominion settlement. Carlson had been named in the discovery material, and his private messages about the election claims had been read out in pretrial filings. Fox said the decision was mutual and had been reached some weeks earlier, and the network's critics noted that the announcement came four days after it paid 787.5 million dollars to avoid a trial.

Lemon's departure had its own history. He had apologized in February for a comment about a woman's age, had been moved from prime time to mornings in 2022, and had been criticized inside the network for a combative manner with colleagues. The announcement described the end of a thirty-five-year career, and the network's chief executive said the company wished him well, which is the language it uses when a contract has been bought out. He had been moved to mornings in 2022 after a decade in prime time, and the morning show never recovered the audience it lost.

Both men occupied the same structural position and stood for opposite things. Carlson built a programme around the argument that institutions lie and that the audience is being managed, and Lemon built one around the argument that institutions need scolding. Both lost the confidence of the company that employed them within a year, and both left with a large audience that had been told the network was the problem.

Business explains part of it: cable news is shrinking. Prime-time audiences have fallen by a third since 2020, the networks are being folded into streaming services that lose money, and the contracts that made anchors famous are now the largest line in budgets that are being cut. Carlson's replacement drew about half his audience in the first week, and CNN's morning show has been rebuilt twice since. The parent company wrote down the value of its cable assets in the following quarter, and the contracts of the anchors who remain are ones that nobody is now willing to match.

The departures also illustrated how much of the industry's output depended on two people's judgments. Carlson's show set the agenda for a party and for a movement, and Lemon's interviews set the tone for a network's coverage of race and of politics. Neither network has explained what will replace them, and the audience that either man could move is the asset that the industry is now trying to count.
