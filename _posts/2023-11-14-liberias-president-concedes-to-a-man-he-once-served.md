---
title: "Liberia's president concedes to a man he once served"
date: 2023-11-14 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Joseph Boakai defeated President George Weah in Liberia's runoff on 14 November 2023 by 50.64 per cent to 49.36, and the former footballer conceded three days later, giving the country its first peaceful transfer between elected presidents in more than a decade."
---

Joseph Boakai won Liberia's presidential runoff on 14 November with 50.64 per cent of the vote against 49.36 for the incumbent, George Weah, and the president conceded on the evening of 17 November before the final results were published. The margin was about twenty thousand votes out of more than 1.6 million cast, and turnout was high enough that election officials took several days to complete the count in the country's fifteen counties.

Weah, a former striker who won the Ballon d'Or in 1995 and was elected in 2017, accepted the result in a statement that praised the electoral commission and asked his supporters to accept the outcome. His concession removed the tension that had built during the count, when both campaigns traded accusations about the handling of ballots in Nimba and Bong counties. International observers from the African Union and the regional bloc described the process as credible, with some irregularities logged and no evidence of systematic fraud.

Boakai, who is seventy-eight, served as vice president under Ellen Johnson Sirleaf for twelve years and lost to Weah in 2017. His campaign promised to repair roads, restore electricity and attack corruption in an economy that had struggled since the Ebola epidemic and the fall in commodity prices. His running mate, Jewel Howard-Taylor, was Weah's vice president and ran against her former principal, a split that said a great deal about how Liberian politics is organized around personalities and patronage.

The election matters beyond Liberia because it is one of a series of close votes in West Africa in which incumbents have been held to account. Sierra Leone, Nigeria and Liberia all held elections in 2023, and in each case the result was contested and the institutions were tested. Liberia's own history makes the stakes higher: the country fought two civil wars between 1989 and 2003, and the peace that followed has depended on elections being accepted by the losers.

The incoming government inherits a thin budget and a heavy debt. Rubber and iron ore account for most exports, and the price of both has been volatile. The country relies on remittances and on foreign aid, and its currency has been under pressure for two years. Boakai has promised to publish contracts and to cut the size of the cabinet, promises that every incoming administration in Monrovia has made and that few have kept.

Weah leaves office with a mixed record. He built roads and kept the peace, and his supporters credit him with stability after the years of the virus. His critics point to the collapse of the health system, the failure to prosecute the war-era crimes that remain unaddressed, and a cabinet that was several times accused of corruption. The transition is scheduled for January, and the fact that it is happening at all is the most important thing about the result. Observers logged a handful of polling stations that opened late and one county where the count slipped by a day, details the commission published in its final report and that the losing party accepted without going to court.
