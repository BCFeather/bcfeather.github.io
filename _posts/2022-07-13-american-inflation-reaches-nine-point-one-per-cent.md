---
title: "American inflation reaches nine point one per cent, the highest since 1981"
date: 2022-07-13 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "Petrol and rents drive the June figure, and the core measure also rises as the central bank prepares another large increase."
---

Consumer prices rose by nine point one per cent in the year to June, the highest rate since November 1981, and the monthly increase was one point three per cent. The figure was above the eight point eight that the economists had forecast. Petrol contributed about half of the monthly rise, and the national average price of a gallon reached five dollars in the middle of the month. The core measure, which excludes food and energy, rose by five point nine per cent.

The analysts were less impressed by the headline than by its breadth. The prices of shelter, of medical care and of transport services all rose, and the cost of air fares and of hotels continued the increase that began in the spring. Food prices rose by ten point four per cent over the year, the fastest since 1981, and the price of eggs was up by more than thirty per cent. The categories that are set by the market and the ones that are set by contracts have both moved.

Within the hour the White House had answered. The president said the figure was unacceptably high and that the country had to bring it down, and he pointed to the fall in the price of petrol that had begun in the last two weeks. The Republicans said the figure was the result of the spending that the administration had passed, and they blamed the release of oil from the strategic reserve for a temporary relief. The two arguments have been made in every month since the spring.

By the afternoon the forecast for the July meeting had moved to a full point. The officials had said that they would consider an increase of three quarters, and the figure changed the arithmetic of the meeting. The governor of one of the regional banks said on the day that a full point was on the table, and the futures market moved to price it. The bank has raised by three quarters once, in June, and it has not raised by a full point since 1994.

Rarely do American officials compare the figure with the rest of the world. Inflation in the euro area is eight point six per cent and in Britain it is nine point four. The causes are the same: the price of energy, the disruption of supply chains that the pandemic left and the tight labour markets that the rich countries have in common. The difference is that the American economy is growing more slowly and the dollar is rising, which lowers the price of imports and raises the cost of everything for the countries that borrow in it.

The central bank now finds itself where it has not been since 1980. It has to raise rates fast enough to break the expectations that prices will keep rising, and not so fast that the economy stops. The instruments that it has are blunt and the lags are long, and the officials know that the effects of the increases will not be visible until the end of the year. The next report is due in August, and by then the petrol price will have fallen and the core will still be rising.
