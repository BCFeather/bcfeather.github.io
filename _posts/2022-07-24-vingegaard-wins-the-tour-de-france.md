title: "Vingegaard wins the Tour de France and Pogačar is beaten at last"
date: 2022-07-24 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [sport]
description: "The Danish rider takes the yellow jersey in the Alps and holds it through the final time trial, three minutes and twenty-one seconds ahead."
---

Jonas Vingegaard won the Tour de France on Sunday in Paris, and the race ended with the yellow jersey on the shoulders of a rider who had been a lieutenant two years ago. He finished three minutes and twenty-one seconds ahead of Tadej Pogačar, who had won the race in the two previous years. He is the first Danish winner since 1996 and the second in the country's history. The final stage was a procession into the city until the last laps of the Champs-Élysées.

Everything changed in the Alps in the third week. Vingegaard attacked on the Col du Granon and dropped Pogačar, who had not been isolated from his team in the mountains before. The next day the two of them traded attacks on Alpe d'Huez, and the day after that Pogačar won a stage but could not take back the time. The final time trial on the penultimate day confirmed the margin, and the last stage was a formality.

For three years Jumbo-Visma had been assembled around one goal. The squad rode at the front of the peloton on the flat stages to protect him and to keep the breakaways in check, and its riders were with him in the mountains until the last kilometre. The budget of the team is the largest in the sport, and the sponsor is a supermarket chain that has said it will stay for another five years.

It also answered a question about the Slovenian rider, and he will not like the answer that the three weeks gave. Pogačar had won seven stages in two years and had looked unbeatable in the time trials, and this year he was beaten in the mountains by a rider who was stronger on the long climbs. He said afterwards that he had made mistakes in the heat and in the feeding, and that he would return. He is twenty-three and he has a decade in front of him.

Heat and illness shaped the three weeks. The temperatures in the south of France passed forty degrees in the second week, and several riders left the race with covid. The organizers shortened one stage because of the heat, and the riders' union asked for more. The crowds on the climbs were as large as they have ever been, and the police used tear gas at one point on Alpe d'Huez to keep the road clear.

The Tour can still be won by a rider who ranks below the best in raw talent, provided his team rides as one unit from the first flat kilometre to the last climb. Vingegaard was a better climber in the third week and his team was stronger in the flat, and the combination was enough. The sport now has a rivalry rather than a dynasty, and the next edition will begin with the two of them as favourites. The Danish flags on the Champs-Élysées were the sign that the country has a new hero, and the country has waited twenty-six years for one.
