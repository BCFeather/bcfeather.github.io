---
title: "A grand jury indicts a former president"
date: 2023-03-30 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A Manhattan grand jury voted to indict Donald Trump on 30 March 2023 over a payment to a pornographic film actress before the 2016 election, the first criminal charge against a former American president."
---

The indictment was filed on Thursday evening and remained sealed, and the news came from the district attorney's office and from the former president's own statement. The grand jury had been hearing evidence for months about a payment of a hundred and thirty thousand dollars made to an actress before the 2016 election, and about the way the payment had been recorded in the accounts of a company. The charge was reported as falsification of business records, and the number of counts became public only at the arraignment. The filing was made public only at the arraignment, and the counts were read out in a courtroom where cameras had been banned.

Made by the former president's lawyer, the payment was later reimbursed in instalments that were described as legal fees. The arrangement was the part that prosecutors had been examining since 2018, when the lawyer pleaded guilty to a campaign finance charge and said he had acted at his client's direction. The federal prosecutors had declined to charge the former president, and the state prosecutor had continued the inquiry, which is why the case arrived in Manhattan rather than in Washington.

Whether the records amounted to a misdemeanour or a felony was the legal question. Falsifying business records is a misdemeanour in New York unless it is done to conceal another crime, and the prosecution's theory was that the concealment covered a campaign finance violation. The theory had been tested in the lawyer's case and had never been tested against a candidate, and its novelty was the reason the case was described as uncertain by lawyers on both sides.

Reaction came within two hours and followed the lines drawn in 2016. The former president called the indictment a political prosecution and asked his supporters for money, and the Speaker of the House said the prosecutor was attacking the country's democratic process, and the Senate majority leader said the rule of law applied to everyone. The statements were issued within two hours of each other and contained no new information.

The city prepared for the arraignment before the indictment was unsealed. The police put up barriers around the courthouse, the Secret Service met the local agencies, and the mayor told residents to go about their business. The former president travelled from Florida the following Monday and was arraigned on Tuesday, and the courtroom photographs were the first of their kind. The courthouse was surrounded by barricades for a week, and the arraignment was set for the afternoon so that the morning commute would be unaffected.

What the indictment established was that a former president could be charged by a local prosecutor and could be required to appear in a state court. The establishment was procedural and it was the whole of the story for the week, because the facts of the case were a payment and a ledger and the politics were a country that had already decided what it thought. The trial was scheduled for a year later, and the campaign that the defendant was running continued throughout.
