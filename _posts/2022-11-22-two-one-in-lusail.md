---
title: "Two one in Lusail"
date: 2022-11-22 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [sport]
description: "Saudi Arabia beat Argentina 2-1 at the World Cup, ending a thirty-six match unbeaten run and turning the group upside down."
---
Argentina arrived at Lusail as one of the favourites and left it with a defeat that nobody in the stadium had planned to see. The first half went as the form book suggested it would, with the ball spent almost entirely in the Saudi half and a penalty converted by Lionel Messi after ten minutes. The goal was his fourth in the tournament's opening round across his career, and it made him the first Argentine to score in four different World Cups. The Argentine supporters had travelled in numbers, and by kick-off the stands were a wall of pale blue shirts.

Changes came at half time, and they came from the Saudi bench. The team that returned pushed its line higher up the pitch, closed the space in midfield and refused to sit in front of its own penalty area. Two goals arrived within eight minutes of the restart, the first from Saleh Al-Shehri at the near post and the second from Salem Al-Dawsari, who cut inside two defenders and curled the ball into the far corner. The stadium, which had been overwhelmingly Argentine, went quiet in a way that could be heard on the broadcast. Al-Dawsari's goal was the kind that players rehearse in training and rarely reproduce in a match of that size.

Argentina pushed for an equalizer and had the ball in the net three times, and each of those goals was ruled out for offside by a margin measured in centimetres. The line that decides such things is drawn by a machine, and it produced verdicts that the crowd did not accept and could not dispute. The coach made three substitutions in the last twenty minutes, and none of them changed the shape of the game. The Saudi defence stepped up on every occasion, and the trap worked often enough to leave the Argentines arguing with the assistant referee.

The result ended a run of thirty-six matches without defeat, a sequence that had included a Copa América title and a continental record. It also put Argentina at the bottom of the group with one match played, and left the team needing to win against Mexico and Poland to be sure of progressing. Reporters in Buenos Aires wrote about a curse, and the players spoke about a mistake they intended to correct. The coach said at his press conference that his team had lost a match it expected to win, and that the group had become a different problem.

For Saudi Arabia the win was the largest in its football history, and the country declared a public holiday the following day. The coach, Hervé Renard, had spent the weeks before the match telling his players that they could compete, and the performance made his argument look reasonable. The goalkeeper made two saves in the second half that kept the lead intact, and the defence held for eight minutes of stoppage time. In Riyadh the streets filled within minutes of the final whistle, and the celebrations went on past midnight.

Afterwards the talk was of a group that had become unpredictable, because Poland and Mexico had drawn earlier in the day. A team that had been written off before kick-off now had three points and a chance, and the team that had been expected to win it had none. The second round of matches would decide whether the result was a turning point or an exception.
