---

Bassirou Diomaye Faye won the presidential election in Senegal on the twenty-fourth of March with about fifty-four per cent of the vote, according to the official count, and his main opponent conceded before the results were published. Faye had been released from prison ten days earlier, and he campaigned from a car with a small team after his opponent, Amadou Ba, the former prime minister, had spent months touring the country with the resources of the state.

Result came as a shock to the political class but not to pollsters, who had recorded a swing towards the opposition in the weeks after Faye's release. Turnout was about sixty-one per cent, and Faye won majorities in most of the country's regions, including areas that had voted for the governing party in previous elections. His coalition, which includes his party, Pastef, and several smaller groups, also won a majority in the legislative elections that followed in November.

Origin of the campaign is a political imprisonment. Faye was arrested in 2023 on charges of inciting insurrection and was held for eleven months, along with Ousmane Sonko, the leader of Pastef, who was barred from the ballot. Both were released under an amnesty law that the outgoing president, Macky Sall, proposed in March, and Faye ran as the candidate of a movement whose leader could not stand. He had been a tax inspector and had never held elected office.

Programme that he ran on is radical in its economics. Faye promised to renegotiate contracts with foreign oil, gas and mining companies, to create a national currency instead of the CFA franc that Senegal shares with other west African states, and to halve the cost of basic food imports. He also promised to reduce the size of the government and to publish the salaries of senior officials. Economists said the currency proposal would be difficult to carry out alone, and the regional central bank said in April that it saw no basis for a change.

Foreign policy in his campaign was framed around sovereignty. He said Senegal would remain a partner of France but would end the presence of French troops, and he repeated the promise after taking office. He also said the country would maintain its ties with the United States and with China, and that it would seek to attract investment on terms that keep a larger share of the value in the country.

Consequence of the twenty-fourth of March is a government that will be judged on whether it can deliver a break with the past without breaking the economy. Faye took office on the second of April and appointed Sonko as prime minister. The country's finances are constrained by a programme with the International Monetary Fund and by a debt load that has grown since the discovery of offshore gas, and the promises of the campaign cost money that the budget does not have. Senegal has been one of the more stable democracies in west Africa, and the election was conducted without violence in a region where many votes are not. Whether the new government's radicalism survives contact with creditors is the question that the next two years will answer.
