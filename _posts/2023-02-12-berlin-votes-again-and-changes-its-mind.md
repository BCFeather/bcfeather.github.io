---
title: "Berlin votes again and changes its mind"
date: 2023-02-12 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Berlin re-ran its 2021 state election on 12 February 2023, and the Christian Democrats won 28.2 per cent while the Social Democrats fell to 18.4 per cent, their worst result in the city."
---

Berlin repeated its state election on 12 February, eighteen months after the original vote had been annulled by the constitutional court because of the chaos at polling stations. The Christian Democrats won 28.2 per cent, up more than ten points on the annulled result, and the Social Democrats fell to 18.4 per cent, their worst score in the city since the war. The Greens took 18.4 per cent as well, the Left 12.2 per cent, and the Free Democrats 4.6 per cent, below the threshold in the city for the first time in decades.

The annulment was the reason the vote happened, and it was also the reason the result was read as a verdict on the city government. In September 2021 voters had waited in queues for hours, some polling stations had run out of ballots, and the marathon that ran the same weekend had closed roads around them. The court ruled that the failures were serious enough to require a re-run, and the re-run became a referendum on the coalition that had governed since 2016. The court's ruling had listed the failures in order, and the list read as an indictment of an administration rather than of a polling station.

Local matters that had become national symbols shaped the campaign. Housing was the first of them, in a city where rents had risen by half in a decade and where the senate had nationalized thousands of apartments in a contested deal. Transport was the second, after a year of strikes and broken escalators, and the third was the administration itself, which the opposition described as a machine for producing excuses.

The result produced a coalition that had been unthinkable a year earlier. The Christian Democrats had campaigned against the governing mayor and ended up governing with her party, and Kai Wegner became the first Christian Democrat to lead Berlin since 2001. The agreement was signed in April, after a month of talks in which the Social Democrats extracted a promise to keep the rent cap legislation alive.

Turnout was high for a repeat election, and the cost was the part that the city's auditors complained about. The re-run cost about forty million euros, and it produced a parliament with a different majority but the same problems, including a housing market that no city government had been able to cool. The lesson that the election taught was that annulling a vote is easier than governing after the annulment.

What the city got was a government with a thin mandate and a long list of inherited failures. Wegner's coalition held a bare majority in the city parliament, and its first year was spent on the housing and transport files that had brought the previous government down. The repeat election had been intended to restore legitimacy, and it produced a result that its winners described as a mandate and its losers described as a correction. A mandate that thin would be tested by the budget, which the city had been running on a reserve since the autumn.
