The first of May is a five-day holiday, and the evening before it is the hour in which a very large number of people decide how to spend it. The decisions were made weeks earlier in the bookings and the tickets, and they are made again on the evening itself by the people who did not book, and the two populations produce the traffic that the news reports as a number of vehicles on a highway.

The arithmetic of the trip has three variables and they are traded against each other. The first is the time, which is five days and two of which are spent travelling if the destination is far. The second is the money, which is the price of the transport and the room, and the price rises with the demand and falls with the inconvenience. The third is the crowd, which is the variable that the traveller cannot control and always underestimates.

The destinations that fill up are the ones that are within a half-day's travel of a large city, which is a consequence of the shape of the holiday rather than of the quality of the place. A five-day break rewards a destination that can be reached in four hours and punished one that takes twelve, because the twelve-hour destination consumes half the holiday in the getting there. The resorts that have invested in a high-speed rail connection have understood this and the ones that have not are still waiting for a highway.

The economics of the holiday for the places that receive the visitors are a mixture. A county that fills its hotels for five days earns a month of revenue in a week, and it also spends a month of its maintenance budget in the same week, and the roads, the toilets and the rubbish are the items that the balance sheet does not capture. The places that have learned to manage the peak have built the infrastructure for the peak, and the infrastructure is idle for the rest of the year, which is the permanent problem of a seasonal economy.

The travellers who do not travel are the majority and their holiday is a different calculation. A five-day break at home is a period in which the city is quiet, the roads are empty and the shops that stay open are the ones that the residents use. The holiday at home has become a plan rather than a failure, and the platforms that sell it have a name for it that translates as lying flat, which is a phrase that has moved from the internet into the travel business.

What the evening before produces is a nationwide pause in which the same question is asked in a hundred million households, and the answer is a decision about the next five days that will be remembered for a year. The holiday is short and the decisions are large, and the disproportion is what a holiday is for, which is the reason the travel industry can sell the same five days to the same person every year.
