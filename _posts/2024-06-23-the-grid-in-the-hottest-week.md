title: "The grid in the hottest week"
date: 2024-06-23 21:20:00 +0800

A heat wave pushed the national power load past a record this week. The figure the grid operator watches passed fourteen hundred million kilowatts. Peak arrived at half past two in the afternoon, when offices were still open and apartments were still cool. The shape of the peak has changed in the past decade. It used to arrive in the evening, when the factories ran a night shift and the households turned on their lights. It now arrives in the afternoon, because air conditioning has become the largest single load on a summer day.

Air conditioning is concentrated in the cities of the east. A province with a hundred million residents can add tens of millions of kilowatts within an hour of a temperature rise. A grid cannot store that much power, so it has to be available at the moment it is asked for. The reserve is made of several parts. Coal plants that were idle are started in the morning. Hydro plants in the mountains hold water for the afternoon. A few pumped storage stations move water uphill at night so that it can fall again at noon.

Demand response is the other tool. A factory that agrees to stop its furnaces for two hours is paid for the interruption. A shopping centre that raises its thermostats by one degree is paid a smaller sum. Payments are organised by the provincial grid and contracts are signed in May.

An old neighbourhood's transformer is the weakest point. A block built in the eighties was wired for a fraction of the load that its residents now ask for. When every apartment runs an air conditioner at once, the transformer overheats and the protection trips. Repair takes four hours and residents spend them in the corridor. Outdoor work is suspended between noon and four in several provinces. A regulation that also fixes a payment for high temperature contains the rule. A courier who delivers through the afternoon is entitled to the payment and rarely receives it, because the platform counts him as self-employed.

Hospitals see the consequence. Heat stroke is rare and heat exhaustion is common, and the wards that treat it fill in the second day of a wave. Patients are old people who live alone and who did not turn on the machine. The river temperature is part of the calculation. A plant that draws water for cooling cannot return it above a limit, so a hot week limits its output as well as its fuel. Two numbers are watched, air and water, and the lower of them sets the ceiling.

Readings come once a month, billed on a rising scale. A household that uses twice the baseline pays several times the price for the second block, which is the reason the thermostat goes up by a degree in poor neighbourhoods before it goes up in rich ones. The grid will hold this summer, as it held the last one. The margin is small and the reserve is thinner than the annual report suggests, which is the reason the operator publishes a warning before every wave.
