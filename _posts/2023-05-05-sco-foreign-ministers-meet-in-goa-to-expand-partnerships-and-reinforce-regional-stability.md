---
title: "SCO foreign ministers meet in Goa to expand partnerships and reinforce regional stability"
date: 2023-05-05 18:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "SCO foreign ministers met in Goa, India, to prepare for the July summit and admit four new dialogue partners. The talks focused on counter-terrorism, trade connectivity and the accession of Iran and Belarus, amid wider global geopolitical tensions."
---

Foreign ministers of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) gathered in Goa for the bloc's Council of Foreign Ministers meeting, charting the agenda for the upcoming SCO Heads of State Summit and welcoming four new dialogue partners amid rising global fragmentation and supply-chain uncertainty.

Hosted under India's rotating SCO presidency, the two-day meeting brought together top diplomats from all eight full member states: China, India, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Russia, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. On the sidelines, the SCO secretariat signed memorandums granting dialogue-partner status to the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, the Maldives and Myanmar, broadening the organization's footprint across South Asia and the Gulf region. These states will now take part in SCO working groups covering trade, energy, transport, security and cultural exchange, without full voting rights.

The core discussion centred on the 'Shanghai Spirit', the SCO's founding principle of mutual trust, equality, shared benefit and non-alignment. Ministers reaffirmed their commitment to counter the 'three evil forces' of terrorism, separatism and extremism, alongside cross-border drug trafficking and organised crime. Delegates also exchanged views on the situation in Afghanistan, calling for an inclusive political structure and sustained anti-terror action within the country.

Economic connectivity remained a major focus. Ministers backed projects to streamline cross-border transport corridors, stabilise regional supply chains and boost trade in agricultural goods, energy and digital services. Participants highlighted the SCO Interbank Consortium and the Business Council as practical tools to finance infrastructure, while aligning many initiatives with the Belt and Road framework. Delegates warned against unilateral sanctions and protectionist barriers that disrupt regional commerce.

The meeting also advanced the formal accession process for Iran and Belarus, which were set to become full members at the July summit in New Delhi. Once admitted, they would expand the SCO's geographic reach and strengthen its voice in global governance. Analysts noted that the expansion reflects a wider shift towards multipolarity, as more states seek partnerships outside traditional Western-led blocs.

Diplomatic observers pointed to the unusual setting of the talks: India held both the SCO presidency and the G20 presidency in 2023. Unlike the deadlock seen at several G20 working sessions, SCO members maintained a collaborative tone, prioritising regional consensus over deep global divides. The presence of Pakistan's foreign minister, visiting India for the first time in twelve years, also drew attention as a small, confidence-building step between the two nuclear-armed neighbours.

Still, the grouping faces internal complexities. India and Pakistan hold long-standing territorial disputes, while member states hold divergent views on major global conflicts. SCO officials stress that the bloc is not a military alliance and does not demand uniform positions on every international issue. Instead, it offers a platform for pragmatic cooperation on shared regional risks.

Before closing, the foreign ministers finalised draft documents for the July summit, covering food security, energy cooperation, climate resilience and digital economy rules. As the SCO adds new partners and members, its challenge will be balancing the diverse interests of a fast-growing community while keeping its focus on stability and economic integration across Eurasia.
