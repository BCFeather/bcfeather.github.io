title: "The runoff that decided the next premier"
date: 2024-09-27 21:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The ruling party of Japan chose a new leader in a runoff on the twenty-seventh. The winner is a veteran who has run for the post four times, and the party he inherits has lost its majority in two elections."
---

Liberal Democratic Party of Japan elected a new leader on the twenty-seventh of September. Vote went to a runoff between two candidates who were separated by twenty-one members of parliament. The winner was Shigeru Ishiba, who had stood for the leadership four times and lost each of them. He is sixty-seven and he has held the defence and the agriculture portfolios, and he is known for a style that is drier than the one the party usually rewards.

Runoff was close. The first round gave the conservative candidate a lead among the members of parliament and the former defence minister a lead among the party's rank and file. Second round combined the two electorates. The party has lost its footing. A general election a year ago reduced its majority. Coalition it leads has governed with a partner that is smaller and less popular than it is.

Campaign turned on the policies that divide the party. A revision of the constitution, the level of defence spending and the treatment of the imperial household were the three questions that every candidate answered differently. The economic policy is the one that the markets watched. A candidate who promised to raise spending and to cut taxes was the favourite of the investors. Winner has promised a more cautious fiscal line.

Coalition leadership makes him prime minister. A vote in the parliament is a formality when the coalition holds the seats. Cabinet is formed within a week. The general election is due within a year. A prime minister who takes office in September has a year to prepare for a ballot that his party is currently projected to lose.

Opposition benefits from the party's argument. A public that has watched a governing party campaign against itself for a month is a public that is open to a change. Polls moved accordingly. The new premier's first task is the budget. A spending plan that has to pass the parliament in the autumn is the test of whether the coalition can hold. Members who backed the other candidate will be watching the first draft.

Factions decided the vote. A member of parliament who owes a seat to a local organization votes with the organization's preference. Factions that control the organizations negotiated through the week. The alliance with the United States is the file that the new leader inherits. A commitment to raise defence spending to a share of output that was agreed with the previous government is the first test, and the candidate who won has promised to keep the commitment. The leadership vote is a party procedure and it is reported as a national event. A country with a parliamentary system elects a government through a party room, and the room in Tokyo was watched by the ministries that will have to work with its result. By the middle of October the cabinet was formed, the budget was being written, and the factions that lost the vote were counting what their support would be worth in the general election.
