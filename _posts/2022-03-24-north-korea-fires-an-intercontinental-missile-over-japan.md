---
title: "North Korea fires an intercontinental missile over Japan"
date: 2022-03-24 23:52:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A missile that climbs 6,248 kilometres and lands an hour later in the sea, a photograph of a leader in a leather jacket, and a programme that has given up pretending to test satellites."
---

North Korea launched a missile from the area of Pyongyang on 24 March that flew for 67 minutes, reached an altitude of 6,248 kilometres and travelled 1,090 kilometres east before it fell into the sea. The state news agency said the weapon was the Hwasong-17, an intercontinental ballistic missile that had been displayed at a parade in 2020 and never flown, and it published photographs of Kim Jong Un watching the launch in a leather jacket with a cigarette in his hand. The flight was the longest that any North Korean missile had achieved.

The trajectory was designed to demonstrate range without crossing a neighbour. A missile fired on a lofted path climbs steeply, travels a short distance and falls back, and the altitude and the time in the air allow analysts to calculate what it would do on a normal trajectory. The range that the calculations produced was above 10,000 kilometres, which would put the west coast of the United States within reach, and the flight was the first test of an ICBM since November 2017.

Japan and South Korea detected the launch within minutes. The Japanese government issued a warning to residents in the north of the country to shelter, the coast guard told ships to avoid the area where the missile was expected to fall, and the prime minister condemned the test. South Korea's military said it had fired its own missiles in response, and the two countries' foreign ministers spoke on the telephone and agreed to press for a new resolution at the Security Council.

A resolution did not follow. China and Russia, which had vetoed the previous tightening of the sanctions in 2019, said the launch was a response to joint exercises in the region and that the dialogue had to be revived, and the meeting ended without a statement. The American ambassador said the council's silence was a license for more tests, and the sanctions that the panel of experts had been discussing were left in draft.

Domestic politics had a place in the launch as well. North Korea had spent two years closing its borders against the pandemic, had executed or imprisoned officials over the economic failure, and had told its people that the nuclear programme was the guarantee of survival. A successful ICBM launch, broadcast on the front page of the newspaper with a photograph of a smiling leader, was the kind of achievement that the system could report at a moment when the harvest and the currency were both bad.

What the launch showed about the programme was the progress that had been hidden by the failures of 2020 and 2021. The missile that flew in March was larger than the one displayed at the parade, it carried what the state called a hypersonic glide vehicle and a camera, and the photographs released afterwards showed the earth from an altitude that no North Korean camera had reached before. The next test in May flew a shorter distance and was judged a failure, and the programme went on through the year at a pace that the council could not slow.
