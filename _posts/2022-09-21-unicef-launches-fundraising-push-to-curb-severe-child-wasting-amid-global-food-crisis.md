---
title: "UNICEF launches fundraising push to curb severe child wasting amid global food crisis"
date: 2022-09-21 18:43:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "UNICEF secured major donor pledges at UNGA to treat severe child wasting, as war, drought and floods push millions of children into acute malnutrition."
---

UNICEF and partner governments convened a high‑level pledging event on the sidelines of the 77th UN General Assembly, mobilizing hundreds of millions of dollars to treat severe child wasting across the world's most vulnerable regions. The appeal came as overlapping shocks drove up rates of life‑threatening malnutrition.

Since July, donors had pledged around 577 million US dollars for nutrition programmes, with more than half of the funds channelled to UNICEF operations in 15 priority countries. Canada, Ireland, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom joined major philanthropic foundations in committing resources for ready‑to‑use therapeutic food, clinical treatment, and community nutrition screening.

The crisis stemmed from multiple overlapping pressures. Conflict in Ukraine disrupted grain and fertilizer exports, while prolonged drought in the Horn of Africa and catastrophic floods in Pakistan destroyed farmland, livestock and local food systems. For children under five, severe wasting weakens immunity and raises mortality risk, even if they survive the immediate hunger.

UNICEF officials emphasized that therapeutic feeding works, but funding gaps slow scaling. Many rural and remote communities lack regular health checks, so cases are detected only after children reach critical condition. The organization combines medical treatment with caregiver training, clean water support and cash transfers to address the root causes of hunger.

Speakers at the event warned that the global response was still falling behind rising need. Forecasts showed that malnutrition rates would keep climbing if harvests failed and humanitarian funding stayed short. Unlike one‑off food distributions, the new funding stream was designed to support sustained treatment and build local health capacity.

The pledging drive also highlighted equity gaps. Families hit hardest by food price spikes often have the least access to international support. The event called for richer nations to deliver existing aid commitments and stabilize global food markets, so low‑income states could maintain nutrition services for young children.
