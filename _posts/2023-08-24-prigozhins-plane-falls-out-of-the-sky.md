title: "Prigozhin's plane falls out of the sky"
date: 2023-08-24 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Yevgeny Prigozhin, the founder of the Wagner group, died on 23 August 2023 when his private jet crashed in the Tver region north of Moscow, two months after he led an armed mutiny against the Russian defence ministry."
---

An Embraer Legacy 600 flying from Moscow to St Petersburg crashed in a field near the village of Kuzhenkino on the evening of 23 August, and all ten people aboard were killed. The passenger list published by the aviation authority included Yevgeny Prigozhin, his second in command Dmitry Utkin, and several bodyguards. The flight lasted about thirty minutes, the aircraft appeared on tracking sites to climb and then fall, and the wreckage was spread across a field rather than in a line.

Two months earlier Prigozhin had marched a column of his fighters towards Moscow, shot down several military aircraft on the way and stopped only when a deal was announced that let him leave for Belarus. He returned to Russia within weeks, appeared at a summit in St Petersburg, and continued to recruit for operations in Africa. His mutiny was the most serious challenge to the Kremlin's authority since 1991, and his continued freedom was a puzzle to everyone who watched it, including the officials who had to explain why no charges were filed against him for it.

The investigation announced itself quickly and has said little since. Russia's investigative committee opened a criminal case on charges of air traffic violation, and officials said DNA testing would be needed to confirm the identities. American officials said a surface-to-air missile most likely brought the aircraft down, a view shared by several Western intelligence agencies, and the Kremlin's spokesman called the allegation an invention. Ukrainian officials, asked whether they had done it, said only that everyone knew what happens to people who betray the Russian president.

Vladimir Putin described Prigozhin as a man of talent and complicated fate, spoke of him in the past tense, and said the investigation would take time. In the days after the crash, Wagner's bases in Belarus began to be dismantled, its convoys withdrew, and the group's future was taken up by the defence ministry, which had been the target of the mutiny. Recruiters who had been calling for volunteers in Russian prisons stopped, and the group's remaining commanders were offered contracts with the defence ministry. Satellite images of the camp near Osipovichi in Belarus showed the tents being taken down within a week.

The crash removes a man who had become inconvenient and leaves the structures he built behind him. Wagner operates in Mali, Sudan, Libya and the Central African Republic, and its contracts were signed with governments that dealt with Prigozhin personally. The Russian state has been absorbing those relationships, and the question that follows the crash is who now collects the gold and the fees.

A bomb or a missile, this was an execution in public, carried out two months after the mutiny and a day after a meeting of the defence ministry's board. Russians who had wondered why the mutineer was allowed to live now have an answer, and the answer is the one they expected. The men who had marched on Moscow in June are now being folded into the army that they marched against.
