Germany controlled the first half in the way that a much larger team controls a smaller one, with the ball moving from side to side and Japan chasing it. The lead came from a penalty, converted by İlkay Gündoğan after the referee had been called to the screen by the video assistant. The Germans had chances to double it before the break, and the goalkeeper made two saves that kept the score at one. The German midfield kept the ball for long stretches, and the Japanese goalkeeper was called into action twice before the interval.

Japan changed its approach at half time. The coach brought on players who could run at defenders, pushed the wing-backs higher and told the midfield to press the moment the ball arrived at a German centre-back. The equalizer came in the seventy-fifth minute, when a loose ball fell to Ritsu Dōan inside the area and he finished low past the goalkeeper. Eight minutes later Takuma Asano ran in behind the defence and hit the near post from a narrow angle. The substitutions were made with a purpose that the starting eleven had lacked, and the crowd noticed the change before the goals arrived.

The turnaround had been prepared rather than improvised. Japan's squad contained players from the Bundesliga, and several of them had spent years playing against the men they beat. The coach said afterwards that his plan had been to stay in the match for an hour and then use the pace on the bench, and the substitutions followed that plan exactly. Germany's own changes came later and made the team slower. Japan's players knew the men opposite them from club football, and that knowledge showed in the way they closed down without being drawn out of position.

Germany finished the match with most of the possession and few clear chances, and its defenders were exposed twice in the last quarter of an hour. The result followed the pattern of the previous day, when Saudi Arabia had beaten Argentina from a similar position of inferior possession. Two of the tournament's favourites had lost to teams from outside the European and South American core, and both had lost in the second half. Germany's defenders were left chasing the ball in the last twenty minutes, and the two goals came from the same space behind the full-backs.
