---
title: "Hunter Biden agrees to plead guilty to tax charges"
date: 2023-06-20 18:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Hunter Biden agreed on 20 June to plead guilty to two misdemeanour tax charges and to enter a diversion programme on a firearms charge, an arrangement that would keep him out of prison and that his father's opponents immediately attacked."
---

Hunter Biden, the president's son, agreed on 20 June to plead guilty to two misdemeanour charges of failing to pay federal income tax in 2017 and 2018, and to enter a programme that would allow a felony charge over a firearm purchase to be dismissed if he completes conditions set by the court. The agreement was disclosed in a court filing by the prosecutor appointed to the case, and it would resolve a five-year investigation without a prison sentence if the judge accepts it. The White House said the president had no comment and that he loved his son.

The facts behind the charges are mostly a matter of public record. Biden earned substantial income from lobbying, from a law practice and from board positions, including at a Ukrainian energy company, and he failed to file and pay taxes on time in the two years covered by the agreement. The firearms charge arose from a 2018 purchase of a handgun at a time when he was using drugs, which he later described in a memoir, and the diversion programme requires him to remain drug-free and to agree not to possess firearms.

Legally, the case is likely to end without a trial, and that is the part that his father's political opponents object to. Republicans in Congress, who had been investigating the business dealings that the prosecutor examined, described the arrangement as a sweetheart deal and demanded to know why the charges were misdemeanours rather than felonies. Two committees had already been examining whether the justice department had slow-walked the case, and the plea will now be used in those investigations as evidence of either leniency or independence.

Politically the effect is different from the legal one. Hunter Biden has been a target of conservative media for years, and the deal gives that coverage a concrete outcome to argue over, which is useful for a campaign that wants to talk about the president's family rather than about the president's record. It also gives the White House a subject it would rather avoid, because any comment draws attention to the details of the case and any silence is read as an admission.

A court still has to accept the agreement, and the judge has said she will consider whether the terms are appropriate. If she does, the case ends with probation and with a programme that the defendant must complete, and the political argument will continue without a verdict to settle it. If she does not, the case becomes a trial, and the president's son becomes a defendant in an election year. Either way, the arrangement announced on 20 June is the closest the case has come to an ending, and it arrived at the moment when the campaign was beginning to take shape.

She will decide in the coming weeks whether the terms are acceptable, and the prosecutors have said they will not oppose them. If she accepts the arrangement, the case closes without a trial and the argument moves to the campaign, where it will be conducted by people who have read the filing and by people who have not.
