title: "A decree of three hundred and sixty-six articles"
date: 2023-12-20 23:10:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Javier Milei signed a decree on 20 December 2023 that rewrote or repealed 366 rules covering rents, labour contracts, price controls and state companies, and announced that congress would be asked to approve it in a single vote."
---

Argentina's new president signed a decree on 20 December that runs to 366 articles and rewrites large parts of the country's economic law, ten days after taking office. It repeals the rent law that had capped increases, allows dismissal without severance in some cases, removes price controls on food and medicine, converts state companies into corporations that can be sold, and ends the system of import permits that had governed access to foreign goods since 2014. The measure was announced on television in a list read out by the president, an unusual format for a legal instrument of that size.

The constitutional device is a decree of necessity and urgency, which Argentine presidents have used 600 times since 1983 and which takes effect immediately. Congress must review it within ten days, and the chamber is split evenly enough that the outcome is uncertain; the decree can also be challenged in court, and the unions and consumer groups announced challenges before the ink was dry. The government's argument is that the country's situation admits no delay, which is the argument every use of the device has made.

What the text contains is a programme rather than a set of corrections. Rent contracts may now be written in any currency and without a fixed term, which reverses a law passed in 2020 that had been blamed for a collapse in construction and a shortage of rental housing. Labour rules lose the presumption that an employment relationship is permanent, and severance is replaced by a fund. The state's companies become subject to the same accounting as private firms, and the registry of subsidies that had supported transport and energy for two decades is dismantled.

Protests began the following morning. Thousands of people marched in Buenos Aires and in the provincial capitals, the general confederation of labour announced a strike for 24 January, and the pickets that had been a feature of Argentine life returned to the avenues. The government answered by announcing that it would charge the cost of policing demonstrations to the organizations that called them, a measure that its lawyers described as a deterrent and that its critics described as a fine on constitutionally protected activity.

The economics will show in January, when the devaluation of the peso feeds through to prices. The government has said inflation will get worse before it gets better, and its own projections put the monthly rate above twenty per cent for the first quarter, with a fall in output that it calls unavoidable. The fiscal adjustment of five per cent of output is the largest attempted in the country since 2001, and it lands on a society where four in ten people were already below the poverty line.

Milei's bet is that a shock delivered fast enough will break the indexation that has kept Argentine inflation high for twenty years, and that the political cost will be paid in 2025 rather than now. The bet rests on a congress he does not control, on provinces that will lose transfers, and on a public that voted for the chainsaw without necessarily voting for the loss of the rent law. December gave him the decree, the devaluation and the first month of goodwill; January will give him the bill.
