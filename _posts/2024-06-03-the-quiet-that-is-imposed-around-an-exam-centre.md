title: "The quiet that is imposed around an exam centre"

description: "Before the university entrance exam a city suspends construction, diverts traffic and asks shops to lower their speakers. The rules describe what a city is willing to stop for."

In the week before the university entrance exam a city suspends construction near the schools that will host candidates. It diverts heavy vehicles away from a section of road. It asks the shops along that road to lower their loudspeakers. The notice is issued by the district office and enforced by officers who walk the street. Each measure is narrow. A site within a set distance of a centre stops work for the days of the exam. A market that opens at dawn is told to keep the noise under a level that an inspector can measure. The rules are published a fortnight ahead so that contractors can move their schedules.

Cost is what makes the quiet expensive at city scale. More than thirteen million candidates sat the exam this year, spread across thousands of centres, and each centre needs invigilators, a sealed room and a working speaker. The city that closes a road for three days is protecting a procedure that runs on printed paper, at a fixed hour, for a single national cohort. Every part of that procedure has to work at the same time in every province, which is why the notice about noise is issued nationally rather than left to a head teacher. A listening paper is the reason. In the foreign language test a section is played from a recording, through a speaker, to a room of thirty desks. A truck passing outside can make one sentence inaudible. A candidate hears it once and the paper moves on.

Enforcement varies by district. An office with staff closes the sites. An office without staff posts the notice and hopes. The difference shows in the complaints that parents file and in the reports that inspectors write. A parent who has paid for a room near a centre is sensitive to a drill that starts at nine in the morning.

The measures cost money that nobody budgets for. A contractor who stops for three days loses the days and pays the crew anyway. No compensation is written into the notice. The argument is settled between the contractor and the developer, and it is settled quickly because the alternative is a fine. Meaning beyond acoustics is what the quiet carries. A city that stops its building for three days is ranking the exam above everything else it could be doing. No concert or conference would stop the city this way. The suspension tells residents what the place values.

The candidates notice less than their parents. A student who has prepared for a year is looking at the paper. The parent who waits at the gate hears the truck. Both the candidate and the parent are served by the quiet.

What a city can switch off when it chooses to is what the week shows. Construction, traffic and a loudspeaker are the ordinary noise of a place that is being rebuilt. The exam suspends all three at once, on a date that the calendar fixed long ago. Year after year the arrangement repeats without much argument.
