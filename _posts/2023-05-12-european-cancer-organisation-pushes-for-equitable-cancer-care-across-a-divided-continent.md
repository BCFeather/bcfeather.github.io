---
title: "European Cancer Organisation pushes for equitable cancer care across a divided continent"
date: 2023-05-12 03:54:00 +0800
categories: [society]
description: "The European Cancer Organisation is campaigning to cut wide regional disparities in cancer survival across Europe. It supports EU-wide screening, digital cancer infrastructure and long-term survivor care, while urging governments to fix gaps in funding and access to modern treatment."
---

The European Cancer Organisation (ECO), a federation uniting oncology specialists, patient groups and research bodies across Europe, is stepping up calls to close stubborn gaps in cancer outcomes, warning that where a person lives still shapes their chance of survival after diagnosis.

Cancer remains the second leading cause of death across Europe. Roughly 40 per cent of cancer cases are preventable, yet screening uptake, access to modern therapies and survivorship support vary sharply between western, eastern and southern European regions. Survival gaps for common tumours such as colorectal and breast cancer can exceed 25 per cent between high-performing and lower-resource EU member states, according to pooled data tracked by the European Cancer Inequalities Registry.

Founded to build consensus between clinicians, patients and EU policymakers, ECO does not run hospitals directly. Instead, it coordinates cross-border working groups, drafts evidence-based policy recommendations and amplifies patient voices in the design of Europe's Beating Cancer Plan, the European Commission's flagship public health framework launched in 2021. Its work spans the full cancer pathway: prevention, early detection, timely diagnosis, multidisciplinary treatment, and long-term support for survivors and carers.

One core priority is fixing overlooked parts of patient care. ECO roundtables have highlighted underfunded services including cancer-related nutrition support, management of infection risks during chemotherapy and tailored rehabilitation after treatment. Many national health systems focus heavily on drug delivery and surgery, while neglecting follow-up care that shapes quality of life after remission. The federation also advocates for the rollout of the Cancer Survivor Smartcard, a digital tool designed to help survivors access insurance, employment support and continuous medical follow-up across borders.

Digital oncology infrastructure is another central theme. ECO backs federated cancer imaging databases, shared genomic platforms and the EU Network of Comprehensive Cancer Centres, which aims to spread high-standard multidisciplinary care to regions with fewer specialised oncology units. These projects allow clinicians to share anonymised data without moving sensitive patient records, accelerating clinical trials and helping smaller centres adopt best practice from leading institutions.

Funding pressure remains a major barrier. ECO members warn that national budget squeezes, workforce shortages and uneven rollout of EU4Health grants slow progress. Many eastern European health systems still struggle with long waiting times for scans and limited access to newer immunotherapies. Patient advocates within ECO also flag discrimination against survivors in the labour market and financial services, a hidden burden that persists even after successful treatment.

ECO’s leadership stresses that equity must sit at the heart of European cancer policy. 'Cancer does not respect borders, income levels or regional budgets,' said a senior policy adviser at the organization. 'A European cancer plan only succeeds if a patient in a rural town or a lower-income member state can access the same standard of care as someone in a major western capital.'

The federation will present its updated recommendations ahead of the European Week Against Cancer later this year, pushing national governments to translate EU commitments into local screening programmes, workforce training and survivorship services. For millions living with cancer across Europe, the fight is no longer only about new drugs — it is about guaranteeing fair access to care for everyone.
