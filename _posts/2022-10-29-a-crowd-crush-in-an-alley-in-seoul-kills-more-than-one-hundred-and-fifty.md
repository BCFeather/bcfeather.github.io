---
title: "A crowd crush in a narrow alley in Seoul kills more than one hundred and fifty"
date: 2022-10-29 23:59:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Halloween crowds in Itaewon are squeezed into a sloped alley beside a hotel, and most of the dead are in their twenties."
---

The crush began at about a quarter past ten in the evening in an alley beside the Hamilton Hotel in Itaewon, and by midnight the toll had reached one hundred and fifty-one dead and more than eighty injured. Most of the dead were in their twenties, and many were women. Emergency services said the casualties had been squeezed in a space only a few metres wide. The neighbourhood had been filling up since the afternoon for the first unrestricted Halloween since the pandemic.

Itaewon is a district of narrow streets that climb a hill, and the alley where the crowd stopped is about four metres wide and slopes downwards from the main road. Witnesses said the pressure built from both ends at once, with people pushing down from the street above while the crowd at the bottom could not move. Those who fell were unable to get up, and the people behind them kept walking. Video from the scene showed police and passers-by trying to pull bodies from the pile while a siren played over the noise.

Police had received at least eleven calls from the same area before the crush, the earliest at half past six, and the callers had asked for crowd control because the street was already too full to move in. The district had expected about ten thousand people and the crowd was several times that. Officials said later that no plan had been made for a crowd of that size, and that nobody had been assigned to manage the flow into the alley. The national police agency said it would investigate how the calls were handled.

Response took shape on the street itself. Firefighters and paramedics performed chest compressions on people laid out on the pavement, and passers-by joined them because there were more casualties than hands. About one thousand seven hundred emergency workers were sent from across the city, and the injured were taken to more than a dozen hospitals. Identification became the next problem, since many of the dead had been carrying no documents.

President Yoon Suk-yeol declared a period of national mourning and said the priority was to identify the dead and support the families. He convened an emergency meeting at midnight and asked for a thorough investigation of the cause. The mayor of Seoul cancelled the city's Halloween events and the district closed the street. Messages of condolence came from several governments, and the foreign ministry said it was in contact with the embassies of the countries whose citizens were among the dead.

Questions that will be asked are about responsibility, and the answer will be found in no single place. Crowd management in Korea is divided between the police, the district office and whoever is running the events, and nobody had been given the job for a night with no official event. The country's record on public safety has been under scrutiny since the ferry disaster of 2014, and the inquiry into that sinking ended with recommendations that were partly implemented. The families outside the hospitals were asking on the night why nobody had closed the alley.
