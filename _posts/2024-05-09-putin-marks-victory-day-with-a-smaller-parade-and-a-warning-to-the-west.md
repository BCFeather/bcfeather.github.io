---
title: "Putin marks Victory Day with a smaller parade and a warning to the West"
date: 2024-05-09 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Russia held its Victory Day parade in Red Square on 9 May with fewer troops and no heavy armour, and President Putin used his speech to accuse Western governments of rewriting history and to say the country's strategic forces were on alert."
---

Russia held its annual Victory Day parade in Red Square on the ninth of May, the holiday that marks the defeat of Germany in 1945, and it was smaller than in previous years. About nine thousand soldiers took part, no tanks appeared, and the flyover of aircraft was cancelled, which officials attributed to the weather. Security was heavier than usual, with mobile barriers and jamming equipment visible along the route, in response to drone attacks that had reached Moscow in recent weeks.

Speech by President Vladimir Putin was short and directed outward. He accused Western governments of trying to rewrite the history of the war and of supporting what he called a neo-Nazi regime in Kyiv, and he said that Russia's strategic forces were in a state of combat readiness. He did not announce a new mobilisation or a new offensive, and he thanked soldiers fighting in Ukraine as heirs of the generation that took Berlin.

Absence of heavy armour was read in two ways. Some analysts said it reflected the drain of equipment to the front, where losses have been large and production has not kept pace. Others noted that the parade has been shrinking since 2021 and that the Kremlin has been reducing the political risk of a display that can be compared with the war's actual costs. Either way, the visual message was of a country at war rather than one celebrating a victory.

Foreign leaders were largely absent, and the guest list was thinner than in past years. Leaders of Belarus, Kazakhstan and several other post-Soviet states attended, and the Chinese president did not, which was noted in Moscow. Ambassadors from countries that Russia calls unfriendly were invited and attended, which is a convention that the foreign ministry has maintained through the war and which produces a strange scene every year.

Victory Day has become the central ritual of Russian official memory, and its meaning has shifted since 2022. The holiday was for decades about the defeat of fascism and the sacrifice of the Soviet people, and it has been repurposed as a justification for the current war, which officials describe as a continuation of the same struggle. That reframing has been accepted by much of the population and resisted by part of it, and the smaller parade is a reminder that rituals can be maintained only at the cost of the material they were built on.

Parade also served a purpose inside the armed forces, where morale has been affected by heavy losses and by the length of the war, and the leadership used the occasion to award medals and to promote officers who have commanded units at the front.

Consequence for the year ahead is mostly symbolic. Holiday marked the start of a summer in which Russian forces are expected to press along several parts of the front, and it came after a winter of missile attacks on Ukraine's energy system. Nothing in the speech changed the military situation. What it did was to restate the terms on which the leadership has framed the war, which are that the conflict is defensive, that the West is responsible for it, and that Russia will not accept a settlement that leaves its gains in place.
