---
title: "Beijing opens the Winter Games inside a closed loop"
date: 2022-02-04 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "Ninety-one delegations and 2,846 athletes compete in 109 events at a Games that is sealed off from the city that hosts it."
---

Beijing opened the twenty-fourth Winter Olympics on the evening of 4 February with a ceremony in the Bird's Nest, the stadium built for the summer Games of 2008, and the city became the first to have held both editions. Ninety-one delegations sent athletes, 2,846 in all, and they will compete for 109 sets of medals in fifteen disciplines at venues in the city, in Yanqing and in Zhangjiakou. The opening was shorter than the ceremonies of past Games and it was watched by a small crowd.

Everything about the fortnight is organized around a boundary. Athletes, officials, staff and journalists live and travel inside a loop that connects the venues, the villages and the hotels, and nobody inside it may leave except to fly home. Tests are frequent, masks are required, and a positive result sends a competitor to an isolation hotel rather than to the start line. The organizers say the arrangement kept the Games running; the athletes say it kept them from seeing the country they had come to.

Sport produced its own stories in the first week. Norway brought a team built for cross-country skiing and biathlon and began to collect medals at a rate that the record book had not seen. The host country won its first golds in short-track speed skating, then more in freestyle skiing, and the crowd inside the venues, small as it was, responded to every one of them. A fifteen-year-old figure skater from the Russian team landed quads that no woman had landed at a Games, and the case around her sample became a legal argument that ran through the second week.

Money and politics followed the flame as they always do. Sponsors paid for the broadcast, the broadcasters paid for the rights, and the rights were sold in a country where the Games were the only subject on television for three weeks. Diplomats from several governments stayed away because of the pandemic and because of disagreements with the host, and the ceremony went ahead with fewer of them in the stands than the organizers had planned.

For the athletes the arithmetic is simpler than the argument around them. Four years of training come down to a run of two minutes on a course that was built for the event, and most of the 2,846 will leave without a medal. The ones who win will be photographed with the mascot and asked about their childhood, and the ones who finish twentieth will go back to jobs and to the next season.

The Games close on 20 February, and the next edition belongs to Milan and Cortina in 2026. What remains of this one, beyond the medal table, is a set of venues in three places that were built for two weeks of competition and a model of how to hold a large event during a pandemic that other organizers will study and probably avoid. The sliding track at Yanqing will be used by national teams in winter, the ski runs at Zhangjiakou by a public that had little access to snow before, and the bill for all of it falls on the city.
