---
title: "The Fed signals three cuts and the market runs"
date: 2023-12-13 22:20:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The Federal Reserve held rates at 5.25 to 5.5 per cent on 13 December 2023 and projected three cuts in 2024, a pivot that sent the ten-year yield below four per cent and the Dow Jones index to a record."
---

The Federal Reserve left its policy rate at 5.25 to 5.5 per cent on 13 December, the third consecutive meeting without a change, and published projections in which the median member expected three quarter-point cuts in 2024. The statement dropped the phrase about additional policy firming that had been in every version since March, and the chair told the press conference that the committee had begun to discuss when it would be appropriate to reduce the rate. Markets had expected a softer tone and received a stronger one.

The projections are the part that moved prices. Nineteen members put the end of 2024 at 4.6 per cent, which implies three cuts, and eleven of them placed it at 4.5 or lower. Two members expected no cut at all. The same document revised growth up for 2023 to 2.6 per cent and inflation down to 2.8 per cent, which is a combination that describes an economy doing better than the committee thought while prices cool faster, and it is the reason the pivot could be justified in public.

Prices of everything repriced within an hour. The two-year Treasury yield fell twenty-five basis points, the ten-year went below four per cent for the first time since August, and the Dow Jones industrial average closed above 37,000 for the first time. Gold rose, the dollar fell against every major currency, and emerging market bonds had their best day since the banking crisis in March. The financial conditions index that the Fed watches loosened by an amount that does some of the work of a rate cut before any cut has happened.

Economic data behind the decision were mixed in a way that supports the soft landing story. Inflation on the consumer price index had fallen to 3.1 per cent in November, with core at four per cent, and the monthly pace of core prices over three months was running below the rate consistent with the target. Unemployment was 3.7 per cent, payroll growth averaged about 200,000 a month through the autumn, and retail sales were still rising. Wage growth had slowed without collapsing, which is the outcome the committee has been trying to engineer since 2022.

Risks are asymmetric, and the chair named both of them in the questioning. If inflation stalls above three per cent, three cuts would be a mistake that has to be reversed; if the labour market breaks, waiting would be the mistake. The committee's own record of forecasting is poor in both directions, and its members know that the market is now pricing more easing than the dot plot contains, which means that any data surprise will be amplified by positioning.

Elsewhere the pivot was read as a signal about the global cycle. The European Central Bank and the Bank of England were expected to follow in the first half of 2024, the Bank of Japan was the last major central bank still holding negative rates, and the IMF had already cut its forecast for world growth. The American consumer, whose spending has held up the world economy for two years, was being told that borrowing would become cheaper exactly when the savings accumulated during the pandemic were running out.
