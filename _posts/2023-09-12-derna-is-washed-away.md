---
title: "Derna is washed away"
date: 2023-09-12 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Two dams above the Libyan city of Derna collapsed in the early hours of 11 September 2023 after Storm Daniel, sending a wall of water through the city and killing more than eleven thousand people."
---

Storm Daniel reached the Libyan coast on 10 September with rain that fell for a night, and in the early hours of 11 September the two dams above Derna gave way. The upper dam, built in the 1970s, and the smaller one below it released a surge that ran down the dry riverbed through the centre of the city and into the sea, carrying buildings, cars and people. The United Nations estimated the death toll at more than eleven thousand, with thousands still missing, and the mayor of Derna said the number could be much higher.

The city had been warned and had no way to act. The dams had not been maintained for years, and a Libyan economist had published a paper in 2022 warning that they were cracked and that Derna was at risk. The World Meteorological Organization said its warnings had reached the authorities, and that the emergency plans that would have moved people out of the valley did not exist. The two governments that claim Libya, one in Tripoli and one in the east, blamed each other.

Aid arrived slowly and unevenly. The eastern administration closed the road from the coast at first, and convoys from Tripoli and from Misrata had to negotiate their way in. Rescue teams from Turkey, Egypt, Tunisia and the United Arab Emirates worked on the seafront, where bodies were still being recovered a week later, and the International Committee of the Red Cross distributed body bags. A ship from the Emirati Red Crescent docked with equipment and volunteers, and the Italian navy sent a hospital ship.

Libyan politics shaped the disaster and the response. Derna sits in the east, under the authority of the Libyan National Army commanded by Khalifa Haftar, and its municipal services have been run by unpaid staff for years, with equipment that had not been replaced since the revolution. The city's residents had been treated as a security problem through a decade of conflict, and its rebuilding had been left to a family firm with a construction contract. The rival government in Tripoli, which is recognized by the United Nations, has been unable to send money that the east will accept.

The climate connection is the one that hydrologists have been making. Daniel formed as a tropical storm in the Mediterranean, which is unusual, and it drew its energy from sea temperatures that were well above normal for September. The storm dumped a year's rain on the region in a day. A country with functioning dams would still have flooded, and the flood would have been a flood rather than a disaster, because the water would have been released in a controlled way.

A city of a hundred thousand people has lost a quarter of its buildings and part of its population, and the two governments that have spent a decade fighting each other are now arguing about the rescue. The dams are gone, the water is gone, and the argument about who is responsible will outlast the reconstruction.
