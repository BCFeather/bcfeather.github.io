title: "The last weekend of August"

The last weekend of August is the one where the season changes in the space of two days. On Friday the evening is still warm enough to sit outside, and on Sunday the same hour requires a jacket. The change is not gradual and it does not happen in September. It happens in this weekend, and everyone notices, and everyone says the same thing about the summer having gone quickly.

The city changes with it, and the change is easiest to see in the small shops. The outdoor tables at the restaurants are still out and nobody is sitting at them. The pool has closed. The supermarket has moved the sun cream to a shelf near the door and the school supplies to the aisle in the middle. The bus at seven in the morning is full of children again, and the driver has to wait at stops he has not waited at since June.

The light is the part that is hardest to describe and easiest to see. In June the sun at seven in the evening is still high and the shadows are short. By the end of August the sun at seven is close to the rooftops, the shadows are long, and the light has a quality that photographers call the golden hour, which arrives an hour earlier than it did a month ago. A person who walks home at the same time every day sees the change without being able to name it.

The month itself has a shape that is mostly administrative, and the shape is the same every year, which is a comfort and a warning at once. Vacations end, the term begins, the quarterly reports are due, the shop that was closed for two weeks reopens. The summer in a Chinese city is a period in which a large number of institutions operate on a reduced schedule, and the last weekend is when that schedule is restored, which is why the roads are suddenly busy at eight in the morning.

There is a version of the weekend that is melancholy, and it is the version the poetry is about. The season that everyone complained about is over, and the complaint was itself a form of pleasure, and the person who spent July saying it was too hot now says it is cold. The complaint is the same and the object has changed, which is how most nostalgia works.

What the weekend actually leaves is a set of practical things to do. The fan goes into storage. The winter clothes come out of the box and smell of the box. The air conditioning filter is cleaned for the last time. The receipts for the summer's repairs are filed. None of it is a season, and all of it is how a season is recorded.

By Monday the city is running on its ordinary schedule, and the summer will be a thing that happened, and the next one is ten months away, and the evenings will be dark by half past five.
