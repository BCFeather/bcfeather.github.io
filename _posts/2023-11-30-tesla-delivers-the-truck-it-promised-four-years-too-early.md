---
title: "Tesla delivers the truck it promised four years too early"
date: 2023-11-30 23:10:00 +0800
categories: [technology]
description: "Tesla handed over the first Cybertrucks on 30 November 2023, four years after unveiling a stainless-steel wedge, with prices from sixty thousand dollars, a range of about 340 miles in the top version and a body that has been the hardest part of the project."
---

Tesla delivered the first production Cybertrucks to customers at its plant in Austin on 30 November, four years after the company unveiled a prototype with a stainless-steel body that looked like nothing else on the road. The launch event was held in a dark room with a light show, and the specifications arrived with the prices that had been promised in 2019 and then changed. The single-motor model is listed at 60,990 dollars, the all-wheel-drive version at 79,990, and the three-motor version at 99,990.

Its body is built around an exoskeleton of cold-rolled stainless steel, a material that resists dents, scratches and rust and that is difficult to form into complex curves. Tesla's answer was to make the truck from flat panels folded into a wedge, which gave it a shape that survives a collision test differently from a conventional body-on-frame design. The company says the structure is stronger than a standard truck's, and the first independent crash tests have begun to appear.

Engineering claims are large. The top version produces about 845 horsepower, reaches sixty miles per hour in 2.6 seconds, and carries a range estimated at 320 miles with an optional battery extender. The truck has a 48-volt electrical architecture, which reduces the copper in the wiring harness, and steer-by-wire, which removes the mechanical link between the wheel and the front axle. Both are unusual in a production vehicle and both have been the source of delays.

Delays have been the story. The prototype was shown in 2019 with a price of 39,900 dollars and a delivery date of 2021. The truck that arrived costs fifty per cent more in its cheapest form and took two years longer, and the company has blamed the steel, the battery and the difficulty of building a new assembly line. Tesla has not published production figures, and the first deliveries were to employees and to customers who had reserved early.

The market it enters is changing. American buyers have shifted towards smaller and cheaper trucks, and the electric pickup segment has grown crowded, with the Ford F-150 Lightning, the Rivian R1T and the Chevrolet Silverado EV all on sale. The Cybertruck's price puts it above most of them, and its size and shape make it a statement rather than a work vehicle. Reservations were reported at more than a million, and the conversion rate will decide whether that list becomes a business.

For Tesla, the truck is a test of whether the company can still do difficult manufacturing. Its share price has been driven by promises about autonomy and about volume, and the Cybertruck adds a product that is hard to build and hard to sell outside a narrow audience. The company's next vehicle is supposed to be a cheaper car on a new platform, and the lessons from this one, about steel, about 48 volts and about the cost of ambition, are already being applied there.
