Every blog has to start somewhere, and this one starts with a small admission: I have wanted a public notebook for years and kept finding reasons to postpone it. The reasons were respectable. A private file is easier to abandon, and an abandoned file leaves no evidence. Writing in public removes that comfort, which is the point. What follows is the first entry, and it is mostly an explanation of why there will be more.

The name is BlogFeather, and the tagline under it says that your view also matters. That phrase is doing more work than a slogan usually does. It is a way of saying that what I put here is one reading of a subject, offered by someone who has read a certain amount and not everything, and that a reader who disagrees deserves an answer rather than a correction. Disagreement is the reason to write in a place where other people can reply, and it is the only reliable test of whether a sentence survives contact with someone who knows the subject better than the person who wrote it.

Three kinds of writing will appear here. Current affairs, because the world keeps producing events that deserve a considered paragraph rather than a reaction. Science and technology, because the interesting questions in those fields are usually about how a claim was established rather than about the claim itself. Social questions, because the way people live together is the subject that touches all the others. Essays like this one will be rarer, and the reason is simple: a notebook of opinions is worth less than a notebook of observations.

There is a case for keeping quiet, and it deserves an answer. Plenty of writing on the internet is produced to fill a space, and the space does not need filling. My defence is narrower than a general argument for more speech. A person who writes something down, dates it and leaves it visible has made a record that can be checked later, including by the person who wrote it. That record is useful even when it turns out to be wrong, and especially then, because a dated archive of judgments kept where anyone can read it imposes a discipline that no private notebook does.

What I expect from the exercise is modest. Some posts will be short, some will take a week of reading, and a few will be corrections of earlier posts. The frequency is unknown and I will not promise a schedule that I cannot keep. What I can promise is that nothing here will be written to win an argument that the facts do not support, and that a correction will be visible rather than quietly folded into an old file.

So this is the beginning, and the invitation is straightforward. Read what interests you, skip what does not, and write back if something is wrong or worth arguing about. The page is public, the archive is dated, and the standard I am setting for it is one that later entries will have to meet. Thanks for stopping by.
