---
title: "Mikhail Gorbachev dies at ninety-one and leaves a country that has not settled his record"
date: 2022-08-30 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The last Soviet leader, who ended the cold war and lost his own state, dies in a Moscow hospital after a long illness."
---

Mikhail Gorbachev died on Tuesday evening in a Moscow hospital at the age of ninety-one, and the news was announced by the hospital that had treated him. He was the last leader of the Soviet Union and the general secretary of its communist party from 1985 until the union dissolved in December 1991. He introduced the policies that he called glasnost and perestroika, and he signed the treaties that ended the division of Europe. He was awarded the Nobel peace prize in 1990 and he remained unpopular in his own country for the rest of his life.

The record is the one that the obituaries will argue about. He ended the arms race with the Americans, he withdrew the army from Afghanistan and he allowed the countries of eastern Europe to choose their own governments. He also presided over the collapse of the economy, the emptying of the shops and the dissolution of the state that he had promised to reform. Supporters say the end of the cold war without a war was his achievement, and critics say the chaos that followed was his responsibility.

The funeral arrangements were announced within a day and they were modest. The ceremony will be held in the hall of the house of unions, which is where the funerals of the Soviet leaders were held, and the president is expected to attend. The government has not announced a state funeral on the scale of the one that was held for the last leader who died, and the newspapers have been cautious. The opposition figures who were allowed to speak said he was the person who made their careers possible.

At the same time, the war has changed how his policies are read. The withdrawal from Afghanistan is being compared with the withdrawal of 2021, and the enlargement of the alliance that he allowed is now the subject of the argument between Moscow and the west. The people who write about the period say that he believed the countries of eastern Europe would remain friendly and that he did not believe they would join a military bloc. The documents that were declassified in the 1990s show that the American and the German governments promised him things that were never written down.

He spent his later years on foundations and on interviews, and he defended the course that he had set. He supported the annexation of Crimea in 2014 and he criticized the war that began in February, which is the combination that his obituaries will find hard to explain. He was ill for a long time and he appeared in public rarely after the pandemic began. His daughter died in 1999 and his wife in the same year, and he has one surviving daughter.

What his death does is close the period that the world calls the end of the cold war. The men who ended it are almost all gone, and the settlement that they made has been undone piece by piece since. He was the only leader of the Soviet Union who left office alive, and the country that he left has spent thirty years arguing about whether he was a liberator or a gravedigger. The argument will not be settled by his funeral, and the war has made it harder to have.
