---
title: "Putin signs the annexation of four regions and does not say where the border runs"
date: 2022-09-30 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Treaties are signed in the Kremlin with the four occupation administrations, and Ukraine applies to NATO the same day."
---

President Vladimir Putin signed treaties on Friday with the administrations installed in four Ukrainian regions, and the ceremony in the Kremlin's St George's Hall was staged as the formal admission of new territory. The regions are about fifteen per cent of Ukraine's land area, and the documents describe them as Russian subjects of the federation. None of the four is fully under Russian control, and the front line runs through two of them. The signing followed referendums that the Ukrainian government and the Western allies described as a sham held at gunpoint.

His speech was an argument about history. Putin said the regions had been Russian for centuries, that the Soviet Union had given them away by mistake and that the west had spent thirty years cheating Russia. He repeated the claim that the war was a defence of Russian speakers, and he did not mention the retreat from the north-east in the previous month. He also said that any attack on the new territory would be treated as an attack on Russia, which is the sentence the capitals were listening for.

The borders are the gap in the document. The treaties do not define where the regions end, and the Kremlin's spokesman said the question would be settled with the administrations in the territories. That leaves the status of the parts that Russia does not hold, including the city of Zaporizhzhia and the whole of the Kherson region's northern half. Ukrainian officials said the omission exposed the claim as a fiction, and they noted that the Russian army was withdrawing from one of the regions it had just annexed.

Ukraine's answer came within hours. The president said the country had submitted an application for accelerated membership of NATO, and he said the alliance's guarantee of collective defence was the only thing that could stop the war. The application was signed by the speaker of the parliament and delivered in a statement, and the secretary general said the alliance would consider it. Members have disagreed for years about whether Ukraine should be admitted, and the application does not change the arithmetic.

Reaction from the rest of the world was immediate and mostly uniform. The European Union said it would never recognize the annexation, and it prepared a new package of sanctions that includes a price cap on oil. The American administration announced sanctions on the officials involved and on the companies that support the occupation. Türkiye said it would not recognize the vote, and Serbia, which has refused to join the sanctions, said the same. China's foreign ministry called for a ceasefire without endorsing the annexation.

What the ceremony does is close the space for a settlement. A government that has written the new territories into its constitution cannot trade them away without a domestic crisis, and a government that has lost them cannot accept their loss without one. The war has now produced two claims of sovereignty over the same ground, and neither of them can be negotiated. The next phase will be fought over the land that the documents describe, and the documents will be the reason.
