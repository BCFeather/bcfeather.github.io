The autumn equinox falls on the twenty-second this year, when the day and the night are the same length. In the calendar of solar terms it is the sixteenth of the twenty-four. It divides the autumn in half.

Since 2018 the day has also been the festival of the harvest. The designation was created to recognise the farmers who produce the grain. The ceremonies are held in the counties rather than in the cities.

The harvest itself is earlier in the south and later in the north. A rice field in the river valley is cut in September. A field of maize in the northeast is cut in October. The same date is a different task in the two places.

The machinery has changed the labour. A combine that cuts and threshes in a day does the work that a village did in a week. The hands that used to be hired for the harvest are now hired for the drying.

The drying is the step that decides the price. A grain that is stored wet spoils in a silo. A farmer who has no dryer sells at the gate for whatever the buyer offers on the day.

The price is set by a minimum that the state guarantees. A buyer who pays less than the floor is not allowed to buy. The floor has been raised in a year when the input costs rose.

The subsidies arrive after the harvest. A payment per hectare is calculated from the area that was planted and it is made in the winter, which is when the farmer who borrowed for the seed needs it.

The festival is a fair in the county town. A stage, a competition for the largest pumpkin and a stall that sells the local product. The ones that draw a crowd are the machines rather than the speeches.

The weather is the variable that the day cannot fix. A week of rain before the equinox puts the harvest back and lowers the grade. A dry September is worth more to a farmer than any subsidy.

The date is also a reminder of the season that follows. A field that is cut in September is ploughed and sown with wheat in October. The cycle that began in the spring closes before the frost.

The cities mark the day with a meal. A restaurant that serves a dish of the new rice in September sells it as a seasonal item. The ones who buy it are the ones who came from a county.

The cooperatives are the buyers in the counties that have them. A collective that owns a dryer and a storage shed can hold the grain until the price rises. A farmer who is a member sells to the collective rather than at the gate.

The fruit is the other harvest and it is perishable. An orchard that picks in September has three weeks to sell, and a cold store that is rented by the day is the difference between a profit and a loss.

The festival is a day off for the offices in the county town. A bureau that closes for the ceremony keeps a duty phone on, and the staff who are on duty are the ones who did not go to the fair.

The equinox passes without a ceremony in most places. The day is the same length everywhere, and the festival is the part that the calendar added to it.
