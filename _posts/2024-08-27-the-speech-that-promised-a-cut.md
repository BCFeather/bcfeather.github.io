The chairman of the Federal Reserve spoke at a conference in Wyoming on the twenty-third of August. The sentence that the markets took from the speech was that the time had come for policy to adjust.

The conference is held in a lodge in the mountains and it is the smallest room in which a large policy is signalled. A speech of twenty minutes moves a trillion dollars of assets before the question session begins.

The inflation argument was the first half of the speech. The rate of price increases had fallen to within sight of the target. The chairman described the risk of inflation as balanced against the risk of unemployment.

The labour market was the second half. A month of weak payroll numbers was revised down and a month of strong numbers was revised up. The chairman said that the cooling was unmistakable.

The markets moved in both directions within an hour. The yield on the two-year note fell, the equity market rose. The dollar gave up a point against the euro before recovering it by the close.

The traders had already decided. A futures market that prices the next meeting had put a probability of more than eighty per cent on a cut before the speech. The speech changed the size of the bet rather than the direction.

The cut came in the middle of September. A half point rather than a quarter was the decision. The reasoning was that a labour market that is cooling faster than it looks needs a larger move.

The committee is not unanimous. A governor who voted against the larger cut argued that a half point signals panic. The argument will be settled by the data rather than by the vote.

The year ahead has two paths. A soft landing is a cut that is followed by a pause. A recession is a cut that is followed by another cut, and the difference between them is a set of numbers that arrive one month at a time.

The revisions to the payroll numbers were the data point that the speech leaned on. A month that was reported as strong was revised down by more than a hundred thousand jobs, which is the kind of revision that changes a judgment about the labour market.

The balance sheet is the other instrument. The central bank has been letting its holdings run down, which removes money from the system at the same time as the rate is being cut, and the two instruments are not pulling in the same direction.

The other central banks are moving differently. The European bank has cut once and the Japanese bank has raised, which means that the currency market is being driven by three policies rather than by one.

The speech will be quoted for a year. A phrase that describes a judgment rather than a rule is the one that the market remembers, and the phrase about the time having come is already in the textbooks.
