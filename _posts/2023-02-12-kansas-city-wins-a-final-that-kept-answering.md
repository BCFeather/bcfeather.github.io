title: "Kansas City wins a final that kept answering"

The Chiefs beat the Eagles 38-35 in Glendale, Arizona, on a field goal with eight seconds left, in a game that had produced sixty-five points and no punts in the second half. Patrick Mahomes, playing on an ankle that had been injured three weeks earlier, threw for three touchdowns and ran for another, and was named the most valuable player for the second time. The winning kick came after a penalty on the Eagles' defence had given Kansas City a first down inside the ten-yard line.

Two plays that the officials had to explain turned the game. A catch by the Eagles' receiver in the fourth quarter was ruled a touchdown after a review that lasted several minutes, and a fumble that was returned for a touchdown by Kansas City was overturned because the receiver had not completed the catch. Both decisions were defended by the league the following week and both were argued about for longer than that.

Brothers Kelce became the first siblings to play against each other in a Super Bowl, and the broadcast spent the fortnight on the story. Travis Kelce caught a touchdown for Kansas City, Jason Kelce snapped for Philadelphia, and their mother was filmed in a suite wearing a jersey split between the two colours. The league's marketing had not had a family story this convenient since the Manning brothers.

Rihanna's halftime show was the other event of the night, and it was received as a return to form after several years of shows built around nostalgia. She performed on a platform suspended above the field, announced a pregnancy during the performance, and did not release a new album afterwards, which was the part the audience had been hoping for. She had released no album since 2016, and the performance was read as a commercial announcement as much as an artistic one. The league had booked her before the tour that never happened, and the booking turned out to be the most watched twelve minutes of the year.

The business of the week was measured in advertising and in betting. A thirty-second spot cost about seven million dollars, the highest price the game had ever commanded, and the legal betting market took more money than in any previous year. The league had spent a decade fighting the expansion of gambling and had then become one of its most valuable partners, which is the kind of contradiction that the sport had learned to live with.

What the game left was a season that had been decided by a handful of plays and a quarterback who had been doubted in the autumn. Kansas City had traded away its fastest receiver, its defence had been ranked in the middle of the league, and it had won its second title in four seasons anyway. The result was read as proof that the system mattered more than the parts, which was the argument its coach had been making all year. Its coach had spent the season arguing that the scheme could absorb the loss of a receiver, and the final was the evidence he had been waiting for.
