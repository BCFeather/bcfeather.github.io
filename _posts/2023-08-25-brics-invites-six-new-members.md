---
title: "BRICS invites six new members"
date: 2023-08-25 22:35:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The BRICS group agreed at its summit in Johannesburg on 24 August 2023 to admit Saudi Arabia, Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, Argentina and the United Arab Emirates from January 2024, in the club's first expansion since 2010."
---

A summit in Johannesburg ended on 24 August with an invitation list rather than a membership. Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa said they would admit Saudi Arabia, Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, Argentina and the United Arab Emirates from 1 January 2024, doubling the group's size and adding two of the world's largest oil exporters. The five invited in Asia and Africa account for a third of the world's population between them. More than twenty countries had applied in the months before the meeting, and the six were chosen by consensus after a day of argument about how large the club should become. The list mixes oil producers, a country under Western sanctions, and two democracies whose governments change with elections.

Argument was mostly between China and India. China has pushed for a bigger organisation that would speak for the developing world and has the money to finance it; India has worried that an expanded group would become a vehicle for Chinese positions and has been building its own coalitions, including with the United States. Brazil and South Africa wanted members who would not dilute the group's ability to agree on anything. The list that emerged includes countries with incompatible interests, which is a compromise in itself.

What the group is for remains unsettled. Its leaders spoke about a multipolar world, about lending without conditions, about local currencies and about reform of the institutions created in 1945. The New Development Bank has been lending for eight years with a balance sheet of about ten billion dollars, which is small next to the World Bank's, and no member has proposed a mechanism that would let the club trade in its own currencies at scale. The bank's own lending has slowed as its members argue about which projects deserve the money. The summit's declarations were long on principles, short on instruments and vague about deadlines.

Russia used the meeting to show that isolation has its limits. Vladimir Putin did not attend in person, because a warrant from the International Criminal Court would have obliged South Africa to arrest him, and he spoke by video instead. Sergei Lavrov attended and was received by the hosts, and the presence of Iran, which is under American sanctions, was read in Moscow as a diplomatic gain.

Politics of the expansion are already visible. Argentina's government said it would consider the invitation while its opposition candidate promised to refuse it, and the United States said it would keep working with partners who choose to join. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have not said whether they accept, and both care about what the invitation implies about their relationship with Washington.

The group has made itself larger, and size brings its own limits. A club of eleven countries with different systems, different currencies and different enemies will find agreement harder, and the communique was rewritten three times to keep everyone inside it. The test of the expansion will be whether anything the members wanted in August actually happens in January.
