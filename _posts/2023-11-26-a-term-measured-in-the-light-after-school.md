description: "In the last week of November 2023 the first term at Chizhou No. 1 Middle School ended, a term that began in a heat wave and finished with the lights on the hill coming on before the last class was out, and with a set of marks that meant something different from the ones in June."
---

The first term ended in the last week of November, and the last thing anybody remembers about it is the light. In September the walk down the hill at half past five was still bright, the playground orange, the buses visible from the top of the steps. By the middle of November the same walk happened in the dark, with the lamps along the road on and the shops at the bottom of the hill lit up, and the classroom windows above the gate glowing like a row of small screens.

September concealed the shape the term would take. For three years the work had been aimed at an examination in June, and every week of it could be counted down. The work now is aimed at an examination three years away, which means that a badly written exercise book in September has consequences that nobody can feel in November, and that a good one has rewards that arrive too late to be enjoyed. Teachers said this in the first week, and the class heard it as a speech rather than a fact.

The first set of marks arrived in October and did the work that marks do. A few people discovered that the methods that had carried them through the entrance examination fell short, and spent November learning to write down the steps that they had previously done in their heads. Others discovered that the subjects they had dreaded were survivable, and one or two discovered a subject they had not expected to like. The ranking at the end of the term was published on a sheet of paper, and everybody looked at it and then said they had not.

What the term actually taught was a rhythm. Up at six, on the bus at seven, four classes before the break, two after, evening study until half past nine, home before eleven. The rhythm differs from student to student: some students study in the morning and sleep in the afternoon, some do the opposite, and the ones who live close enough to walk home have a version of the day that the ones on the bus do not. By November the class had divided itself into groups that shared a table in the library and a set of notes, and those groups were the real achievement of the term.

Weather helped the mood. Chizhou in late November is grey and damp, with the river low and the hills behind the school holding a mist that does not lift until ten. The classrooms are warm enough to make the windows steam, and the walk between buildings is cold enough that everybody runs. There is a particular kind of afternoon in that season when the sun comes out for an hour after the fourth class and the whole building seems to stop and look at it.

Three years remain, and the school has already begun to talk about them. The teachers mention the final examination in the way that people mention a distant city: often, without detail, as a fact of geography. In the last week of term the class cleaned its room, carried the books home in bags that were too heavy, and stood at the gate saying see you in February. The hill was dark by then, and the lights were on, and nobody thought about June at all.
