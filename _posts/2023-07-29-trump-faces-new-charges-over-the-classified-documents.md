---
title: "Trump faces new charges over the classified documents"
date: 2023-07-29 23:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "A superseding indictment filed on 27 July added charges against Donald Trump for retaining classified material and obstructing the investigation, and named a third defendant, the property manager at Mar-a-Lago."
---

The indictment filed on 27 July replaced the one returned in June and added charges to it. Donald Trump faces forty counts in the case concerning the boxes of classified documents recovered from his club and residence in Florida, three more than before. The new charges accuse him of retaining national defence information and of obstructing the investigation, and the document names a third defendant, Carlos De Oliveira, who worked as the property manager at Mar-a-Lago.

New allegations centre on a photograph and a conversation. Prosecutors say Trump asked an employee to delete security camera footage after the government demanded it, that De Oliveira told a maintenance worker that the boss wanted the server wiped, and that the worker said he did not know how to do it. The indictment also describes Trump showing a classified map of a military operation to a visitor without clearance, and includes a recording of him discussing a document he described as secret. Prosecutors also quote a message in which the property manager told the worker to keep quiet about the request and to leave the cameras alone.

The case has been assigned to Judge Aileen Cannon, who was appointed by Trump and who ruled in his favour in a preliminary proceeding last year before an appeals court reversed her. Her handling of the trial calendar has drawn scrutiny, and the classified material rules that govern how lawyers and jurors may see the documents make the case unusual and slow. Her courtroom will need a secure facility for the evidence, and none has been agreed with the security services yet.

Trump's response was familiar. He called the charges political, described the prosecution as an attempt to derail his campaign, and repeated his claim that he was entitled to keep whatever documents he took. His campaign has raised money on each indictment, and it says the sums exceed those that followed the earlier ones. His lawyers have argued that the Presidential Records Act gives him discretion over his own papers, a theory that prosecutors say misreads the law and that several courts have rejected in related proceedings.

Political and legal calendars now overlap. Trump leads the Republican primary polls by a wide margin, has agreed to debates, and faces the prospect of a criminal trial in Florida in May of next year, in the middle of the general election campaign. The other case brought against him in New York, over payments to a former adult film actress, is set for March. The party's rules, drafted before any of this, would bind delegates to a candidate under indictment if he wins the primaries.

A former president is now a criminal defendant in a case that involves the country's secrets and his own employees as witnesses, and the witnesses include the man who carried his boxes. The trial judge's docket, the classification rules and the election timetable will determine whether the country learns what happened before it votes, and none of those three is in the hands of the prosecutors.
