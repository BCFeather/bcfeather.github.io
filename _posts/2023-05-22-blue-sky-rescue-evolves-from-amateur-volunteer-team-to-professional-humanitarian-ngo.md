---
title: "Blue Sky Rescue evolves from amateur volunteer team to professional humanitarian NGO"
date: 2023-05-22 14:14:00 +0800
categories: [society]
description: "Blue Sky Rescue, China's major volunteer rescue NGO, gained international recognition for its Türkiye earthquake mission. It provides free disaster search and rescue while working to standardize training and tackle funding shortages."
---

Blue Sky Rescue, China's largest civilian volunteer rescue organization, has drawn global attention after its deployment to earthquake zones in Türkiye and Syria earlier this year, marking a new chapter for the homegrown humanitarian NGO. What began as a loose group of outdoor enthusiasts has grown into a disciplined network capable of both domestic disaster relief and cross-border emergency missions.

The movement traces its roots back to the years after the Wenchuan earthquake, when untrained volunteers rushed to disaster sites only to find they lacked proper tools and coordination. That painful lesson shaped its later development: Blue Sky Rescue now emphasises standardised training, equipment certification and command protocols. Its members receive long-term training in search-and-rescue, first aid, flood response and urban disaster recovery before joining missions.

In February 2023, more than 300 Blue Sky Rescue volunteers travelled to southern Türkiye. Equipped with life detectors, lighting drones and structural rescue tools, they searched collapsed buildings and recovered survivors and remains. International observers noted that the team's technical capability matched many official foreign rescue units. The operation also brought home a clear reality: civilian volunteers face limits on funding, insurance and cross-border logistics. Most volunteers cover their own travel and gear costs.

At home, the group responds to floods, mountain missing-person cases, wildfires and maritime emergencies. It works alongside official emergency services, filling gaps in remote valleys, isolated river sections and rural areas where state teams take longer to arrive. The organization maintains a neutral, non-profit stance and does not charge communities for rescue services.

Analysts point out that Blue Sky Rescue reflects the maturation of China's volunteer humanitarian sector. Yet structural hurdles remain. Regional teams vary widely in skill and funding; some local branches struggle to retain experienced members. The organization is pushing for better national training frameworks, volunteer accident insurance and clearer coordination mechanisms with official emergency departments.

For volunteers, the motivation stays unchanged. They do not join for payment or fame. Many speak of the simple goal of reaching people trapped when disasters strike. As extreme weather events become more frequent, this civilian rescue network will continue to serve as a vital supplement to formal disaster response systems across China and beyond.
