---
title: "Shanghai shuts half the city and then the other half"
date: 2022-03-28 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Four days for Pudong and four for Puxi, announced as a pair of short pauses and extended into a siege of twenty-five million people that lasted into June."
---

Shanghai announced on 28 March that it would lock down the city in two stages to conduct mass testing, with the districts east of the river closed first and the districts west of it closed from 1 April. The original plan gave each half four days, which the government described as a short and decisive pause, and residents were told to prepare food for the period. The eastern half included Pudong, where the financial centre and the container port are, and the western half included the old city and the airport.

The pause did not end on the fourth day. Cases continued to rise through the first week of April, the restrictions were extended, and the city of 25 million people spent the spring indoors. Compounds were fenced at their gates, deliveries were stopped at the entrance, and residents organized group purchases through messaging applications to buy vegetables and rice. The city government said it had sent 40,000 medical workers from the army and from other provinces to help with testing and with the distribution of food.

Numbers that the city reported were modest by the standards of the wave and large by the standards of its own policy. A few thousand new cases a day in late March grew to more than 20,000 a day by the middle of April, most of them asymptomatic, and the total for the outbreak reached several hundred thousand. Almost none of the cases were severe, because the population had been vaccinated, and the policy was aimed at the infection rather than at the illness, which is the argument that its defenders made and that its critics turned against.

Costs fell on people who needed something other than food. Patients with kidney disease could not reach their dialysis, cancer patients could not reach their chemotherapy, and a nurse who had tested positive was separated from her child. A man with an asthma attack died outside a hospital that would not admit him, and the video of his wife pleading at the entrance circulated for a week before the authorities apologized. The city set up a hotline and a system of permits for emergency care, and the systems arrived after most of the incidents.

The economy felt it at once. The port of Shanghai is the largest container port in the world, and the volume of containers it handled fell by about a fifth in April, with ships waiting offshore and trucks unable to move between the port and the factories. Tesla's plant stopped for three weeks, the components that the electronics industry imports from the city and its suburbs were delayed, and the government's growth target for the year became arithmetically impossible before the summer.

Restrictions were lifted in stages from 1 June, and the city returned to something like normal by the end of the month. What the spring established was the limit of the instrument: a city can be closed for two weeks and reopened, and a city of 25 million cannot be fed by volunteers for two months without a cost that shows up in the hospitals and in the port statistics. The policy that had worked in Shenzhen in March failed in Shanghai in April, and the difference between the two cities became the argument that shaped the rest of the year.
