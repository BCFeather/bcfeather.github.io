---
title: "Meta cuts ten thousand more jobs"
date: 2023-03-14 22:40:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "Meta announced on 14 March 2023 that it would cut another 10,000 jobs and cancel 5,000 open positions, bringing its reductions since November to about 21,000, and described the year as one of efficiency."
---

The company told its employees on 14 March that it would cut another ten thousand jobs, and that it would cancel five thousand roles that had been advertised but not filled. The cuts followed eleven thousand in November, and the two rounds together removed about a quarter of the workforce. Mark Zuckerberg described the period as a year of efficiency, and he said the company would flatten its management layers and ask its remaining staff to do more. The letter was signed by the chief executive and posted on the internal site at six in the morning, which was the hour that the previous round had used.

Behind the cuts lay a financial logic that was visible in the cost line. The company had spent 2021 and 2022 hiring for a metaverse project that had not produced revenue, and its operating expenses had grown faster than its sales for six consecutive quarters. The advertising market had weakened in the second half of 2022, and the company's revenue had fallen for three quarters in a row, which it had not done since it became public. Advertising revenue had fallen by four per cent in the previous quarter while the cost of the computing behind the products had risen.

Behind the second announcement lay a strategic shift. The company said it would restructure its technology groups, reduce its office space, and treat artificial intelligence as the priority for new investment. The metaverse division that had been the centrepiece of the previous strategy was folded into a smaller unit, and the head of it left, and the message was that the company's bet had changed without the company saying so directly.

Word of the cuts arrived in a letter to staff and in a filing, and the letter was written in the flat register that the industry had adopted for these occasions. Employees were told that they would be notified within two days, that the company would pay sixteen weeks of severance plus two weeks per year of service, and that recruitment would be frozen. The details were reported as generosity by the standards of the sector, and as arithmetic by the people receiving them.

The effects on the company's shares were immediate and positive. The stock rose on the day, as it had on the day of the November cuts, because the market had decided that cost reduction was the only thing it could reward. The company's market value had fallen by more than half in the previous year, and the recovery that began in the spring was built on the assumption that the expenses had been brought under control.

What the announcement illustrated was how a large technology company adjusts to a slower market. It cuts the projects that have no revenue, keeps the ones that might, and describes the whole thing as a philosophy. The metaverse had been a bet on a future that had not arrived, and artificial intelligence was a bet on one that was arriving, and the same company made both within three years. Investors rewarded the cuts because they had spent a year asking for them, and the asking had become the strategy.
