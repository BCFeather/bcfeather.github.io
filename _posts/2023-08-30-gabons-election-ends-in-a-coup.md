---
title: "Gabon's election ends in a coup"
date: 2023-08-30 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Soldiers in Gabon announced on 30 August 2023 that they had taken power and detained President Ali Bongo Ondimba, hours after the electoral commission declared him the winner of a third term with 64 per cent of the vote."
---

Officers appeared on the country's television station at dawn on 30 August and announced that the election had been annulled, the institutions dissolved and the borders closed. Within hours they had detained President Ali Bongo Ondimba, whose family had governed Gabon for fifty-six years, and named General Brice Oligui Nguema, the commander of the republican guard and a cousin of the president, as the head of a transitional committee. The electoral commission had declared Bongo the winner on 29 August with 64.27 per cent of the vote, in an election that observers had described as orderly and whose turnout the opposition disputed.

Crowds applauded in the streets of Libreville. Crowds gathered outside the presidency and along the boulevard by the port, and soldiers were filmed being hugged by civilians. The country of two and a half million people produces about a tenth of Africa's oil, and it is the fifth-largest producer of manganese, and its wealth has been concentrated in a small elite for the whole of the Bongo era. Unemployment among the young is high, the state's services outside the capital are thin, and a third of the population lives below the poverty line in a country that earns billions from oil.

A family record explains the mood. Omar Bongo ruled from 1967 until his death in 2009, and his son succeeded him in an election that observers said was flawed, then won again in 2016 by a margin of a few thousand votes after a recount. Ali Bongo suffered a stroke in 2018 that kept him out of the country for months, and his government's response to the illness became a symbol of how little the system told its citizens. The constitution was amended, the parliament was weakened and the opposition was absorbed or harassed.

The region has seen this before, and often. Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso, Chad, Sudan and Niger have all seen soldiers take power since 2020, and each coup has been followed by an argument about whether the new rulers would hold elections. The Economic Community of West African States has suspended some of those governments and negotiated with others, and its credibility is thinner after Niger. Gabon belongs to a different regional bloc, which has said it will consider the situation.

France has a complicated stake. Gabon was a French colony, it hosts a small French military presence, and French companies have operated its mines and its ports. Paris condemned the coup and called for the result to be respected, while acknowledging that the election had been contested. The officers said they had acted to restore the constitution and would hold elections, without saying when.

A dynasty that lasted fifty-six years ended in a morning, and the officers who ended it are the men who guarded it. Africa's coups have become a routine that the regional bodies cannot stop, and the question in Libreville is whether this one produces an election or another general in a tailored uniform, and how long the answer takes.
