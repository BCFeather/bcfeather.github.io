---
title: "Apple puts a satellite link in the iPhone and a bigger watch on the wrist"
date: 2022-09-07 23:40:00 +0800
categories: [technology]
description: "The new phones keep the Lightning port, add an always-on display and promise emergency messages from places with no signal."
---

Apple held its annual September event on Wednesday and announced four new phones, a watch for divers and a pair of earbuds with a new chip. The iPhone 14 keeps the design of its predecessor, and the Pro models replace the notch with a pill-shaped cutout that the software fills with animations. The pro camera becomes a forty-eight megapixel sensor that can crop to a twelve megapixel image without losing detail. The prices rose by about fifty pounds in the American market and by more in Europe, where the dollar has strengthened against the euro.

The feature that the presentation spent most time on is the satellite link. The phones can send a short message to an emergency centre when there is no cellular coverage, by pointing the device at a satellite that moves across the sky. The service is free for two years and will start in the United States and Canada in November. The engineering is the interesting part: the antenna is small, the link is slow, and the phone has to compress a questionnaire into a few hundred bytes. Apple bought the capacity from a satellite operator rather than building one.

Watch line gained a rugged model with a titanium case, a larger screen and a battery that lasts thirty-six hours in normal use. It is aimed at people who run in mountains and dive, and it costs about eight hundred pounds. The standard watch added a temperature sensor for womens' health tracking and a crash detector that calls the emergency services. The earbuds got a new chip and a better case, and the price stayed the same, which is unusual for the company this year.

What did not change is the port. The European Union had agreed earlier in the year that all small devices would have to charge through a universal connector by 2024, and the new phones still use the proprietary cable. Apple's argument is that the rule harms innovation and creates waste, and its engineers have said the change would arrive with the next generation. The company has been moving the rest of its products towards the universal connector for years, and the phone is the last one left.

The software release came two days later, and the betas had been public for months. The lock screen becomes configurable, the messages app can edit a message after sending it, and the maps app adds a three-dimensional view of cities. The most discussed addition is a feature that lets a user lift a subject out of a photograph and paste it into another app, which works well enough to be unsettling. The update runs on phones going back to 2017, and the oldest supported model loses some of the new effects.

Every September event is the last one before the holiday quarter, and the company's revenue depends on it. Analysts expected the prices to rise and the volumes to fall, and the first estimates after the announcement were that the mix would shift towards the pro models. The satellite service is the feature that competitors do not have, and it is a feature that most buyers will never use. The company's pitch is that the phone should work when everything else has failed, which is a promise about rare events rather than about the product on the shelf.
