---
title: "A train to Kyiv and a visit that had been denied"
date: 2023-02-20 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "President Biden visited Kyiv on 20 February 2023, his first trip to the Ukrainian capital as president, and announced a further half billion dollars in military assistance."
---

President Biden arrived in Kyiv by train from Poland on the morning of 20 February, in a visit that had been announced as a possibility for a year and had been ruled out each time for security reasons. He met President Zelensky at the Mariinsky palace, walked through the courtyard while air raid sirens sounded over the city, and announced a further 500 million dollars in military assistance. It was the first visit by a sitting American president to Kyiv since 2008. The journey took ten hours and was announced to the public only after the president had left the country, which is how such visits had been arranged the year before.

Timing followed the anniversary rather than the calendar of the war. The visit came four days before the first anniversary of the invasion, and it was intended to answer the question that European capitals had been asking since the autumn, which was whether American support would hold through a second year. Biden's answer was delivered in person and in front of cameras, and it was repeated in Warsaw the following day.

The package was smaller than the ones that had preceded it, and that was the point. The announcement covered ammunition, anti-armour systems and medical supplies rather than the artillery and air defence that Ukraine was asking for, because those were being delivered under separate authorities and at a slower pace. The public number was a signal, and the signals had become the currency of the alliance. What the package did not include was the long-range artillery that Kyiv had been requesting since December, and the omission was noted in public by officials in Kyiv.

Security for the visit was arranged in secret and in a way that the American and Ukrainian services described afterwards as improvised. Two aircraft flew decoy routes, the train was escorted, and the Russian government was informed of the visit shortly before it happened, in a message that was described as a deconfliction notice rather than as a courtesy. Russia did not attack the city that day, and the absence of an attack was read as an answer. It was the first time the two presidents had met in the capital since the war began.

Europe's conversation changed for about a fortnight after the visit. Governments that had been hesitating over tanks and over long-range artillery announced new deliveries in the days that followed, and the German chancellor, who had been criticized for the pace of his decisions, used the visit to defend them. The photograph of two presidents in a courtyard became the image that the year's diplomacy was remembered by.

What the visit could not change was the arithmetic of the war. Ukraine's ammunition consumption was several times its production, and the factories that could fill the gap were being built in Europe and in the United States at a pace measured in years. A president standing in Kyiv could promise that support would continue, and the promise was real, but the shells that the promise referred to were still being manufactured.
