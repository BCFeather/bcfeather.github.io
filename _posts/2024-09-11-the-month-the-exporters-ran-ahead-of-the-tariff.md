---

Customs administration published the August trade figures in the second week of September. Exports rose by nearly nine per cent on the year, which is faster than the ports had been expecting in July. The growth is concentrated in a few categories. Machinery and electronic components carried the increase. Shipments of consumer goods that a household buys at Christmas were already loaded in the summer.

Pull-forward is the explanation analysts give. A tariff that takes effect in the autumn makes an order that is placed in the summer cheaper. A buyer who expects the duty to apply in October asks for delivery in September. The shipping rates fell in the same month. A container that cost four thousand dollars on a route to Europe in July cost three thousand in September. Fall is the sign of a capacity that arrived faster than the demand.

Numbers are read against the American market. A share of the exports that would have gone to the United States went to Southeast Asia instead. A factory that ships components to a plant in Vietnam is counted as an export to Vietnam. The import figures were weak. A country that is building fewer flats imports less iron ore and less copper. Domestic economists read the import line first.

Trade surplus widened. A surplus that is counted in hundreds of billions of dollars a year is the figure that a trading partner cites in a dispute. Widening is the part that the negotiations will address. The exchange rate is the other variable. A currency that has weakened against the dollar makes an export cheaper in the currency of the buyer. Effect on the volume is visible within a quarter.

Ports worked a longer week. A terminal that handles ten thousand containers a day ran an extra shift in August. Trucking companies that serve it hired drivers for the peak. The small exporters are the ones that the figures do not show. A factory with fifty workers that makes a component for a larger one is counted in the same line. Owner who received an order in July for delivery in September is the reason that the line moved.

Inventory is the other explanation. A factory that expects an order in October can build in September and hold the goods, and a warehouse that is full in the last week of the month is a warehouse that will be empty in the first week of the next one. The competition is the reason that the price is thin. A factory that makes a component for a hundred buyers quotes a margin of five per cent, and a buyer who asks for a discount in September receives one because the line is already running. The fourth quarter is the test. An order that was pulled forward is an order that will not arrive later, and the analysts who expect a dip in October are reading the same numbers as the ones who expect a continued rise. By the middle of October the September figures had been published, the pull-forward was visible in them, and the question was whether the fourth quarter would show what had been taken from it.
