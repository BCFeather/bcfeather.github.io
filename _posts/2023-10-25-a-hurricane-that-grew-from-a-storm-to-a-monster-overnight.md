---
title: "A hurricane that grew from a storm to a monster overnight"
date: 2023-10-25 22:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Hurricane Otis reached Category 5 with 165 mph winds in about twelve hours before landfall at Acapulco on 25 October 2023, the strongest eastern Pacific landfall on record, and left dozens dead and billions of dollars in damage."
---

Hurricane Otis made landfall near Acapulco at about 1.25 in the morning on 25 October with sustained winds of 165 miles per hour, a Category 5 storm, and it had been a tropical storm with 70-mile winds less than twenty-four hours earlier. The intensification was among the fastest ever recorded, and the forecast issued the day before had called for a hurricane of a much lower category. Residents and tourists went to bed with a warning and woke to a city without power, communications or roads.

Acapulco is a resort of nearly a million people built around a bay, with high-rise hotels along the waterfront and informal settlements on the hills behind it. The storm damaged or destroyed thousands of buildings, stripped the facades from hotels, knocked out the airport terminal and closed the coastal highway. Water and power failed across the city, and the airport closed with part of the terminal roof on the runway. The governor of Guerrero said the state would need federal support for reconstruction, and the federal government declared an emergency in the municipalities along the coast.

The death toll rose slowly because the phones were down. Officials initially reported twenty-seven dead, then forty-six, then more than fifty, and the final count from the hurricane center is fifty-two, with damage estimated at twelve to sixteen billion dollars. The figures took weeks to establish because the affected area was larger than the communications network that served it, and because the informal settlements that suffered most were the ones with the least documentation. Foreign tourists were among the dead, and the state opened shelters in schools and churches.

Meteorology has drawn the most scientific attention. Rapid intensification of that magnitude depends on very warm surface water, and the eastern Pacific in 2023 was running well above average after an El Nino developed. Forecast models can capture the conditions that allow a storm to explode, and they did poorly on the timing, which left a gap of hours between the warning and the impact. Researchers have argued that the gap is closing more slowly than the risk is rising.

Reconstruction will be long. Acapulco depends on tourism, and the hotels that employ most of the city are the buildings that were damaged worst. Mexico's government has promised funds and has used the army to clear roads, and the state's economy will feel the loss of a season before any of the money arrives. Insurance penetration in the region is low, and most of the losses will fall on households and small businesses.

Otis is a case study in the cost of a warming ocean. A storm that strengthens faster than the warning system can react turns preparation into improvisation, and the cities most exposed are the ones with the least capacity to evacuate. The category on the map said five, and the twelve hours before landfall were the part that mattered. The storm also flattened the coconut and mango farms that supply the region's markets.
