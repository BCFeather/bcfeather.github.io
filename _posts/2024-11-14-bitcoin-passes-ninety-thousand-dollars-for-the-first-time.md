---
title: "Bitcoin passes ninety thousand dollars for the first time"
date: 2024-11-14 20:40:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "Bitcoin crossed ninety thousand dollars on 13 November and touched a record near 93,000, extending a rally that began when the American election result became clear and that has been driven largely by purchases through new exchange-traded funds."
---

Bitcoin rose above ninety thousand dollars for the first time on the thirteenth of November and traded as high as 93,000 dollars before settling, a gain of about thirty per cent since the American election eight days earlier. Price had been around 69,000 dollars on the eve of polling and had spent most of the year between 55,000 and 70,000. Move made the cryptocurrency worth more than 1.8 trillion dollars in total, a figure that places it above the market value of all but a handful of public companies.

Drivers were identified quickly by the market. Inflows into the exchange-traded funds that hold bitcoin directly, which were approved in January, accelerated after the election, and the funds bought tens of thousands of coins in a week. Candidates and incoming officials have made favourable statements about digital assets, and the prospect of a friendlier regulatory posture pulled in buyers who had stayed out because of enforcement actions. Short positions were squeezed as the price rose, which added fuel to the move.

Structure of the market has changed since the last cycle. Funds and custody arrangements now let pension funds and advisers hold bitcoin through ordinary brokerage accounts, and the largest of the funds has become one of the fastest-growing products of its kind. That has made the price more sensitive to flows from institutional buyers and less dependent on retail trading on offshore exchanges, which is a difference that has shown up in the volatility: daily swings have been smaller than in 2021 even as the price has risen further.

Sceptics have not disappeared. Economists point out that bitcoin produces no cash flow and that its value rests on the willingness of the next buyer to pay more, which makes it a poor store of value for anyone who may need the money at short notice. Regulators in Europe and Asia remain cautious, and several large asset managers still exclude it from their model portfolios. Argument that the funds have legitimised the asset is answered by the observation that the funds also made it easier to sell.

Volatility has been a feature of the asset since it was created in 2009, and three previous rallies have ended with declines of seventy per cent or more. This one has run for two years from a low of about sixteen thousand dollars in late 2022, and the gains have coincided with a period in which the dollar was strong and equity markets were also rising. Whether the two will continue to move together is the question that matters for anyone holding both, and it has not been tested in a downturn since the funds were launched.

Attention now turns to the policy that will follow the transition. Incoming administration has promised to replace the leadership of the securities regulator, to review enforcement cases against crypto firms and to consider a national stockpile of the asset. Congress has been debating market structure legislation for two years and may pass it in the next session. None of that is certain, and the price has already moved in anticipation, which is a risk in itself: expectations of friendly regulation are now embedded in a valuation that has no earnings to support it.
