---
title: "The pact that the general assembly adopted"
date: 2024-09-23 22:00:00 +0800
categories: [world]
description: "The summit before the general debate produced a document of fifty-six pages. It commits the members to changes that the members themselves will have to make, which is the limit of what a pact can do."
---

United Nations held a summit on the future on the twenty-second and twenty-third of September, ahead of the general debate. Outcome was a document of fifty-six pages that the members adopted on the first morning. It covers development finance, the governance of artificial intelligence, the reform of the security council and the commitments on climate. The adoption was not unanimous. A group of seven countries, including Russia and Iran, declined to sign. A paragraph that refers to the security council reform was the reason that was given.

Pact is a statement of intent rather than a treaty. A member that signs it commits to a direction and not to a schedule. A treaty would provide a schedule. The finance chapter is the one that the developing countries pushed hardest. It asks for a reform of the international lending system so that a country borrowing for a railway pays the same rate as a country borrowing for a road in Europe.

Artificial intelligence chapter is new. A set of principles about the testing of models, the sharing of benefits and the protection of data was agreed in outline. Working group that will write the detail was created in the same paragraph. The security council reform is the oldest item and it remains the least settled. A permanent seat for Africa and for Latin America is discussed in every session. Countries that hold a veto have not agreed on a text.

Youth chapter is what delegations under thirty care about. A commitment to include young people in the negotiations was written into the document. Mechanism for doing it was left to the assembly's next session. The climate language repeats an earlier commitment. A transition away from fossil fuels was agreed at another conference last year. Pact refers to it without adding a date.

Follow-up is a set of meetings. A review is scheduled for the seventy-ninth session. Ministers who attend it will report on the paragraphs that their own governments have acted on. The critics are the ones who have watched the previous pacts. A document that is adopted by consensus and implemented by nobody is the pattern of the institution. Authors of this one say that the monitoring chapter is the answer.

Documents adopted with the pact are the part that smaller delegations care about. A declaration on future generations and a set of guidelines on digital cooperation are shorter than the pact and easier to implement. Organisation's cash is the background. A budget that is paid late by the largest contributor leaves a secretariat that is short of money in the month it hosts a summit, and the liquidity is the subject of a letter that the secretary general sends every autumn. The general debate opened the next morning. A hundred and thirty heads of state spoke in a week, and the pact was mentioned in fewer than a dozen of the speeches that followed it. By the end of the week the general debate was over, the document was on the shelves of a hundred missions, and the paragraphs that require money had no budget attached to them.
