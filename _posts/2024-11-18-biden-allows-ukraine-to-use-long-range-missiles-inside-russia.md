---
title: "Biden allows Ukraine to use long-range missiles inside Russia"
date: 2024-11-18 21:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "American officials said on 17 November that President Biden had lifted restrictions on Ukraine's use of long-range missiles against targets inside Russia, a decision taken weeks before he leaves office."
---

American officials said on the seventeenth of November that President Joe Biden had authorised Ukraine to use long-range missiles supplied by the United States against military targets inside Russia, ending a restriction that had been in place since the weapons were first delivered. Decision covers the army tactical missile system, known as ATACMS, which has a range of about three hundred kilometres, and it was reported by American newspapers before any official confirmation. First use followed within days, according to Ukrainian and Russian accounts.

Restriction had been the subject of an argument for more than a year. Ukrainian officials asked repeatedly for permission to strike airfields, ammunition depots and command posts from which Russia launches attacks on their cities, and the American position was that the weapons were for defence of Ukrainian territory. Change came as North Korean troops were reported to have been sent to fight alongside Russian forces in the Kursk region, which American and Ukrainian officials described as a new escalation that justified a new response.

Range limits what the decision can achieve. Missiles can reach targets in the border regions of Kursk, Bryansk and Belgorod, where Russian forces assemble and where supply routes run, and they cannot reach the deep rear where most Russian aviation is based. Military analysts said the effect would be to complicate Russian logistics near the front and to reduce the number of glide bombs launched against Ukrainian cities, which are dropped from aircraft based within reach. They also cautioned that Russia would adapt by moving depots further back.

Kremlin response was immediate. Spokesman said the decision would escalate the conflict and that Russia would respond in ways it considered appropriate, and the foreign ministry issued a statement calling it a direct involvement of the United States in the war. Russian military bloggers described the permission as a change in the character of the conflict, and the government revised its nuclear doctrine two days later, lowering the threshold for the use of nuclear weapons.

European allies had been divided on the question. Britain and France had already allowed their own long-range weapons to be used in limited circumstances, and Germany had refused to send its Taurus cruise missiles on the ground that the decision would draw it into the war. Decision by the outgoing American president therefore aligned him with the British and French position and left the question of what comes next to a successor who has said he wants a rapid settlement.

Timing is the part that attracted most comment. Decision came two months before the inauguration and after an election in which the incoming president promised to end the war quickly. Supporters said the departing administration was giving Ukraine the means to defend itself and to negotiate from a position of strength. Critics said it was tying the hands of a successor and extending a conflict that the voters had signalled they wanted finished. Both readings will be tested by what happens on the ground in the first months of the new administration.
