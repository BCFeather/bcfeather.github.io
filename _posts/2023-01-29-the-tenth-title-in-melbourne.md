Novak Djokovic won the Australian Open on 29 January, beating Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-3, 7-6, 7-6 in a final that was closer than the score suggests and never really in doubt. It was his tenth title in Melbourne, which no man had done before, and his twenty-second Grand Slam, which put him level with Rafael Nadal at the top of the list. He returned to number one in the world the following day. The match lasted two hours and fifty-six minutes, and the two tie-breaks were separated by a single mini-break each.

A year earlier he had been deported from Australia on the eve of the tournament, after a fortnight in which his visa was cancelled, restored and cancelled again by three different courts and one minister. He had watched the 2022 tournament from Belgrade. The return was therefore not an ordinary defence of a title, and the crowd that had booed him in 2022 had changed its mind by the final. The deportation had ended with a court ruling in Melbourne and a ministerial decision that the courts declined to overturn, and it had cost him the chance to defend the title.

Tsitsipas was playing his first Australian Open final and had beaten him in their previous meeting. He held his own serve throughout and lost both tie-breaks by the smallest of margins, which is how a final between two players of that quality is usually decided. The Greek player's backhand was the shot that broke down in the second-set tie-break, and it was the shot that broke down again in the third. He had beaten Tsitsipas in most of their previous meetings, and the Greek player's best surface had always been the clay that Melbourne lacks.

Djokovic's hamstring had been strapped since the second round, and the tape became the tournament's most photographed object. He had described the injury as a tear that he and his team had decided to manage rather than treat, and the decision was vindicated by six matches in which he lost one set. He had also been ill during the tournament, and the combination of the hamstring and the illness was why his movement looked cautious in the fourth round.

He was thirty-five, and the arithmetic of his career had begun to be discussed in the terms that are used for records rather than for matches. Twenty-two titles, ten in Melbourne, and a head-to-head against the other two members of his generation that had moved slightly in his favour over the previous two years. The record he took from Nadal was the one that had seemed least likely to fall when the two of them were twenty-five, because Nadal was winning on clay every spring.

Melbourne is the tournament that has defined him in both directions. The city that refused to let him play in 2022 watched him win for the tenth time in 2023, and both events were part of the same career. The two events belong to the same career, which is the part the tournament's own history will have to absorb.
