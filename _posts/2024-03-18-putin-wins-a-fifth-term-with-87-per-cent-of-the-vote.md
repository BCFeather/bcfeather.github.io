---
title: "Putin wins a fifth term with 87 per cent of the vote"
date: 2024-03-18 23:20:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Vladimir Putin was declared the winner of Russia's presidential election on 18 March with 87.28 per cent and a record turnout of 77 per cent, a result that extends his rule to 2030 and that Western governments said was neither free nor fair."
---

Central election commission declared Vladimir Putin the winner of the presidential election on the eighteenth of March, with 87.28 per cent of the vote and a turnout of 77 per cent, both of which were the highest figures of his twenty-four years in power. Result gave him a fifth term and, with the constitutional changes of 2020, the possibility of two more after that, which would take him to 2036. Communist candidate came second with 4.4 per cent, and the two other candidates took less than five per cent between them.

Scale of the victory was produced partly by the way the vote was administered. Turnout was higher in regions where the government controls public sector employment and in the occupied territories of Ukraine, and it was reported at more than ninety per cent in some districts of the Caucasus. Electronic voting, which was used by a large share of voters in Moscow, produced results that independent analysts said could not be audited, and the commission declined to publish the raw data.

Opposition response came in the form of an action rather than a campaign. Supporters of Alexei Navalny, working with his organization abroad, called on voters to turn out at noon on the final day and to vote for anyone other than Putin, a tactic that produced long queues at some polling stations in Moscow and in other cities. Authorities warned that participants would be prosecuted, and police detained people who gathered inside stations. The action was described by organizers as a demonstration that the opposition still existed, and by officials as a provocation.

International reaction was quick and dismissive. The White House said the election had been neither free nor fair, and the European Union said the vote had taken place in a climate of repression, with independent media blocked and candidates excluded. Several governments said they would not recognize the result in the occupied territories. China, India and several other states congratulated Putin, and the Russian foreign ministry said the criticism demonstrated the weakness of the countries making it.

Domestic meaning of the result is harder to assess than the number suggests. Independent polling before the vote showed that Putin's approval had risen during the war, and that most Russians expected him to win, which means the outcome commanded no serious doubt in any scenario. Support for the war has been stable but not enthusiastic, and the main instruments of the result were the absence of alternatives, the control of television and the incentive for public employees to be seen voting.

Consequence of the eighteenth of March is a leadership that has no plan for its own end. Putin has removed or imprisoned every figure who might have succeeded him, and the system he built is personalized to the point where the question of what comes after him has no institutional answer. War in Ukraine continues, and the mobilization that would be needed for a longer conflict remains politically costly. Mandate delivered by the vote is real in the sense that it reflects a population that has been persuaded or silenced. Its content stops at the policies already being carried out.
