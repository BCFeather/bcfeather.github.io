---
title: "Djokovic beats Kyrgios to win a seventh Wimbledon title"
date: 2022-07-10 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [sport]
description: "A fourth consecutive title at the club takes him to twenty-one grand slams, one behind the record, in a final of long rallies and short tempers."
---

Novak Djokovic won the men's singles at Wimbledon on Sunday, beating Nick Kyrgios in four sets, and the title was his seventh at the club and his fourth in a row. He now has twenty-one grand slam singles titles, one behind Rafael Nadal and one ahead of Roger Federer. The score was four-six, six-three, six-four, seven-six. The match took three hours and one minute and it was played in the heat that had settled on the south of England.

Kyrgios took the first set, and it was the best tennis he played all afternoon. He served with a first-serve percentage above eighty and he returned with a looseness that the crowd enjoyed. Djokovic lost his serve once and spent the changeovers talking to himself. From the second set he began to return deeper and to move the taller man from side to side, and the rallies lengthened as the match went on.

Temper ran underneath the tennis all afternoon. Kyrgios argued with the umpire about a line call and with a member of the crowd, and he asked for a spectator to be removed. He was given a warning for swearing and he complained about the speed of the balls. Djokovic, who has been on the other side of such evenings, stayed quiet and held his serve, and the crowd began to support him as the fourth set reached its tie-break.

Whether he would be allowed to play at all had been the question when the tournament began. He had been deported from Australia in January because he had not been vaccinated, and he had missed the Australian Open and the tournaments in the United States. The rules at Wimbledon did not require the vaccine, and he said in the first week that he had no plans to be vaccinated. He did not play a warm-up event on grass and he still won the title without dropping a set until the final.

One title now separates him from Nadal at the top of the men's record. Nadal, who has won the Australian Open and the French Open this year, withdrew before the semi-final with an injury and remains at twenty-two. Federer has not played since last year and has said that he will return for one more tournament. The three of them have won sixty-one of the last seventy-four titles at the four majors, and the next one will be decided in New York in September.

Championship number seven was won without his best tennis, which may be the point of it. He was broken early and he was outplayed for a set, and he found the returns and the patience to take the next three. The crowd that had come to see the other man ended by applauding the winner, and the winner said afterwards that the title meant more than the ones he had won before. He will be thirty-six in the spring, and the record that he is chasing belongs to a man who is thirty-six now.
