title: "A gunman kills ten people in a Buffalo supermarket and streams it live"
date: 2022-05-14 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "An eighteen-year-old from Conklin drove more than two hundred miles to a Tops supermarket on Jefferson Avenue on 14 May, shot thirteen people, killed ten of them and broadcast the first minutes of the attack on a gaming platform before the police took him into custody."
---

A gunman opened fire in a Tops Friendly Markets supermarket on Jefferson Avenue in Buffalo on the afternoon of 14 May, killing ten people and wounding three more. All ten of the dead were Black, the neighbourhood is predominantly Black, and the eighteen-year-old arrested at the scene had driven more than two hundred miles from Conklin, near the Pennsylvania border, to reach it. He was wearing body armour and a helmet, carried a rifle bought legally, and streamed the attack live on a gaming platform for about two minutes before the platform removed the video.

The first person to confront him was a retired Buffalo police officer working as a security guard at the store, Aaron Salter Jr, who fired at the gunman and hit his armour before being killed. The gunman then moved through the aisles and out into the car park, where he was confronted by police and taken into custody. Thirteen people were shot in all: a grandmother who had come for groceries, a deacon, a man filling a prescription, and a woman who worked at the store, among them.

What made the attack unusual was the paperwork that came with it. Before driving to Buffalo the gunman had posted a long document online that described himself as a white supremacist, repeated the idea that white people are being deliberately replaced, and listed the other mass shootings he had studied. The document named the supermarket's zip code and cited the number of Black residents in it, which made the choice of target a calculation rather than an opportunity.

Prosecutors moved quickly, and the case was charged as a hate crime and as domestic terrorism under New York law, with twenty-seven counts in the state indictment and separate federal counts that carry a possible death sentence. A grand jury in Erie County returned the state indictment within weeks, and the federal case was brought in July, which meant the same evidence would be argued twice in two courts under two different sets of rules. The governor ordered the state's attorney general to investigate the platforms that had hosted the manifesto and the livestream, and the companies involved said they were reviewing how the material had spread so quickly.

The political response followed the pattern of the previous decade, with one party calling for stricter gun laws and the other calling for attention to mental illness and to the policing of online extremism. New York already had some of the tightest gun rules in the country and the rifle had been bought legally in another state, which is the kind of detail that turns a national argument into a debate about borders between jurisdictions.

Ten people were killed in a supermarket on a Saturday afternoon in a city that had spent years arguing about food deserts and about the same street's distance from where the shooting happened. The supermarket reopened in the days that followed, and the store's car park filled with flowers, and the document that explained the choice of address remained online in copies that the platforms could remove one at a time.
