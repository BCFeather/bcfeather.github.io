---
title: "A jury convicts the man who built FTX"
date: 2023-11-02 20:10:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "A Manhattan jury found Sam Bankman-Fried guilty on all seven counts of fraud and conspiracy on 2 November 2023, after a four-week trial that turned on the testimony of his closest colleagues at FTX and Alameda Research."
---

A federal jury in Manhattan took less than five hours to convict Sam Bankman-Fried on all seven counts of fraud, conspiracy and money laundering on 2 November, closing a trial that had run for four weeks and had drawn crowds to a courthouse on Pearl Street. The verdict came a year after FTX collapsed in a run that froze customer withdrawals and exposed a hole of roughly eight billion dollars in the exchange's accounts. He had pleaded not guilty to every charge.

The prosecution built its case from the inside. Caroline Ellison, who ran the trading firm Alameda Research, Gary Wang, a co-founder who wrote the code, and Nishad Singh, the former engineering director, all pleaded guilty and testified against him. Ellison told the court that she had prepared balance sheets at his direction to hide billions in loans that Alameda had taken from FTX customers, and Wang described code that let Alameda withdraw customer money without limits. The defence argued that the three were lying to save themselves and that their former boss had acted in good faith on a business that grew faster than its controls.

Judge Lewis Kaplan presided, and the trial produced a record of messages and spreadsheets that the defence found hard to answer. Bankman-Fried took the stand himself, against the advice of his lawyers, and spent three days under cross-examination. His answers were often long, and the judge several times told him to answer the question. Jurors heard evidence that he had directed the creation of documents to be shared with lenders and investors that misstated the firm's position.

The collapse left a trail across the industry. FTX had been the second-largest crypto exchange and its founder had been the most visible advocate for the asset class, with endorsements, a stadium naming deal and a fortune once valued at more than twenty billion dollars. Customers in dozens of countries lost access to deposits in November 2022, and the bankruptcy estate has since recovered assets that it says may allow a partial repayment. The company's new management has been negotiating with creditors while pursuing claims against insiders.

Sentencing was set for March, and the counts carry decades in prison under the guidelines, though judges in white-collar cases rarely impose the maximum. The conviction is the most prominent outcome of a series of prosecutions that followed the crypto downturn of 2022, including cases against the founders of Terra and Celsius. Regulators have said the failures strengthened the argument for rules that would separate custody from trading and require the disclosure of related-party dealings.

For the exchange's former customers, the verdict settles only part of the question. The money is being recovered through a bankruptcy process that has already run for a year, and payouts will depend on the value of assets at the time of distribution rather than the prices at which people bought. Bankman-Fried's defence has signalled an appeal, and the record of the trial will be read by lawyers on both sides for years, because the case turned on the oldest question in fraud law: what a founder knew when he told investors that the books were sound.
