---
title: "Kenya's deputy president wins the election by half a point and the result is contested"
date: 2022-08-15 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "William Ruto is declared the winner with fifty point five per cent, four commissioners refuse to sign the result and the loser goes to court."
---

William Ruto was declared the winner of Kenya's presidential election on Monday with fifty point five per cent of the vote, and his opponent, Raila Odinga, took forty-eight point eight. The margin was about two hundred thousand votes and the turnout was about sixty-five per cent. The declaration came after six days of counting and it was made by the chairman of the electoral commission in a hall where the opposition's agents were shouting. Odinga said the result was a fraud and promised to challenge it in the Supreme Court.

The dispute inside the commission is the part that worried the observers. Four of the seven commissioners said they could not sign the declaration, and one of them said the numbers had been changed after the count was completed. The chairman said the four had refused to do their job and that the result stood, and he published the tally sheets. The European and African Union observers said the process had been conducted in a way that was mostly transparent and that the disagreement inside the commission had damaged confidence.

The election was close in the way that the polls had predicted and different in the way that the region expected. Odinga had the support of the outgoing president and of the machinery that comes with it, and Ruto had campaigned against that machinery for five years. He presented himself as the candidate of the small traders and the motorcycle taxi riders, and he promised to cut the price of fuel and to fund a loan scheme for the poor. The message worked in the districts where the price of maize had risen fastest.

At the same time, the economy is the reason the vote turned. Kenya's debt has doubled in a decade, the shilling has fallen, and the price of food and fuel has risen since the war in Ukraine. The outgoing president had spent on infrastructure that the voters could see and on borrowing that they will pay for, and both candidates promised to reduce the burden. The budget for the next year assumes that the new government will cut the subsidy on fuel, which is the item that the voters notice most.

Meanwhile, the legal challenge will take weeks and the court has done this before. The Supreme Court annulled the result in 2017 and ordered a new election, and the process then produced a lower turnout and the same winner. The chief justice has said the court will hear the petition within fourteen days, and the constitution requires the winner to be sworn in within fourteen days of the declaration unless the court orders otherwise. The parties are preparing for both outcomes.

What the result shows is a country that has changed its government and kept its institutions. The transfer of power from a president to an opponent who was once his deputy is the third such change since the constitution of 2010, and the commission has published the results at the polling station level. The court will decide whether the numbers hold, and the answer will matter beyond Kenya. The region has watched the count and the region will watch the ruling.
