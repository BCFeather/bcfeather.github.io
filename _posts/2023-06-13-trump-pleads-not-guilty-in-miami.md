title: "Trump pleads not guilty in Miami"
date: 2023-06-13 22:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Donald Trump appeared before a federal magistrate in Miami on 13 June and pleaded not guilty to thirty-seven charges that he retained classified documents and obstructed the investigation, and he was released without conditions."
---

Donald Trump appeared in a federal courtroom in Miami on 13 June and pleaded not guilty to thirty-seven charges arising from his retention of classified documents after leaving office. The hearing, before a magistrate judge, lasted less than half an hour. Trump was fingerprinted, photographed and released without conditions that restricted his travel, and his co-defendant, a former aide, was processed alongside him. The judge set no date for a trial and instructed both sides to begin the procedures that will decide when one happens.

The arrangements for the day reflected the unusual position of a defendant who is running for office. Security was heavy, the courthouse was surrounded by supporters and reporters, and a local official had issued a permit for a demonstration that did not materialise. Trump flew to Miami in his own aircraft and flew to his golf club in New Jersey afterwards, where he delivered a speech that described the prosecution as an abuse of power and the election as the real trial. His campaign said it had raised several million dollars in the days since the indictment.

Legally the case will be slow, and its course depends on the judge who is assigned the trial rather than the one who accepted the plea. The charges include counts under the Espionage Act, which carries a heavy maximum sentence, and counts of conspiracy and obstruction, and the evidence includes recordings and photographs that the indictment says show the documents being moved and discussed. Trump's defence has signalled that it will argue that he was entitled to declassify material and that the law governing presidential records is a civil statute rather than a criminal one, which is the argument that the case will turn on.

Politics shaped the schedule. Trump's lead in the Republican primary is large, and his rivals have spent the week denouncing the prosecution rather than attacking him, which is the clearest sign that the party has decided that its interests and his are the same. The indictment is a threat to his nomination and an asset to it at the same time, because it lets him cast the election as a verdict on the justice system, and because the party's grassroots have believed that story for years.

Whatever the outcome, the case establishes that a former president can be charged, arraigned and released, and that the constitution has no answer to a candidate who is defending himself in court while campaigning. That is untested ground for the country, and the institutions that were designed for a smaller state are being asked to handle it at their own pace. The next hearing will be procedural and the argument will be public, and both will take place while the campaign continues, which is what the founders did not plan for and what everyone has now seen.

A procedural hearing follows in July, and a trial is unlikely to begin before the primary is decided. The case will therefore be argued in courtrooms and in caucuses at once, which is what the law permits and what the country has never tested.
