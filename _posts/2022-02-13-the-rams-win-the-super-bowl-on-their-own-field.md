---
title: "The Rams win the Super Bowl on their own field with a late touchdown"
date: 2022-02-13 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "Los Angeles beats Cincinnati 23-20 at SoFi Stadium, and Cooper Kupp scores with 1:25 left after a drive that the defence had kept alive."
---

Los Angeles won Super Bowl LVI on the evening of 13 February, beating the Cincinnati Bengals 23-20 at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, and the decisive score came with 1 minute 25 seconds left on a one-yard pass from Matthew Stafford to Cooper Kupp. The Rams had trailed by four points at the start of the fourth quarter and had managed little on offence for most of the second half. Kupp, who caught eight passes for 92 yards and two touchdowns, was named the most valuable player.

The game turned on the pressure that the Rams' defensive line put on Joe Burrow. Cincinnati's quarterback was sacked seven times, which equals the record for a Super Bowl, and Aaron Donald and Von Miller were in the backfield on most of the snaps that mattered. Burrow still completed 22 of 33 passes for 263 yards and a touchdown, and he had put his team ahead early in the third quarter with a 75-yard pass to Tee Higgins on the first play of the half.

Los Angeles had built its lead in the first half with two touchdown passes from Stafford, one of them to Odell Beckham Jr., and then lost Beckham to a knee injury before the interval. The offence stalled without him, and Cincinnati took the lead with the long pass to Higgins and a field goal. A 41-yard kick from Matt Gay cut the gap to four, and the last drive covered 79 yards in fifteen plays, helped by three penalties on the Bengals' defence.

Stafford finished with 26 completions on 40 attempts for 283 yards, three touchdowns and two interceptions, and at 34 he had won a championship in his first season after twelve years in Detroit. The team had been assembled for exactly this: the trade for Stafford, the signings of Miller and Beckham, and a coach in Sean McVay who had reached a Super Bowl three years earlier and lost it. The Rams became the second team to win a Super Bowl in their own stadium, after Tampa Bay the year before.

The halftime show put five acts on a stage in the middle of the field, with Dr Dre, Snoop Dogg, Eminem, Mary J Blige and Kendrick Lamar, and it was received as the best of the recent editions. The broadcast drew about 112 million viewers, a little below the year before, and the advertisements cost 6.5 million dollars for thirty seconds. The stadium had opened six years earlier and had cost 5 billion dollars to build, most of it paid by the owner, Stan Kroenke.

For Cincinnati the loss closed a season that had begun with a four-win team and ended with a conference title, and Burrow's protection, which had been poor all year, finally cost the team a game. The city had not seen a championship since 1990, and the run to the final lifted it for a month. The Rams, with an ageing roster and a first-round pick already spent, face the arithmetic of a team that has just won: the players who made the difference will want to be paid.
