---
title: "The Speaker race: fifteen ballots and a gavel without weight"
date: 2023-01-07 01:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Kevin McCarthy was elected Speaker of the House on the fifteenth ballot at 12.38 a.m. on 7 January 2023, by 216 votes to 212, after four days in which the chamber could not swear in its own members."
---

The 118th Congress opened on 3 January and spent four days failing to organize itself. Kevin McCarthy, the Republican leader from California, lost nineteen members of his own conference on the first ballot and took 203 votes, while the Democratic leader, Hakeem Jeffries, took 212 from a party that had no dissent at all. The House could not swear in its members until a Speaker existed, so for four days the new majority governed nothing. The chamber sat with its members-elect in the aisles and its clerk at the desk, and the television cameras stayed on the floor through the night.

The clerk called the roll fifteen times. The fourteenth ballot, on the Friday night, ended one vote short, and the chamber became disorderly in a way that congressional reporters had to reach back a century to describe. Matt Gaetz voted present, McCarthy walked up the aisle to confront him, and a colleague had to be physically restrained from going after Gaetz. Then the House voted on a motion to adjourn, and enough Republicans changed their votes to defeat their own motion so that a fifteenth ballot could be held. Between the ballots the negotiators met in the Speaker's office, and the deal was assembled clause by clause in the way that coalition politics always is.

At 12.38 a.m. the clerk declared the result. McCarthy had 216, Jeffries had 212, and six Republicans had voted present, which lowered the majority he needed. The count was the first time a Speaker had needed more than one ballot since 1923 and the longest contest since 1859, when the House took forty-four ballots over two months. Six Republicans had voted present, which is the device that lowers the number of votes needed and is used by a member who wants to abstain without abstaining.

Visible in the rules package adopted the following week was the price of the gavel. Any single member could move to vacate the chair, which meant that any single member could threaten the Speaker's job at any moment. Committee seats and procedural guarantees went to the faction that had held out, and the House committed itself to votes on bills that its leadership had hoped to avoid.

McCarthy's majority is four seats, which is the narrowest in modern memory and narrower than the faction he had just bought off. A Speaker who can be removed by one member governs with the permission of the least satisfied member of his conference, and the debt limit, the appropriations bills and the investigations all sat ahead of him. The Freedom Caucus had spent the four days extracting commitments, and it had spent the previous two years learning that a threat works only if it is repeated.

A gavel that can be taken away by one member has already been taken away. The fifteen ballots were the price of admission, and the admission was to a chamber that had demonstrated, before it passed a single law, how hard it would be to pass one.
