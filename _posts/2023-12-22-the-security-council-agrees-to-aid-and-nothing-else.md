---
title: "The Security Council agrees to aid and nothing else"
date: 2023-12-22 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The Security Council adopted resolution 2720 on 22 December 2023 by thirteen votes to none, calling for the scaling up of aid into Gaza and the appointment of a coordinator, after a week of negotiations in which the word ceasefire was removed from the text at the request of the United States."
---

The Security Council adopted resolution 2720 on 22 December, thirteen votes to none, with the United States and Russia abstaining, after a week in which the draft was rewritten four times. The text calls for urgent steps to allow immediate, safe and unhindered humanitarian access, asks the secretary general to appoint a senior coordinator for the reconstruction effort, and demands that the parties comply with international law. It does not call for a ceasefire, because the United States removed that word with its veto in reserve.

Negotiations were about verbs. The first draft, written by the United Arab Emirates, called for a suspension of hostilities; the American delegation objected, and the language became a call for creating the conditions for a sustainable cessation of hostilities, which is a promise about a process rather than a demand about behaviour. The reference to a monitoring mechanism was softened at the request of Israel, and the phrase about land crossings was added because the aid that arrives by air and by sea cannot substitute for the trucks that wait at Rafah.

Aid numbers explain the urgency. Before October, about 500 trucks entered the strip each day; in December the daily average was under 200, and the inspection regime at Kerem Shalom and Rafah meant that convoys queued for hours and sometimes for days. Warehouses were empty, the World Food Programme said 90 per cent of the population had skipped meals for a full day at least once, and the health ministry reported that the hospitals still functioning were operating without anaesthetics for surgery.

Implementation was left to the coordinator, who had not been named on the day of the vote and who will have no staff of his own, no armed protection and no authority over the crossings. The resolution requests a report every 90 days, which is a device that gives the council a regular occasion to argue rather than a mechanism that moves trucks. Israel controls the crossings, Egypt controls Rafah, and the parties that would have to cooperate to make the resolution work disagree about whether the coordinator should be allowed to bypass them.

Politics around the vote were about the American position. The United States had vetoed ceasefire resolutions in October and in December, and its abstention on 2720 was designed to show that it was engaged without conceding the demand that Israel stop. Russia abstained because the text did not go far enough, which meant that the two permanent members with the most influence over the parties voted against each other while both voting yes to the resolution.

What the council can do in Gaza has been clear since October. It can name the problem, it can authorize a coordinator, it can publish a report, and it cannot deliver a truck or stop a bomb. The resolution passed because it was written to be passable, and the test of it will be the January report, which will say how many trucks arrived and how far the aid reached, in a territory where the answer to both questions has been shrinking for three months.
