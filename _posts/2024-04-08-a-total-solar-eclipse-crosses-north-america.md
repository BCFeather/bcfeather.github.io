---
title: "A total solar eclipse crosses North America"
date: 2024-04-08 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [science]
description: "The moon covered the sun along a path from Mexico through Texas and the north-east of the United States to Newfoundland on 8 April, and about thirty-two million people lived inside the zone of totality."
---

Moon passed directly between the earth and the sun on the eighth of April, and the resulting total eclipse traced a path about four hundred kilometres wide from the Pacific coast of Mexico through Texas, Arkansas, Missouri, Indiana, Ohio, New York, Vermont and Maine, and on across Newfoundland. Totality lasted up to four minutes and twenty-eight seconds, which is longer than the seven minutes of 2017, because the moon was closer to the earth in its orbit. About thirty-two million people lived inside the path, and millions more travelled to see it.

Physics of the event is a coincidence of scale. The sun is about four hundred times wider than the moon and about four hundred times further away, so the two discs appear to be almost the same size in the sky. When the alignment is exact, the moon covers the photosphere and the corona becomes visible, which is the part that makes a total eclipse different from a partial one. That coincidence is a product of the current distances and will fade over geological time, since the moon is moving away from the earth by about four centimetres a year.

Scientific work attached to the eclipse was organized weeks in advance. Researchers launched sounding rockets from Virginia to study how the sudden loss of sunlight affects the ionosphere, and the space agency flew aircraft along the path to make observations from above the weather. Teams measured the temperature of the corona, tested instruments that will be used on future missions and collected data on how animals respond to a sudden night, which is a subject that has produced anecdotes for centuries and measurements only recently.

Weather was the main uncertainty, and it cooperated in parts of the path. Skies were clear over much of Texas and over the north-east, and they were cloudy in parts of Mexico and in Ohio. Forecasters had warned for weeks that the difference between a partial and a total eclipse was worth travelling for, and the highways in Arkansas and in Maine were reported to be congested for hours before and after.

Spectacle was also a commercial event. Hotels and campsites in the path had been booked for a year, some towns organized festivals, and airlines added flights that were advertised as offering a view from the air. Retailers sold eclipse glasses by the million, and health authorities repeated warnings about looking at the sun without protection, which is the injury that ophthalmologists see after every eclipse.

Next opportunity is the reason the event drew so much attention. A total eclipse will cross the United States again in 2044, and one will pass over Spain and Iceland in 2026. The path of the eighth of April ran through more populated territory than any eclipse in the country's history, which is why the number of people who saw totality in person is likely to be the largest ever recorded for an astronomical event. Several observatories published live images from telescopes along the path, and the data collected will take months to analyse.
