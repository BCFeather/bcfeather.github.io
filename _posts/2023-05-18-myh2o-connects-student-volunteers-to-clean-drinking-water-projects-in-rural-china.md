title: "MyH2O connects student volunteers to clean drinking water projects in rural China"
date: 2023-05-18 21:20:00 +0800
categories: [society]
description: "Chinese grassroots NGO MyH2O organizes university volunteers to test rural drinking water, install purification facilities and train local residents to maintain safe water systems."
---

MyH2O, a grassroots Chinese NGO focused on rural water safety, has built a volunteer network of university students to test drinking water sources and deliver practical water solutions for remote villages across the country. Founded in 2015, the group operates on a simple belief: safe water should not be a luxury only available to urban residents.

Most of its field investigators are college volunteers. They travel to villages, collect water samples and send results back to the organization’s central database. By 2023, the team had covered more than 1,000 villages across 26 provincial-level regions and analysed over 5,500 samples. The data reveals hidden risks including excess fluoride, heavy metals and bacterial contamination, especially in villages that rely on cellars and shallow wells for daily water supply.

The NGO does not stop at testing. Where pollution is confirmed, it works with local communities to install low-cost water purification stations and train villagers to maintain the equipment. In dry areas such as Gansu, these stations have reduced reliance on stored rainwater and groundwater of uncertain quality. Unlike short-term charity deliveries, MyH2O prioritises local ownership. Villagers learn routine checks and simple repairs so facilities can keep running after volunteers leave.

The organization's leadership says rural water issues are often overlooked in national environmental reporting. Many families cannot afford commercial water filters, and scattered settlements make official monitoring harder. Student volunteers fill this gap while gaining hands-on experience in environmental science and community work. Thousands of young participants have turned classroom knowledge into field action.

Funding remains a persistent challenge. The group relies on foundation grants, small public donations and occasional government procurement projects. Staff members admit scaling is slow, as every village needs tailored assessment rather than one standard water device. Still, demand keeps growing. More county governments and schools have reached out to partner on water screening for boarding students.

For MyH2O, progress is measured by small, steady changes. A village school where children no longer suffer repeated stomach trouble, a household that stops buying expensive bottled water, a community committee that starts regular water monitoring. These outcomes show how grassroots NGOs can bridge gaps between technical expertise and marginalised rural groups. As climate change worsens drought and groundwater depletion, MyH2O's work on water security will only grow more valuable.
