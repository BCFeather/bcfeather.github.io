Rioting spread across English towns for a week in early August. First disturbance was in Southport, a seaside town where three children were killed at a dance class on 29 July. Within two days the targets had changed. False was the claim. Suspect was born in Britain to Rwitan parents. Misinformation travelled faster than the correction. Targets changed within two days. A hotel that housed asylum seekers was attacked in Rotherham, a mosque was threatened in Southport, and shops owned by immigrants were looted in Hull and Manchester.

On the first night the police were outnumbered. A force that deploys a few hundred officers on a Saturday cannot hold a street against a thousand people. Reinforcement came from neighbouring counties four hours later. A standing arrangement of specialist officers was announced by the prime minister. Briefing used the phrase a standing army, which is not a phrase that a British politician uses lightly. Courts sat through the night. Magistrates heard cases at speed. First sentences were handed down within a week, which is faster than the normal timetable by months.

Sentences came out longer than the guidelines suggest. A man who threw a bin at a police van received three years. Court of Appeal upheld the approach in a judgment that cited deterrence. Causes are argued about and the argument is not new. A country that has spent fourteen years cutting public services has fewer police on the street and fewer youth workers off it. Two numbers are connected in a way that no single statistic shows.

Spontaneity is the wrong word for what happened. A handful of accounts with large followings announced a time and a place. Crowd that arrived was people who had seen the same post. Police found the pattern within a day and started to watch the accounts. Football season opened in the middle of it. A match in the north was played behind closed doors because the away supporters could not be policed. League moved two fixtures to a Sunday afternoon.

The insurance bill will be counted in the autumn. A shop that was looted claims on a policy that covers riot damage. Premium for the street rises at renewal, which is a cost that the shopkeeper pays twice. Community work is the sentence courts prefer for the young. A teenager who was in the crowd and threw nothing received a curfew and a referral order. Order requires him to meet the shopkeeper whose window was broken.

In some towns the clergy calmed the street. A vicar who stood in front of a burning bin and asked the crowd to go home was listened to in Middlesbrough, and the same appeal failed in Sunderland an hour later. Inquiry will focus on the misinformation. A false name circulated on social media within an hour of the attack, and the platforms removed it slowly. A claim that spreads faster than a correction is a problem that moderation does not solve. By the end of August the courts had heard more than a thousand cases, the streets were quiet, and the argument about what caused the week had moved into the inquiry.
