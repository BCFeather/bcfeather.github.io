Rioting spread across English towns for a week in early August. The first disturbance was in Southport, a seaside town where three children were killed at a dance class on 29 July.

The crowd that gathered the next day was told that the suspect was an asylum seeker. The claim was false. The suspect was born in Britain to Rwitan parents. The misinformation travelled faster than the correction.

The targets changed within two days. A hotel that housed asylum seekers was attacked in Rotherham, a mosque was threatened in Southport, and shops owned by immigrants were looted in Hull and Manchester.

The policing was outnumbered on the first night. A force that deploys a few hundred officers on a Saturday cannot hold a street against a thousand people. The reinforcement came from neighbouring counties four hours later.

The prime minister announced a standing arrangement of specialist officers. The phrase that was used in the briefing was a standing army, which is not a phrase that a British politician uses lightly.

The courts sat through the night. Magistrates heard cases at speed. The first sentences were handed down within a week, which is faster than the normal timetable by months.

The sentences were longer than the guidelines suggest. A man who threw a bin at a police van received three years. The Court of Appeal upheld the approach in a judgment that cited deterrence.

The causes are argued about and the argument is not new. A country that has spent fourteen years cutting public services has fewer police on the street and fewer youth workers off it. The two numbers are connected in a way that no single statistic shows.

The organisation was not spontaneous. A handful of accounts with large followings announced a time and a place. The crowd that arrived was made of people who had seen the same post. The police found the pattern within a day and started to watch the accounts.

The football season opened in the middle of it. A match in the north was played behind closed doors because the away supporters could not be policed. The league moved two fixtures to a Sunday afternoon.

The insurance bill will be counted in the autumn. A shop that was looted claims on a policy that covers riot damage. The premium for the street rises at renewal, which is a cost that the shopkeeper pays twice.

The community work is the sentence that the courts prefer for the young. A teenager who was in the crowd and threw nothing received a curfew and a referral order. The order requires him to meet the shopkeeper whose window was broken.

The clergy were the ones who calmed the street in some towns. A vicar who stood in front of a burning bin and asked the crowd to go home was listened to in Middlesbrough, and the same appeal failed in Sunderland an hour later.

The misinformation is the part that the inquiry will focus on. A false name circulated on social media within an hour of the attack, and the platforms removed it slowly. A claim that spreads faster than a correction is a problem that moderation does not solve.
