---
title: "ABC pays fifteen million dollars to settle Trump's defamation suit"
date: 2024-12-17 20:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "ABC News agreed on 14 December to pay fifteen million dollars to a Trump foundation and a million dollars in legal fees to end a defamation case brought over remarks by the anchor George Stephanopoulos, and published an editor's note."
---

ABC News agreed on the fourteenth of December to settle the defamation suit that Donald Trump had brought over remarks by one of its anchors, and the terms were disclosed in a court filing in Florida. Network will pay fifteen million dollars to the presidential foundation and museum that Trump has established, and one million dollars towards his legal fees. Settlement was accompanied by a note from the network's editor, published on its website, expressing regret for the error, and the case was dismissed.

Dispute concerned ten seconds of television. George Stephanopoulos, interviewing a Republican congresswoman in March, said that Trump had been found liable for rape by a jury in a civil case brought by the writer E. Jean Carroll. Jury had found him liable for sexual abuse and for defamation, and the judge in that case had specified that the finding did not amount to rape under the legal definition used in New York. Suit argued that the statement was false and defamatory, and it named both the anchor and the network.

Settlement was reached at a stage when the case had become uncomfortable for the network. Discovery was under way, and Trump's lawyers had sought to question executives about editorial standards and about the network's coverage of him. Deposition of the anchor had already been scheduled, and legal analysts said the cost of a trial, in money and in publicity, was likely to exceed the sum agreed. Neither side admitted liability, and the payment went to a foundation rather than to Trump personally, which avoided questions about whether a sitting or incoming officeholder can receive such a payment.

Precedent is what media lawyers will study. American law gives public figures a high bar in defamation cases, requiring proof of actual malice, and until now most such suits against large outlets had been dismissed or settled quietly. A payment of this size, from a broadcaster to a politician who had just won an election, is unusual, and it arrives at a moment when the incoming administration has promised to review the legal treatment of the press and to pursue cases against broadcasters over their coverage.

Chilling effect is the concern raised by the network's own journalists and by press freedom groups. Argument is that a settlement which requires an editor's note and a payment creates a template for complaints by well-funded plaintiffs, and that local outlets, which cannot afford to litigate, will become more cautious. Counter-argument is that the statement at issue was demonstrably inaccurate and that correcting it was the right thing to do, whatever the cost, and the network's note said as much.

Money paid will go to a building project rather than to its namesake directly. Trump's foundation has been raising funds for a presidential library and museum, and the settlement directs the sum there, which means the payment is publicly accounted for and cannot be characterized as a personal payment to a politician. Case closed with the dismissal of the complaint, and the note that accompanied it was short, which is the way these things are usually handled when both sides want the argument to end.
