---
title: "Lula wins the Brazilian runoff by one point eight and Bolsonaro says nothing"
date: 2022-10-30 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Forty-nine days after the first round, the count gives Lula fifty point nine per cent, and the incumbent declines to speak."
---

A second round that closed at five in the afternoon produced a result before eleven at night, and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won with fifty point nine per cent of the valid vote against forty-nine point one for Jair Bolsonaro. The margin is about two million votes out of a hundred and twenty-four million cast, the narrowest in the six elections since the return of civilian rule. Turnout was just under eighty per cent. The result makes Lula the first person to return to the presidency after being imprisoned, and the first to hold the office for a third term.

Bolsonaro said nothing. The president's campaign had promised a statement after the count, and by midnight it had not come, and his supporters gathered outside his residence in Brasília without instruction. The electoral court certified the result within an hour of the last district reporting, and the president of the court thanked both campaigns for the conduct of the vote. Several of Bolsonaro's allies in the Senate acknowledged the result while he stayed silent. The silence was read as a decision that had not yet been made.

Lula put his coalition together in a month. Geraldo Alckmin, a former governor who ran against him in 2006, was the running mate, and Simone Tebet, who finished third in the first round, endorsed him in the final week. Ciro Gomes, who has spent his career attacking the party's economic record, gave a qualified endorsement that his followers did not all follow. The party won the largest bloc in the lower house, though short of the majority that would let it legislate alone.

Geography shows the division clearly. Lula won every state in the north-east, and he won Minas Gerais and the three southern states' rival in the south-east, while Bolsonaro took São Paulo, Rio Grande do Sul and most of the centre-west. The north-east voted for the party that built the cash transfer programme, and the south voted for the government that cut its value in real terms during the campaign.

The economy Lula inherits has a budget already committed to the payments that Bolsonaro increased in the election year, an inflation rate that has begun to fall and a central bank that has held rates at the highest level in five years. His advisers have said the first task is to find room in the spending ceiling for the social programmes, which will require Congress to pass a new rule. The markets fell on the news and the currency weakened, and the bankers who had bet on the incumbent were disappointed.

Inauguration is on 1 January, and the transition begins this week, with the president-elect asking for the names of the officials who will hand over the accounts. Bolsonaro has not said whether he will attend the ceremony, and no Brazilian president has ever refused. What remains is the arithmetic of governing: a hostile lower house, a senate that will want something for its votes, and a country that has just voted almost evenly for two men with opposite views of its history.
