---
title: "Fox pays to avoid a trial about what it broadcast"
date: 2023-04-18 23:30:00 +0800
categories: [business]
description: "Fox News settled Dominion's defamation lawsuit for 787.5 million dollars on 18 April 2023, minutes after a jury was sworn in, ending a case that had put the network's internal messages about election claims on the public record."
---

Fox Corporation agreed on 18 April to pay Dominion Voting Systems 787.5 million dollars to settle a defamation case that had been due to go to trial that afternoon in Delaware. The jury had been empanelled and the judge had announced a short break when the parties told him that a deal had been reached. Dominion had sued for 1.6 billion, and the settlement is the largest publicly disclosed in an American defamation case. The figure was read into the record by the judge, who had spent the morning choosing twelve jurors from a panel of more than two hundred.

Central to the case was what the network broadcast after the 2020 election. Dominion argued that Fox had aired false claims that its machines had switched votes, that the network knew the claims were false because its own reporters had debunked them, and that it had kept them on air because the audience wanted them. Fox's defence was that the statements were newsworthy and that a broadcaster is entitled to report what a president's lawyers say.

Discovery produced the material that decided the case in the public mind. Internal messages showed that executives had worried about losing viewers to smaller networks that were promoting the claims, that hosts had described the allegations as nonsense in private, and that the chairman had told a colleague the audience would not accept the truth. The judge ruled that the record contained no genuine dispute about whether the statements were false, which left Fox to argue about malice alone. That ruling, more than any document, is what made a settlement the rational choice for both sides.

By settling, Fox avoided a trial that would have put Rupert Murdoch, the executive chairman, and several of the network's best-known hosts on the witness stand. It included no admission of liability and no requirement that the network apologize on air, which Dominion's lawyers called a compromise they accepted because the money was certain and a verdict would have been anything but. The judge had already sanctioned Fox for withholding evidence, and the sanction survived the settlement.

Consequences spread beyond the case. Another voting machine company, Smartmatic, has a similar suit pending, and it is seeking a larger sum. Shareholders filed a derivative action arguing that the board had failed to oversee the risk, and the network's parent company set aside the payment in its quarterly results. The case also produced a public record of how a newsroom's private judgment can differ from its broadcast output, which is the part that other networks studied.

Money is large and the principle is unresolved. American defamation law makes it very hard for a public figure to win, and the settlement bought Fox out of a trial in which the judge had already narrowed the dispute to a single question. Dominion's chief executive said the settlement vindicated the company and the country's election system, and Fox said the settlement reflected a commitment to move forward. Both statements were written by lawyers, and neither described what the jury would have decided.
