description: "Germany announced on 25 January 2023 that it would send fourteen Leopard 2 tanks to Ukraine and allow partner countries to re-export theirs, and the United States announced deliveries of Abrams tanks the same day."
---

Fourteen Leopard 2A6 tanks from the Bundeswehr's own stocks were what the German cabinet decided to send to Ukraine on the morning of 25 January, together with training for the crews, ammunition, spare parts and logistics. The same decision allowed countries that operate the tank to send theirs, which removed the legal obstacle that had kept Poland, Spain and Norway waiting for a German signature. The package was announced by the government spokesman after a cabinet meeting moved forward by a day, and the defence ministry published the list of equipment in the afternoon.

A year of argument preceded it. Kyiv had asked for modern Western tanks since the spring Kyiv had asked for modern Western tanks since the spring, and the argument inside the coalition had turned on the question that German politicians call the leadership question, which is whether Germany should move first or last among its allies. The defence ministers met at Ramstein on 20 January and left without a decision, and the meeting was reported as a failure in the German press before the weekend was over. The coalition's junior partners had argued in public for the delivery while the chancellor's party argued in private about the risk of being blamed for escalation.

The American announcement came the same day. The United States said it would send Abrams tanks, which are heavier, thirstier and harder to maintain than the Leopard, and which will take months to deliver. The sequencing was the point: the chancellor had said for weeks that Germany would move only together with the United States, and the American decision was the key that unlocked the German one. The American tanks will come from new production rather than from existing stocks, which is why they arrive later and why the announcement was about politics as much as about armour.

The military reality is smaller than the political one. Fourteen tanks are a company, and the group of countries that agreed to send Leopards expected to assemble two battalions, which is fewer than a hundred vehicles. Training takes weeks, the maintenance chains take months, and the tanks will arrive in time for a counter-offensive that has not yet been planned. A battalion of Leopards needs a workshop, a supply of spare tracks and a crew that has trained together for months, and the Ukrainian crews began their training in Germany in February.

Quick and predictable was the Russian reaction. The ambassador in Berlin called the decision extremely dangerous The ambassador in Berlin called the decision extremely dangerous and said it raised the conflict to a new level, and the foreign ministry in Moscow promised consequences that it did not specify. The German government described the decision as a contribution to a defence that Ukraine had a right to conduct.

A tank is a machine for making a decision visible. The fourteen vehicles will change less on the battlefield than the fact of their delivery changed in the capitals that had been waiting for Berlin to say yes. Whether the decision shortens the war or lengthens it is a question the next year will answer.
