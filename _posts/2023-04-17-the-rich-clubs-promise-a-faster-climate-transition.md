title: "The rich clubs promise a faster climate transition"
date: 2023-04-17 23:10:00 +0800
categories: [science]
description: "Energy and environment ministers of the Group of Seven met in Sapporo in April 2023 and promised to accelerate renewables, with targets of 150 gigawatts of offshore wind and a terawatt of solar by 2030, while leaving the question of new gas investment open."
---

Ministers from the Group of Seven met in Sapporo on 15 and 16 April and produced a communique that promised to accelerate the energy transition and to end new investment in coal. The text set collective targets of 150 gigawatts of offshore wind capacity and more than a terawatt of solar by 2030, and it called for the reform of fossil fuel subsidies by 2025. The numbers are larger than anything the group has previously committed to, and none of them binds anyone.

Offshore wind is the most concrete of the targets. The group currently has about 60 gigawatts installed, and reaching 150 within seven years requires a rate of construction that no country in the group has achieved. Supply chains for turbines, cables and installation vessels are already tight, and the industry has warned that a shortage of vessels is the binding constraint rather than a shortage of sites or of money. Grid connections are the third: the queues for new transmission in several member states are longer than the queues for turbines.

Gas was the argument that the text could not settle. Japan and Germany wanted language that recognized gas as a transition fuel and that allowed continued investment in liquefied natural gas terminals, and Canada and the European members wanted the communique to be silent on new supply. The compromise described investment in the sector as appropriate only where it helps to displace more carbon-intensive fuels, a formulation broad enough for both sides to claim it.

Coal was handled with a date. The group said it would end new direct support for unabated coal generation by the end of 2023, which extends a commitment made in 2021 to a sector that several members still subsidize. Environmental groups read the gas paragraph as a licence for new terminals, and the Japanese delegation called it a recognition that supply has to be secured before it can be replaced. Japan, which had been the holdout, agreed to the language after receiving assurances about ammonia co-firing and about its own energy security, and the text asks the group to report progress at the next summit.

Science was present in the room as well. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change had published its synthesis report in March, and it said that emissions must fall 43 per cent from 2019 levels by 2030 to keep the 1.5 degree goal within reach. The group's emissions had fallen by about a tenth since 2019, which means the remaining distance is several times the distance already covered in four years, and the group's share of global emissions has fallen from about a third in 1990 to about a fifth today.

Politics will be decided in Hiroshima in May, where the leaders will meet and where the language of Sapporo will be tested against the war in Ukraine and against the price of energy. A communique written by ministers is a statement of intent, and the group has produced one every year since 2015. What was different this year was the specificity of the numbers, which gives the next presidency something to measure.
