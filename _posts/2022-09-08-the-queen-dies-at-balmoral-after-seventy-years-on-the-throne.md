---
title: "The Queen dies at Balmoral after seventy years on the throne"
date: 2022-09-08 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Elizabeth II was ninety-six and had reigned since 1952, longer than any British monarch, and her son becomes King Charles III."
---

Buckingham Palace announced at half past six in the evening that the Queen had died at Balmoral, and the news had been prepared for years under a plan whose code name was London Bridge. She was ninety-six and had reigned for seventy years and two hundred and fourteen days, longer than any monarch in British history. She had been under medical supervision since the morning, and her family had been summoned to the castle in Scotland through the afternoon. The new king is her son Charles, who becomes Charles III at seventy-three.

Her reign began in February 1952, when she was twenty-five and in Kenya on a royal tour, and the news reached her as a princess who had climbed a tree to watch the animals. The coronation was held in June the following year and was the first to be televised. She worked with fifteen prime ministers, from Churchill to Truss, and she met thirteen American presidents. Her husband died in April 2021 after seventy-three years of marriage, and she described him in a broadcast as the constant strength behind her.

Constitutional role changed with the empire. She was crowned as the head of a Commonwealth that still had colonies, and she died as the head of an association of fifty-six states, most of them republics. She made more than two hundred visits to other countries and rarely gave an opinion in public. The one exception that is remembered is a broadcast in 1997, after the death of Diana, when the palace had misjudged the public mood and the Queen went on television to speak about the family as a family.

The last two years were the hardest. The pandemic took her to Windsor, where she sat alone at her husband's funeral in a pew marked with tape, and she continued to work through the boxes of state papers that arrive every day. She lost weight and mobility, and she appeared in public with a stick. Her last official act was to appoint a prime minister at Balmoral, and she did it two days before she died.

The country's reaction was quiet and immediate. Flags went to half mast within the hour, the broadcasters changed their schedules, and the queue for the palace gates began to form before midnight. The funeral will be held in about ten days, and the body will lie in state in Westminster Hall for the public to file past. The government has published the arrangements, and the courts and the parliament will pause.

What the death changes is the shape of the monarchy as much as its head. Elizabeth was the last monarch to have been crowned in the imperial style, and the institution she leaves is smaller, poorer in political capital and more exposed to argument. Charles inherits a family that has been in the news for the wrong reasons and a public that has less patience for the arrangement than it had in 1952. The tributes on Thursday were for the woman who kept her counsel for seventy years, and they were also a way of saying that the counsel will not be kept in the same way again.
