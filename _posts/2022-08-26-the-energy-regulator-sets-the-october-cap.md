---
title: "The energy regulator sets the October cap at three thousand five hundred pounds"
date: 2022-08-26 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [economy]
description: "The bill for a typical household rises by eighty per cent, and the forecast for January is already above five thousand."
---

The energy regulator announced the level of the cap on Friday, and a household that uses a typical amount of gas and electricity will pay three thousand five hundred and forty-nine pounds a year from October. The previous cap, set in April, was one thousand nine hundred and seventy-one pounds. The increase is about eighty per cent, and the regulator said it reflects the price that the suppliers paid for the fuel in the wholesale market. The cap does not limit the total bill, only the rate per unit, and the households that use more will pay more.

The forecast for the next quarter is worse. The consultants that model the market expect the cap in January to be above five thousand pounds, and the cap in April to be close to six. The regulator's chief executive said in a statement that the price of gas had risen to a level that the market had never seen, and that the cap was designed to protect the customers from the volatility rather than from the level. The suppliers have said that several of them will not survive the winter without help, and the regulator has allowed them to recover some of the costs later.

At the same time, the government's response is being written by two people who have yet to take office. The prime minister is leaving in ten days and the two candidates to replace him have promised different packages, one of them a set of tax cuts and the other a set of payments. The treasury has said that either would need to borrow, and the borrowing would come on top of a deficit that the pandemic has already enlarged. The payments that have been announced so far amount to four hundred pounds for every household and a larger sum for those on benefits, and the sums are small compared with the increase.

Meanwhile, the effects are already appearing in the high street. The energy companies have begun to disconnect the customers who cannot pay, and the charities that advise them say the number has risen by a third in a year. The local authorities have set up warm rooms in libraries and community centres, and the schools have asked the parents to send children with an extra layer. The landlords have begun to pass the cost of the cap into the rent, and the tenants who are protected by a benefit have not been protected from that.

Against that, the comparison with the rest of Europe is the one the politicians avoid. The French government has capped the rise at four per cent and the German government has announced a package worth sixty-five billion euros. The British support is smaller per household and it is paid as a rebate on the bill rather than as a cap on the price. The analysts said the difference is that the British market is more exposed to the wholesale price and that the government has less room to borrow after the pandemic.

What the announcement does is fix a number that every household in the country will now discuss. The cap is a mechanism that was designed to protect the customers from the sharp practices of the suppliers, and it has become the instrument through which the war reaches the kitchen. The bills will arrive in October, the payments will arrive with them and the gap will be the subject of the winter. The next announcement is due in November, and by then the country will know who is paying.
