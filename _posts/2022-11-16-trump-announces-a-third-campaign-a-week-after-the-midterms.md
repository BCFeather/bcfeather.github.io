---
title: "Trump announces a third campaign a week after the midterms go wrong for his party"
date: 2022-11-16 23:50:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The former president files the paperwork for 2024 from Mar-a-Lago while his endorsed candidates lose and the party argues about the blame."
---

Donald Trump announced on Tuesday evening that he would run for president a third time, and he made the announcement from the ballroom at Mar-a-Lago in front of an audience of donors and club members. The paperwork was filed with the election commission within the hour, which makes him the first declared candidate of the cycle. He is seventy-six, and he would be eighty-two at the next inauguration. He spoke for an hour and repeated most of the themes of the last two campaigns.

The timing is what his party is arguing about. The midterm elections a week earlier had gone worse for the Republicans than the polls and the party's own projections had promised, and the Senate stayed with the Democrats after a runoff in Georgia was set. Several of the candidates he had endorsed lost, and some of them lost by margins that the party's committees attributed to his interventions. His answer on Tuesday was that the party had failed to follow his advice on early voting and on the message.

His record in office is the one he ran on: the tax cut of 2017, three appointments to the Supreme Court, the border policy, the withdrawal from the climate agreement and from the nuclear deal with Iran. He also carries the record of the defeat in 2020, the challenge to the count in several states, the storming of the Capitol on 6 January and the two impeachments. The audience at Mar-a-Lago was smaller than the one at his previous announcement, and the room had been arranged to look full.

The party's reaction was split within hours. Some senators said openly that the party needed a different candidate, and one of them described the announcement as a gift to the Democrats. Others said that his base of supporters could not be transferred to anybody else and that a primary without him would be a contest between unknowns. Ron DeSantis, who won re-election in Florida by nineteen points, declined to comment, and the question of whether he runs is now the first question of the cycle.

His legal exposure is the reason some of his advisers had argued for waiting. The Justice Department is investigating the documents taken from the White House and stored at his club, and a grand jury has heard testimony from his staff. The House committee on the Capitol attack has subpoenaed him for documents and testimony, and the deadline falls this week. A special counsel had yet to be appointed, and the announcement does not change any of the cases.

What the announcement does is freeze the party for a year. Donors who had been waiting to see whether he would run now have an answer, and the ones who wanted a different candidate have a problem they did not have on Monday. The Democratic National Committee's response was a statement about the record of the last campaign, and the president said at a briefing that he intends to run as well and that the decision would be announced after Christmas.
