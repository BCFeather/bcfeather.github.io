---
title: "The Beatles release a song from beyond the grave"
date: 2023-11-02 21:40:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "Now and Then, assembled from a 1970s demo by John Lennon and finished with surviving band members and machine-assisted audio restoration, was released on 2 November 2023 as the last new Beatles single."
---

The Beatles released a new single on 2 November, more than fifty years after the group broke up and forty-three after the death of John Lennon. The song, Now and Then, began as a piano demo that Lennon recorded in the late 1970s, and it had sat in a vault since his widow, Yoko Ono, handed copies of his home recordings to Paul McCartney in the 1990s. Two of those demos became Beatles songs in 1995; the third was abandoned because the tape was too damaged to work with.

The obstacle was technical. Lennon's voice on the demo was buried under a hiss and a piano that could never be removed cleanly with the tools of the 1990s, and the project stalled for decades. McCartney said in interviews that the song had stayed with him, and that he had wanted to finish it while he still could. The recovery came from software developed for film and television, which can separate a voice from a mix by learning what speech looks like in the frequency domain.

What emerged is a track of roughly four minutes, with Lennon's restored vocal, a new bass and guitar part from McCartney, drums and backing vocals from Ringo Starr, and a guitar solo assembled partly from recordings made during the 1995 sessions, when George Harrison was still alive. The producers, McCartney and Giles Martin, said the string arrangement was written to match the period, and the strings were recorded at Capitol Studios in Los Angeles. It was released as a double A-side with Love Me Do, the band's first single from 1962.

Reaction split along a predictable line. Some critics called the song a moving piece of closure and praised the restraint of the arrangement, while others argued that a synthetic reconstruction of a dead man's voice belongs in a different category of object, closer to a memorial than to a record. McCartney answered the point directly in interviews, saying that Lennon's voice was his own and that the band had simply finished something he had started. The single went to the top of the British chart within a week.

Its arrival also landed in the middle of a broader argument about machine learning and recorded music. The same tools that recovered a damaged tape can clone a voice from a few minutes of speech, and several artists spent the year pressing platforms and legislators for rules on consent and disclosure. McCartney had earlier joined a campaign warning about artificial intelligence in music, and he described his own use of the technology as a restoration rather than a creation, a distinction that the law does not yet draw clearly.

For the surviving members, the project closes a catalogue that has been reopened several times. Anthology in the 1990s, the remastered albums in 2009, the Get Back film and the rooftop concert in 2021, and now a single that no one expected. Starr, asked what the song meant, said it was a chance to have the four of them in a room again, and that the room had been quiet for a very long time.
