---
title: "The Nobel for literature goes to a quiet Norwegian"
date: 2023-10-05 19:50:00 +0800
categories: [culture]
description: "Jon Fosse, the Norwegian novelist and playwright, won the 2023 Nobel Prize in Literature on 5 October 2023, honoured for plays and prose that the Swedish Academy said give voice to the unsayable."
---

Jon Fosse writes in Norwegian Nynorsk, a written standard used by a minority of his countrymen, and he has spent four decades producing plays, novels, poetry and essays that circle the same few subjects: grief, distance, a God who does not answer. The Swedish Academy awarded him the 2023 prize on 5 October for what it called his innovative plays and prose, which it said give voice to the unsayable. He told Norwegian television that he was overwhelmed and that the prize would be shared with the language he writes in. He has published more than forty works, and translations exist in more than fifty languages, most of them added in the past two decades.

His plays are the part most readers outside Norway know. They are built from short, repeating lines, with characters who circle each other and rarely finish a sentence, and the stage directions often specify silence. A typical Fosse drama has a small cast, a single room and an accumulating pressure that critics have compared to Ibsen stripped of plot. Productions in Europe and Asia have made him one of the most performed contemporary playwrights, even though he has said he stopped writing for the stage years ago. He wrote most of them in a burst in the 1990s, and he has described the act as listening rather than composing.

The prose is slower and more explicit about faith. He converted to Catholicism in 2012, and the later novels treat religious life without irony, which is unusual in Scandinavian writing. Septology, a seven-part work published in three volumes and running to more than eight hundred pages, follows a painter and a doppelgänger through a single continuous sentence, and the English edition by Damion Searls was shortlisted for the International Booker Prize in 2022. The early novels had gone out of print in Britain until a small press reissued them, which is how most English readers found him.

Fosse's career ran through small presses and state arts grants, and he has been candid that he wrote for years with little income. The award will sell books regardless: the publisher Fitzcarraldo has reprinted the Septology volumes since the announcement, and translations into languages that had no Fosse in print are now being commissioned. The eleven million kronor that accompany the prize are, by the standards of Norwegian publishing, a large sum.

Academy members chose a writer whose work resists summary, after several years in which the prize went to journalists, poets and novelists with clearer public profiles. Fosse has been a candidate for a decade, and the citation's emphasis on the unsayable reads as a statement about what literature can do that reporting cannot, which is a theme the academy has returned to repeatedly.

Whether readers take to the repetition is the open question. Fosse's method either draws a reader into a trance or keeps them at a distance, and there is little in between. The prize does not resolve that, and the books are widely available now for the argument to be had. Publishers have printed extra copies, and theatres that staged him are reviving the early plays.
