---
title: "Ukraine marks independence day and six months of war with burnt Russian vehicles on the main street"
date: 2022-08-24 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Kyiv forbids large gatherings and warns of missile attacks, and a strike on a railway station kills twenty-five people the same day."
---

Ukraine marked thirty-one years of independence on Wednesday and six months since the invasion began, and the commemoration in Kyiv was held with the wreckage of Russian tanks and armoured vehicles arranged along the main street. The authorities had warned for days that the date would be used for attacks, and they banned large gatherings in the capital. The president spoke in a short address and said the country had been reborn on the day the war started. Air raid sirens sounded in several cities during the morning.

The strike came in the afternoon. A rocket hit the railway station at Chaplyne, in the Dnipropetrovsk region, and the coaches of a passenger train were set on fire. The authorities said twenty-five people were killed and more than thirty were hurt, and the rescue work continued into the night. The town is a junction where the trains from the east change direction, and the station was crowded with people who were being evacuated. The presidency said the attack had been timed for the holiday.

The mood in the capital was the one that the holiday produced. People brought flowers to the memorials that have been set up outside the buildings that were hit, and the queues at the recruitment offices were longer than usual. The shops that sell the embroidered shirts that the day is associated with had run out, and the museums had moved their exhibitions into the metro stations. The mayor said the city had not been this quiet on the holiday since 1991, and the quiet was the point.

At the same time, the war itself has changed shape in the six months. The first phase was an attempt to take the capital, and the second was a battle for the east that the Russians won slowly. The third began in the summer, when the Ukrainians used the long-range rockets that the west supplied to hit the supply depots behind the front, and the front has since moved little. The army has said it is preparing a counteroffensive in the south, and the officials have asked the population not to discuss the preparations.

Meanwhile, the allies marked the day with statements and with money. The American administration announced a further package of weapons worth about three billion dollars, and the British prime minister, who was leaving office, visited Kyiv for the third time. The European Union's leaders said the country would receive the status of a candidate, which it was granted in June, and the finance ministers discussed a new loan. The promises are large and the delivery is slow, and the officials in Kyiv have said so in public.

What the day shows is a country that has learned to conduct a war and a holiday at the same time. The commemorations were smaller than they would have been, and the casualties were higher than they would have been on an ordinary Wednesday. The war has become the frame in which everything else happens, and the frame will stay for as long as the front does. The next six months will be fought in the south, and the next independence day will be celebrated in whatever the country looks like then.
