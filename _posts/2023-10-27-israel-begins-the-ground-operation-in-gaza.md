---
title: "Israel begins the ground operation in Gaza"
date: 2023-10-27 23:55:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Israeli forces moved into northern Gaza on the night of 27 October 2023 after three weeks of air strikes, and the same day the General Assembly passed a resolution calling for a humanitarian truce by 120 votes to 14."
---

Israeli armour and infantry moved into northern Gaza on the night of 27 October, three weeks after the attack that started the war, and the military described the operation as a widening of the ground campaign that had begun with limited raids days earlier. The entry followed a night of intense bombardment along the northern edge of the strip, and it was accompanied by the restoration of some internet and phone service after a blackout that had cut the territory off for more than a day. The army said its columns moved along the northern edge of the strip, supported by naval fire from the Mediterranean.

The stated objectives are the destruction of Hamas's military capacity and the recovery of the hostages taken on 7 October. Those two aims pull against each other, because the hostages are held in tunnels that the army has described as the centre of the enemy's infrastructure, and because a campaign that moves through dense urban areas puts the people it is meant to rescue at risk. Officials have said the release of hostages remains a condition in the negotiations that Qatar and Egypt are conducting, and the talks have produced a small number of releases. Commanders have said the campaign will take months rather than weeks, and that the tunnels are the hardest part of it.

Humanitarian conditions deteriorated faster than the aid system could respond. The United Nations agency for Palestinian refugees said more than 600,000 people were sheltering in its schools and warehouses, and that its fuel stocks would run out within days. Hospitals in the north reported that they were operating without power for ventilators, and the World Health Organization said patients and staff were moving south as facilities closed.

The diplomacy moved to the General Assembly after the Security Council failed three times. A resolution drafted by Jordan, which called for an immediate truce, the protection of civilians and the delivery of aid, passed on 27 October with 120 votes in favour, 14 against and 45 abstentions. Israel and the United States were among those opposed, and the text stopped short of condemning Hamas by name, which was the reason several European states abstained.

Regional escalation stayed a live risk. Hezbollah continued to exchange fire across the Lebanese border, and the Houthis in Yemen launched missiles and drones towards Israel that were intercepted. American officials said they had warned Iran against opening a wider front, and the deployment of air defence systems and naval groups was intended to make that warning credible without direct engagement. Militias backed by Iran have also fired at American positions in Iraq and Syria, which the Pentagon has answered with strikes.

Ground operations change the shape of the war and not its politics. Israel has said it will not accept a ceasefire that leaves Hamas in place, and the governments that voted for a truce have said they will not fund a reconstruction that does. The space between those positions is where the next phase will be fought, and it is narrow.
