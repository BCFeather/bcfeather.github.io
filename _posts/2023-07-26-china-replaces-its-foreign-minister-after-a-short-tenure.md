---
title: "China replaces its foreign minister after a short tenure"
date: 2023-07-26 22:40:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "The standing committee of China's legislature removed Qin Gang as foreign minister on 25 July, seven months after appointing him, and reinstated Wang Yi, a former foreign minister who had moved to the party's foreign affairs office."
---

China's top legislature, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, voted on Tuesday to remove Qin Gang from the post of foreign minister and appoint Wang Yi to take over the role. The same session also approved a change at the helm of the People's Bank of China, replacing Yi Gang with Pan Gongsheng as central bank governor. The decisions were announced in official releases following the meeting.

Qin Gang had assumed the foreign minister portfolio in December 2022, succeeding Wang Yi. A career diplomat with experience in the ministry's protocol division and a prior posting as ambassador to the United States, Qin led diplomatic engagements covering a wide range of bilateral and global issues during his months in office. Wang, by contrast, brings long-standing familiarity with the foreign ministry's work, having served as foreign minister for a decade before moving to the Central Foreign Affairs Commission in 2022.

Diplomatic observers note that Wang's return brings continuity to China's external outreach at a complex global juncture. The international agenda in 2023 carried heavy demands: protracted conflict in Europe, shifting dynamics in major-country relations, and pressure to stabilise trade and external economic ties amid sluggish global demand. Wang's well-established network with diplomatic counterparts across regions helps sustain steady communication channels.

The parallel reshuffle at the central bank marks a transition in monetary leadership. Pan Gongsheng, the new governor, is a seasoned financial administrator who previously headed the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. His appointment came as policymakers focused on currency stability, cross-border capital flows and support for domestic economic recovery. The two personnel changes were released together as part of the legislature’s routine appointment and removal agenda.

The foreign ministry will continue to advance China's established foreign policy framework, centred on safeguarding national interests, promoting dialogue and advancing mutually beneficial cooperation with all countries. Analysts expect the ministry to maintain steady engagement with ASEAN partners, the United States, Russia and other major players, while pushing forward multilateral cooperation on food security, energy and global governance.

The reshuffle reflects normal personnel adjustment within state institutions. The legislature follows constitutional procedures for such appointments, and the newly installed officials will carry forward policy priorities set by the central government. For diplomatic and financial circles, the appointments signal a focus on stability and institutional continuity amid persistent external uncertainties.
