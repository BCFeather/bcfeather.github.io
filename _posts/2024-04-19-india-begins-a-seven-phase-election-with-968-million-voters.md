---
title: "India begins a seven-phase election with 968 million voters"
date: 2024-04-19 23:45:00 +0800
categories: [news]
description: "Polling began on 19 April in the first of seven phases of India's general election, in which about 968 million people are registered to vote and in which the prime minister, Narendra Modi, is seeking a third term."
---

Polling began on the nineteenth of April in the first phase of India's general election, in which about nine hundred and sixty-eight million people are registered to vote across twenty-eight states and eight union territories. The election is held in seven phases because the commission moves security forces and officials from one region to the next, and voting will end on the first of June, with the count on the fourth.

Scale of the operation is without parallel. About twelve million officials and security personnel are deployed, and electronic voting machines are transported to polling stations in areas that include deserts, mountains and islands. The commission said it would set up about a million polling stations, and that some would serve a single voter.

Contest is between the National Democratic Alliance, led by the Bharatiya Janata Party, and the opposition alliance of the Congress party and regional parties. Narendra Modi is seeking a third term and campaigned on economic growth, on infrastructure and on welfare payments to poor households, and he set a target of three hundred and seventy seats for his alliance in a chamber of five hundred and forty-three. The opposition campaigned on unemployment, on the treatment of minorities and on the concentration of power in the prime minister's office.

Issues that dominated the campaign included the arrest of opposition leaders, which the government said was a matter for the courts and which the opposition described as political, and the role of the commission, which the opposition accused of bias after several of its officials were transferred. Turnout and the economy were the other subjects, with the government citing growth of about eight per cent and the opposition citing the fall in real wages for workers.

First phase covered a hundred and two seats in twenty-one states, including Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan and parts of the north-east. Violence was limited, and the commission reported isolated incidents and a small number of deaths. Turnout in the first phase was about sixty-five per cent, which is lower than the seventy per cent recorded in 2019.

The commission said it had deployed more than two million electronic voting machines and that it had trained officials to carry them to villages that can be reached only on foot. Observers from several countries were accredited, and the commission published turnout figures for each constituency within hours of the close of polling. The commission also announced that it would publish the number of voters at each station, a change that it said would make the conduct of the poll easier to audit afterwards.

Consequence of the nineteenth of April is a campaign that will run for six weeks in a country where the result is likely to be decided by regional parties and by the turnout of women. The scale of the operation and the length of the schedule are features of a system that has managed elections in a country of this size for seventy years. What the phases will establish is whether the prime minister's appeal has held in the south, where his party has never been strong, and whether the opposition can turn dissatisfaction into seats.
