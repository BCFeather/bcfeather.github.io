The price of pork rose through the early summer. The wholesale price of a pig climbed by a fifth between March and July. The retail price in a wet market followed it with a lag of a fortnight.

The cause sits three quarters earlier. A breeding sow that is kept in the autumn produces piglets in the winter and finished pigs in the summer. A farmer who reduced his herd when the price was low in the previous year has produced the shortage that the market now pays for.

The cycle is old and it has not been abolished. A price below the cost of feed drives small farms out of the business. A price above it brings them back. The large farms that now dominate the industry are supposed to smooth the cycle, and their share is large enough to matter without being large enough to stop it.

The feed is the largest cost. Maize and soya meal are bought on a futures market and milled into a ration. A farmer who buys at the wrong moment spends a fifth of his revenue on a price that he cannot control.

The reserve is the government's answer. A stock of frozen pork is bought when the price falls below a threshold and released when it rises above another one. The purchases support the market at the bottom and the releases cap it at the top, and the stock is finite.

The consumer sees the result in a hundred dishes. A bowl of noodles with minced pork, a dumpling, a stir-fry, a sausage. The pork is the ingredient that a household notices when it moves, because it is the one that is bought by weight every week.

The restaurants absorb the change for a month or two. A menu is reprinted twice a year and the price on it is set for the season. A restaurant that sells a braised pork dish at a fixed price loses a margin in July and recovers it in December.

The slaughterhouse is the bottleneck when the herd is large. A line that kills two thousand pigs a day cannot kill three thousand without a second shift, and a second shift needs staff that the county does not have. A farmer who cannot get a slot sells at a discount to a trader who can.

The cold chain decides where the meat goes. A carcass that is chilled within an hour can travel a thousand kilometres. A carcass that is not is sold in the county. The cities that eat the most pork are supplied by the provinces that have the most chilling capacity, which is why a shortage in one province becomes a price in another.

The import is the other valve. Frozen pork bought from Europe or from South America is held in a bonded cold store and released when the domestic price rises. The volume is small enough to be a signal and large enough to be a threat, which is how the reserve works.

The cycle will turn again. The sows kept this spring will produce pigs in the autumn. The price will fall in the winter, and the farmer who expanded will lose money in the following spring.
