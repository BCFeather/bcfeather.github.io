---
title: "The pool that was too shallow"
date: 2024-08-01 21:30:00 +0800
tags: [olympics, swimming, sport, france]
categories: [sport]
description: "The swimming pool built for the Paris Games is two metres deep, a metre less than the standard. No world record has been set in it, which is the first time that has happened at a Games since 1988."
---

The swimming venue for the Paris Games was built inside an arena that already existed, which is why the pool is two metres deep. Two metres is a metre short of the depth that the sport has used for decades.

Depth matters because of the water under the swimmer. A body travelling at speed pushes a wave ahead of it, that wave reaches the floor of the pool. The reflection comes back to disturb the stroke. A shallow pool returns the disturbance sooner, so the swimmer works harder for the same time.

Nobody has broken a world record in this pool. The last Games without one was Seoul in 1988. Several swimmers have come within a tenth of a second of a record and stopped there.

The organisers published the depth in the technical manual before the venue opened. The manual is read by the national federations, and two of them asked for a variance to be considered. The variance was refused, because the arena could not be rebuilt in the time available.

Coaches have adapted the training in the week before the meet. A session that would normally be swum in a three-metre pool was moved to a shallower one at a training centre outside the city. The adaptation is about the number of repeats rather than the total distance.

The times are still fast and the comparison is unfair. A swimmer in Paris is racing in water that costs a hundredth of a second per length. The same swimmer in Tokyo three years ago raced in water that did not. The medal is decided inside one pool on one night.

The venue will stay after the Games. The arena is a public facility for the northern district. The pool will be used by clubs and schools. A shallow pool is a cheaper pool to run, because the volume of water to filter and heat is smaller.

The depth is also a safety question. A pool that is two metres deep cannot host the diving events, so those were moved to a separate venue on the other side of the city. The platforms need five metres beneath them.

The governing body sets a minimum depth of two metres for a competition pool and recommends three. The Paris venue met the minimum on the day it was inspected, which is why the complaint that the swimmers filed was answered with a rule rather than an apology.

The television does not show the difference. A camera above the water sees a swimmer who is level with the swimmer beside him, and the cost of the shallow floor is paid in the last fifteen metres of a two hundred metre race.

The stands were built for fifteen thousand and they were full for every final. The acoustics of an arena designed for basketball make a cheer that sounds thin on television, which is the other complaint that the swimmers made in the mixed zone.

The record that was not broken will be mentioned in the reports for years. A statistician will compare the Paris times with the Tokyo times and find a gap that no training explains, and the manual will be cited in the footnote.
