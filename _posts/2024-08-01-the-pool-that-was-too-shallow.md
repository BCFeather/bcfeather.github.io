---
title: "The pool that was too shallow"
date: 2024-08-01 21:30:00 +0800
categories: [sport]
description: "The swimming pool built for the Paris Games is two metres deep, a metre less than the standard. No world record has been set in it, which is the first time that has happened at a Games since 1988."
---

An arena that already existed holds the swimming venue for the Paris Games, and its pool is shallow. Two metres is a metre short of the depth that the sport has used for decades. Depth matters because of the water under the swimmer. A body travelling at speed pushes a wave ahead of it, that wave reaches the floor of the pool. Reflections come back to disturb the stroke. A shallow pool returns the disturbance sooner, so the swimmer works harder for the same time.

No world record has fallen in this pool. Seoul in 1988 was the last Games without one. Several swimmers have come within a tenth of a second of a record and stopped there. The organisers published the depth in the technical manual before the venue opened. National federations read the manual, and two of them asked for a variance. Variance was refused, because the arena could not be rebuilt in the time available.

Coaches adapted their training in the week before the meet. A session that would normally be swum in a three-metre pool was moved to a shallower one at a training centre outside the city. Adaptation is about the number of repeats rather than the total distance. The times are still fast and the comparison is unfair. A swimmer in Paris is racing in water that costs a hundredth of a second per length. Three years ago in Tokyo the same swimmer raced in water that did not. Medal is decided inside one pool on one night.

After the Games the venue will stay. Arena is a public facility for the northern district. Pool will be used by clubs and schools. A shallow pool is a cheaper pool to run, because the volume of water to filter and heat is smaller. The depth is also a safety question. A pool that is two metres deep cannot host the diving events, so those were moved to a separate venue on the other side of the city. Platforms need five metres beneath them.

Two metres is the minimum depth a competition pool may have, and three is the recommendation. The Paris venue met the minimum on the day it was inspected, which is why the complaint that the swimmers filed was answered with a rule rather than an apology. The television does not show the difference. A camera above the water sees a swimmer who is level with the swimmer beside him, and the cost of the shallow floor is paid in the last fifteen metres of a two hundred metre race.

Fifteen thousand seats filled for every final. The acoustics of an arena designed for basketball make a cheer that sounds thin on television, which is the other complaint that the swimmers made in the mixed zone. Reports will mention the record that was not broken for years. A statistician will compare the Paris times with the Tokyo times and find a gap that no training explains, and the manual will be cited in the footnote.
