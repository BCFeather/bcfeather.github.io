title: "The village that had to be built in a year"
date: 2024-07-24 21:30:00 +0800

description: "The athletes' village north of Paris opened two days before the ceremony. It was built on a former industrial site. The flats inside it will be sold as housing after the Games."
---

North of Paris, the athletes' village opened two days before the ceremony. Ten thousand athletes and their staff will live in it for a month. Site was a former industrial estate beside the river. It took four years to clear. The buildings are blocks of flats that will be sold after the Games. A unit of three rooms holds two athletes during the fortnight and a family afterwards. The design was fixed by the housing authority rather than by the organising committee, which is why the village looks like a neighbourhood.

Transport was the first problem. A village that sits between two motorway junctions needs a bridge to the stadium, a new station on the regional line. A shuttle that runs every four minutes. Bridge finished in the spring and the station opened in the summer. The food is served in a single hall that seats three thousand. A menu of four hundred dishes rotates through the fortnight. Kitchen runs on a supply chain that was contracted two years ahead. A national team can request a dish from home with a week of notice.

Largest operation in the city this year is security. Eighteen thousand police and gendarmes are deployed for the period. A perimeter around the village is closed to anyone without an accreditation. A resident of the district needs a pass to reach her own front door. The river is the second site. Boats along six kilometres of water stage the opening ceremony. Water had to be clean enough for the swimmers who will use it in the following week. A storage basin upstream was built to hold the storm water that used to overflow into it.

Cleaning worked, and the rain still threatens it. A heavy storm in the week before the ceremony raises the bacteria count. Organisers have a plan to postpone a swimming event by a day. Plan is part of the schedule rather than an emergency. The cost has been counted by the auditors. Large is the public share of the bill, spread across a regional budget, a national budget and a transport authority. Something will be earned back from the village, because the flats have a buyer.

A small hospital's size is what the medical station has. Two hundred beds, a pharmacy, a laboratory and a set of imaging machines were installed in the basement. Staff are volunteers from the city's hospitals and from the army, and they will close the station when the last team leaves. The volunteers are the part of the operation that is hardest to count. Forty-five thousand people applied to help, and the organisers trained them in a hall for three evenings. A volunteer who hands out water at a finish line has been through a background check and a first aid course. Keys to the flats will be handed to the tenants in the autumn, after the last team has checked out.

Swimming pool, sports hall and bridge will stay with the district. The rest of the site will be sold, and the families who move in will live in a building that was designed for a fortnight of athletes.
