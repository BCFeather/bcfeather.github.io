AMD's revenue rises 71 per cent

The chip maker reported record revenue on 3 May and took share from Intel in servers.

Posted 3 May 2022
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

AMD published results for the quarter to March on 3 May, and revenue rose 71 per cent to 5.9 billion dollars. The figure was a record and it was above the 5.5 billion forecast. Net income was 786 million, and the gross margin was 48 per cent. Its shares rose 6 per cent the next day. Its operating income was 900 million dollars, and its free cash flow was 1.2 billion.

Revenue from server chips was 2.5 billion dollars, and the company said it had taken 10 points of market share from Intel in the year. The data centre business doubled. Its share of the server market was 20 per cent, and it had been 5 per cent in 2019. The company said it would grow 60 per cent for the year, and it raised its margin forecast to 54 per cent.

Revenue from personal computer chips was 2.8 billion, and the company said it had gained share in laptops. The client business grew 30 per cent. Its gaming business was 1.9 billion, and its embedded business 600 million after the acquisition of Xilinx. The Xilinx deal had closed in February. The company paid 49 billion dollars in stock, and the deal added 600 million dollars of revenue in the quarter. AMD said the combined company would sell to 10,000 customers, and that it would keep the FPGA business separate. Its average selling price rose 15 per cent, and it shipped 2 million server chips.

Guidance for the June quarter was 6.5 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 6.1. The company said it would grow 60 per cent for the year, and it raised its gross margin forecast to 54 per cent. It said the supply of chips was still short, and that it had prepaid 3 billion dollars to its foundry.

Intel's Sapphire Rapids was a year late, and its margin had fallen. The company's share of the server market had risen because Intel's new chips had been delayed. AMD's chips used fewer watts, and the cloud providers had bought them in volume. The personal computer market had begun to weaken. The company said sales of consumer chips would fall in the second half, and that its growth would come from servers. It said it expected the market to shrink 10 per cent in 2023, and it did. It said its foundry had allocated more capacity for the second half of the year. The company had prepaid 3 billion dollars to its foundry, and it held 5 billion in cash.

Shares had fallen 30 per cent in the year before the results, and they rose 6 per cent after them. The company's value was 150 billion dollars, and it was 40 per cent below its peak. Its revenue for the year was 23.6 billion, up 44 per cent, and its net income was 1.3 billion.
