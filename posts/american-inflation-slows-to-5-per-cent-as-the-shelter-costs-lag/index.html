American inflation slows to 5 per cent as the shelter costs lag

The consumer price index rose by 5 per cent over the year in March, the slowest since May 2021, while the core measure stayed at 5.6 per cent on the shelter and the services.

Posted 12 Apr 2023
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

American consumer prices rose by 5 per cent over the year to March, down from 6 per cent in February and the smallest increase since May 2021. The monthly change was 0.1 per cent. The core measure, which strips the food and the energy, rose by 0.4 per cent on the month and by 5.6 per cent over the year. The index has fallen from the peak of 9.1 per cent in June, and the descent has been slower than the Fed wanted.

Energy prices did the work. The petrol prices fell by about 4 per cent over the month as the crude came down from the peak of the winter, and the electricity was flat. The food rose by 0.1 per cent, the smallest monthly gain since the summer of 2020, as the egg prices fell. The petrol is about 20 per cent cheaper than a year ago, and the wholesale prices suggest another decline in April.

Shelter kept the core high. The rent and the owners' equivalent rent rose by about 0.5 per cent each, and they contribute about a third of the index. The measure lags the market by about a year, which is why the economists expect it to cool through the summer. A lag that is long is a disinflation that is late. The measure for the new tenants, which the bureau publishes separately, has been rising by about 8 per cent, and the gap with the stock is the reason the index lags.

Services outside the shelter were firm. The transport services fell as the air fares dropped by about 1.4 per cent, and the car insurance rose by about 1.2. The medical care services were flat, and the recreation rose slightly. The car repairs rose by about 0.4 per cent, and the personal care services by about 0.3, which are the lines that the tight labour market touches.

Markets read the report as a mixed signal. The headline was below the forecast of 5.1, and the core was in line. The two-year yield fell by about 5 basis points, and the traders kept the odds of a quarter-point rise in May at about 70 per cent. The ten-year yield was little changed, and the dollar was flat against the euro after the release. The forecasters at the banks said the next two reports would decide whether the Fed pauses in June, and the shelter is the variable in both.

A pattern that has held since the winter is unchanged in this report. The goods have stopped pushing the index up, and the services now carry it, which is the slower and the stickier half. A core that stays at 5.6 per cent is a core that the Fed will not ignore. The services employment is the measure to watch, and the wage report of the last month was the first sign of the easing.
