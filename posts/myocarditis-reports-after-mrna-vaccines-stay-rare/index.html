Myocarditis reports after mRNA vaccination stay rare and cluster in young men

Adjudicated reports to the American vaccine safety system put myocarditis at a few dozen cases per million doses, with the highest rates in adolescent boys after a second dose.

Posted 5 Jan 2022
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

Among the hundreds of millions of mRNA doses given in the United States during the first eight months of the vaccination campaign, reports of heart muscle inflammation formed a small and heavily age-skewed set. Clinicians and regulators had seen the signal in Israel and in American hospital case series by mid-2021, but passive reporting systems accumulate noise, and a denominator large enough to price the risk had not been assembled.

What the American surveillance group did was adjudicate every myocarditis report filed with the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System between December 2020 and August 2021, a period covering 192,405,448 people older than 12 years who received 354,100,845 mRNA doses. Expected rates by age and sex came from insurance claims filed in 2017 to 2019, which supplied a background incidence against which the reported cases could be set. The work appears in JAMA, 4 January 2022.

Of 1,991 reports, 1,626 met the case definition. Median age was 21 years, with an interquartile range of 16 to 31, and median onset came two days after vaccination, with an interquartile range of one to three. Males accounted for 82 per cent of cases where sex was recorded. Crude reporting rates within seven days exceeded background in several strata, and the peak sat after a second dose: 70.7 per million BNT162b2 doses in boys aged 12 to 15, 105.9 per million in boys aged 16 to 17, and 52.4 and 56.3 per million in men aged 18 to 24 for BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 respectively.

Clinical records for 826 patients younger than 30 carry the diagnostic weight. Troponin was elevated in 792 of 809 patients, or 98 per cent, the electrocardiogram was abnormal in 569 of 794, or 72 per cent, and cardiac magnetic resonance was abnormal in 223 of 312, also 72 per cent. Hospital admission followed for 784 of 813, or about 96 per cent. The two-day median onset fits a post-vaccination inflammatory window rather than an incidental finding months later, and the male skew matches the known effect of testosterone on cardiac inflammation in young animals.

Passive surveillance has structural limits. Reporting depends on a clinician choosing to file, so mild cases are missed and the true rate is likely higher than the crude figure, while the absence of a concurrent unvaccinated comparison group means the background rate does the work of a control. Adjudication reduces misclassification but cannot remove it, and the claims data used for expected rates carry their own coding drift.

What would settle the question is active surveillance with defined populations and cardiac imaging at fixed intervals, of the kind several Nordic countries began in 2021. In the meanwhile the practical question is one of age and dose spacing: the absolute risk remains in the tens per million, the rate falls sharply with age, and the comparison that matters for a 16-year-old boy is with the cardiac risk of the infection itself.
