A docked library yields a non-hallucinogenic antidepressant

Docking 75 million tetrahydropyridines against a model of the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor produced two agonists with half-maximal effective concentrations of 41 and 110 nanomolar that lacked psychedelic activity in mice and matched fluoxetine at a fortieth of the dose.

Posted 28 Sep 2022
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

A receptor that a psychedelic drug activates is a receptor that also mediates an antidepressant effect, and the two effects have been hard to separate with the molecules that nature supplies. A virtual library built to fit the binding pocket is an attempt to separate them by design. The receptor makes both effects in the same molecule, and separating them has meant looking for a chemistry the natural compounds do not offer.

We docked a bespoke virtual library of 75 million tetrahydropyridines against a model of the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor and synthesized 17 of the initial hits. The paper appeared in Nature, 28 September 2022. A library shaped around one scaffold is a library that samples the chemistry the receptor prefers, and 17 hits from 75 million compounds is a reasonable yield for a first cycle.

Four of the first molecules had low-micromolar activity against the 5-HT2A or the 5-HT2B receptor, and structure-based optimization produced the agonists (R)-69 and (R)-70, with half-maximal effective concentrations of 41 and 110 nanomolar. Potency is two orders of magnitude better than the first hits, which is what a structure-guided cycle is supposed to deliver. Seventeen compounds from 75 million is a hit rate a targeted screen would call ordinary and a general one would call excellent.

Cryo-electron microscopy confirmed the predicted binding mode, and the confirmation is the part that turns a docking score into a structural claim. A predicted pose that a structure validates is a pose that a medicinal chemist can build on. Whether the pose survives in solution as well as in a crystal is a question that cryo-electron microscopy cannot answer.

Signalling kinetics of the two agonists differ from those of the psychedelic agonists, and neither produced psychedelic behaviour in mice, while both had antidepressant activity in mouse models at as low as a fortieth of the fluoxetine dose. A behavioural difference between two agonists of the same receptor is the finding that the field has been looking for. The assumption that one receptor produces both the hallucination and the therapy is what these two molecules put under strain.

High brain permeability of the compounds is what made the behavioural assays possible, and the permeability came from the favourable physical properties of the scaffold. A molecule that cannot reach the brain cannot be tested for a central effect, and the chemistry is the gate. Permeability decided whether the compounds could be tested at all, and it came from the scaffold rather than from a designed modification. Tetrahydropyridine is a scaffold that a general billion-molecule library samples poorly, and a library built around it is the reason the hits were found. A bespoke library is an argument that chemical space is not uniform and that the sampling has to be targeted. A general library of a billion molecules would have missed this scaffold, which is the argument for bespoke collections.

The prospect such libraries raise to sample pharmacologically relevant space is the larger claim, and the claim is about method rather than about the two molecules. A method that produces a non-hallucinogenic agonist is a method that other receptors can be asked the same question with. Whether other aminergic receptors have similar niches is the test the method invites.
