The last week of the working year and the meetings that end it

The week before the holiday is the week in which an office does its annual accounting, and the meetings that fill it are part review and part ritual.

Posted 21 Jan 2025
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

In the week before the holiday an office does its annual accounting. The targets that were set in the spring are compared with the numbers that the finance team closed in December, the bonus is calculated on a formula that was written in March, and the meeting that presents the result is scheduled for the end of the week rather than the beginning.

Annual reviews are the formal part and the ritual is the part that the staff talk about afterwards. The department that meets to eat a meal together, the tickets that are raffled, the performance that a group of colleagues rehearses after work. The ritual is a management practice with a long history, and its function is to mark the year as finished in a way that a spreadsheet cannot.

Timing is what makes the week distinctive. The work that arrives in the last days of the lunar year is the work that cannot wait until the year after, and it is usually the work of closing rather than the work of opening. The invoices that have to be issued, the contracts that have to be signed, the delivery that has to reach a customer before the holiday. The week is busy in a narrow and specific way.

Travel that the week produces is the other half of its character. A colleague who leaves on the twenty-fifth has a ticket that was bought in December, and the office empties gradually rather than on a single day. The desk that is covered with a cloth on the twenty-seventh belongs to someone who has already gone home, and the corridor on the last afternoon holds the people who live in the same city.

A rule sits inside the holiday, and the week reveals it. A country that closes for a week and slows for two has to decide what stops and what continues, and the answer is that the retail, the transport and the health services continue while the offices and the factories stop. The week before the holiday is when that division is visible, because the two halves of the economy are working at different speeds on the same street.

What the ritual does for the people who take part in it is the part that the management literature understates. A year of work is a long sequence of small obligations, and the meal at the end of it is a way of acknowledging the sequence. The bonus is the material recognition and the meal is the social one, and the offices that skip the meal are the ones where the staff describe the year as flat.

For the person who is new to the office, the week is also the week in which the informal map of the place is drawn. Who organized the meal, who was thanked in the speech, who was left out of the photograph. The map is not written down and it is more useful in February than the organization chart.
