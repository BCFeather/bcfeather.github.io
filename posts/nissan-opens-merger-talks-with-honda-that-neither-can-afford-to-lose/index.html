Nissan opens merger talks with Honda that neither can afford to lose

Two Japanese carmakers signed a memorandum to explore a holding company, and the talks are a response to a Chinese market that has rewritten their volume assumptions.

Posted 24 Dec 2024
By Caozhe Bao
2 min read

Nissan and Honda signed a memorandum of understanding on 23 December to explore a joint holding company that would own both businesses, with a target of concluding the structure within a year. Mitsubishi, which is partly owned by Nissan, was named as a possible third member, and the talks follow a year in which Nissan cut its forecasts twice. A memorandum that sets a year for a structure is a memorandum that leaves the hard decisions outside. A memorandum that sets a deadline for a structure leaves the hard decisions outside its scope.

Pressure is volume. A carmaker's cost per vehicle depends on how many it builds, and Nissan's factories were sized for a market that has shrunk. A company that cuts nine thousand jobs and twenty per cent of capacity is a company that has admitted its assumptions were wrong, and the admission came after a decade of trying to grow in markets where its rivals were stronger. A factory that was built for a volume that did not arrive is a factory that has to close.

Chinese market is the reason. A carmaker that earns a large share of its profit in China is a carmaker whose earnings depend on a competition with local electric-vehicle manufacturers who price below the joint ventures. A joint venture that once sold a sedan at a premium is a joint venture that now sells it at a discount. A joint venture that was the profit centre is now the problem, and the partner is a competitor. A joint venture that was the profit centre is now the problem.

Merger logic is scale in the parts that both companies buy. A platform, a battery, a software stack and a factory are expensive to develop twice, and two companies that share them can spread the cost over twice the volume. A holding company is the structure that allows sharing without merging the brands. A shared platform is a saving that arrives two years after the agreement.

Obstacle is the product overlap. Two companies that sell similar cars in the same segments have to decide which factory closes, and a decision that closes a plant in a country with strong unions is a political decision. A merger that is announced as a partnership of equals is a merger that has not yet named the plants. A plant closure is a decision that a government will be asked to approve. A closure that a government must approve is a closure that a minister will delay.

What the talks show is that consolidation in the car industry has moved from the luxury end to the volume end. A company that cannot fund a platform on its own is a company that has to find a partner, and the partner it finds is a rival that faces the same arithmetic. A merger between rivals is a merger that the antitrust authorities in three countries will review.
