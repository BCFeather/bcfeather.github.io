Anthropic sets a 70 to 78 dollar price range for its Nasdaq listing

The Claude developer opened its roadshow on 10 November with an offer that could raise 90.4 billion dollars.

Posted 10 Nov 2026
By Caozhe Bao
3 min read

Anthropic opened the roadshow for its listing on Tuesday, and it plans to sell 825 million Class A shares at 70 to 78 dollars each. At the middle of that range the public offer would raise about 64.4 billion dollars, and a private placement of 26 billion dollars with cornerstone investors, among them Google, NVIDIA and AMD, would take the total to as much as 90.4 billion. At the top of the range the fully diluted valuation would approach 1.4 trillion dollars. Shares are expected to trade on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT from 19 November. The shares will be priced after the roadshow closes.

Demand for the shares will be tested against a business that is growing fast and spending faster. The company's annualized revenue run rate reached about 81 billion dollars at the end of October, according to the figures circulated with the roadshow, though a run rate is not audited annual revenue. It carries heavy computing costs and long-term commitments to data-centre capacity, and the prospectus asks investors to weigh those obligations against the growth. The roadshow runs for a week before the price is fixed.

What came before the offer was an unusual sequence. Preliminary terms were circulated to investment banks and then withdrawn, and the updated prospectus carries revised pricing and revised proceeds. Anyone comparing the current document with the earlier reports will find different numbers in both, and the difference matters because the private placement depends on the same pricing. Cornerstone investors have committed to buy at the offer price, which reduces the risk of a weak debut but concentrates the buyer base. Bankers said the withdrawal had cost the company time rather than money.

A listing at this size would set a benchmark for the whole frontier of artificial intelligence. Anthropic competes with OpenAI and with Google's own models, and the three are spending at a pace that only public markets or very large private rounds can finance. TSMC has approved investment of up to 1 billion dollars in Anthropic and in OpenAI, a sign that the suppliers expect the spending to continue. Competitors will read the prospectus for the cost of computing rather than for the revenue.

Two new models were released in the same week. Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 share an architecture but carry different safeguards, and Anthropic says they answer up to 30 per cent faster at 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens, about 20 per cent below the previous generation. The company reported a score of 49.1 per cent on its own coding evaluation, against 32.4 for the earlier model.

What the market does with the shares after 19 November will say more than the roadshow does. A first-day gain would encourage other model developers to file, and a fall below the range would chill the queue. The offer also lands in a week when American technology stocks have been falling and when NVIDIA, one of the cornerstone investors, is without its chief executive. Whether investors separate the two stories is the question the pricing will answer. The first quarterly report as a public company will follow in the new year. Employees holding options will watch the fixing price closely.
