On the quarter, output rose 3.9 per cent, which is the fastest in a year. The National Bureau of Statistics published the figures on 24 October, and gross domestic product rose 3.9 per cent in the year to September. The forecast had been 3.3, and the reading was up from 0.4 per cent in the second quarter.

Industrial output rose 4.8 per cent in September, and retail sales rose 2.5. The second quarter had been hit by lockdowns in Shanghai and in Jilin, and the comparison flattered the third. Fixed asset investment rose 5.9 per cent in the first nine months, and infrastructure investment rose 8.6. The unemployment rate was 5.5 per cent, and the youth rate was 18.7.

Property was the drag. Investment in real estate fell 8.8 per cent in the first nine months, and sales by floor area fell 22 per cent. New starts fell 38 per cent in September, and land sales fell by half. Local governments depend on land sales for a third of their revenue, and several of them cut spending on services. Developers defaulted on 3 trillion yuan of debt in the year, and 40 per cent of them missed payments.

Exports held up better than expected. Shipments rose 5.7 per cent in September, and the surplus widened to 82 billion dollars. The growth was in volume rather than in price, and the destinations shifted towards Southeast Asia and Europe as shipments to America fell. Factories reported that orders for the fourth quarter were weaker. The figures were published late. The statisticians had planned to release them on 18 October and postponed the date without explanation, which is unusual. Several banks said the delay suggested that the numbers had been revised, and the final figure was in line with the private estimates. Orders from Europe rose 12 per cent, and orders from America fell 4.

Consumption was the missing part. Retail sales had grown 0.7 per cent in the first nine months, and services were held back by restrictions on travel. Household deposits rose 13 trillion yuan, and surveys showed that consumers intended to save rather than spend. The government had cut taxes on car purchases and had subsidized appliances. The delay was the first for a quarterly release since the statisticians began the practice in 2015.

Economists said the realistic figure was about 3 per cent, and the year ended at 3. The annual target of 5.5 per cent was out of reach by the autumn. The premier said at the party congress that the economy would grow at a reasonable rate, and he did not repeat the target. Growth recovered after the reopening in December. The fourth quarter grew 2.9 per cent, and the year as a whole grew 3 per cent. The property market did not recover, and investment fell again in 2023. The government announced measures in the summer of that year and did not revive the sector. The savings rate had risen to 33 per cent, and household income grew more slowly than output.
