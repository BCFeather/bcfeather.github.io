Prices carried the concern. The survey said the owners had raised their prices 4 per cent in the month, and that 70 per cent had planned to raise them again. It said the owners had reported the fuel and the wages as the worst costs, and that the margins had fallen. The group's economist said the owners had cut their hiring plans. The index fell from 95.2, the lowest since April 2020, below the 95 forecast. Thirty per cent named inflation as their biggest problem, the most since 1981. Hiring remained the second complaint, the group said.

Hiring was hard. The survey said 47 per cent of the owners had reported jobs they could not fill, and that it was the highest on record. It said the owners had raised wages 4 per cent, and that 30 per cent planned another rise. The group said the shortage had cut the sales. Owners raised prices 4 per cent in the month, and 70 per cent planned another rise. Fuel and wages were the worst costs, and margins fell. The group's economist said owners had cut their hiring plans.

Sales were slowing. The survey said the owners had reported weaker sales in the month, and that the expectations had fallen 5 points. It said the owners had cut their plans to invest, and that the inventories had risen. The group said the war had cut the confidence. Forty seven per cent reported jobs they could not fill, a record. Wages rose 4 per cent, and 30 per cent planned another rise, with the shortage cutting sales. Sales weakened as prices rose, the survey said. Sales expectations fell for a third month.

A fall to 93.1 in April, and to 93.7 in May. The survey said the prices had risen 10 per cent, and that the owners had reported the worst since 1981. The bank raised rates by a half point in May. Sales weakened, and expectations fell 5 points. Investment plans were cut, and inventories rose as the war hit confidence.

A fall to 89 in June, and to 91 in September. The survey said the owners had reported weaker sales, and that the hiring plans had fallen. The group said the owners had expected a recession. Prices rose 10 per cent by May, the worst since 1981. The bank raised a half point in May. New orders fell, and the index stayed near 93 through the summer.

A reading of 91 in December 2023, and 95 in December 2024. The survey said the prices had fallen to 3 per cent, and that the owners had reported the shortage had eased. The survey said the owners had reported the worst labour shortage since 2020, and that the wages had risen 4 per cent. Owners reported weaker sales and cut hiring plans, expecting a recession. Prices fell to 3 per cent in 2024, and the labour shortage eased. The index rose to 95 in December 2024 as prices eased.
