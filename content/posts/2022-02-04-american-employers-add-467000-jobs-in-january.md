Omicron did less damage than feared. Firms said 4 million people had been absent in the middle of January, and that absences peaked in the third week. The payroll survey week fell before the worst of the wave, and restaurants added 108,000 jobs. Bars added 12,000, and hotels added 30,000 as travel held up. The unemployment rate rose even as hiring beat forecasts, because the workforce grew.

Wages rose 5.7 per cent in the year, and they had risen 5.0 in December. The average hourly pay was 31.63 dollars, and the gain was the fastest since 2007. Pay rose most for the low paid, and the leisure sector raised pay 13 per cent. The report said firms had raised pay to fill vacancies, and that 10 million jobs remained open. Absences cost factories and shops about 3 per cent of hours, and hospitals said staffing was short.

Six hundred thousand people returned to work, and the number caring for family fell by 200,000. The participation rate rose to 62.2 per cent, and it was 1.2 points below its level of February 2020. Economists said the wave had pushed people back to work, and that the rate would keep rising. The rate for prime age workers reached 82.3 per cent, close to its level before the pandemic. Average pay had risen 4.2 per cent in 2021, and the gain was the fastest in 15 years.

Professional services added 86,000 jobs, and health care 60,000, and retail 61,000. Government added 19,000, and manufacturing 13,000, and construction 20,000. Hiring was broad, and only the utilities cut jobs. The report said the gains had been concentrated in the private sector, which added 444,000. The rate for women reached 57.8 per cent, and for teenagers 34.6 per cent.

A March rate rise had been signalled, and the report confirmed the plan. Its chairman said the labour market was tight, and that inflation was too high. The market priced five rises for the year, and the yield on the two year note rose to 1.3 per cent. Economists said the report made a half point rise in March more likely. Health care had added 60,000 jobs a month since March, and it was still 2 per cent below 2020.

Hiring stayed strong through the year. Employers added 6.7 million jobs in 2022, and the rate fell to 3.5 per cent in July. The bank raised seven times, and the rate reached 4.25 per cent in December. Inflation reached 9.1 per cent in June, and it was the fastest since 1981. The revisions in 2024 cut the 2022 gain by 300,000. The report said the hiring had been concentrated in health care and in government, and that the leisure sector had recovered all the jobs it lost in 2020. The chairman had said the bank would act if inflation did not fall by the summer. The leisure sector added 2 million jobs in 2022, and it recovered all it had lost.
