Moody's changed its outlook on the American sovereign rating to negative on 10 November while affirming the Aaa grade, and cited the size of the deficits and the difficulty of reaching a budget agreement. The change does not affect the grade, and the agency said the outlook could return to stable if the budget improved. The agency said the change reflected the risk that the fiscal position would deteriorate faster than it had expected.

Arithmetic is the reason. The deficit was about 1.7 trillion dollars in the last fiscal year, or about 6 per cent of the output, and the interest bill has passed the defense budget. A deficit that is structural is a rating that is watched. The interest payments reached about 660 billion dollars in the last fiscal year, and they are expected to pass 800 billion next year.

Politics is the second reason. The agency said the polarization in the Congress had made the fiscal planning harder, and the shutdown in the autumn was the evidence. A shutdown that is avoided by a week is a budget that is not settled. The agency has warned about the debt ceiling brinksmanship for a decade, and the last agreement suspended the limit until the start of 2025. The agency said the two shutdowns avoided in a year had shown how narrow the margin in the Congress is, and the margin is the risk.

Its rating is unchanged. Moody's is the last of the three agencies to keep the top grade, and the other two have already cut it, which means the market has priced most of the news. A grade that is already low is a cut that moves less. The other two agencies cut the rating in 2011 and in 2023, and the markets have not treated the grade as a constraint on the borrowing. The American grade is still the highest of the three agencies, and the others have been below it since 2011 and 2023 respectively.

Yields reacted little. The ten-year yield rose by about 5 basis points, and the dollar was flat, which is the reaction of a market that has heard the argument before. A reaction that is small is a warning that is heard. The foreign holders have been buying fewer of the Treasuries, and the share held abroad has fallen to about a quarter from a third a decade ago. The borrowing costs have risen by about a point over the year, and the interest bill is the fastest growing line in the budget.

What the change shows is that the fiscal trajectory has become the constraint on the rating rather than the willingness to pay, and the willingness has never been in doubt. An outlook that changes is a warning that the arithmetic will be judged by the next recession, and the next recession is when the bill comes. The agency said it would review the outlook again in the spring, and the review depends on the budget that the Congress passes.
