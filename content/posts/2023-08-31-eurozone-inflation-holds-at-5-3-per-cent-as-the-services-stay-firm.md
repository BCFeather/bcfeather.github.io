title: "Eurozone inflation holds at 5.3 per cent as the services stay firm"
date: "2023-08-31T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The August flash estimate left the headline unchanged from July, and the core eased only to 5.3 as the wages kept the services rising."
---

Eurozone consumer prices rose by 5.3 per cent over the year to August, according to the flash estimate, unchanged from July and above the forecast of 5.1. The core measure, which strips the energy, the food, the alcohol and the tobacco, eased to about 5.3 from 5.5. The figures were the last before the September meeting. The figure is the highest of the three summer months, and the headline has fallen by about half from the peak of 10.6 per cent in October. The unemployment rate was about 6.4 per cent in July, which is the lowest on record for the euro area.

Energy eased. The energy prices fell by about 3.3 per cent over the year, which is a smaller decline than in July, as the base of last summer dropped out. The gas in the wholesale market was about 35 euros per megawatt hour, up from about 30 in July on the strike news in Australia. The electricity fell by about 8 per cent over the year, and the fuels by about 2 as the base dropped out. The storage was about 90 per cent full at the end of August, which is above the target for the winter.

Food slowed. The prices of the food, the alcohol and the tobacco rose by about 9.8 per cent, down from 10.8, and the one of the fresh food by about 7. The easing has been faster in the vegetables than in the meat, and the processors say the costs of the feed are still high. The prices of the dairy rose by about 8 per cent over the year, and the one of the eggs by about 5.

Services held. The prices of the services rose by about 5.5 per cent, and the one of the industrial goods by about 4. The negotiated wages have been rising by about 4.5 per cent, and the bank expects the services to hold around 5 into the winter. The air fares rose by about 5 per cent, and the one of the hotels by about 4 as the summer bookings held.

National figures were mixed. The inflation in Germany was about 6.4 per cent, and the one in France about 5.7, and the one in Spain about 2.4. The Baltic states were above 7, and the Netherlands about 4.8. The inflation in Italy was about 5.5 per cent, and the one in Portugal about 3.3.

This report is the one that decides the September tone. A headline at 5.3 with the core at 5.3 is a slower decline than the bank had hoped, and the council will have to choose between another increase and a pause. The council meets on 14 September, and the market sees about a 50 per cent chance of a pause.
