China's exports rose by 14.8 per cent over the year in March to 383.8 billion dollars, far above the forecast of about 5 per cent decline. The imports fell by 1.4 per cent to 227.4 billion, and the trade surplus widened to 88.2 billion. The rebound follows four months of the falling exports. The figure compared with a fall of about 7 per cent in February, and the economists had expected another decline as the American and the European demand cooled. The exports to the Belt and Road partners rose by about 25 per cent, and their share of the total reached about 40.

A base effect did part of the work. The comparison is with March 2022, when the lockdown in Shanghai had started to disrupt the ports and the shipments. The two-year average growth is about 7 per cent, which is the cleaner read and still a recovery. The lockdown of Shanghai began at the end of March 2022, and the port congestion lasted through the spring. The exports in the first quarter rose by about 0.5 per cent over the year, which is the figure that the government has been quoting.

Destinations were broad. The exports to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations rose by about 35 per cent, and the ones to the European Union by about 3. The shipments to the United States fell by about 10, and the ones to Russia rose by about 140 as the trade shifted. The shipments to Japan fell by about 5 per cent, and the ones to Korea by about 8 as the electronics cycle turned. The trade with Russia has doubled since the invasion of Ukraine, and the cars and the electronics are the goods that the sanctions have redirected.

Products were uneven. The exports of the electric vehicles rose by about 120 per cent, and the ones of the lithium batteries by about 80. The smartphones fell, and the furniture and the toys fell by about 10 as the American demand cooled. The exports of the cars rose to about 400,000 in the quarter, and the electric ones were about a third of the total. The machinery rose by about 15 per cent, and the textiles fell by about 12 as the orders moved to the cheaper producers.

Imports were weak. The imports of the semiconductors fell by about 20 per cent, and the ones of the iron ore by about 5 as the steel mills ran below the capacity. The domestic demand is the part that the reopening has not lifted yet. The imports of the crude oil rose by about 12 per cent in volume, and the ones of the copper fell by about 8 as the construction slowed. The imports from the United States fell by about 5 per cent, and the ones from the European Union by about 3.

Customs officials said the orders from the spring trade fairs had been stronger than last year. A surplus of 88 billion dollars is a surplus that the trading partners will notice, and the notice is the part that the exporters cannot control. The customs office said the exports in April would be weaker, and the base of comparison becomes harder from the summer. A surplus of 88 billion dollars in one month is a number that the trade partners will cite, and the exporters have no way to spend it down.
