Container ship Dali struck a support pier of the Francis Scott Key Bridge in Baltimore at twenty-eight minutes past one in the morning on the twenty-sixth of March, after its crew reported a loss of power and propulsion and issued a mayday call. Bridge, a steel truss span of about two and a half kilometres that carried Interstate 695 over the Patapsco River, collapsed within seconds, and most of it fell into the water. Six construction workers who had been repairing potholes on the deck were killed.

Sequence of events was recorded by the ship's data recorder and by the calls that the pilot made to the coast guard. Crew asked for tugs minutes before the impact, and the pilot ordered the anchor dropped, which slowed the vessel but did not stop it. Police officers who had been notified stopped traffic on both approaches within about ninety seconds, which prevented a larger loss of life during the morning rush, and the two officers were later honoured for it.

Cause of the power loss is the central question of the investigation. Ship had experienced electrical problems in port before departure, and the investigation by the National Transportation Safety Board focused on the transformers and breakers that supply the propulsion system. Board said in its preliminary report that the vessel had suffered two separate blackouts in the minutes before the collision, and it issued recommendations about the way the bridge was protected against ship strikes.

Bridge itself had been built in 1977 for the ships of its own decade, and the vessels that pass beneath it have grown far larger since. It had a protective system around its piers, which was designed to absorb the impact of a smaller vessel, and the Dali displaced about a hundred thousand tonnes. Engineers said the collapse raised questions about the thousands of bridges that carry traffic over commercial waterways, and the federal agency that oversees them began a review of the most vulnerable spans.

Port of Baltimore is a specific kind of infrastructure. It handles more cars and light trucks than any other port in the United States, along with farm equipment and construction machinery, and it employs tens of thousands of people. Channel was blocked by wreckage for eleven weeks, and the government reopened a limited channel for some traffic within four weeks. Shipping was diverted to other ports on the east coast, and the effect on national supply chains was smaller than early estimates had suggested.

Cost and responsibility became a political argument. Rebuilding the bridge will cost between 1.7 and 1.9 billion dollars, and the federal government said it would pay for the whole replacement, which drew objections from some members of Congress who wanted the cost shared with the ship's owner and insurer. Legislation passed in the autumn authorized the funding, and the first contracts for the design were awarded in the summer.

Consequence of the twenty-sixth of March is a lesson about the systems that carry goods. A bridge that had stood for forty-seven years was destroyed by a single failure on a ship that had already reported a problem, and the loss of the port lasted longer than the loss of the bridge. What the investigation will establish is who knew what before the departure and whether the protections that were meant to prevent the collapse were adequate. What the collapse established is that the infrastructure of trade depends on assumptions about the size of ships and the strength of the structures they pass under, assumptions that had not been updated.
