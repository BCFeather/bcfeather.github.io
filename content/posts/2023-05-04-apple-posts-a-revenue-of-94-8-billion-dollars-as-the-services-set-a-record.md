description: "The quarter to March beat the estimates on the profit, while the revenue fell for a second quarter and the iPhone and the services held up."
---

Apple reported a revenue of 94.8 billion dollars for the quarter to March, down 3 per cent, and a profit of 24.2 billion. The earnings per share of 1.52 dollars beat the forecast of 1.43. The revenue was about 2 billion above the estimate, and the shares rose about 2 per cent after the close. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 5 percentage points from the growth. The revenue in the Americas fell by about 1 per cent, and the one in China by about 3, while the one in Europe rose by about 1.

IPhone revenue held up. The revenue from the phones was 51.3 billion, up about 2 per cent, and the company said the demand in the emerging markets had offset the slower upgrade cycle in the West. The Pro models took a larger share, and the average selling price rose. The company has been offering the trade-in credits and the instalment plans, and the finance arm now covers about 40 per cent of the sales in the United States. The company said the installed base of the iPhones had reached a record, and the upgrades were running ahead of the previous cycle in China.

Services set a record for the quarter. The revenue was 20.9 billion dollars, up about 5 per cent, and the gross margin on it was about 71 per cent. The subscriptions reached about 2 billion across the services, and the company said the advertising and the cloud were the fastest-growing parts. The App Store revenue rose by about 6 per cent, and the one from the advertising by about 20 as the search placements expanded.

Macs and iPads fell. The Mac revenue fell by about 31 per cent to 7.2 billion as the channel worked through the stock of the previous models, and the iPad fell by about 13. The wearables fell by about 1 per cent, and the company said the AirPods had grown. The company said the wearables had been hurt by the launch timing, and the new models would come in the autumn.

Buybacks were raised again, with the board authorizing 90 billion dollars. The board authorized about 90 billion dollars of the repurchases, which is the same as the previous year, and the dividend rose by 4 per cent. The company has about 166 billion dollars of the cash, and the net cash position was about 60 billion. The company said it would keep the buyback at the current pace while the cash generation holds.

This is the second consecutive quarter of declining revenue. A company that grows the services by 5 per cent while the hardware falls is a company whose value is shifting to the subscription, and the shift is the one the margin shows. The installed base of the devices reached about 2 billion, and the services attach to it is the measure the investors watch.
