title: "Europe's gas storage is full while prices stay nervous"
date: "2024-11-16T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "Tanks are at about ninety-five per cent as winter begins, and a market that remembers two winters of scarcity is still pricing the risk of a cold spell."
---

European storage sites entered November close to capacity, a level that in a normal year would be a comfortable position, and the wholesale price for next-month delivery was still around forty-five euros per megawatt hour. That is a price that reflects a memory rather than a stock, because a continent with full tanks should not be paying a premium for gas it already owns. A tank that is full in November is a tank that can supply the winter, and it is not a contract for next winter. A winter that begins with full tanks begins with a choice about how much to hold back.

Memory has two parts. The first is the winter of 2022, when storage was low and the price went to levels that forced governments to subsidize bills. The second is the loss of the pipeline that carried Russian gas across Ukraine, a route that will stop at the end of the year when the transit contract expires and is not expected to be renewed. A crisis that forced governments to subsidize bills is a crisis that a voter remembers at the next election.

Remaining routes are narrower. Pipeline capacity from Norway is at its limit, liquefied gas arrives at terminals that were built or expanded in a hurry, and the marginal molecule is priced against a global market where Asian buyers compete. A cold spell in one region raises the price in another, and the arbitrage is fast. A terminal that was expanded in a hurry is a terminal with a long-term contract attached. A cargo that is diverted to Asia is a cargo that Europe has to replace at a higher price.

Industrial demand has already been destroyed. Factories that use gas as a feedstock reduced output during the crisis and some of them have not restarted, which is why the storage is full: the consumption that would have drawn it down no longer exists. A full tank can be a sign of weak demand as much as of good preparation. A factory that shut a furnace is a factory that does not reopen it because the price fell.

Policy question is what happens after the transit stops. A country that borders the route, such as Slovakia or Austria, loses a supply that its contracts assumed, and the replacement is a combination of storage, liquefied cargoes and connections to neighbours. The interconnection is the part that makes the loss survivable. A country that loses a route is a country that has to buy from a neighbour at the neighbour's price.

What the market is pricing is the tail. A winter that is mild ends with storage still full and prices falling, and a winter with a long cold snap ends with withdrawals that exceed the refill capacity before spring. The premium is the cost of not knowing which one it will be. A premium that reflects a tail risk is a premium that disappears in a mild March.
