Global growth will settle at 2.6 per cent this year, the World Bank said on 11 June, an upgrade from the 2.4 per cent it forecast in January, and the pace will remain below the average of the decade before the pandemic. It described the outlook as the weakest in thirty years outside a recession. The bank's economists said the upgrade came from the strength of the American consumer and from the resilience of the service sector, both of which had been underestimated in January. The bank's president said the report described a world in which the poorest countries are being left behind, and he used the phrase to argue for a larger capital base.

Distribution is the point. The forecast for the richest economies was raised, and the forecast for the poorest was cut, and the bank said that a quarter of the developing countries would be poorer at the end of the year than they were before it began. An average that improves can conceal a group that does not. The poorest countries also face the cost of adaptation, and the money that they need for it is not available on the terms that they can afford.

Interest bill is the mechanism. The countries that borrowed in dollars when the rate was low now refinance at a rate that is several times higher, and the money that would have gone to schools goes to the bondholders instead. A refinancing at a higher rate is a budget that shrinks without anyone deciding to cut it. The debt service ratio is the number the bank uses to measure this, and it has reached a level in some countries that the finance ministries describe as a permanent constraint.

Trade has not recovered either. The growth of the volume of trade has been slower than the growth of output for two years, which is the opposite of the pattern that the bank's models assume, and the restrictions that the largest economies have imposed are the reason. A world that trades less is a world that grows less. The shipping disruptions in the two canals added to the cost of moving goods, and the bank said the effect was larger on the countries that import their food.

Lending capacity is the constraint on the bank's advice. The institution has been asked to do more with a balance sheet that the shareholders have not enlarged, and the replenishment that it needs is a decision for the governments that fund it. A lender that is asked to rescue more is a lender that has to be given more. The shareholders have been discussing a larger capital increase for two years, and the countries that would pay for it are the ones whose budgets are also under pressure.

What the report shows is that the recovery from the pandemic is complete in the statistics of the rich world and incomplete in the statistics of the poor, and the difference is now the main subject of the institutions that forecast. A forecast that is revised upward while the poorest are revised down is a forecast about divergence, and divergence is the harder problem for the institutions than the average.
