description: "The quarter to March grew by 2 per cent on the year, with the Chinese commerce flat and the cloud losing ground, and the company set out the six units it will separate."
---

Alibaba reported a revenue of 208.2 billion yuan for the quarter to March, up 2 per cent, and a net profit of 23.5 billion. The revenue was slightly below the estimate, and the shares fell about 3 per cent in the American session. The company said the year to March had been the one of the restructuring. The profit was about 4 billion yuan above the estimate, and the adjusted earnings per share beat the forecast. The company said the number of the employees had fallen by about 10,000 in the quarter, the fifth consecutive decline.

Chinese commerce was flat on the year. The revenue from the Taobao and the Tmall was about 140 billion yuan, down about 3 per cent, as the consumers spent less on the discretionary goods. The number of the active users rose, and the company said the loyalty programme had kept the higher-spending customers. The direct sales and the fresh food business fell by about 10 per cent, and the company said it was reducing the loss-making parts. The customer management revenue, which is the advertising the merchants buy, fell by about 3 per cent.

Cloud lost ground. The revenue fell by about 2 per cent to 18.5 billion yuan, and the company blamed the loss of a large customer and the delays in the projects. The management said the AI demand was the part that would turn the unit around, and the listing of the cloud is planned within a year. The company said it had begun the process of the separate listing of the cloud, and the timetable was about 12 months.

International commerce grew. The revenue rose by about 29 per cent, and the company said the orders from the European buyers had doubled. The loss in the segment widened as the company spent on the logistics and the marketing, and the management said the investment would continue. The revenue from the international retail was about 15 billion yuan, and the one from the wholesale about 5.

A split into six units was the other news of the quarter. The company set out six units, with the commerce, the cloud, the logistics, the local services, the media and the international each to be run with its own board. The plan includes the separate listings of the cloud and the grocery chain, and the dividends would depend on the unit. The company declared a dividend of about 0.98 dollars per share, which is the first since the listing in 2014.

This is the first quarter since the restructuring was announced. A company that grows by 2 per cent while it separates the units is a company that is betting the parts are worth more than the whole, and the bet is the one the market is pricing. The shares are down about 30 per cent since the peak of 2020, and the split is the answer the management has given to the investors.
