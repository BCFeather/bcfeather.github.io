Simon Harris was confirmed as prime minister by the Dail on the ninth of April, two weeks after Leo Varadkar announced his resignation as leader of Fine Gael. At thirty-seven he is the youngest person to hold the office, and he took over a coalition of Fine Gael, Fianna Fail and the Green Party that has been in power since 2020 and that must face an election by February 2025.

Route to the office was quick and uncontested. Several senior ministers declined to stand after Varadkar's announcement, and Harris was elected party leader on the twenty-fourth of March without a vote among members. He had served as minister for health during the pandemic, where he became the public face of the government's messaging, and then as minister for higher education, a post in which he built a reputation for announcing policies at a pace that his critics described as relentless.

Record he inherits is dominated by housing. Rents in Dublin rose by more than a third during the coalition's term, and the number of new homes built each year, while higher than a decade ago, has stayed below the government's targets. Homelessness reached record levels, with more than thirteen thousand people in emergency accommodation, including several thousand children. The opposition parties have made housing the centre of their campaigns and have argued that the government's schemes for first-time buyers only pushed prices higher.

Political situation is a coalition that has lost ground in by-elections and in two referendums held in March. Both referendum proposals, on the definition of family and on the role of women in the home, were defeated, which was read as a sign that the government had lost touch with parts of its own electorate. Harris said after taking office that he would listen more and announce less, which is a change of style that his colleagues noted.

Agenda he set out in his first weeks was deliberately small. He promised to speed up the construction of housing on state land, to reduce the cost of childcare and to complete the reforms to the health service that had been started by his predecessor. He also said the government would serve its full term, and he repeated that commitment after the local and European elections in June, when the parties lost seats but held their share of the vote.

Consequence of the ninth of April is a government with a new face and the same record. Harris is a better communicator than Varadkar, and his age is an asset in a country where the opposition parties are led by figures who have been in politics longer. The problems that produced the coalition's unpopularity have not changed, and an election that must be held within ten months will be fought on them.

What the transition bought was time, and what it did not buy was a new argument. The Dail confirmed him by a majority that included independents, and the coalition's junior partners said they would serve under him for the rest of the term.
