Baku rewards straight-line speed and punishes the walls, and the layout makes a clean pole worth more than at most circuits on the calendar. Verstappen took it with a lap of 1 minute 41.117 seconds, and from there the race became a question of whether anyone could follow him rather than of whether he could hold the lead. The first sector is a sequence of right angles through the old town, and the long straight that follows rewards a low-drag setup that teams run nowhere else.

He led all 51 laps. Russell finished second, 14.609 seconds behind, and Sainz came through to third for Williams, a result the team had not managed at any earlier round of the season. The gap understates the race: once the leaders settled, the field behind spread out and the podium places were decided by pace instead of by incidents. Russell's gap grew steadily after the first stops, which points at a pace difference. Sainz held third under pressure for the whole final stint.

The final classification gives the shape of it. The race took 1 hour 33 minutes 26.408 seconds, an average speed of 196.520 kilometres per hour over a circuit where average speed is usually lower than at a permanent track of the same length. The fastest lap was 1.43.388 on lap 50, an average of 209.026 kilometres per hour for that tour. Those figures come from the official classification, which times the leader across the line and averages the distance over the race.

Piastri's race ended on the first lap in a crash, which left one car at the front of the field for a team that had arrived with both drivers in contention for the podium. A street circuit punishes a small mistake with a retirement instead of a lost place, and that is the risk a team accepts when it sets up for low downforce. The second car was carrying the team's hopes on a day when constructors' points were all that remained to defend.

The win was Verstappen's 67th in Formula 1, and the manner of it completed a grand slam: pole position, victory, fastest lap and every lap led. The statistic is a convention rather than a championship point, and it is one that a driver can only collect on a day when both the car and the circuit cooperate. Only a few drivers have collected six, and the list is short because the feat needs a dominant car and a clean weekend at once.

One race on a street circuit is weak evidence about form at the next round. Baku's walls and its long straight reward a car that is quick in a straight line, which is not the same as a car that is quick through a medium-speed corner, and the teams left with aero packages built for one track. What travels to the next round is the car's behaviour in slow corners, and Baku's layout does not test that.
