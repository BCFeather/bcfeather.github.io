Cisco published results for the quarter to April on 18 May, and revenue was 12.8 billion dollars, unchanged on the year. Net income was 3.3 billion, and earnings per share were 73 cents against the 71 forecast. The company cut its revenue guidance for the July quarter to a fall of 1 to 3 per cent, and the shares fell 11 per cent. Its product revenue fell 3 per cent, and its service revenue rose 5.

Supply was the reason. The company said it had 3 billion dollars of orders it could not fill, and that the lockdowns in Shanghai had stopped production at its contract manufacturers. It said the shortage of components would last into 2023, and that its backlog had doubled in a year. The subscription business grew. Revenue from recurring sources was 8.2 billion dollars, which is 83 per cent of the total, and it rose 5 per cent. The company said it had 3.8 billion dollars of remaining performance obligations, up 8 per cent. Its hardware revenue fell 3 per cent. Analysts had expected revenue of 13.2 billion dollars, and the shortfall was 400 million.

Margins held up. The gross margin was 64.4 per cent, and it had been 64.2 a year earlier. The company said it had raised prices to cover the cost of components, and that it had bought chips on the spot market at three times the contract price. Its operating margin was 33 per cent. Deferred revenue was 20 billion dollars, up 8 per cent on the year.

It said 40 per cent of its revenue came from security, cloud and collaboration, and that it would divest its hardware businesses that did not fit. It also said it would buy back 10 billion dollars of stock in the year. Its chief executive said the demand was there and the supply was not. The company said it would spend 1 billion dollars to hold more inventory.

Analysts said the guidance was the problem rather than the quarter. The shares had fallen 30 per cent in the year before the results, and they fell to 42 dollars from 47. The company's value was 180 billion dollars, and it was 10 per cent below its level of 2019. It said it would divest its cable business, and that it had hired advisers. Free cash flow was 3.2 billion dollars in the quarter, and it held 20 billion in cash.
