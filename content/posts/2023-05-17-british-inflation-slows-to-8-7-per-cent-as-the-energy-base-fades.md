title: "British inflation slows to 8.7 per cent as the energy base fades"
date: "2023-05-17T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The April consumer prices fell from 10.1 per cent, but the core measure rose to 6.8 per cent as the services and the wages kept the pressure."
---

British consumer prices rose by 8.7 per cent over the year to April, down from 10.1 per cent in March and above the forecast of 8.3. The fall is the first below 10 per cent since the summer of 2022. The core measure rose to 6.8 per cent from 6.2, which is the highest since 1992. The monthly change was 0.8 per cent, and the figure is the first below 10 per cent since August 2022.

An energy base effect did the work. The regulator raised the cap on the household bills in April 2022, so the price of the electricity and the gas stopped rising this year. The energy contributed about minus 1.5 percentage points to the change, and the effect will fade again from July. The cap was raised by about 54 per cent in April 2022, and it was raised again by 20 per cent in October of that year.

Core went the other way. The services prices rose by about 6.9 per cent, and the one of the recreation and the culture by about 8. The air fares rose by about 12 over the month as the Easter holidays fell in April, and the package holidays by about 6. The services measure has been above 6 per cent for six months, and the bank has said it is the one that matters for the target.

Food is still the highest line. The food and the drink rose by 19.1 per cent over the year, and the prices of the bread and the cereals by about 19. The vegetables have come down from the winter shortage, and the milk fell by about 1 per cent on the month. The prices of the oils and the fats fell by about 5 per cent over the month, and the one of the sugar rose by about 3.

Markets reacted strongly. The traders raised the odds of another increase in June to about 90 per cent, and the two-year gilt yield rose by about 20 basis points. The pound rose by about 0.4 per cent against the dollar, and the mortgage rates followed the swap rates higher. The mortgage market has been repricing since the mini-budget of the autumn, and the average two-year fix is about 5.5 per cent. The average five-year fix was about 5 per cent, and the remortgaging in the spring has added about 200 pounds a month to the typical payment.

This report is the one that the bank said would decide the June meeting. A core at 6.8 per cent while the headline falls is the pattern the bank has been warning about, and the warning is now the data. The committee meets in June, and the governor has said the bank would act if the pressure persisted. The bank's own measure of the underlying pay pressure was about 7 per cent in the spring, and the wage settlements of the public sector are still being negotiated.
