title: "Venezuela's election authority declares a winner without tallies"

description: "The government claimed a narrow victory while the opposition produced receipts from the voting machines that pointed to a large one, and the region has split over which to believe."
---

Electoral officials in Venezuela declared the incumbent president the winner of the election held on 28 July, with about 51 per cent of the vote, and did not publish the tally sheets from the individual polling stations that would allow the result to be checked. The opposition said its receipts from a large share of the machines showed a lead of more than twenty points. The turnout figure that the authority published was also disputed, because the opposition's receipts implied a higher number of voters than the official count.

Receipts are the evidence. In a system that prints a paper record for every voter, the parties are entitled to the copies, and the opposition published what it collected. A result that cannot be verified from the paper is a result that the loser will not accept, and the argument is about arithmetic that both sides can see. The opposition collected the sheets through volunteers who photographed them at the machines, and the collection was organized within hours of the close of voting.

Regional response was fast and divided. Several governments in the hemisphere refused to recognize the result, and others in the region accepted it, and the split followed the existing alignments rather than the evidence. A dispute that is settled by alignment is a dispute in which the facts matter less than the alliances. Sanctions and recognition are the levers that the hemisphere has, and neither of them changes a count that the government controls.

The economy is the reason the vote mattered. A country that has been through hyperinflation and emigration is a country whose voters were deciding about food and medicine as much as about politics, and the government's campaign promised stability. A promise of stability is a promise that a currency board or a subsidy can keep for a while. Emigration had removed millions of working-age people, and the remittances they send back are a larger share of the economy than the oil industry.

Security services were the instrument of the aftermath. Protesters were arrested, a rival candidate's headquarters were surrounded, and the government described the demonstrations as a foreign-backed attempt at a coup. A state that answers an election dispute with arrests is a state that has decided the argument is over. The attorney general's office announced charges against the opposition candidate within days, and the charges were described as an attempt to remove him from the argument.

What the election shows is that a vote is not the same as a count. A country that holds an election and does not publish the records has held a ritual rather than a contest, and the region will be arguing about the difference for years. A government that does not publish the records has decided that the count is a political asset rather than a public one, and the decision will be tested at the next election.
