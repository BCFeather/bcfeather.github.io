April is the month when the pollen arrives, and for a large and growing number of people it is the month of itchy eyes, a blocked nose and a headache that arrives at the same hour every morning. The allergy is a misdirected immune response to a protein in the pollen of a wind-pollinated plant, and the plants that cause it are the ones that release enormous quantities of light pollen into the air, the plane trees, the birches, the poplars and the grasses.

The measurement of it is more sophisticated than most people assume. Cities that maintain a pollen monitoring station draw air through a trap, count the grains under a microscope or a flow cytometer, and publish a daily index. The index is a count of grains per cubic metre, and it is only as useful as its location, because a station on a roof measures a different air from a street lined with the trees that are producing the pollen. A person who checks the index and then walks down a avenue of planes will have a worse day than the index predicted.

The treatments are old and effective. An antihistamine taken before the exposure works better than one taken after it, a nasal steroid spray takes a week to reach its effect and is the treatment that the guidelines recommend for a persistent case, and a mask that fits reduces the dose substantially. The folk remedies, the local honey and the nettle tea, have been tested and do not work beyond the placebo, which is a strong effect and not a reason to rely on it.

What has changed is the length of the season. The observations that the monitoring networks have accumulated show the start of the pollen season moving earlier and the end moving later across the temperate zones, and the total load rising, because a warmer atmosphere holds more carbon dioxide, the plants grow faster, and a longer growing season produces more flowering. The effect is largest for the grasses and the ragweed, and it is measurable in the counts rather than inferred from the temperature.

In cities the cause comes down to a planning decision. A city that plants only male trees of a species to avoid the fruit and the litter has planted the trees that produce the pollen, because the female trees are the ones that produce the seed and the fruit and trap some of the pollen on their surfaces. The imbalance that this creates is documented in several cities, and the correction is to plant a mix, which is a slower and more expensive instruction for a landscaping contractor.

What April does, then, is convert a botanical fact into a public health one. The pollen is a natural product, the allergy is an immune response that the modern environment has made more common, and the season is being extended by a climate that the city has some influence over and no control of. The person with the blocked nose is at the end of a chain that begins with a tree.
