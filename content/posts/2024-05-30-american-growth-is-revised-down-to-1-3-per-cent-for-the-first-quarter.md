American output grew at an annual rate of 1.3 per cent in the first quarter, according to the second estimate published on 30 May, down from the 1.6 per cent reported a month earlier. The revision was the largest downward adjustment in more than a year. The revision was the second of three, and the third estimate in June is expected to confirm the picture rather than change it.

Composition explains most of it. Imports were revised up, which subtracts from the calculation, and the buildup in inventories was smaller than first reported, while consumer spending was revised slightly higher. A revision that comes from trade and from stocks is a revision about the measurement rather than about the demand. The trade deficit widened as importers brought goods in ahead of the tariffs that were announced later in May, which is a timing effect that will reverse in the next quarter.

Consumption held up. Households spent at an annual rate of two per cent, which is slower than the pace of last year and still enough to keep the services sector expanding, and the saving rate was revised down. A consumer who is saving less is a consumer whose spending cannot continue at the same rate. The services sector grew faster than the goods sector, which is the pattern of an economy in which the household spends on experiences rather than on objects.
