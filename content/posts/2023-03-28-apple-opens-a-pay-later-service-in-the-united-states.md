description: "Eligible users could split a purchase into four payments over six weeks, with no fees and a soft credit check."

Apple Pay Later lets a customer split a purchase of between 50 and 1,000 dollars into four payments over six weeks. The service was announced on 28 March and was available to a random group of users at first. The first payment is taken at checkout and the rest every two weeks. There is no interest and no fee, and the company said it would report late payments to the credit agencies. The service was limited to purchases made with the company's card or with the wallet in shops that accept it.

Lending is done by a subsidiary set up in 2022, which is a licensed lender rather than a technology provider. That structure puts the credit risk on the company's own balance sheet, which is unusual for a phone maker. The company said the subsidiary would fund the loans and that it had set aside capital for losses. The subsidiary had 100 employees at launch, and it reported to the company's finance chief.

Users can see a calendar of upcoming payments and can pay early without penalty. The service was integrated into the wallet rather than into a separate app. The application is a soft credit check, which does not affect a credit score, and the decision is made in seconds. The wallet had been available since 2014, and the company said 90 per cent of its users had linked a card. Card networks and buy-now-pay-later firms offer competing services. Those firms charge merchants about 4 per cent, and the company said it would charge merchants nothing at first. That subsidy is the reason analysts said the product would be expensive to run at scale. The card networks had launched similar products in 2021, and the largest had 20 million users.

Announced in June 2022, the service had been delayed twice, once because of engineering and once because of the credit assessment. It was only available in the United States, and the company said it would consider other markets later. Its wallet had 500 million users, and the company said the service would be offered to a small share of them. The delay in 2022 was reported as a change in the engineering approach, and the company did not comment.

Regulators had been examining the sector since the previous year, and the consumer finance agency had said it would treat the firms as lenders. That guidance, published in 2024, required the firms to provide the same disclosures as credit cards. The company's subsidiary was licensed in the state where it was incorporated, and it was subject to state rules. The agency's guidance covered the disclosure of fees and the handling of disputes. The company's services revenue was 85 billion dollars for the year, and the wallet was part of it.

Expansion to all users came in the autumn, and the company said it would report the volume with the wallet figures. The buy-now-pay-later market was estimated at 100 billion dollars in the United States, and the four largest firms held about 60 per cent of it. The company shut the service down in 2024 and replaced it with a partnership with a bank.
