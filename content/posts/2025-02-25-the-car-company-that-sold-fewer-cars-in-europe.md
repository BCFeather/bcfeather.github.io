The registrations of one electric car brand in Europe fell by about half in January compared with the same month a year earlier, and the fall was sharpest in the largest markets. The figure is published by the industry associations that compile the registration data, and it is a hard number that the analysts can check rather than a claim from a press release.

The model cycle explains part of it. The brand's best-selling vehicle was due for a refresh, the factories that build it were re-tooling, and a buyer who knows that a new version is coming waits. A production change of that kind produces a dip in the registrations two quarters before it produces a rise, and the dip is visible in the monthly data.

The price explains another part. The brand cut the list price of several models in several markets in the preceding year, and the resale value of the cars that were already on the road fell with it. A buyer who is considering a car with a weak resale value asks for a larger discount, and the discount is a cost that the manufacturer bears.

The reputation explains the rest and it is harder to measure. The chief executive has become a political figure in some of the markets where the cars are sold, and the surveys that measure the consideration of a brand show a change that the sales data confirms. The effect is uneven by country, which is why the fall is larger in some markets than in others.

The competition has also changed. The European market now offers a large number of electric models at the price point that the brand once occupied alone, and the buyer who wanted an electric car in 2021 had few alternatives and the buyer in 2025 has many. The share that the brand held was a share that the absence of rivals protected.

The company's response is a set of new models at lower prices and a cheaper version of the volume car, and the plan that it presented to the investors depends on the new cars arriving on time. The factories in Europe and in China are both involved, and the tariff that the European Union imposed on the Chinese-built cars is a cost in the plan.

What the fall shows is that a car is sold on three things at once. The product cycle, the price and the reputation of the maker are separate variables, and a quarter in which all three move in the wrong direction produces a number like the one in January. The registrations are the record of that quarter, and the record is the only part the industry can agree on. The company will report its own figures in a few weeks and the two sets of numbers will be close, because the registration data is compiled from the licensing offices rather than from the marketing department, and the licensing offices have no reason to round the number in either direction.
