Nanning's 22nd China-ASEAN Expo and the associated business and investment summit closed on 21 September after five days. Organizers reported 3,260 firms from 60 countries and regions, a floor of 160,000 square metres split between the main venue in Nanning and a tourism section in Guilin, and cumulative attendance of 226,000 visits, a record for the event and about a fifth more than the previous high.

Numbers of that kind are standard for the expo, which has run since 2004 and functions as the region's annual trade fair and diplomatic occasion at once. What distinguished this edition was the decision to make artificial intelligence the centrepiece. A hall of 10,000 square metres, the largest single themed hall in the expo's history, held roughly 1,200 exhibits: some 20 large models, 60 robots, 520 AI devices and 230 digital service platforms, with about 200 companies taking space, including 432 firms the organizers classify as leading enterprises, up 7.5 per cent on last year.

Trade in that hall was reported at 140 million yuan, a modest figure against the floor's cost and meaningful as a signal that buyers exist for the products on show. Forty-four projects described as AI-plus were signed, covering manufacturing, services and agriculture, and the organizers published a list of 100 Chinese AI firms they expect to expand into Southeast Asian markets. Delegations from the ASEAN states used the week to sign agreements on models adapted to local languages and to local industrial data, and a ministerial forum on AI ran for the first time alongside the trade programme.

Trade policy formed the other half of the programme. The upgraded China-ASEAN free trade agreement, version 3.0, was the subject of the opening session and of a good share of the 88 side events; negotiators have finished the text, and the signing is expected before the end of the year, which is why the fair's programme read as a preview of the rules rather than a review of the trade. The outline covers digital trade, green investment and supply chains, which are the areas where the existing agreement of 2010 had least to say.

For the exhibitors the arithmetic is simpler than the closing statement. A buyer from Guangxi signed 5 million yuan of orders for durian oil on the first day; a Vietnamese coffee brand that has attended for 22 years reported rising sales and credited the rules of origin and paperless customs under the regional agreement. These are the transactions the fair exists to produce, and they are what the attendance figure measures, rather than the number of robots that danced.

Next year's edition is scheduled for 17 to 21 September, with the Philippines as the country of honour and Changchun as the Chinese city in focus. Whether AI remains the theme or returns to being one exhibit among many will be the thing to watch, because a first hall is easy to build and a market is not.
