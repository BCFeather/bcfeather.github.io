Alphabet reported a profit of 19.7 billion dollars for the third quarter on 24 October, up from 13.9 billion a year earlier, on the revenue of 76.7 billion, which rose 11 per cent. The shares fell about 6 per cent in the after-hours trade as the cloud missed the forecast. The revenue was above the forecast of 75.9 billion, and the operating margin rose to 28 per cent from 25 a year earlier, which the finance chief called the result of the cost work. The search business is still about 60 per cent of the revenue, and the company has said the advertising on the maps and the shopping is growing faster than the search itself.

Advertising did the work. The search revenue rose by about 11 per cent and the YouTube advertising by about 12, the fastest since the start of last year, as the retailers returned to the spending. An advertiser that returns is a quarter that beats. The retail advertising grew fastest, and the travel and the finance were the weakest, which reflects the spending of the households on the services.

Cloud disappointed. The revenue rose by about 22 per cent to 8.4 billion, below the 8.6 that the analysts had expected, and the unit made a small operating profit for the second quarter in a row. A cloud that grows slower than the rivals is a cloud that the investors discount. The cloud is the third largest behind Amazon and Microsoft, and the company has said it will keep investing in the data centers that the AI customers need. The company said the cloud's operating margin was about 3 per cent, and the unit is still small against the scale of the advertising business.

Costs are under control. The company has cut about 12,000 jobs since the start of the year, and the head count is about 4 per cent lower than a year earlier, which is the reason the margin rose. A payroll that is smaller is a profit that is larger. The company said the head count would rise again next year in the technical roles, and the pace of the hiring will be the test of the discipline.

AI spending is the question. The capital spending was 8.1 billion in the quarter and is expected to rise next year as the data centers are built for the models, and the investors have been asking when the spending will pay. A data center that is built is a cost that arrives before the revenue. The capital spending has risen by about 30 per cent this year, and the guidance for next year is above the 30 billion that the analysts had modeled.

What the quarter shows is that the advertising has recovered from the slowdown and the cloud has not, and the difference is the reason the shares fell despite the profit. An advertising business at 11 per cent is a business that has recovered, and the recovery is what the market had already priced.
