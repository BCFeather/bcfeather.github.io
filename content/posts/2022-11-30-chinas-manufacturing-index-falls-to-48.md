Manufacturing activity fell to 48.0 in November, the National Bureau of Statistics reported on 30 November. The reading was down from 49.2 in October and the second month below 50. The index has been below 50 for five of the eleven months of the year. The index has been below 50 in nine of the twelve months since January 2022. The figure was the lowest since April, when the city of Shanghai was in lockdown.

New orders were 46.4, the weakest since April, and new export orders were 46.7. Production was 47.8, and inventories of finished goods rose, which means that factories were producing more than they could sell. The statisticians said the restrictions had disrupted logistics and had cut demand. The services index was 46.7, down from 48.7, and it was the weakest since the spring. The catering and entertainment components fell most, and transport was flat. The survey covers 4,000 firms, and it asks whether activity rose or fell compared with the previous month. The delivery time index fell to 44, and the inventory index rose to 48.

Several large cities reported record numbers of cases, and the government maintained its policy. The restrictions had tightened in November. The number of cities under full lockdown was 30 at the end of November, and the ports reported delays. The survey's reading for delivery times fell to 44. The private survey, published by Caixin, came in at 49.4 and was stronger than the official one. The gap between the two is usual, because the official survey covers more large state-owned firms and the private one covers more small exporters. Both were below 50. The services index was 46.7, and the construction index was 55. The private survey from Caixin came in at 49.4, and its export orders component was 47.

Measures had been announced in the autumn. It had cut the quarantine period for arrivals from seven days to five, and it had reduced the number of PCR tests required. It had also promised 1 trillion yuan of infrastructure spending, and it had cut taxes for small firms. The quarantine period for arrivals was cut from seven days to five in November.

Reopening was announced on 7 December, and the index rebounded to 51 in January. The rebound was driven by the return of consumption rather than by investment, and the manufacturing index stayed below 51 through the spring. The property market did not recover, and the construction index stayed below 50. The rebound was led by services, and manufacturing recovered more slowly.

Growth for the year was 3 per cent, half the official target. The government said in December that it would focus on growth in 2023, and it set a target of 5 per cent. The economy grew 5.2 per cent in 2023, and the property market remained the largest drag.
