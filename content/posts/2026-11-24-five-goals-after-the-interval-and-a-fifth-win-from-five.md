Real Madrid scored five goals in 37 minutes after the interval to beat PSV Eindhoven 5-1, and the interval itself is the part worth reading. The home side had won its first four matches of the league phase; the visitors had arrived with a pressing plan that held for 45 minutes. A 5-1 scoreline usually describes a mismatch. This one describes two halves, and the second half was where a bench changed the terms of the game.

Pressing high from the first whistle, PSV won the ball in the areas where Madrid wants to build. Sven Mijnans and Ruben van Bommel each had a chance before the quarter-hour, and the home crowd heard the first whistles early. Madrid's rhythm never settled, and its best moment of the half was a Vinícius Júnior shot that came off the crossbar. The goal, when it came, was a penalty that Ivan Perišić converted in the 32nd minute.

The reply took six minutes of the second half. Arda Güler played the ball through the middle for Federico Valverde to finish at the far post, and the equalizer on 50 minutes changed the temperature of the ground. Jude Bellingham, one of the interval substitutions, put the home side ahead on the hour with a volley from the edge of the area that Matěj Kovář did not move for. PSV's midfield, so compact before the break, was now chasing the ball from side to side.

Kylian Mbappé made the third at 64 minutes, and PSV's resistance ended there. He added a fourth at 84 and Vinícius completed the scoring at 87, which is five goals in 37 minutes from a team that had not scored in the first 45. Mbappé took the player-of-the-match award with two of them, and the record now reads five wins from five with three matches left, enough to put Madrid top of the 36-team table.

PSV's first half is the part the scoreline will bury. Peter Bosz had set his team to press a home side that wants to play out from the back, and for 45 minutes the press produced turnovers in the areas where Madrid is most exposed. Both early chances came from that source. The penalty came from a defensive error instead, which is the sort of detail a coach keeps when the next opponent defends deeper.

What the result cannot settle is whether the second half was a correction or a collapse on the other side. A team that concedes five goals in 37 minutes has usually stopped running, and the visitors' shape after the third goal suggested as much. Their press, which had worked, became a shape that could not absorb a straight pass through the middle. What survives the noise is narrower than the scoreline: a deficit that lasted until the 50th minute, five finishes in 37, and a bench that turned a difficult night into a routine one.
