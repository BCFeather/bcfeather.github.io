The WNK kinases regulate ion transport in the kidney and in the vasculature, and their activity responds to osmotic stress and to chloride. A paper in *eLife* on 25 November 2024 explains how a solute can switch a kinase. Kinases are switches, and a switch that responds to an ion rather than to a phosphate is a switch that a cell can throw without touching a kinase cascade.

The unphosphorylated kinase domain of WNK1 forms an asymmetric dimer, and crystal structures of that state contain water molecules at positions that recur across structures. Conservation of position is the first hint that the waters are functional. Recurring waters in a crystal are a common artefact as well as a possible mechanism, and the paper distinguishes the two by perturbing the solvent. A sensor that reads a solute directly is a sensor that a cell can use without a signalling cascade behind it.

For the same reason, the waters form two networks, and the paper shows that both are part of the mechanism rather than of the lattice. A solute that displaces them changes the dimer. A solute that de-dimerizes a crystal demonstrates sensitivity of the interface but not its physiological ligand, and the gap between the two is bridged by the chloride experiments.

PEG400 applied to crystals induces de-dimerization, which is the structural demonstration that the interface is solvent-sensitive. A crystal that comes apart when the solvent changes is a crystal whose contacts are held by water. Chloride is the ion whose concentration the kidney actually varies, so a structural role for chloride is a role that maps onto a physiological variable.

Chloride is the physiological counterpart. The ion binds where the waters sit and stabilizes the inactive arrangement, so a rise in chloride turns the kinase off and a fall turns it on. Allosteric inhibition by water occupancy is a mechanism that would make the kinase sensitive to osmolality as well as to chloride, and the two inputs are linked in the distal nephron.

Allostery by solvent is the general claim. A kinase whose activity depends on the occupancy of water sites is a kinase the cell can regulate without phosphorylating anything. Dietary salt changes chloride in the tubular fluid, which means the kinase reads the diet without any hormonal intermediary, and that directness is unusual. The tubule measurement is the experiment the structure invites, and an invitation is where a structural paper usually stops.

On the modelling side, the kidney is where the consequence is most direct, since chloride in the distal nephron varies with diet.
