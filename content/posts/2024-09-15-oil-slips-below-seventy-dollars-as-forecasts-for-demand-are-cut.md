Brent crude fell below seventy dollars a barrel on 10 September, the first time it had traded at that level since December 2021, and the futures curve moved into a shape that signals ample supply rather than shortage. The producers' group responded by postponing the increase in output that it had planned for October. The drop came after a month in which the price had fallen by about a fifth, which is the kind of move that forces funds to sell rather than to wait.

Demand is the reason. Two forecasting agencies cut their estimates for the growth in consumption this year, and the Chinese data showed imports falling for several months in a row, which is the market that had driven the previous rally. A forecast that is cut twice in a year is a forecast that the traders stop trusting. The agency that represents the consumers cut its estimate for the growth in demand, and the producers' own research arm cut its number by a similar amount, which is unusual.

Supply is the counterweight. Output from the Americas has been rising faster than the forecasters expected, and the members of the producers' group have been producing above their quotas, which is a pattern that repeats whenever prices are high enough. A cartel that cannot enforce its own limits is a cartel whose limits are a suggestion. The members that have been producing above quota are the ones with spare capacity, and they have little incentive to stop when the price is still above their fiscal needs.

Financial flows matter as much as the barrels. Funds that had been long the market reduced their positions through the summer, and a market with fewer speculative buyers is a market that falls on its own weight when the physical data are soft. A position that is closed is a position that no longer supports the price. A market in which the paper contracts trade below the physical cargoes is a market that the traders expect to be oversupplied, and the curve has been in that shape since August.

Effect on producers is uneven. The budgets of the exporters that need a hundred dollars to balance are under pressure, and the importers that had been paying for subsidies are relieved, which is the transfer that a lower price makes. A price that falls is a tax cut for the countries that buy. The importers in Asia have been buying less as their economies slow, and the refiners have cut runs rather than hold stocks that lose value.

What the move shows is that the market has stopped pricing a shortage and started pricing a surplus, which is a judgment about the next two years rather than the next two weeks. A barrel at seventy dollars is a barrel that the producers' group will defend, and the defence has a limit. The group's next meeting is in December, and the delay it announced is the third postponement of a plan that was supposed to have started in the spring.
