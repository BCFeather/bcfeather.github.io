Quarantine for inbound travelers ended on 8 January, three years after the first lockdown in Wuhan. Inbound travelers no longer had to spend weeks in a designated hotel, and the requirement was reduced to a negative test taken within 48 hours of departure. Airports in Beijing and Shanghai reopened arrival halls that had been closed since 2020, and the first flights from Southeast Asia landed before dawn. The first arrivals came from Bangkok, Singapore and Seoul, and the queues at immigration were short.

Word of the change came in late December, together with the decision to downgrade the disease from a notifiable emergency to a managed infection. Officials said the variant then circulating was milder, and that hospital capacity had been expanded through the autumn. The timing was tied to the lunar new year, which is when families travel. The downgrade also ended the health code requirement on domestic travel, which had been in place since 2020. The change was announced on 26 December and applied to arrivals from the first day of the year.

Outbound travel recovered faster than inbound. Visa applications in the largest cities rose sharply in the first week, and agencies reported queues at passport offices that had not issued new documents at scale since 2019. Airlines restored some routes within days, though the total number of international flights stayed at about a fifth of the 2019 level for most of the winter. Airlines restored 20 routes in January, and the aviation regulator said more would follow in the spring.

Other countries responded unevenly. A dozen imposed testing requirements on arrivals from the country, and a few suspended flights entirely for a period in January. The measures were relaxed through the spring, and by March most had been withdrawn. Tourism authorities in Thailand, Japan and France said they expected Chinese visitors to return in numbers only in the second half. Tourism groups criticised the testing requirements, and several countries dropped them within six weeks.

Reopening the border did not settle the question of how many people would come back. Business travel resumed first, because companies had meetings that could not wait. Family visits followed, and package tours were the slowest, since agencies had let their licenses and staff go during the years of closure. Business travel resumed first, and conference organizers in Shanghai reported bookings at 60 per cent of 2019. Package tours restarted in March, and the first groups went to Thailand and Switzerland.

Domestic travel had already begun to recover in the previous month. The lunar new year figures published in late January showed 2.06 billion trips over forty days, and the international total for January was a fraction of that. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism said the full-year inbound figure would be published in January 2024. The full-year inbound figure, published a year later, was about 20 per cent of the 2019 level. Domestic tourism reached 2 billion trips over the holiday season, which is the number officials cited all spring.
