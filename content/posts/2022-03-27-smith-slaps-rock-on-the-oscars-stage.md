The ninety-fourth Academy Awards were held at the Dolby Theatre in Los Angeles on 27 March, and for the first two hours the ceremony was going according to a plan that had been written to make it shorter. Eight awards had been presented before the broadcast began, the host was a comedian who had been hired to keep the evening moving, and the producers had promised a show that would end before midnight. Chris Rock, presenting the documentary category, made a joke about Jada Pinkett Smith's shaved head and about a sequel to a film in which she had appeared.

Will Smith walked up the steps, struck him across the face with an open hand, and returned to his seat, where he shouted at the stage twice. The audio feed carried the first words and cut the second, and the cameras stayed on the audience, which could not tell whether the moment had been rehearsed. Rock said that it had been the greatest night in the history of television, and the show went on with a presenter who had just been hit by a nominee.

Forty minutes later Smith won the award for best actor for his role in King Richard, and he apologized to the Academy and to the other nominees in a speech that lasted five minutes. He did not apologize to Rock, and the room gave him a standing ovation. The Los Angeles Police Department said afterwards that Rock had declined to file a report, and the Academy said it had asked Smith to leave and that he had refused.

The Academy's board met the following week and opened a disciplinary proceeding. Smith resigned from the organization on 1 April, and the board banned him from its events for ten years, including the ceremony, which he had attended every year since 1996. He issued a statement that described his behaviour as unacceptable, and he apologized to Rock in a video that he posted in July.

Other business that evening was overshadowed and worth recording. CODA won best picture, the first film from a streaming service to take the prize, and Troy Kotsur became the first deaf man to win an acting award. Ariana DeBose won for her role in West Side Story, forty years after Rita Moreno had won for the same part. President Zelensky appeared by video and asked the audience to support his country, and the ceremony observed a moment of silence for the war.

What the slap established was the value of a live moment in an industry that had spent a decade trying to script them. The audience figures rose to about 16 million from 10 million the year before, the clip circulated for weeks, and the questions that followed were about the difference between a joke and an insult, about the duty of a nominee who is also a subject, and about what a broadcast owes to the people watching. The ceremony that had been designed to be shorter became the one that nobody could stop watching.
