BP stopped all its tanker traffic through the Red Sea on 18 December, joining a list of shipping companies that had already suspended the route after a series of attacks on merchant vessels by forces in Yemen. Maersk, which had paused and resumed twice in December, told its customers the following day that every vessel scheduled for the passage would be rerouted around Africa via the Cape of Good Hope. Within a week four of the five largest container lines, whose combined share of the global market is above half, had left the route.

Attacks are launched by the Houthi movement that governs the north of Yemen, and they are aimed at ships the movement links to Israel, though the vessels hit in December included ships owned in Norway, in Germany and in Panama. Drones fired from the coast, missiles fired from pickup trucks and helicopters landing on a car carrier have all been used, and the naval task force assembled by the United States under the name Prosperity Guardian began escorting convoys in the last week of the month.

The economics arrive through two channels. The Suez route carries about twelve per cent of world trade and roughly thirty per cent of container traffic between Asia and Europe, and the diversion around Africa adds about ten days and several thousand nautical miles to each voyage. Container rates from Shanghai to Rotterdam, which had fallen below a thousand dollars in November, more than doubled by the end of December, and insurers added war risk premiums of a size that some cargo owners simply would not pay.

Egypt is the party that loses most without firing a shot. The Suez Canal contributed about nine billion dollars to the government's foreign currency earnings in the last fiscal year, and transits fell by roughly a third in the second half of December, which arrives at a moment when the country is negotiating a loan programme with the IMF and running short of dollars for wheat and fuel. The canal authority offered discounts and said it was monitoring the situation, which is what an authority can say when the situation is in someone else's hands.

Energy markets reacted more slowly and then all at once. Brent rose from about 73 dollars at the start of December to above 79 by the middle of the month, and European gas prices rose by a fifth in a week because tankers carrying Qatari cargo to Europe were among those that turned around. The fundamental balance of oil has been comfortable all year, with production cuts from OPEC and record output from the United States cancelling each other out, which means that the price is now carrying a risk premium rather than a shortage.

Strategy is the part that worries the shipping industry most. The route can be restored by escorting convoys, as the naval mission has begun to do, and it can be closed again by a single successful attack on a large ship. The companies will keep routing around Africa while the insurance market refuses to cover the passage, and the insurance market will refuse to cover it while the attacks continue. That is a decision made in Cairo, in Sanaa and in Tehran rather than in the boardrooms that have already made it.
