Hurricane Beryl made landfall on the Texas coast on 8 July and moved inland across Houston, and the power system failed across a wide area, leaving more than two million customers without electricity at the peak. The storm had crossed the Atlantic as a record-setting early-season hurricane and arrived weakened but still dangerous. A storm that strengthens over warm water before it reaches land is a storm that the forecast models have to revise hourly.

Grid is the recurring problem. A network that has been rebuilt after two major storms in a decade is a network whose redundancy is still thin, and the failures were concentrated in the distribution system rather than in generation. A distribution network with overhead lines is a network that a wind field can dismantle. A utility that has not buried its lines is a utility that will rebuild them overhead, because the cost of doing otherwise is a rate case.

Heat is the second hazard. A storm that removes power in July leaves households without cooling, and the death toll from heat after a hurricane often exceeds the toll from the storm. A response that restores power slowly is a response that puts the elderly at risk. A household without air conditioning in a heat wave is a household that a city has to open cooling centres for. A city that opens cooling centres is a city that has learned the lesson from the last storm and has not yet paid for the fix.

Insurance market is the third. Coastal property in the state is increasingly covered by a public insurer of last resort, and a storm that generates many claims is a storm that the residual market absorbs. A market that depends on a state-backed insurer is a market that taxpayers eventually fund. A public insurer is a subsidy that is invisible until a storm exhausts it, and then it is a bill.

Forecast was accurate. The track was predicted several days ahead, and the warnings were issued in time, which is the part of the system that works. A forecast that is accurate converts a disaster into a cleanup, and the cleanup is what the state has to fund. A cleanup that takes weeks is a cleanup whose cost lands on the municipality rather than on the utility. A warning that reaches a household on a mobile phone is a warning that works only if the phone has a charge. The utility said most customers would be restored within a week, and the ones on the barrier islands would wait longer.

What the storm shows is that the infrastructure and the climate are out of step. A system designed for a storm every twenty years is a system that is asked to survive one every three, and the answer has to be money spent before the storm rather than after it. A resilience programme that is funded after the event is a programme that rebuilds the same network in the same place.
