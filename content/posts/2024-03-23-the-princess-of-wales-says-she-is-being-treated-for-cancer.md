Catherine, the Princess of Wales, said in a video message recorded at Windsor on the twenty-second of March that she had been diagnosed with cancer and was in the early stages of preventative chemotherapy. Statement ended weeks of speculation that had grown since she withdrew from public duties in January for what the palace described as planned abdominal surgery. She said the surgery had been successful and that tests afterwards had found cancer, which her medical team recommended be treated with chemotherapy.

Message was about four minutes long and was delivered without an interviewer. She said it had taken time to explain the situation to her three children in a way that was appropriate for their ages, and she asked for privacy while she completed treatment. She also said she was well and getting stronger, and that she had been advised by her doctors to avoid public engagements for a period that the palace left open.

Speculation that the announcement ended had become a story in its own right. Palace had released a photograph of her with her children on Mothering Sunday in March, and several news agencies withdrew it after discovering that it had been edited, which produced a wave of conspiracy theories about her whereabouts. She had also been photographed in a car with her mother, and the images were published despite a request from the palace that they not be. Statement did not address the photograph directly, and it did say that she had needed time to recover and to tell her children.

Reaction was dominated by sympathy and by criticism of the coverage. The prime minister said the country's thoughts were with her and her family, and political leaders suspended campaigning for a day. Several newspapers that had published speculation about her marriage or her appearance apologized, and commentators noted that the episode had shown how quickly unverified information about a public figure becomes a global story.

Medical detail in the message was limited and appropriate to the situation. She did not say what kind of cancer had been found or what stage it was, and she said only that the chemotherapy was preventative, which in British usage means treatment given after surgery when there is no evidence of remaining disease but a risk of recurrence. She began treatment in late February according to the palace, and she returned to some public duties in the summer.

Consequence of the twenty-second of March is a family that will be out of public view for months and an institution that has lost some control of its own narrative. The royal household has been through a year of illness, with the King also being treated for cancer announced in February, and the number of working members has fallen. What the statement did was to replace speculation with a fact that no one could argue with. It did not resolve the question of how the palace communicates, which is the part of the episode that the institution will have to address.
