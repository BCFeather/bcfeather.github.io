---

Amazon published results for the third quarter on 27 October, and net sales rose 15 per cent to 127.1 billion dollars. The forecast had been 127.5, and it was the first miss in two years. Operating income was 2.5 billion, above the guidance of 2 billion, and net income was 2.9 billion. The cloud business was the strongest part. Amazon Web Services grew 27 per cent to 20.5 billion dollars, and its operating margin was 26 per cent. Growth had slowed from 33 per cent in the previous quarter, and the company said customers were cutting spending as the economy weakened.

Sales in North America rose 20 per cent, and the operating loss in that segment was 412 million dollars. The retail business was the weak part. The company had doubled its fulfilment capacity during the pandemic, and it was now carrying costs it could not fill. It said it would slow the expansion of its warehouse network. Advertising revenue rose 25 per cent to 9.5 billion, and the company said it was one of its fastest growing units. Its operating margin was 2 per cent, and it had been 6 per cent a year earlier.

Guidance for the holiday quarter was 140 to 148 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 155. The company said it expected the weak trends to continue, and the shares fell 7 per cent the next day and 14 per cent over the week. The stock had lost 40 per cent in the year. The company's headcount was 1.5 million, and it had added 100,000 in the quarter.

A charge of 2.5 billion dollars was taken on its investment in Rivian, the electric truck maker. It also recorded a loss on its stake in a Indian retailer, and it said the two items together reduced net income by 1.4 billion. The market valued those stakes at a fraction of what they had been worth in 2021.

Costs were rising in every segment. The company said it had hired 100,000 people in the year and that its headcount was 1.5 million including warehouse staff. It said it would freeze hiring in the corporate roles and would review its spending, and it announced 10,000 job cuts in November. The company said capital spending would fall by 2 billion dollars next year.

Holiday sales came in at 149 billion dollars, in line with the lowered guidance. The company said it had a record holiday season, and it said the cloud business had stabilized. The shares recovered 20 per cent in the first quarter of 2023 as costs fell. The company announced 18,000 job cuts in January 2023, the largest in its history. It also said it would stop building some new warehouses and would sublease space. The retail margin turned positive in the second quarter, and the shares rose 80 per cent in the year.
