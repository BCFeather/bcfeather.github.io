description: "An evaluation of forest carbon offset projects finds that the emission reductions they claim are largely not additional, according to work published in *Science* on 24 August 2023."
---

A carbon offset is a claim that an emission avoided somewhere compensates for an emission made here, and the claim rests on additionality. A project that would have happened anyway is not a reduction.

The evaluation in *Science*, 24 August 2023 tests additionality for forest projects. The authors compare the deforestation rates in project areas with those in matched control areas, and the difference is the reduction that the projects can legitimately claim.

The result is a shortfall. The reduction that the projects claim is much larger than the reduction that the comparison supports, and the gap is the credits that buyers paid for and did not receive.

The method is the standard one for evaluation. A matched control is a set of similar forest areas without a project, and the counterfactual is what the control estimates.

The leakage is the second problem. A project that protects one area can displace logging to another, and the displacement is not counted in the project's accounting.

The permanence is the third. A forest that is protected for a decade and then cleared has stored carbon for a decade, and a credit that assumes a century is a credit that may not exist.

The market consequence is direct. A credit that is not additional is a credit that lets a buyer emit without a reduction, and the market's integrity is what the paper's numbers undermine.

The methodological dispute is about baselines. A project's baseline is a projection of what would have happened, and a projection is an assumption rather than a measurement.

The limitation is the sample. A set of projects in one country is a case study, and the market's other project types are not evaluated.

What the paper does not establish is a remedy. A better baseline method is a suggestion, and the suggestion's adoption depends on the registries that issue the credits.

For climate policy, the value is evidence about a mechanism that has been used at scale. A policy instrument that does not deliver is one that a government should fix rather than defend.

The matching is the evaluation's core. A control area is chosen to resemble a project area on forest type, on access and on pressure, and an unmatched comparison would confound the two.

The leakage is the systemic issue. A market that protects one forest and logs another has moved the emission rather than removed it.

The permanence is the accounting's horizon. A tonne stored for a decade and released later is a tonne that the atmosphere sees, and a credit's time horizon is a policy choice.

The registry's role is the institutional one. A registry defines the method that projects use, and a method with a generous baseline produces credits that the atmosphere does not honour.

The comparison with other offset types is useful. A cookstove project and a forest project fail for different reasons, and the common failure is a baseline that the seller controls.

The reform's direction is measurable baselines. A method that uses observed control areas rather than projected trends is one the paper's approach could supply.
