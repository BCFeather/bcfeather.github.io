Microsoft 365 services went down across much of the world on Thursday, in the second disruption to the suite this year. Outlook was the most visible casualty: users could not sign in, send mail or load their inboxes, and the failure spread into Teams, the Copilot assistant and other parts of the productivity bundle. Microsoft's status page listed the incident as a service degradation covering Outlook on the web, the desktop client and the mobile apps, and Copilot returned errors for some users inside both Outlook and Teams.

Reports began around midday on the American east coast. By just after 13:00 local time, close to 130,000 users had flagged problems with Outlook on the tracking site Downdetector, and thousands more reported trouble with Teams, Copilot and the wider suite. Volume peaked in the early afternoon and then fell as engineers in different regions applied fixes. Users in Asia met the failure in the middle of the night, while European offices were closing for the day.

Microsoft said on X that parts of its infrastructure had seen elevated load, which it described as the most likely cause of the connection errors. The company said it had found no malicious activity behind the failure and was reallocating traffic, adjusting traffic protocols and applying strategic throttling. Partial recovery was observed in the regions where those changes had been made, and engineers kept working on server stability through the afternoon.

Government systems were dragged in. Agencies in Australia, Japan and the United States reported problems, and several airlines warned passengers that departure information might arrive late. Singapore's GovTech office confirmed that some public systems were affected, and the tax authority IRAS, the media regulator IMDA, the Health Sciences Authority and the Singapore Land Authority all warned of delays or failed deliveries. Singpass and the emergency numbers 995 and 999 stayed up. Banks and courts in the city-state also reported delays to mail-based filings, and help desks at several firms switched to phone calls while the service was down.

Artificial-intelligence firms felt it too. OpenAI and Anthropic both said the Microsoft connectors their models rely on had failed, which interrupted some customer traffic while the outage lasted. Anadolu counted disruption in 74 countries and territories, a reminder that a single cloud tenant now sits under a very large share of office work.

Repeated failures of this kind renew questions about concentration risk, because one vendor carries mail, chat, documents and, increasingly, the assistant layer that sits on top of them. Earlier this year the same suite failed once, and procurement teams at large employers have been asking for fallback routes since. Microsoft said engineers were working to raise server stability, and analysts expect the incident to feature in contract reviews over the coming weeks. Supervisors in Europe already ask large cloud customers for continuity plans, and this outage gives them a concrete case to test those answers against.
