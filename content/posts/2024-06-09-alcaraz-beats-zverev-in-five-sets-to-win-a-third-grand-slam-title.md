Carlos Alcaraz won the French Open on 9 June, beating Alexander Zverev 6-3, 2-6, 5-7, 6-1, 6-2 after three hours and forty-one minutes. It was his third major title and his first on clay. The first set was over in thirty-five minutes, and the next two were lost as the serve percentage dropped and the errors accumulated on the forehand. He became the third man in the open era to win his first three majors on three different surfaces, and the youngest to do it.

Match turned on fitness. The German player had spent more time on court in the previous rounds, and the fourth and fifth sets were decided by the length of the rallies, which the younger player extended. A rally that lasts twenty shots is a rally that costs the older legs more. The trainer was called in the third set for cramping in the forearm, and the treatment was the moment the match changed rather than the point that followed it.

Surface is the achievement. The twenty-one-year-old has now won on hard courts, on grass and on clay, which is a spread that only a handful of players in the open era have managed before their twenty-second birthday. A player who can win on any surface is a player who does not have a season to fear. The clay rewards the player who can defend and then attack from the same position, and the drop shot that the winner used in the fourth set is the shot that the surface makes possible.

Draw was kind and the final was not. Three of the seeded players in his half withdrew or retired, and the final was against a man who had never won a major, which is the context in which the record will be read. A title is a title, and the strength of the field is a separate question. The opponent had beaten two of the top ten on the way to the final, and the run was the best of his career, which is why the defeat was described as a step rather than as a failure.

Ranking is now settled at the top. The winner is the youngest man to hold the number one position, and the gap to the next player is large enough that a single defeat will not change it. A ranking that is stable is a ranking that the tour can sell. The tour now has four players who have won majors in the last two years, and the rivalries that the sport hoped for have begun to form. The tour's next major is on grass in three weeks, and the player who has just won on clay will arrive with two days of practice on the surface.

What the fortnight shows is that the men's tour has found a player who can win on every surface at an age when most are still learning one, and the question is how long the body sustains it. A champion who wins on every surface is a champion that the calendar tests twice a year, and the grass season that follows is the next examination.
