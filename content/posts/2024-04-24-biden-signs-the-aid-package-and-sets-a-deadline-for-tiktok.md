description: "President Biden signed the ninety-five billion dollar foreign aid package on 24 April, and the same law gives ByteDance until January 2025 to sell TikTok or see the app banned in the United States."
---President Joe Biden signed the national security package on the twenty-fourth of April, a day after the Senate approved it by seventy-nine votes to eighteen, and he said in remarks at the White House that the weapons would begin moving within hours. The law provides about sixty-one billion dollars for Ukraine, twenty-six billion for Israel and eight billion for Taiwan and the Indo-Pacific, and it includes a provision that requires the sale of TikTok or its removal from American app stores.

First deliveries were drawn from stocks that had been held in Europe. The Pentagon said it would send artillery ammunition, air defence interceptors and vehicles from its own inventories, and that contracts for new production would follow. The administration had said before the vote that Ukraine's shortage of shells was the main constraint on its defence, and the Ukrainian government had reported that its units were rationing rounds.

TikTok provision gives ByteDance two hundred and seventy days to divest the app, with a possible extension of ninety days if the president certifies that a sale is under way. The law also allows the president to designate other applications controlled by companies in countries that are adversaries, which is a power that civil liberties groups said was too broad. ByteDance said it would challenge the law in court and that it would rather shut the app than sell it.

Announcement of the signing was accompanied by a promise of more sanctions on Iran, which the Senate had attached in a separate measure, and by a statement on the war in Gaza. The president said the aid to Israel came with an expectation that humanitarian assistance would increase, and he said he had told the Israeli government that the conduct of the war had to change.

Political reaction at home split along the lines that the vote had set. Republicans who opposed the package said the money would be wasted and that the border was the priority, and Democrats who supported it said the delay had cost Ukrainian lives. The speaker, Mike Johnson, faced a motion to vacate from a small group of his own members, and he survived it with Democratic votes a week later.

The Consequence of the twenty-fourth of April is a war that was given a second year of funding and an application that was given a deadline. Weapons began arriving in Ukraine within days and were used to slow the Russian advance in the east, and TikTok filed its suit in May, which meant that the deadline would be decided by a court. What the signing settled was that America would keep supplying Ukraine.

What it left open was whether the aid would arrive fast enough to change the war. The Pentagon said the first tranche included ammunition that could be delivered from depots in Poland and in Germany, and that it would be followed by contracts for new production of artillery shells and interceptors. Ukraine's finance ministry said the economic support would cover part of the budget deficit that had been financed by central bank credit.
