Broadcom completed its purchase of VMware for about 69 billion dollars on 22 November, after the antitrust regulators in China approved the deal at the last moment. The transaction is the largest in the history of the company. The company paid about 61 billion dollars in the cash and the shares, and it assumed about 8 billion of the VMware debt. The approval came after the regulators in the European Union and the United Kingdom had cleared the deal in the summer, and the Chinese review was the last one outstanding. The software unit will be reported separately from the chips, and the company has said the two businesses will share the sales force.

Regulation caused the delay. The review in China took about eighteen months, and the clearance came with the conditions on the interoperability of the software. A deal that waits is a premium that grows. The approval came on the condition that the company keeps the interoperability with the rival products for the next decade.

Buying the margin is the plan. The company has said it will cut about 1.3 billion dollars of the costs at VMware, and it has begun to move the customers to the subscription licences that cost more. A licence that is repriced is a customer that must decide. The company has said it will focus VMware on the largest customers, and the smaller ones will be pushed to the partners.

Debt is the price. The company has borrowed about 60 billion dollars to pay for the deal, and the interest bill will be about 3 billion a year at the current rates. A debt that is large is a dividend that is constrained. The company has raised the dividend by about 3 per cent, and the free cash flow is expected to cover the interest with a narrow margin. The company has said it will pay down the debt by about 10 billion dollars a year, and the rating agencies have put the bonds on review.

Shareholders are sceptical. The stock have fallen by about 5 per cent since the deal was announced, and the analysts say the growth of the software will be slower than the company assumes. A scepticism that is priced is a management that must deliver. The software business will be about a quarter of the revenue, and the chips still pay for the rest.

What the deal shows is that Broadcom is buying the software for the cash flow rather than the growth, and the cash is what the debt needs. A company that buys a software firm for its cash is a company that must not lose the customers, and the customers are the risk. The customers have been told the renewals will be at the higher price, and the ones who leave will be the measure of the plan. The purchase is the largest in the technology sector since the one of the Dell and the EMC, and the debt is the largest the sector has carried.
