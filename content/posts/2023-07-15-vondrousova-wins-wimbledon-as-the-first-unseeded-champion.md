Marketa Vondrousova won the Wimbledon singles title on 15 July, beating Ons Jabeur 6-4, 6-4 in about an hour and 20 minutes. She is the first unseeded woman to win the title in the Open era, and the first Czech since 2014. The ranking was 42 at the start of the fortnight. The title is the first Grand Slam for the 24-year-old, who had lost the final of the French Open in 2019 as a teenager. The prize money was about 2.35 million pounds, and the total for the tournament was about 44 million. She had played about 12 matches on the grass in her career before the fortnight, and the one she lost was in 2021.

Her final was short. Vondrousova broke in the first game of each set and held through, and the Tunisian could not convert the four break points she had. The Czech player made about 10 unforced errors, and the consistency was the difference. She won about 70 per cent of the points on her first serve, and the one on the second about 50. The match was the shortest women's final since 2016, and the crowd was quiet for much of it. She lost only about 12 points on her serve in the second set, and the one break in the seventh game was the margin.

Her year was the story. She had missed about six months of 2022 after the wrist surgery, and she had come to Wimbledon last year as a spectator with a cast. She said afterwards that she had not expected to play the tournament at all in the spring. She had played about 20 matches since the return, and the one on the grass was her first of the year.

Jabeur was the favourite again. The Tunisian had reached the final in 2022 and in the two previous Grand Slam finals, and she has now lost three. She said the pressure of the expectation had been the hardest part, and the crowd support had been the consolation. The Tunisian had been the favourite of the bookmakers at about 2 to 1, and the defeat was the third final she had lost.

Her draw was the proof. Vondrousova beat four seeded players on the way, including the fourth seed in the quarter-final, and she saved two match points in the first round. The route is the one the statisticians will cite against the argument that the draw was soft. The first round was against a qualifier, and the match points saved were in the third set.

Victory moves her to about 10th in the ranking. A champion who wins seven matches after a year out with the surgery is a champion who has rebuilt the game around the placement rather than the power, and the placement is what the grass rewards. The next Grand Slam is the US Open in August, and the hard courts have been her best surface.
