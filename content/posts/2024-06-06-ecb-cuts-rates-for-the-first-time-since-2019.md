Deposit rates at the European Central Bank fell to 3.75 per cent on 6 June, the first reduction since 2019, and the council declined to say what it would do next. The decision was unanimous among the members who voted. The cut had been signalled since the meeting in April, and the market had priced it with a probability close to certainty, which is why the reaction came from the guidance rather than from the decision.

Case for cutting was built on the forecast. Inflation had fallen to 2.6 per cent and the staff projections showed it at the target by the end of next year, and the council judged that the restrictive stance it had adopted was no longer needed at the same strength. A forecast that is met is a justification that arrives on schedule. The staff also cut their forecast for growth, and the combination of slower growth and lower inflation is the condition in which a central bank can ease without appearing to be responding to a crisis.

Wages are the reason the council is cautious. Settlements in the first quarter came in at more than five per cent, and the council said that the pass-through into service prices would take a year to work through. A wage round that is still running is a reason to move slowly rather than to stop. The council has asked the social partners in the largest economies to moderate their claims, and the response has been that the workers are recovering the purchasing power they lost.

Divergence with America is the constraint on the pace. The American central bank has not cut, and the gap between the two policy rates is the widest in years, which weakens the euro and imports inflation into the bloc. A cut that is one-sided is a cut that undoes part of its own effect through the exchange rate. The president said the council was not concerned about the exchange rate as such, which is the formula used when a central bank does not want to be seen to be managing one.

Market had priced the move and was looking for guidance. The statement removed the reference to the need to keep rates restrictive for as long as necessary, and the president said the council was not committing to a particular path. A guidance that is withdrawn is a guidance that the market has to rebuild from the data. The statement was read as a signal that the council will not cut again until September at the earliest, and the market moved its expectations accordingly.

What the meeting shows is that the first cut in a cycle is the easiest one, and the difficulty begins with the second, when the council has to decide whether the inflation it can still see is noise or a floor. A cycle that begins with a cut that everyone expected is a cycle that begins with a question, and the question is how far the rate can fall before the service prices stop cooperating.
