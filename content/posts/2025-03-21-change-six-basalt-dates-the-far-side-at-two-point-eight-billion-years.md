title: "Chang'e-6 basalt dates the far side at 2.8 billion years"

description: "Basalt fragments returned by the Chang'e-6 mission have been dated to 2.8 billion years, according to a study in *Science* on 21 March, which makes the far side of the Moon volcanically younger than the near side's record suggests."
---

Samples came back in June 2024 from the South Pole-Aitken basin, a crater 2,500 kilometres across that occupies the southern part of the far side and that is the largest impact structure on the Moon. Every previous sample, from Apollo and from the Soviet and Chinese landers, came from the near side, and the far side had been known only from orbit, from images and from the composition inferred from spectroscopy.

Radiometric dating of the basalt fragments used the lead-lead method on zircons and on the bulk rock, and the result is 2.8 billion years with a small uncertainty. The number is not a surprise, because crater counting on the far side had suggested flows of that age, and a sample that confirms a remote inference is exactly what a sample-return mission is for, because the alternative is a chronology built entirely on orbital imagery.

Composition is where the sample goes beyond the date. The basalts are low in titanium and depleted in the potassium, rare-earth and phosphorus elements that geochemists group as KREEP, which places their source in a mantle region that had already lost those elements in an earlier episode. The near-side basalts come from a source with more of them, and the two sides therefore sample different parts of the lunar interior.

Volume of the flows is the puzzle that the date sharpens. A body the size of the Moon should have cooled enough that its mantle no longer melted by 2.8 billion years ago, and the presence of flows at that age requires a source of heat that the standard thermal history does not supply. Radioactive decay in the mantle, tidal heating and an enrichment in the source are the candidates, and the new sample constrains all three.

Far side is also the side that faces away from the Earth, which matters for a different reason. The near side has a thinner crust and large maria, and the far side has a thicker crust and few maria, and the asymmetry between the two hemispheres is the largest unresolved feature of lunar geology, with no mechanism that commands general agreement. A sample from the far side that differs in composition from those of the near side is evidence that the asymmetry extends into the interior.

Missions planned for the rest of the decade will follow. The International Lunar Research Station that China is assembling near the south pole, and the Artemis landings that the United States is preparing, will both sample terrain that no mission has touched. Each sample reduces the uncertainty in the crater-counting chronology, which is the method that dates every surface in the inner solar system.

What the paper gives the field is a calibration. The crater-counting chronology is calibrated at a handful of points, all of them on the near side, and a point from the far side at 2.8 billion years tests the extrapolation that the method uses across the whole Moon and across every rocky body. The extrapolation held, which is the best outcome that a calibration can produce.
