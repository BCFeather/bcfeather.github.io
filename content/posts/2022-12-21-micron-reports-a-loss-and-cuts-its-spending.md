Micron published results for the quarter to November on 21 December, and revenue fell 47 per cent to 4.09 billion dollars. It reported a net loss of 195 million, the first loss in three years. The gross margin fell from 47 per cent to 22, and the company said it expected a worse quarter next. Analysts had expected a loss of 10 cents a share.

Analysts had expected 4.5 billion, and the shares fell 6 per cent the next day. The guidance was for revenue of 3.6 to 4 billion and a gross margin of 10 per cent. The company said prices for DRAM had fallen 30 per cent in the quarter and for NAND 35. The company said it expected a loss per share of 62 cents, and analysts had expected 30.

Personal computer makers had built stock in 2021, and they were now working it down. The cuts were large. The company said capital spending for the year would be 7 billion dollars, down from 12, and it would cut 10 per cent of its workforce, about 5,000 people. It also said it would reduce wafer starts by 20 per cent and would slow the introduction of new technology. The company blamed inventory at its customers. Smartphone sales had fallen 10 per cent in the year, and data centre customers had cut orders. The company said the industry had oversupplied itself during the pandemic.

Micron was the first large chip maker to report for the quarter, and its numbers set the tone for the sector. Samsung and SK Hynix reported later and both had larger losses. The three companies supply most of the world's memory, and they had all expanded capacity in 2021 when prices were high. The three companies supply 90 per cent of the world's DRAM, and they had all expanded capacity in 2021.

Prices are set by supply and demand, and every producer has an incentive to run its plants at full capacity, which is what creates the cycle. The company's balance sheet was strong enough to absorb the loss. It had 10 billion dollars of cash and had raised 2 billion in debt in the autumn. It said it would keep paying the dividend, and it said the cuts would save 1 billion dollars a year. The industry's problem was that memory is a commodity. The companies had promised to be more disciplined, and they had not been.

Prices bottomed in the summer of 2023, and Micron returned to profit in the quarter to November. The company said the recovery was driven by demand for chips used in artificial intelligence, and it raised its capital spending again. Its shares rose 70 per cent in 2023, and the cycle turned faster than the company had expected. Its shares had fallen 45 per cent in the year before the results, and they rose 8 per cent after them.
