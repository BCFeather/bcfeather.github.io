A Ministry of Health analysis and the health-fund analysis were run in parallel on overlapping populations with different designs, and the second is the one that used individual matching. Both appeared within days of each other, which is a consequence of a small country with centralized data and a policy decision taken in a hurry. A health fund with a century of records and a membership that is representative of the country's insured population is a better instrument than a ministry database for this kind of matching study.

Clalit Health Services matched people aged 60 and over who received a fourth dose with people who had received three doses at least four months earlier, on sociodemographic and clinical variables, and followed them from 3 January to 18 February 2022. The primary analysis included 182,122 matched pairs. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 13 April 2022.

Relative effectiveness against confirmed infection was 52 per cent, against symptomatic infection 61 per cent, against hospitalization 72 per cent and against severe disease 76 per cent, with confidence intervals that excluded one for every outcome. Death was too rare in the matched sample to estimate separately. Estimating death separately was impossible because the matched sample produced too few events, and the absence of a death estimate is a limitation rather than a reassurance.

A gradient across outcomes is the same one seen in every vaccine study of this period: the more severe the endpoint, the larger the protection. That gradient reflects the difference between neutralizing antibody, which blocks infection and decays quickly, and cellular immunity, which limits disease and persists longer.

Matching on observed variables does not remove differences in behaviour, and people who seek a fourth dose may be more health-conscious or more frail than those who do not. The authors address this with a sensitivity analysis using parametric Poisson regression, which produced similar estimates. Frailty is the variable that matching cannot capture well, and a frail older person is both more likely to be offered a fourth dose and more likely to be hospitalized, which would bias the estimate towards a benefit.

At the time of the analysis the fourth dose had been given for a median of about three weeks when the data closed, so the estimates describe early protection during a wave driven by a variant that escapes much of the neutralizing response. Whether the benefit holds at three months is the question that the next analysis would answer.

For a health system deciding who gets a fourth dose, the result supports offering it to people over 60 and to those with conditions that impair the response to vaccination, and it does not support a general fourth dose for younger adults. The alternative to a fourth dose for this population is a monoclonal antibody, and the trial of that approach in immunocompromised people reported a similar magnitude of protection against symptomatic infection.
