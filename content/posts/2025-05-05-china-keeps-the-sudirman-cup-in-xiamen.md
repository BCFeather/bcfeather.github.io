The Sudirman Cup final in Xiamen ended with China beating Korea, and the mixed-team trophy stayed where the tournament was being played. The format is unusual in badminton: five matches across the disciplines, with the winner needing three, and the order of play decided by ranking rather than by choice. A team that is deep in the doubles can therefore protect a weak singles player, and a team with one superstar cannot hide the rest of the squad. which means a team can be strong everywhere and still lose if the draw is unkind.

Xiamen's Fenghuang gymnasium held crowds for eight days. Tickets for the final went on sale in the spring and sold out quickly, and the stands were full for sessions that began in the morning, which is not always the case at team events in badminton, where the early rounds can be thinly attended. Xiamen's organisers had priced the week as a festival rather than as a qualifier, with fan zones outside the hall and a shuttle from the metro station that ran until midnight.

The doubles decided the tie. China's mixed doubles pair carried the opening match, and the men's doubles, which has been the weakest link in the team for several years, held its nerve in a match that went to three games. The pairs had been recombined twice in the previous eighteen months, and the combination that played in Xiamen had been together for only four tournaments. They lost the first game of the decider and won the next two, which is the kind of result that buys a pair another year together. The singles were split, as expected: Korea's women's singles player is among the best in the world, and the men's singles went to China on ranking.

What the tournament shows is the depth of the squad rather than the brilliance of any one player. Team events reward countries that can field four strong pairs and two strong singles players, and China can. Korea can field three. That difference is worth a match, and over five matches it is usually worth the tie. A final decided in the third game is one the coaches will study for a year.

Crowd noise mattered, and not only in the obvious way. Badminton is a sport in which the shuttle is affected by air movement, and a full hall is a still hall. Players from both teams said afterwards that the drift was consistent, which is what a player wants and what a half-empty arena does not provide. The shuttle also slows in thick air, and the hall's ventilation was adjusted between sessions, a detail that the coaches on both benches asked about before the final.

A European city hosts the next edition, and the Chinese team will travel with expectations it did not carry this year. Xiamen answered the question for one week. Whether it answered it for a cycle is a different question.
