Ayman al-Zawahiri was killed by an American drone strike in Kabul over the weekend, and the president announced the operation on Monday evening from the White House balcony. Zawahiri was the leader of al-Qaeda since 2011, when Osama bin Laden was killed in Abbottabad, and he was seventy-one. The strike hit a house in the Sherpur district of the capital, which is a neighbourhood of villas near the old diplomatic quarter. Officials said a small missile was used and that no other person died.

The choice of weapon was reported to be a type designed to kill in a confined space with minimal blast. That would explain how a man standing on a balcony could be killed while the people inside the house survived, and the administration said the operation had been planned for months. The house belonged to a senior aide of the Haqqani network, which is part of the Taliban government, and the Taliban's spokesman said the movement had no knowledge of the presence of a foreign leader in the city.

The timing was deliberate in the way that such announcements usually are. It came a year after the withdrawal from Afghanistan and a month before the anniversary of the attacks of 11 September 2001, and it gave the administration a success in a country where it had left in disorder. The strike also raised the question of whether the Taliban had broken the agreement it signed in 2020, which promised that Afghanistan would not host groups that threatened others. The Taliban's answer was that it had not been told.

Zawahiri was an Egyptian surgeon who became the ideologist of the movement after the death of its founder. He was indicted for the bombings of the American embassies in Kenya and Tanzania in 1998, and he appeared in videos for twenty years, most recently in a recording that was released in the spring. His deputy, Saif al-Adel, is reported to be in Iran, and the leadership of the organization has been a subject of speculation since 2011. Analysts said the group has been weakened by the loss and that it has also been decentralized for a decade.

At the same time, the reaction in the region was muted and careful. Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates welcomed the killing, and Qatar and Pakistan said nothing. The Afghan government's statement called the operation a violation of its sovereignty and said it would investigate, and the Taliban's interior ministry said the house had been empty. The United Nations reported that the movement had been unable to prevent the strike, which is what the agreement was supposed to guarantee.

What the operation shows is a capability that the Americans kept after the withdrawal. It also shows the limits of that capability, because the target had been living in a capital that the Taliban controls and the strike had to be small enough to avoid a public argument about casualties. The next question is whether the Taliban will be pressed to hand over the people who sheltered him, and the answer will decide whether the agreement of 2020 survives.
