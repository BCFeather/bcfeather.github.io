Iran launched a combination of drones, cruise missiles and ballistic missiles at Israel on the night of the thirteenth of April and into the following morning, in the first direct attack on the country from Iranian territory. Israeli officials said about 170 drones, more than thirty cruise missiles and about 120 ballistic missiles were fired, and that almost all of them were intercepted outside Israeli airspace. A small number reached their targets, and the damage was limited.

Attacks were conducted from several directions and by several forces. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had fired the weapons from Iran, and allied groups in Iraq, Syria, Lebanon and Yemen also launched drones and rockets during the same hours. Israel's air force, with assistance from American, British, French and Jordanian aircraft and from American warships in the region, intercepted most of the incoming fire before it crossed into Israeli territory.

Stated reason for the attack was an Israeli strike on the Iranian consulate in Damascus on the first of April, which killed several officers of the Revolutionary Guard, including two generals. Iran said the attack was a response to that strike and that it was acting in self-defence under article fifty-one of the United Nations charter, and it said the matter was now concluded. Israel said the consulate was being used for military purposes, which Iran denied.

Effect was more political than physical. A girl in the south of Israel was seriously injured by debris, and a military base in the Negev was hit, with damage that the army described as minor. Ben Gurion airport closed for several hours and flights were diverted, and the airspace over several countries was shut for a night. Israeli officials said the interception had demonstrated the value of the regional air defence cooperation built over the past decade.

Reaction from allies was a mixture of support and caution. The American president said the defence had been successful and that he would not support an Israeli counterattack, and the Group of Seven issued a statement that condemned the attack and urged restraint. European governments said they would consider new sanctions on Iran's missile programme, and the European Union began work on measures against producers of drones and missiles.

Consequence of the fourteenth of April is a conflict that has crossed a threshold without producing a war. Israel had avoided direct confrontation with Iran for decades and had fought its proxies instead, and the two countries exchanged fire in the open within three weeks, with a limited Israeli strike on Isfahan on the nineteenth of April that Iran played down. What the exchange established was that both governments wanted to restore deterrence without a general war, and that the region's air defences can absorb a large attack.

What it did not establish was a stable balance, since the next round would depend on decisions taken in a few hours by people who had just been attacked. Several European airlines suspended flights to Israel and to Iran for a week after the attack.
