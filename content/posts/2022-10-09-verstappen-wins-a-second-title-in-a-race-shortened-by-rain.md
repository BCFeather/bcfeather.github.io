Max Verstappen crossed the line first at Suzuka on Sunday and became world champion for the second time, and he found out about it several minutes later on the radio. The race had been stopped after twenty-eight of fifty-three laps in torrential rain, and the field was sent out again for a handful of laps behind the safety car before the weather ended it. Full points were awarded even though the race had run less than three quarters of its distance, which is a rule the teams had argued about all season. Verstappen asked his engineer why the celebrations had started.

He had started from pole and led every lap he ran, and the win was his twelfth of the year. The car had been quick since the spring, when a package of updates arrived and the team solved the bouncing that had troubled it in the opening races. Ferrari had the faster car at several circuits and lost points to strategy calls that the team later admitted had been wrong. Pérez, his team-mate, finished second on the road and kept the place after the penalty that decided the title.

That penalty came on the final lap. Leclerc cut the last chicane while defending against Pérez, and the stewards gave him five seconds, which dropped him behind the Mexican. That was enough to put the championship beyond arithmetic with four races left. Leclerc said afterwards that he had been pushed wide and that the penalty was harsh, and the stewards published the telemetry with their explanation. They had deliberated for most of the race and announced the decision ten minutes after the flag.

His first title came in Abu Dhabi last December, on the last lap of the last race, after a safety car procedure that Mercedes still describes as irregular. That season ended with a protest, a dismissed appeal and a change to the job of race director. This one ends with a title won in a race that was stopped twice, and the argument about the points will run for a while. The team's principal said afterwards that the two championships had been won in completely different ways.

Red Bull's year began badly, with two retirements in the first three races and a reliability problem that cost points. The team changed its fuel system and its cooling, and from Imola onwards the car won almost everything. The constructors' title was close to settled by the time the circus reached Japan, and the second seat became the subject of speculation for next season.

Suzuka is a circuit the drivers love and a place where championships have been decided before. Senna and Prost settled one of theirs here, and the crowd stayed through the rain to see this one. Verstappen said in the press conference that he had wanted to win it in a proper race and that the manner of it would take some getting used to. The trophy was handed over in a tent, and the mechanics were still in their wet overalls.
