---

A co-repressor is a protein that a transcription factor recruits to silence a gene, and the textbook picture is a general brake. A survey of the fly genome suggests that each brake is wired to a particular set of enhancers.

The survey in *Science*, 14 July 2023 tests the picture. The authors perturb each co-repressor in Drosophila, measure the expression of a large set of genes, and identify which enhancers respond to which repressor.

The specificity is the result. Each co-repressor has a distinct set of targets, and the overlap between two repressors' targets is smaller than a general-brake model would predict.

The wiring is the mechanism. A repressor's effect depends on the DNA-binding factor that recruits it and on the enhancer's architecture, and the combinations are what produce the specificity.

The developmental consequence is an explanation for how one genome produces many patterns. A general brake would silence everything at once, and a specific brake lets a factor silence one enhancer without touching its neighbours.

The method is the advance. A perturbation of each repressor in a defined tissue, combined with an enhancer-activity readout, is what turns a list of targets into a map of specificities.

The conservation is the question. Drosophila's co-repressors have mammalian homologues, and whether the specificity carries over is the next comparison.

The limitation is the assay. An enhancer's activity is measured in a reporter, and a reporter is not the endogenous locus.

What the paper does not show is the molecular detail. A specificity could come from a direct interaction or from a chromatin context, and the paper's data distinguish the two only partly.

For gene regulation, the value is a correction to a metaphor. The brake is a metaphor of a general mechanism, and the paper's map is a set of specific connections.

The recruiter's identity is the first variable. A repressor that is recruited by one factor acts where that factor binds, and the factor's motif is therefore part of the specificity.

The enhancer's architecture is the second variable. A enhancer with a particular arrangement of sites favours one repressor over another, and the authors' classification of enhancer classes is the test.

The perturbation's precision is the method's strength. A repressor knocked down everywhere would confound the tissue's identity with the target's response, and the authors' tissue-specific driver avoids that.

The comparison across co-repressors is the map's value. A pair of repressors with overlapping targets suggests a common partner, and a pair with disjoint targets suggests different partners.

The developmental example is the evidence. A patterning gene's enhancer that responds to one repressor and not another is a case where the specificity has a visible consequence.

The mammalian test is the extension. A homologue that rescues a fly mutant with the same specificity would argue for conservation, and a partial rescue would not.

The chromatin context is the unresolved part. A repressor that modifies histones acts at a distance, and the distance is a variable the assay does not control.
