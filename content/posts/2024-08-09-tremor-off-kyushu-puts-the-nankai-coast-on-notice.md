---

A magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck off the coast of Miyazaki on 8 August, and the meteorological agency issued an advisory for a possible larger event along the Nankai trough, the first use of a system introduced after a review of the 2011 disaster. The tremor injured several people and damaged buildings, and no tsunami of consequence arrived. The tremor was felt in a region that has been preparing for a megaquake for decades, and the preparation is visible in sea walls, drills and a dense network of sensors.

Trough is the reason for the system. Two plates meet along a line that runs from the coast of Shizuoka to the waters off Kyushu, and the segment has produced large earthquakes on a cycle of roughly a century, with the last ones in the 1940s. A gap that has been quiet for eighty years is a gap that accumulates strain. The last major events along the trough killed thousands, and the models that estimate the next one assume a rupture across segments that have not all moved at the same time.

Advisory is a probabilistic statement. An event of this size raises the chance of a larger one nearby for a period of days or weeks, and the increase is small in absolute terms and large relative to the background. A warning that says perhaps is a warning that officials have to communicate without causing a panic. The agency asked residents to be ready to leave within minutes if a stronger warning came, which is an instruction that most households cannot act on without a plan.

Practical measures were limited. Residents were asked to check evacuation routes and to secure furniture, and some rail services ran at reduced speed. A warning that produces a checklist rather than an evacuation is a warning that the public has to understand as an instruction about preparation. Hotels and hospitals were asked to review their supplies, and the tourism industry in the region reported cancellations within days.

Science is unsettled about the trigger. Some models treat a moderate event as a stress change that can advance a larger rupture, and others treat it as an independent release. A debate about causality is a debate that cannot be settled without another earthquake, and the monitoring network is the only way to test it. The monitoring network includes strain meters on the sea floor, and the data from the tremor will be studied for changes in the stress field.

What the advisory shows is that a country can prepare the machinery of warning faster than it can prepare the public to interpret it. A message about a possible disaster that arrives without a specific instruction is a message that tests the credibility of the next one. The test of the system is not this advisory but the next one, and the credibility that it earns now is the credibility it will spend then.
