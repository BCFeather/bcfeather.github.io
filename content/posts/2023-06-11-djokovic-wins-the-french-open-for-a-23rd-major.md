Novak Djokovic won the French Open on 11 June, beating Casper Ruud 7-6, 6-3, 7-5 in about three hours. The title is the third at Roland Garros and the 23rd major of his career, which takes him past Rafael Nadal for the most in the men's game. He is the first man to win each of the four majors at least three times. The win was his fourth title of the year, and the record on the clay was 21 wins and 3 losses. The match was his seventh final at Roland Garros, and he has now won three of them.

One set decided the match. Ruud led 4-1 and served for the set at 5-4, and Djokovic broke back and took the tie-break 7-1. The Norwegian had won the first set in the final of the previous year against Nadal, and the pattern was the one he wanted to avoid. The tie-break lasted about 15 minutes, and the set lasted about 80. Ruud had won the first set in the final of 2022 and lost the next three, and the memory was the one the Norwegian mentioned afterwards.

His body held. Djokovic had spent about 12 hours on the court through the fortnight, and the longest match was the semi-final against Carlos Alcaraz, which lasted about four and a half hours. He said afterwards that the legs had been the question and the answer had been the ice bath. He had beaten Alcaraz in four sets in the semi-final, and the cramp that struck the Spaniard in the third set was the turning point. He took about 90 minutes to win the second and the third sets, and the trainer was called once for the left thigh.

His records make a long list. The 23rd major puts him level with Serena Williams in the Open era, and the third title at each of the four is the mark no man had reached. He has now won at least one major in each of the last 12 years, and the streak is the longest of the Open era. The prize money was about 2.3 million euros, and the total for the fortnight was about 49 million.

Ranking follows the title. He returns to number one on 12 June, and the gap over Alcaraz is about 400 points. The Spaniard had beaten him in the semi-final of the previous year, and the rivalry is the one that the tour will be built around. He has spent a record 387 weeks at number one, and the record is the one he says means the most to him.

Little drama came with the final. A champion who wins 7-6, 6-3, 7-5 on the clay is a champion who has solved the surface, and the surface has been the last one to give him trouble. He said he would play at Wimbledon in July, and the grass has been the surface he has won seven times.
