American shares fell on Tuesday as a hotter than expected inflation report pushed bond yields higher. The Nasdaq composite lost 0.74 per cent, the Dow Jones industrial average 0.3 and the S&P 500 0.38. The technology index had been down as much as 1.7 per cent in the afternoon before it recovered part of the loss, and yields on the ten-year Treasury rose as the market moved to price another increase in official rates.

NVIDIA fell more than 5 per cent, and it has lost more than 9 per cent over two sessions, with about 420 billion dollars of market value gone. The drop began with the disclosure about its chief executive and continued as lawmakers renewed calls for tighter limits on exports of advanced chips to China. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 0.35 per cent, and the moves inside it were uneven: Broadcom rose nearly 4 per cent, NXP and TSMC more than 2, AMD and Qualcomm more than 1, while Intel and ARM fell more than 2.

Memory chips were the weak corner. SK Hynix fell more than 3 per cent and SanDisk 2.99, after a run in which both had risen on the price of high-bandwidth memory. Among the largest technology companies the day was mixed: Amazon gained 1.48 per cent, Tesla 0.46 and Meta 0.02, while Alphabet lost 0.39, Apple 0.61 and Microsoft 2.18. Volume was heavier than the average of the past month. Traders said the selling was orderly rather than panicked.

Damage came from the inflation report. Consumer prices rose faster than economists had forecast, and Goldman Sachs told clients that it still expects a second increase of 25 basis points in December and now adds a third in March 2027, with the first cut pushed to December 2027. The bank kept its estimate of a peak rate of 3.25 to 3.5 per cent. Higher rates raise the cost of the debt that pays for data centres, which is why the chip shares took the report harder than the rest of the market. Bond investors had expected a softer number.

Selling came in a week of heavy news for the industry. Anthropic opened the roadshow for a listing that could value it near 1.4 trillion dollars, Mercor raised 750 million at a 20 billion valuation, and Oura agreed to buy a healthcare startup for 625 million. Capital is still available for artificial intelligence, but the price of it is rising with the rate. Private markets have not repriced as quickly as the public ones.

Attention now turns to the December meeting. A second increase is largely priced, and the argument is about how far the bank goes before it stops. Equity investors have spent the year assuming that the cycle would turn, and each hot inflation report makes that assumption more expensive to hold. The next test comes with the November report, due before the meeting. Officials have said the decision will depend on the data. A cooler report would change the argument within a week. The bank's next statement is due in three weeks.
