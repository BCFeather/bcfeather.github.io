Strike destroyed the consular section of the Iranian embassy in Damascus on the first of April, killing several officers of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps who were meeting in the building. Iran's government said the dead included Brigadier General Mohammad Reza Zahedi, who commanded the guard's operations in Syria and Lebanon, and his deputy, Brigadier General Mohammad Hadi Haji Rahimi, along with five other officers. The building next to the embassy was reduced to rubble.

Israel did not formally acknowledge the strike, though the country has carried out hundreds of strikes on Iranian and Iranian-linked targets in Syria since 2011 and which rarely confirms them. American officials said the strike had been carried out by Israel, and they described the target as a meeting of officers who were coordinating the supply of weapons to Hezbollah and to other groups. Iran said the building was a diplomatic mission and that the attack violated the convention on diplomatic relations.

Timing of the strike was tied to the war in Gaza. Israel had been striking targets in Syria at a higher rate since October, and it had killed a senior officer of the guard in Damascus in December and another in January. Iran had absorbed those attacks without a direct response, and its government had said publicly that it would not let the killing of senior commanders go unanswered.

Reaction inside Iran was a demand for retaliation. The foreign minister said the attack would not go unpunished, and the chief of the guard promised a response at a time and place of Iran's choosing. Officials in Tehran also said the attack showed that the United States was complicit, since it had supplied the weapons that were used, and they rejected the argument that the target was a military facility.

Regional response was a set of warnings and evacuations. Syria's government condemned the strike and said it would defend its territory. Hezbollah said the killing would strengthen the resistance, and Israel said it had raised the readiness of its forces on the northern border and had called up reservists for air defence.

The building that was destroyed stood beside the embassy compound in the Mazzeh district of Damascus, and the Iranian ambassador said the dead had been guests at a meeting that was being held that afternoon. Rescue teams worked through the night to clear the rubble, and the Syrian government said the strike had also damaged a neighbouring residential block. Iran's mission to the United Nations sent a letter to the Security Council in which it said the attack had been carried out with American intelligence and American weapons, and it asked the council to condemn it.

Consequence of the first of April was the exchange of the following fortnight. Iran launched drones and missiles at Israel on the thirteenth of April, Israel struck near Isfahan on the nineteenth, and both sides described the damage as minimal. The strike on the consulate was the event that broke the pattern of proxy confrontation, and it did so because it killed officers who were too senior for Iran to ignore. What it established was that a war fought through intermediaries can become direct in a single night.
