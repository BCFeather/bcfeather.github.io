Tesla reported revenue of 25.17 billion dollars for the fourth quarter on 24 January and an operating margin of 8.2 per cent, less than half the level of a year earlier. It told investors that the growth in vehicle deliveries this year would be notably lower than the rate of last year. The revenue was slightly below the consensus, and the earnings per share met the forecast because the company earned more from the regulatory credits it sells to other makers. The company said the growth would be lower because the factories are between models, and the shares fell by about ten per cent the next session.

Price cuts are the explanation. The company reduced the prices of its models in several markets through the year to defend the volume, and the effect was a margin that fell faster than the units rose. A discount that is repeated is a price that the buyer stops believing. The gross margin excluding the credits was about 17 per cent, which is the number the analysts use to judge the car business on its own. The price cuts of the last year have compressed the margin, and the company reported an operating margin of 8.2 per cent, half the level of 2022.

Competition has changed. The Chinese makers have entered the segment where the company sells its smaller car, and they have done so at prices that the company cannot match without losing money on each unit. A competitor that is willing to lose money is a competitor that sets the price. The company has said the cheaper model will arrive in the second half of next year, and the delay is the reason the volume warning was issued now.

Energy storage is the brighter part. The storage products have grown faster than the cars, and the margin on them is higher, which is the reason the company has been describing itself as an energy firm as much as a carmaker. A product that is sold to the utilities is a product whose demand is set by the grid. The storage business deployed a record amount of capacity in the quarter, and the backlog extends into next year.

Autonomy is the promise that keeps the valuation. The company has said the software that drives the cars will become a subscription that earns more than the hardware, and the regulators in two states have begun to examine how the system is marketed. A promise that is regulated is a promise that can be delayed. The shares fell by about five per cent after the release, and the decline was limited by the expectation that the cheaper model would restore the growth.

What the quarter shows is that a company that cut its prices to keep its volume has discovered the limit of that strategy, and the growth now has to come from products that the competitors have not copied. A carmaker whose margin depends on the price it sets is a carmaker whose profit is a policy, and the policy has run out of room.
