Messenger RNA acquires a 7-methylguanosine cap at its 5′ end as soon as it emerges from RNA polymerase II, and the cap is then read by a heterodimer, the nuclear cap-binding complex. That complex accompanies the transcript through splicing and polyadenylation, and it is also the point at which export begins. The cap is added co-transcriptionally, so the complex is present while the transcript is still being made, and its cargo is a molecule in flux.

A paper in *eLife* on 16 September 2024 reports the first structure of the complex bound to an export factor. The factor is ALYREF, which connects the cap to the TREX complex and thereby to the nuclear pore, and the structure was obtained by cryo-electron microscopy. Cryo-electron microscopy suits the problem because the complex is large enough to resolve and flexible enough that crystallography has been difficult.

The contact is direct. The RNA recognition motif of ALYREF contacts both NCBP1 and NCBP2, the two subunits of the cap-binding complex, rather than docking on one of them and leaving the other free. Contacts across both subunits are less common than contacts with one, and the difference is what a regulatory interaction usually looks like.

That arrangement matters because the complex has several jobs. A factor that binds across both subunits can be sensitive to the state of the whole heterodimer, so that a conformational change induced by cap binding is transmitted to the export machinery. A factor that spans the dimer can read its closure, so cap occupancy and export competence become the same signal rather than two.

Comparison with unbound structures is where the mechanism lies. Conformational differences between the two states indicate what changes when ALYREF arrives, and those changes are the candidates for a switch that licenses export. If binding closes a cleft that is open in the apo state, then the closed form is the one the export machinery is recruited to.

Structure does not by itself show the order of events. Whether ALYREF binds before splicing is completed, or only after the transcript is mature, remains to be established, and the answer would decide whether export is gated on processing. Order can be inferred from kinetics, and the structural data supply the states between which a kinetic model would interpolate.

Biochemical and genetic data can test the interface, since mutations at the contact points should disrupt export while leaving cap binding intact. That is the experiment the structure invites. The wider significance is that a step usually described as a pathway now has a molecular handover, and handovers are where regulation tends to be installed. Such a separation of functions is the cleanest evidence available for a contact that the structure only suggests. A reconstituted export assay would show whether the interface the structure reveals is sufficient to move a transcript out of the nucleus. The handover is also a place where a drug could intervene, since a small molecule that blocked the interface would stop export without touching the cap itself.
