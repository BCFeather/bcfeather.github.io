Jannik Sinner beat Taylor Fritz in straight sets in the final of the ATP Finals in Turin, finished the tournament without losing a set, and ended the year as the top-ranked man in the sport. He had won his first major in Melbourne in January and his second in New York in September, and the week in Turin was the least dramatic of the three. A year with two majors and a year-end title is a year that would be the best of most careers.

Season was shadowed by a case that began in March. Two tests found a banned steroid in his system at a level measured in billionths of a gram, and the explanation his team offered was contamination from a physiotherapist who had used a spray on a cut finger. An independent tribunal accepted that explanation in August and cleared him to play. A trace measured in billionths of a gram is a trace that a laboratory can find and a tribunal has to interpret.

The case is not over. The world anti-doping agency appealed the decision to the court of arbitration for sport, and the appeal was scheduled for the following spring. A player who is competing while an appeal is pending is a player whose results carry an asterisk in some commentary, and the tour has no rule that removes one. A hearing that is set for the spring is a hearing that will decide whether the season is rewritten. A player who is cleared and then appealed is a player who answers the same question at every tournament.

Tennis is the reason the season stands on its own. Sinner's serve improved through the year, his return position moved forward, and his record against the rest of the top ten was the best on tour. A player who wins on hard courts, on grass and on indoor hard is a player who has solved the surfaces rather than one who has been lucky with a draw. A return that stands closer to the baseline is a return that takes time away from the server.

Comparison with his rival is the frame the tour prefers. Carlos Alcaraz won two majors as well, and the two of them split the four titles between them for the second year running. A sport with two dominant players in their early twenties is a sport with a story that will last a decade. A rivalry that splits the majors is a rivalry that sells tickets in every market.

What the year leaves unresolved is the appeal. A decision that arrives in the spring could remove results or impose a suspension, and either outcome would rewrite a season that has already been recorded. The record is what the ranking measures, and the ranking does not wait. A record that is already on the books is a record that an appeal cannot edit.
