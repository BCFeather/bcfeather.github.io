description: "The survey of openings showed the fewest vacancies since March 2021, and the number of people who quit fell, in a labour market that is loosening from the top."
---

Job openings fell to 8.8 million at the end of July, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 6 September, the lowest since March 2021. The number of hires was little changed and the quits fell to 3.5 million. The survey is published with a month's lag, and the July figure is the last that the Fed will see before its September meeting. The report is one of the three that the Fed has said it will weigh at each meeting, and the reaction in the bond market was muted because the figure was close to the forecast.

Decline is in the vacancies rather than in the employment. Firms have been posting fewer jobs and taking longer to fill them, which is the pattern of a market that has stopped bidding for workers. A vacancy that is withdrawn is a wage pressure that eases. The openings fell by about 340,000 on the month, and the decline was concentrated in the firms that employ more than a thousand people.

Openings per unemployed worker fell to about 1.5, from two at the peak of 2022. The ratio is the measure the Federal Reserve has cited as the sign of tightness, and its fall is the evidence that the imbalance is closing without a rise in the jobless rate. The rate of hiring was 3.5 per cent of the employment, the lowest since the start of the pandemic, and the layoffs were little changed at 1.5 million.

Sector detail shows where. The openings fell in the professional services, the health care and the transport, and they rose in the restaurants. A market that cools in the offices and holds in the kitchens is a market whose composition is changing. The openings in the information sector fell by a fifth from their peak, and the federal government posted more jobs as the spending bills passed. The openings in the restaurants are still above their level before the pandemic, and the owners say the hiring remains the main constraint on the hours they can open.

Quits are the confidence measure. A worker who quits expects to find a better job, and the fall in quits is the sign that the expectation has weakened. A quit that does not happen is a pay rise that is not demanded. The share of the workers who quit is now at its level of 2020, and the wage growth that follows the quits has slowed to about four per cent.

This report shows a labour market cooling through vacancies rather than through layoffs, and that is the path the Fed has been trying to engineer. The economists describe the pattern as a labour market that is normalizing without a recession, which is the outcome the Fed has said it wants. A market that cools through the vacancies is a market that the Fed can steer, and the steering is the point of the policy.
