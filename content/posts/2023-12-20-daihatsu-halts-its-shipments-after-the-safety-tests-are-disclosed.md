Daihatsu stopped the shipments of all its vehicles on 20 December after an investigation found that the crash tests on some models had been manipulated. The halt covers the domestic and the export shipments, and the company has said it will compensate the dealers and the suppliers. The announcement came after the transport ministry said it had found the irregularities in the tests that the company had run for the regulators. The company said the halt would last until the regulators approve the tests again, and the dealers have been told to stop the deliveries.

Test procedures were the problem. The inspectors found that the doors of the test cars had been modified so that the airbags would deploy correctly, and the practice had gone on for about three decades. A test that is changed is a safety that is not. The tests in question are the side-impact ones, and the regulators have said they will re-test the affected models. The inspectors found the irregularities in about six models at first, and the company's own review then found them in the wider range.

Its scale is what shocked the regulators. The company said about 64 models were involved, including the ones it builds for Toyota and for other brands, and the total is about 1.7 million vehicles a year. A small carmaker with a large reach is a problem that spreads. The company has about 8,000 employees in Japan, and the suppliers in the region depend on it for most of their work.

Regulators moved quickly. The transport ministry said it would inspect the plants, and the company has stopped the production at the domestic factories. A plant that is stopped is a payroll that waits. The ministry has asked for the internal documents, and the company has said it will cooperate with the investigation. The company has set aside about 100 billion yen for the compensation, and the suppliers have asked for the support.

Costs land on the brand. Daihatsu builds the small cars that Toyota sells in Asia and in the cities, and the trust in that name is the asset that the scandal touches. A name that is doubted is a price that is cut. The shares of Toyota fell by about 2 per cent, and the analysts said the direct cost would be small and the reputational one would not. The company sells about 1.7 million cars a year, and about half of them are built in Japan and the rest in the plants in Indonesia and Malaysia. The small cars are the entry point for the young buyers in the region, and the brand has about a fifth of the market in Indonesia.

What the disclosure shows is that the testing regime has a hole that the small makers can fall through, and the hole is the part the regulators must close. A scandal that is disclosed by the regulators rather than the company is a scandal that the management did not control, and the control is the question. The parent company has said it would review the governance of the unit, and the review is the part that the shareholders want.
