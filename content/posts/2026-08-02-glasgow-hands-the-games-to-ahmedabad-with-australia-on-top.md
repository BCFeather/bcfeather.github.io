Eleven days of competition in Glasgow closed on 2 August with the Commonwealth flag passed to India, and the medal table read like a return to an older order. Australia finished with 70 golds and 171 medals, England second on 29 golds, Canada third on 19, India fourth with 39 medals and Scotland fifth. Seventy-four nations took part, and the next edition in Ahmedabad in 2030 will be the centenary.

The hosts had a venue problem to solve before they had a sporting one. Glasgow staged the Games at existing facilities across the city rather than building a park, which is why the swimming, athletics and cycling sat within a few miles of one another and why the athletes' village was a hotel arrangement rather than a new complex. A smaller footprint changes the atmosphere as well as the budget, and the crowds at Scotstoun suggested the trade was worth making. A reuse budget is also a test of local goodwill, since the city has to absorb the disruption without the compensation of a new pool or track it can keep afterwards.

Australian athletics produced its best single day at a Commonwealth Games, seven golds and seventeen medals, and the men's 4x100 metres relay won in 38.07 seconds with a quartet that had been disqualified in Birmingham. The sprint depth behind that relay is the part worth noticing: four men under the qualifying standard in the same season is a system result rather than a lucky afternoon, and it follows a decade of investment in coaching and junior competition.

Josh Kerr won the mile in front of a home crowd, having broken the 27-year-old world record earlier in the season, and the race was a slow, tactical one until the last lap. Mel Woods of Scotland took the outstanding athlete award, which is given for conduct and contribution as much as for results, and the para powerlifting produced two world records for Nigeria, with Goodness Nwachukwu also setting a mark in the F42 discus. Behind the relay result sits a set of domestic meets that now run to the same timing standard as the international circuit, which is how a national programme turns juniors into finals.

Athlete welfare is the constraint that shapes these smaller Games. With 74 nations and a compressed programme, travel, classification and anti-doping logistics all scale down with the entry list, and the organizers leaned on a single medical hub rather than a village clinic per sport. That works when the site is compact and fails when it is not, which is the argument the next hosts will have to answer.

Ahmedabad in 2030 will be a different test. India's contingent finished fourth with 13 golds, its boxers took seven of fourteen titles, and the handover ceremony made the centenary the theme. Whether a city of that size can repeat Glasgow's reuse model, or will need new venues for a much larger programme, is the question that decides what the centenary edition actually costs.
