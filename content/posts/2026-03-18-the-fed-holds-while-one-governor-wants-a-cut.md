Three cuts at the end of 2025 took the policy rate down to 3.50 to 3.75 per cent, and the question entering 2026 was whether the committee would keep going or pause. On 18 March it paused for a second meeting, and the statement described activity as expanding at a solid pace, job gains as remaining low and inflation as still somewhat elevated.

Voting was not unanimous. Governor Stephen Miran dissented, preferring a quarter-point reduction, which is the position he has taken at earlier meetings. A single dovish dissent against a hold is a signal about the argument inside the committee and not about the outcome, and markets read it as a sign that the next move, whenever it comes, is more likely to be down than up.

The implementation decisions were routine and are worth stating. Interest paid on reserve balances stays at 3.65 per cent, the primary credit rate stays at 3.75 per cent, and the Board approved both unanimously. The statement added that the implications of developments in the Middle East for the United States economy are uncertain, which is a sentence that does a great deal of work in a document of that length.

Projections moved modestly. Growth in the fourth quarter of 2026 is now put at 2.4 per cent, a tenth higher than before, inflation projections were revised up, and the median path for the policy rate was left broadly intact at one cut this year and another in 2027. The long-run rate projection stayed at 3.00 to 3.25 per cent, the number that matters for every asset with a duration longer than a year.

Why a hold is defensible is arithmetic. Inflation is above target by enough to make a cut look premature, unemployment is low enough that the labour market is not crying out for support, and the energy consequences of a Middle East conflict are the kind of shock that a central bank should not look through until it knows whether it is transient. Waiting costs little when the data are ambiguous and the shock is unresolved.

Why a cut is defensible is arithmetic too. Job gains have been low for months, and a labour market that is not yet weak can weaken quickly once it starts. Real policy rates are restrictive against a long-run neutral level of about 3.1 per cent, and a committee that waits for unemployment to rise before easing will ease late. That is the dissent's argument, and the next two employment reports will test it.

What matters for markets is the path and not the decision. A hold with the median unchanged tells an investor that the committee expects to cut once and stop, which is a forecast of a slow cycle and not a pause before a fast one. Any portfolio positioned for several cuts this year is positioned against the median projection, and the projection is published on purpose.
