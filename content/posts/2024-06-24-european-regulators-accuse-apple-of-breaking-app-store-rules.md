title: "European regulators accuse Apple of breaking app store rules"

description: "The commission said the company's terms stop developers from steering users to cheaper offers, in the first formal charge under the digital markets law."
---

App store terms appeared to breach the digital markets act, the European Commission told Apple on 24 June, because they prevent developers from telling users about cheaper ways to pay. It was the first formal charge under the law, which took effect in March. The company has sixty days to respond, and the commission said it would publish a final decision before the end of the year. The charge was published as a statement of objections, which is the document the commission sends before it decides, and the company will answer it in writing. The commission's internal market commissioner said the case was about whether the law changed the behaviour of the platforms or only their paperwork.

Steering is what the rule is about. A developer who cannot mention a subscription bought on its own website cannot compete with the store's commission, and the commission's preliminary view is that the restriction is a barrier rather than a protection. A rule that forbids a sentence forbids a price. The commission has also opened an inquiry into the fee charged for a subscription in the first year, which is the higher of the two rates.

Security is the company's argument. It argues that the store's review and its payment system are what make the platform safe, and that the alternatives the law requires would expose users to fraud. A defense that rests on safety has to show that the risk is real. The developers who complained include the music services and the reading apps, both of which sell the same content outside the store at a lower price.

Remedies remain open. The commission can impose a fine of up to ten per cent of global revenue and require a change in the terms, and the company has offered a set of changes that the commission described as insufficient. A remedy that changes the contract changes the business model. The fine is calculated on worldwide turnover, and the company's revenue would make the maximum a figure of several tens of billions of euros.

Precedent is why the case matters. The same law applies to the other large platforms, and the outcome will set the standard for the fees they charge and the ways they describe their own services. A first case is a template. The other platforms have been changing their terms in anticipation, and the smaller ones say the changes are designed to keep the commission close to the old level. The other cases that the commission is preparing concern the fees that the stores charge the developers who choose to distribute outside them.

What the charge shows is that regulation of the digital market has moved from principles to contracts, and the contracts are where the revenue is. A law enforced case by case is a law whose meaning is written by the decisions, and the first decision is the one the lawyers will read.
