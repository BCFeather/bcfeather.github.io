title: "Cinemas take 747 million yuan across the May holiday"

description: "Five days of releases brought 747 million yuan to Chinese cinemas, with a dumpling entrepreneur's biopic leading a slate that had no clear favourite."
---

Five days of the May holiday produced 747 million yuan at the box office, according to the ticketing platforms, with more than ten new films competing for the same week. The total was modest by the standards of the spring festival, and the shape of the chart was unusual: no film ran away with the season, and the leader was decided by word of mouth rather than by pre-sales. The five days also overlapped with a holiday week in which travel spending rose faster than cinema spending, so the queue at the box office was competing with the queue at the railway station.

*The Dumpling Queen*, a biopic of the founder of a frozen-dumpling brand, took 192 million yuan across the holiday and finished first. It opened with a modest advance and then grew every day, which is the pattern of a film that audiences recommend rather than one that marketing pushes. Its daily take rose from Saturday to Monday, a pattern that distributors watch closely because it cannot be manufactured by buying screens. It led in twenty-nine provinces, an unusually wide spread for a drama about a woman who built a food business in Hong Kong.

*Play for Gold*, a crime story, took 134 million and finished second. Those two were the only new films to pass 100 million during the holiday itself. *Princess Mononoke*, the Miyazaki film that had never had a mainland release, took about 69.6 million, and a comedy called *The Door of Life* took 64.9 million.

The long tail did much of the work. *Ne Zha 2*, still in cinemas months after its January release, outgrossed several of the new titles on the second day of the holiday, which is a reminder that a market with one enormous film in it behaves differently from a market without one. Exhibitors kept screens for it because it still filled them. The film's persistence has changed how exhibitors plan the spring: a title that stays in the top five for four months is worth more screens than a new release that opens wide and fades in ten days.

The audience mix moved. Male viewers took a larger share than in previous spring holidays, and the average age of the audience rose slightly, which the platforms attributed to the biopic and to the crime film rather than to the animation. Families with children were a smaller part of the total than in the winter holidays, as they always are in May. Teenage audiences, by contrast, were slightly under-represented, which the platforms read as a consequence of the exam calendar rather than of the slate.

For the industry the lesson is about scheduling. A holiday with ten releases and no clear winner leaves money on the table, because screens cannot be reallocated fast enough to follow demand. Distributors have argued for years that the holiday calendar should be spread out, and a week like this one is the argument in data form.
