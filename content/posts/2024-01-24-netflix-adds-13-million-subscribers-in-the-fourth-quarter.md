Netflix added 13.1 million paid subscribers in the fourth quarter and ended the year with 260.8 million, according to the results published on 23 January. Revenue rose to 8.83 billion dollars. The figure was the largest for any quarter since the pandemic, and the shares rose by about ten per cent in the session after the release. The company also said it would raise the price of its standard plan in several markets, the first increase since the password rules took effect.

Growth of that size is now the exception. The company has said the market for new members is close to saturated in the countries where it makes most of its money, and the additions came largely from the regions where the price is lower. A subscriber who pays less is a subscriber who adds less to the revenue. The average revenue per member fell in the quarter because the growth came from the markets where the subscription costs a few dollars a month.

Password sharing explains part of the quarter. The company began charging households that share an account outside the home, and the members who were converted produced a one-off gain that the management said would not repeat. A policy that converts a free rider into a payer is a policy with a limited supply. The company said about half of the additions came from the households that had been sharing an account, and it expects that source to shrink this year.

Advertising is the next business. The cheaper tier that carries the advertisements has grown, and the company said it would build the technology to sell the inventory itself rather than through a partner. An inventory of attention is a business that depends on the number of hours rather than on the number of members. The advertising tier has about twenty-three million users, which is small relative to the total, and the company has said the inventory sells at a lower price than the television networks charge. The advertising tier added members faster than the ad-free one, and the company said that tier would be the fastest growing part of the business this year.

Live events are the third. The company has bought the rights to a weekly wrestling programme and has said it would show more sport, which is the content that the rivals have used to hold their audiences. A live event is a programme that cannot be watched later. The wrestling deal is worth about five billion dollars over ten years, which is the largest commitment the company has made to a single programme.

What the results show is that a company that has finished building its subscriber base has to earn more from each member, and the two routes it has chosen are advertising and the price. A company that converts its growth from members to revenue per member is a company that the market prices on the margin rather than on the subscriber count.
