Producer prices rose 7.4 per cent in the year to November, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 9 December. The reading was down from 8 per cent in October and the slowest since May 2021. The forecast had been 7.2, and prices rose 0.3 per cent on the month. The monthly increase was the smallest since December 2020. The figure had been 11.7 per cent in March, the highest in the series.

Goods were the reason. Energy prices for producers fell 3.3 per cent on the month, and food rose 0.3. The index for processed goods fell 0.2, and for unprocessed goods 5. The pipeline of price increases had emptied faster than at the consumer level. Services rose 0.4 per cent, and they had been the stickier part. Trade services, which measure margins at wholesalers and retailers, rose 0.7, and transportation and warehousing fell 0.8. The margin category is volatile and the statisticians say it accounts for a third of the index. Food prices for producers rose 0.3 per cent, and they had risen 9 per cent over the year.

Core producer prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 6.2 per cent over the year and 0.4 on the month. That figure is the one that feeds into the Fed's models of pipeline inflation. It had peaked at 9.7 in March and had fallen every month since. The transportation index fell 0.8 per cent, and diesel prices were 20 per cent below their peak.

Two days earlier the consumer price figures had shown 7.1 per cent. The two measures together gave the clearest picture of the year: the consumer rate was falling, and it was falling from the goods side first, and services were the last part to turn. The index is published a day before the consumer report in some months, and it is revised twice.

Rates went up by 50 basis points on 14 December, and the Fed said the inflation data had improved. Its projections showed inflation at 3.1 per cent for 2023, and the actual figure was 4.1. The committee said it would need to see several more reports before it paused.

Economists use it to forecast corporate margins, because it measures the prices that firms pay. The producer index has a smaller weight in policy than the consumer one, and it is published earlier. The margin component suggested that retail margins were still above normal in the autumn. Producer inflation fell below 5 per cent in the spring and reached 0.8 per cent in December 2023. The producer index turned negative in the autumn as energy prices fell, and it stayed there until 2024. The consumer rate followed with a lag of about three months. The producer index excluded rent, and that is why it fell before the consumer one.
