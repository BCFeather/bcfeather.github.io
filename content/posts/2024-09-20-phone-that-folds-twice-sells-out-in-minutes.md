A Chinese manufacturer put a phone with two hinges on sale on 20 September, a device that opens from a normal handset into a tablet-sized screen, and the first production run sold out within minutes. The price is about two thousand eight hundred dollars, which is more than most laptops. The pre-orders had run into the millions, according to the company, and the queue at the flagship store in Shenzhen was reported as long as the street.

Engineering is the story. Two hinges mean two folds in the panel, and a folding panel has to survive being bent in two places without creasing at either, which is a mechanical problem that the first foldables did not solve. A device with two hinges is a device that tests the supply of the flexible material as much as the design. The hinges are rated for a large number of folds, and the panel is a single sheet that has been laminated rather than two screens joined, which is what makes the double fold possible.

Form is a statement about how people use a phone. A screen that expands to ten inches is aimed at readers, at people who watch video on a train, and at people who want a tablet without carrying one, and the price tells you that the manufacturer is not yet targeting the mass market. A device that costs a laptop is a device for the people who buy both. The software has to decide which part of the screen to show when the device is in each of its three states, and the transitions are the test that reviewers use.

Market is the reason it exists. The premium segment in China has been contested by a domestic brand that lost its access to the newest chips and has rebuilt its position on design and on software, and a foldable is the product that shows the design capability most publicly. A flagship is a marketing document that ships in a box. The brand's other phones use a domestic chip that was designed to replace the imported one, and the foldable is the product that shows the chip in its best light.

Supply is the constraint. The panel is made by a small number of suppliers, and the yield on a double-folding display is lower than on a single fold, which is why the price is where it is. A component that is hard to make is a component that sets the price of the product. The price includes a margin that the component costs do not justify alone, and the company's executives have said that the next generation will be cheaper.

What the launch shows is that the folding form has moved from a curiosity to a category with a flagship, and the price will fall when the yield rises. A device that sells out at three thousand dollars is a device whose next version will be the one that matters. The launch came a week after the rival's annual event, which is a scheduling decision that the manufacturer has made before and will make again.
