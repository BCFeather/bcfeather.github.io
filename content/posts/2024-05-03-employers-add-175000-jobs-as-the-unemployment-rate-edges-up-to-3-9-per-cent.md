American employers added 175,000 jobs in April, below the 240,000 that forecasters expected, and the unemployment rate rose to 3.9 per cent. The two previous months were revised down by a combined 22,000, which made the report weaker than the headline suggested. The revisions matter more than the month, because a labour market that was weaker than reported in March is a labour market that had already begun to turn.

Composition is what changed. Hiring in health care and in leisure continued, and construction added fewer workers than in the winter, while government employment grew at half the pace of the previous quarter. A total that is more evenly spread is a total that is more likely to persist. The household survey, which is separate from the payroll count, showed a larger rise in unemployment, and the two surveys have diverged by more than usual all year.

Wages rose 0.2 per cent on the month, the smallest increase in more than a year, and 3.9 per cent on the year, which is the slowest since 2021. A wage number that cools without a rise in unemployment is the combination the committee has described as its goal. The number is close to the pace that the committee judges consistent with a stable unemployment rate, which is why the report was read as a return to normal rather than as a warning. The committee has said it wants to see the annual rate settle near three per cent before it stops worrying about inflation, and the month's number is a step in that direction.

Participation held at 62.7 per cent, which means the labour force did not shrink as the hiring slowed, and the economists who watch it said the report showed supply and demand coming into balance rather than demand collapsing. A balance that is reached by adding workers is a balance that costs nobody a job. The share of prime-age workers in employment held near its highest level in two decades, which is the measure the economists prefer to the headline rate.

Market reaction was a rally. Yields fell, the probability of a September cut rose, and the shares that had been hurt by high rates led the gain. A weak report that lowers the cost of borrowing is a report that the market treats as good news, which is an inversion that only happens late in a cycle. The rally was strongest in the shares that had fallen most during the rate rises, which is the pattern of a market that is trading the policy rather than the economy.

What the month shows is that a labour market can cool without breaking, and the test of whether this was a genuine turn will be the next two reports rather than this one. A single month cannot establish a trend, and the committee has said it wants three reports in a row before it treats cooling as established.
