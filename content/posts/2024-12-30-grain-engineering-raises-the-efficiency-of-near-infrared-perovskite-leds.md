Perovskite light-emitting diodes compete best in the near infrared, where they outperform established materials. A paper in *Nature Communications* on 30 December 2024 improves the film rather than the molecule. A boundary is where a carrier can recombine without emitting, so controlling boundaries is the most direct route to efficiency. Boundaries are the first thing a film engineer changes, and changing them is what the paper reports rather than a change of material. A film engineer changes boundaries because boundaries are where the losses are, and the physics of that is not in dispute.

Grain structure is the variable, since a film is an array of crystals and light emission happens within each of them. Boundaries between grains are where non-radiative losses concentrate, and losses are what an efficiency figure measures. Uniformity of grain size is also a uniformity of emission wavelength, and wavelength uniformity is what a display requires. Wavelength uniformity is a display requirement before it is a physics result, and the requirement is what drives the engineering.

Grain engineering means controlling nucleation and growth so that the crystals reach a size and a uniformity that the device needs. A film with few boundaries loses less, and a film with uniform grains emits at one wavelength. Lower photon energy reduces the penalty from deep traps, which is a physical reason the near infrared is the easier target. Deep traps matter less at low photon energy, and the difference is a reason the near infrared commercializes first.

Two properties from one control, which is what makes the approach attractive. Near-infrared emission is easier than blue because the defects that quench luminescence matter less at a lower photon energy. Stability under bias is the half of the problem that efficiency papers do not address, and the omission is structural in the field. Bias stability is where the field's next papers will be, and the field knows it already.

The material's promise is therefore most realizable in the range where the applications are displays and optical communication. Stability is the remaining obstacle, since a perovskite that emits well can still degrade under bias. Solution processing is cheap and variable in equal measure, and the variability is what manufacturing has to solve.

A paper about efficiency is a paper about half of a device problem. The fabrication is solution-based, which is what makes the cost attractive and the reproducibility difficult. Large-area uniformity is a process question, and a laboratory number does not answer it.

Uniformity across a large area is the requirement that a manufacturing process imposes. The reported efficiency is a laboratory number, and the distance to a product is measured in hours of operation rather than in per cent. Hours of operation is the unit that matters for a product, and a percentage is the unit that matters for a paper. A product requirement is measured in hours and a paper's claim is measured in per cent, and the two units are not interchangeable.
