Samsung presented its Galaxy S22 range at an event on 9 February, and the largest model in it, the S22 Ultra, arrived with a slot for a stylus and a shape that anyone who owned a Galaxy Note would recognize. The Note line had been discontinued the year before, after a decade in which it defined the market for large phones with pens, and Samsung has now folded that product into its flagship. The other two models, the S22 and S22+, are smaller and lighter than the phones they replace.

Prices start at 799 dollars for the S22 and reach 1,199 for the Ultra with 256 gigabytes of storage. The cameras on the Ultra include a 108-megapixel main sensor and two telephoto lenses, one of them with a periscope that reaches ten times optically, and the software merges frames to make up for the small size of the sensors. The battery in the smallest model holds 3,700 milliamp-hours, a figure that reviewers noted with some concern given the power the display draws.

Supply was the constraint on the launch. The pandemic had shut factories in Vietnam and disrupted the shipping of components, and the shortage of chips that began in 2021 had reached the makers of image sensors and power management circuits. Samsung is one of the few companies that produces both memory and logic chips, and its own foundries gave it an advantage that most of its rivals lacked.

Competition shaped the design decisions. Apple had taken the premium end of the market with the iPhone 13, and its own chip, designed in-house, set a standard for efficiency that Samsung's processors had struggled to match. The Korean company answered with a chip built on a four-nanometre process and with a display that reaches 120 hertz, and it kept the expansion slot for memory cards that Apple does not offer. Google's Pixel line, smaller in volume, competed on the software instead.

The market that these phones enter is shrinking. Global shipments of smartphones fell about 9 per cent in 2022 from the year before, the first annual decline in a decade, and the average buyer kept a phone for three years rather than two. The premium segment held up better than the rest, because the people who pay 1,000 dollars for a phone are less sensitive to the price of fuel, and the middle of the market moved to cheaper models from Chinese makers.

A phone that carries a pen is a niche product with a loyal audience, and Samsung has built its flagship around that audience while making the cheaper models for everybody else. The Note survived for ten years because it did one thing that no other phone did, and the S22 Ultra inherits the customers without the name. Whether the pen is worth the price is a question that each buyer answers alone, and most of them answer no. Samsung shipped about 260 million phones in 2022 and kept the largest share of the market by volume, and the profit in that share came from the cheaper models rather than from the flagship.
