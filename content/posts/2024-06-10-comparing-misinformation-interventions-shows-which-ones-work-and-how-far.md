Interventions against misinformation are tested one at a time, and a study that compares several in one design is a study that can rank them. A paper in Nature Human Behaviour on 10 June 2024 compares fact-checking, media literacy and other approaches. Ranking is the output that a platform or a regulator needs before investing in one. Fact-checking corrects a claim after it spreads, and correction is a remedy that arrives late. A prompt that warns a reader before exposure differs from a correction after exposure, and the timing is a design variable that changes the size of the effect. A literacy intervention that works in a laboratory may fail in a feed, and a feed is where a platform would deploy it. A curriculum that scales is one delivered by a school rather than by a platform, and schools are slower than feeds and more durable.

Media literacy teaches a reader to resist, and teaching is a remedy that arrives early. Early and late are different products, and a comparison of products is what the paper supplies. Misperception is the endpoint, and an endpoint measured immediately after an intervention is a weak measure of a durable change. Durability is what a literacy programme claims, and a claim of durability needs a delayed measurement. Timing is what a platform can control, and control is why the comparison has practical weight. Deploying in a feed introduces competition with attention, and attention is the resource an intervention spends.

Delayed measurement is where this literature is thinnest. The interventions reduce misperceptions, and the reduction is modest and uneven. Modest reductions matter at scale, and scale is what a platform operates at. Weight in practice is what a behavioural paper rarely has, and this one earns it by measuring both orders.

Operating at scale is what makes the modest end of the range worth reporting. Backfire is the failure that platforms worry about, and worry is why the design includes a check for it. Checks are what turn a worry into a finding, and a finding into a policy. Both orders are reported, and reporting them is what makes the comparison complete.

Policy use depends on format, since a platform can deploy a prompt and cannot deploy a curriculum. A prompt is cheap and a curriculum is expensive, and cheapness is what a platform will choose. Choosing by cost is a rational response, and the response is why the comparison matters.

Comparison across formats is the paper's real contribution, and the contribution is practical. Practical results age as the information environment changes, and change is constant in this field. Constant change is the limitation a reader should hold in mind.

Holding it in mind is what keeps a headline from becoming a policy. A study of interventions is not a study of belief, and belief is what a democracy has to maintain. Maintaining belief is a slow problem, and slow problems are addressed by institutions rather than by prompts.
