---American employers added 303,000 jobs in March, according to the report published by the labour department on the fifth of April, well above the 200,000 that economists had expected and the strongest month since May 2023. Unemployment rate fell to 3.8 per cent from 3.9, and it has now stayed below four per cent for more than two years, which is the longest such stretch since the 1960s. Figures for January and February were revised up by a combined 22,000.

The Composition of the gain was broad and weighted towards the sectors that have driven hiring since the pandemic. Health care and social assistance added about 81,000 jobs, leisure and hospitality about 49,000, and construction about 39,000. Government employment rose by 71,000, mostly in local schools, and manufacturing added a small number. Temporary help services, which are often read as a leading indicator, continued to shed positions.

Wages grew by 4.1 per cent over the year, which is slower than the 4.3 per cent recorded in February and consistent with a gradual return to the pace that the Federal Reserve considers compatible with two per cent inflation. Average hourly earnings rose by 0.3 per cent in the month. Economists said the combination of strong hiring and moderating pay growth was the picture the central bank had been waiting for, though the level of wage growth remained above its target.

Participation in the labour force held at 62.7 per cent, and the rate for workers between twenty-five and fifty-four rose to 83.4 per cent, which is close to the highest on record. Immigration has added to the supply of workers, and the Congressional Budget Office revised its estimate of the labour force upwards in the winter for that reason. Analysts said the increase in supply was one explanation for why hiring could be strong without producing the wage spiral that had been feared.

Market reaction was to push back expectations for a rate cut. Yields on two-year Treasury notes rose, and traders reduced the probability of a cut in June to about fifty per cent. The report followed data that showed inflation running at 3.5 per cent in March, and officials said in speeches over the following weeks that they were in no hurry. The dollar strengthened against most currencies.

The Meaning of the fifth of April is a labour market that has defied the forecasts of a slowdown for two years. Hiring at this pace with unemployment below four per cent is unusual at any stage of a cycle, and the fact that it has persisted while interest rates are at their highest in two decades is the puzzle that economists have been arguing about. Whether the strength is a sign that the economy can sustain higher rates or a sign that the policy has not yet bitten is the question that the rest of the year was going to answer. The report also showed that the number of people working part time for economic reasons fell, which is one measure of underemployment.
