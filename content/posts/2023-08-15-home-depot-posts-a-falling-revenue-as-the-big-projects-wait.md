Home Depot reported a revenue of 42.9 billion dollars for the quarter to July, down 2 per cent, and a profit of 4.7 billion. The earnings per share of 4.65 dollars beat the forecast of 4.45. The comparable sales fell by about 2 per cent, and the shares rose about 3 per cent as the guidance held. The revenue was about 500 million above the estimate, and the operating income was about 6.5 billion. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 1 percentage point from the revenue. The company said the comparable sales in the United States fell by about 2 per cent, and the one in Canada by about 3.

Big projects were weaker. The transactions over 1,000 dollars fell by about 5 per cent, and the ones under 1,000 by about 2. The company said the customers had been postponing the kitchen and the bathroom work as the borrowing costs rose. The number of the transactions fell by about 2 per cent, and the average ticket fell by about 1. The company said the appliance sales had been the weakest, and the paint and the garden the strongest.

Its pro business held. The sales to the contractors fell by about 1 per cent, and the one to the do-it-yourself customers by about 3. The company said the lumber prices had fallen by about 15 per cent, and the volume was about flat. The company has about 2,300 stores, and the one of the new formats is about 20 this year.

Margins were about flat. The gross margin was about 33 per cent, and the operating margin about 15.2. The company said the shrink had risen a little, and the pricing had been stable through the quarter. The company said the inventory was about 22 billion dollars, down about 5 per cent, and the turns had improved. The company said the average ticket was about 90 dollars, and the one of the pro customers about 500.

Its guidance held. The company expects the revenue to fall by about 2 to 5 per cent for the year, and the earnings per share to fall by about 7 to 13. The market had expected a cut, and the shares rose on the confirmation. The company said the housing turnover was the key, and the one has fallen to about 4 million a year from about 6. The company said the dividend would rise by about 10 per cent, and the buyback was about 15 billion dollars on the annual basis.

This quarter is the one in which the rates reached the renovation. A retailer that sells 42.9 billion dollars while the big projects fall is a retailer waiting for the borrowing costs to come down, and the waiting is the part the guidance assumes. The shares are up about 5 per cent this year, and the company is the weaker of the two large home retailers.
