A fossil that preserves an entire body is a fossil that can be reconstructed in three dimensions, and the earliest jawed fishes were known almost entirely from fragments. A site in Chongqing that preserves whole animals changes what can be said about them. A body complete enough to mount is a body that can be compared, character by character, with its relatives.

A Konservat-Lagerstatte from the early Silurian, of Telychian age and about 436 million years old, containing diverse jawed fishes with complete bodies. The paper appeared in *Nature*, 28 September 2022. A Konservat-Lagerstatte is a deposit in which soft tissues and articulated skeletons survive, and the condition that produces one is rare enough that each discovery reorganizes a field.

The dominant species, *Xiushanosteus mirabilis*, is a placoderm-like stem gnathostome that combines characters from the major placoderm subgroups and foreshadows the transformation of the skull roof from the placoderm to the osteichthyan condition. A skull roof that shows both conditions is a skull roof that records a transition rather than an endpoint. A skull roof that shows both conditions records a transition and not an endpoint, and the transition is what the placoderm to osteichthyan transformation looks like in a single animal.

The second species, *Shenacanthus vermiformis*, is a chondrichthyan with extensive thoracic armour plates that had not been seen in the lineage, including a large median dorsal plate as in placoderms, combined with a conventional chondrichthyan body plan. Armour on a chondrichthyan is a character that the textbooks place far earlier in the lineage than the animal's age, and the fossil disagrees with them. Whether the armour was inherited from a common ancestor or evolved independently in this lineage is the question the plates raise.

A Lagerstatte is a deposit in which soft tissues and articulated skeletons survive, and the condition that produces one is rare enough that each discovery reorganizes a field. A site that yields several complete species is a site that supplies the morphological data that the fragments could not. Several complete species from one bed is a data set that the fragments could never supply, and a phylogenetic analysis needs exactly that.

Together the two species reveal a diversification of jawed vertebrates in the early Silurian that the record had not shown, and they supply detailed insights into whole-body morphology at a point when the group was new. A group that is diverse at its first appearance is a group that had been evolving for longer than its oldest fossils suggest. The inference is an argument from absence, and absence in a fossil record this patchy is weak evidence.

Pairing the two papers in one issue, one on teeth and one on bodies, is the kind of pairing that a journal arranges when two discoveries answer the same question from opposite ends. Origins are read from the earliest fragment and the earliest whole body, and both have moved back in time. Two papers in one issue make the case jointly, and the joint case is stronger than either alone.
