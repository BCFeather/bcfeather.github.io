Tesla reported deliveries of about 444,000 vehicles for the second quarter, a decline of nearly five per cent from the same period a year earlier, and the number is the first year-on-year fall in deliveries that the company has reported. Production exceeded deliveries by a margin that the company will have to clear, which means inventory. A manufacturer that builds more than it sells is a manufacturer that discounts, and the discount is already in the price list.

Comparison with the market is the awkward part. Sales of electric cars grew in most large markets in the first half, and a company that shrank in a growing market has lost share rather than met a weaker demand. A loss of share is a slower problem than a fall in demand, and it is harder to reverse. A share that slips for three quarters is a share that the market treats as a permanent loss rather than a pause.

Product line is old. The two models that carry the volume have been on sale for several years without a major redesign, and the newer models are expensive and low-volume. A range that has not been refreshed is a range that competes on price, and the price cuts that the company made this year are evidence of that. A redesign takes three years from the drawing board, which means the cars that compete in 2025 are being designed now.

Competition is now local. In China, the largest electric-car market, a set of domestic manufacturers sell cars with more equipment at lower prices, and the company's share of that market has fallen. A competitor that is domestic has a cost advantage that an importer cannot match. A domestic rival that sells at a lower price is a rival that can absorb a tariff and still undercut. A price cut is a decision that a competitor can match within a week, and the ones that matched were already cheaper.

Energy business is the part that grew. Storage deployments rose and the margin on them is better than on cars, and the company has begun to describe itself as an energy company as well as a carmaker. A business that sells batteries to utilities is a business with a different cycle from one that sells cars to households. A storage project is a contract with a utility that runs for twenty years, and the revenue is more predictable than a car sale.

What the quarter shows is that the first mover's advantage has expired. A company that defined the category is now one of several, and the question for investors is whether the software and the charging network are enough of a moat to defend a margin. A moat made of software is a moat that a competitor can license, and the charging network is the part that cannot be copied. A company that is valued as a technology business is valued on a growth rate that its car sales no longer deliver.
