Jannik Sinner won the Australian Open on 28 January, beating Daniil Medvedev 3-6, 3-6, 6-4, 6-4, 6-3 in three hours and forty-four minutes. It was his first major title and the first for an Italian man since 1976. He had lost in the semi-final here last year and had never been past the quarter-final of a major before this fortnight.

Comeback is the statistic. He lost the first two sets in an hour and then won three in a row, which no player had done in the final here since 1965. A match that turns on fitness is a match that the younger player wins. The fifth set lasted fifty minutes, and the runner-up called for the trainer at the changeover with cramp in the legs. The third set was the longest of the match and the only one that went to a tie-break, and the winner saved a break point at 4-4.

Serve is the change. He had been broken twice in the first set and then held every service game in the fifth, and his first-serve percentage rose by ten points after the third set. A shot that improves during a match is a shot that the opponent cannot plan for. The coach has changed the service motion over the winter, and the players who faced him in the early rounds said the ball was arriving faster.

Draw was the hardest in the tournament. He beat three players ranked in the top ten, including the defending champion, and he did it without dropping a set until the final. A run that goes through the seeds is a run that the rankings have to accept. The ranking will put him in the top four, and the seeding for the rest of the year will follow. The semi-final against the defending champion lasted four hours, and the final was the third five-set match in a row for the runner-up.

Rivalry is now the story of the tour. The winner is twenty-two, the runner-up is twenty-seven, and the two have played each other eleven times in three years. A rivalry that produces five-set finals is a rivalry that the sport sells. The final was the longest at the event since 2012, and the television audience in Italy was the largest for a tennis match in a decade. The tour now has four players under twenty-five in the top ten, and the tournaments have begun to schedule them apart to keep the finals varied.

What the fortnight shows is that a player can win a major by improving within it, and the improvement is what separates the ones who reach finals from the ones who win them. A country that has waited forty-eight years for a men's champion is a country that will now expect more, and the expectation is the next pressure. The prize money is the largest in the sport, and the winner's share was about three million dollars.
