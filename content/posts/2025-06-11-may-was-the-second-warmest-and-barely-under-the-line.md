description: "Copernicus reported on 11 June that May 2025 was 1.40 degrees Celsius above the pre-industrial average, the second warmest May on record and only the second month in 23 below 1.5 degrees."
---

A record that is broken by a small margin is still a record, and a record that is missed by a small margin is the more interesting event. May 2025 was the second warmest May in the ERA5 series, 0.53 degrees Celsius above the 1991 to 2020 average at an absolute temperature of 15.79 degrees, and 1.40 degrees above the estimated pre-industrial average.

Comparison with last year is the number that matters for the trend. May 2025 was 0.12 degrees cooler than May 2024, which had been the warmest, and 0.06 degrees warmer than 2020, which had been third. Three years at the top of a 175-year series, separated by hundredths of a degree, is what a plateau looks like when the plateau is hot.

The 1.5 degree line is a convention and not a threshold in the physics. May was only the second month in the last 23 in which the global average was not more than 1.5 degrees above the pre-industrial level, the other being February, and the run of months above it had stretched from September 2023 to April 2024 and again from October 2024 to March 2025.

La Nina was supposed to help, and it did. The Pacific was in a cool phase through the spring, which usually lowers the global average by a few hundredths, and the cooling was visible in the tropical Pacific and in the sea-surface temperature of the eastern basin. A year that stays near record warmth without an El Nino is a year in which the background has risen.

The regional pattern is the part that people experience. Europe had a warm spring with a dry April in the south, parts of Asia had an early heat season that closed schools, and the Arctic had a May in which sea ice extent was near the low end of its recent range. A global average of 15.79 degrees is an abstraction, and the anomalies behind it are not, which is why the bulletin carries both.

Attribution is now routine and specific. The World Weather Attribution group has repeatedly found that individual heat events of this size would be rare without greenhouse gases, and the Copernicus monthly bulletin does not attempt attribution, which is why the two are read together. A number without an attribution is a number that a minister can call weather, and one with an attribution is a number that a court can use.

What would settle the year is whether the autumn brings the first month below 1.5 degrees since 2023 or another run above it. A single month under the line proves nothing about the target and everything about how thin the margin has become. A plateau at this level is the hardest kind of record to report, because nothing in it looks like an event. The bulletin is a measurement, and the argument about what it means is held elsewhere, mostly by people who have never plotted a monthly anomaly.
