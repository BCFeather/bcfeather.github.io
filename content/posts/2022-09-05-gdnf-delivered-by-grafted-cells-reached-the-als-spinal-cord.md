A growth factor that protects motor neurons has been tried in motor neuron disease for two decades, and the trials have failed because the protein could not be delivered to the right place at the right concentration. A cell that makes the protein where it is needed is the alternative. Patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis lose motor neurons progressively, and a delivery that lasts is what the disease requires.

Surgeons transplanted human neural progenitor cells transduced with GDNF unilaterally into the lumbar spinal cord of 18 participants with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis in a phase 1/2a trial. The paper appeared in *Nature Medicine*, 5 September 2022. A unilateral transplant into the lumbar cord is a design that gives each patient an internal control, and the control is the leg that did not receive cells.

Its primary endpoint of safety at one year was met, with no negative effect of the transplant on motor function in the treated leg compared with the untreated leg. A leg-to-leg comparison within the same patient is the control that a surgical trial can supply, and it is a reasonable one. A leg-to-leg comparison is a reasonable control for a surgical trial and a weak one for a progressive disease that affects both legs unequally.

Tissue analysis of 13 participants who died of disease progression showed graft survival and GDNF production, and one administration provided support cells and growth factor for up to 42 months. A graft that survives for years is a delivery system that a daily injection cannot match. One administration provided cells and growth factor for up to 42 months, and the duration is what the cell-based approach buys. Benign neuromas near the delivery sites were a common incidental finding at post-mortem, and an incidental finding of that kind is the sort that a safety report has to record even when it caused no symptoms. A structural change that nobody noticed during life is a change that a regulator will ask about. The neuromas were benign and incidental, and incidental is a word a safety report has to be able to defend.

The design was small and uncontrolled for efficacy, and the comparison between treated and untreated legs is not a test of whether the disease slowed. A safety endpoint that is met is the permission to run the trial that would answer the efficacy question. The efficacy trial has not been run, and the permission is the whole result.

The progenitor choice that differentiate into astrocytes is deliberate, because astrocytes are the cells that surround motor neurons and that survive in the spinal cord. A delivery cell that becomes part of the local tissue is a delivery cell that does not have to be rejected. Progenitors that become astrocytes settle among the motor neurons, and settling is what the delivery requires.

A growth factor that reached its target for 42 months without a pump is the technical achievement, and the achievement is what the next trial will build on. Efficacy remains unknown, and the unknowns are the ones that a phase 2 study is designed to reduce. Efficacy is the question the next trial must answer, and no trial has answered it.
