title: "IMF cuts its forecast for the world economy to 2.8 per cent"
date: "2023-04-11T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The fund lowered its growth projection for the year and warned that the banking strains and the high inflation would weigh on the investment."
---

Its spring report cut the forecast for world growth to 2.8 per cent for 2023 on 11 April, down from 2.9 in January and from 3.4 per cent last year. The fund said the outlook was the weakest in more than three decades apart from the global recessions. The forecast for 2024 was also lowered to 3 per cent. The fund said the risks were tilted to the downside, and the main ones were the banking strains and the debt of the low-income countries. About 60 per cent of the low-income countries are in or near the debt distress, and the fund has been urging the restructuring.

Advanced economies were cut to 1.3 per cent. The fund raised the forecast for the United States to 1.6, which reflects the resilient consumer, and it cut the one for the eurozone to 0.8 as the energy costs and the tightening bite. Britain was the weakest of the large economies at minus 0.3 per cent. The forecast for Germany was cut to minus 0.1 per cent, and the one for Japan was raised to 1.1 as the reopening of the borders lifted the services. The fund said the energy prices had fallen but the core inflation in the services was still running above the target in most of the advanced economies.

Emerging economies were steady at about 3.9 per cent. India was raised to 5.9 per cent, and China was kept at 5.2 as the reopening lifted the services. The fund said the divergence between the two groups was the widest in decades. The forecast for Brazil was raised to 0.9 per cent, and the one for Nigeria to 3.2. The fund said the reopening of China would add about 0.2 percentage points to the world growth through the trade and the travel.

Inflation was the other theme of the report. The fund expects the global headline to fall to about 7 per cent this year from 8.7, and it said the core measure would come down more slowly. The central banks were urged to keep the tightening until the price pressure was clearly easing. The inflation for the emerging economies was forecast at about 8.6 per cent, and the one for the advanced at about 4.7. The fund said about 90 per cent of the advanced economies are expected to see the inflation above the target at the end of the year.

Banking strains were mentioned. The fund said the failures of the two American regional banks and the rescue of Credit Suisse had added to the uncertainty, and the credit would be tighter than the policy rate alone implies. The estimate is that the tighter credit could subtract about 0.3 per cent from the world growth. The credit conditions have tightened for the smaller businesses, and the fund said the effect would be clearer in the second half. The estimate of the growth loss from the tighter credit is between 0.2 and 0.4 percentage points, depending on the region.

This is the fund's annual argument for cooperation. A forecast of 2.8 per cent is below the 3 per cent that the fund calls the stall speed, and the number is the one the finance ministries will quote. The fund has been calling for the cooperation on the debt and the trade since the pandemic, and the report repeats the appeal. A forecast that is below the stall speed is a forecast that the finance ministers will use to argue for the support, and the argument has been made for three years now.
