German consumer prices rose by 6.4 per cent over the year to June, according to the preliminary figures, and by 6.8 per cent on the harmonized measure used for the eurozone. The May reading was 6.1 per cent. The figures were a little above the forecast of 6.3. The figure is the second consecutive month of acceleration, and the headline has fallen from the peak of 8.8 per cent in the autumn. The statisticians said the end of the 9-euro transport ticket had added about 0.2 points to the services.

Food is still the problem. The prices of the food rose by about 13.7 per cent, and the one of the dairy and the bread by about 15. The government's relief package for the groceries has ended, and the effect is the one the statisticians flagged in the release. The prices of the vegetables rose by about 12 per cent, and the one of the meat by about 10 as the feed costs stayed high. The discount chains have been losing the share to the supermarkets, and the analysts say the customers are buying less but better.

Energy eased. The prices fell by about 1.4 per cent over the year as the base of the summer of 2022 dropped out, and the electricity was about 10 per cent cheaper than a year ago. The fuel rose by about 1 per cent on the month, and the carbon price has added about 2 cents a litre. The gas fell by about 20 per cent over the year, and the heating oil by about 25 as the base of the spring of 2022 dropped out. The government has said the price brakes on the electricity and the gas will run to the end of the year.

Services held. The prices of the services rose by about 5.4 per cent, and the one of the restaurants by about 8. The rents in the largest cities rose by about 6 per cent, and the vacancy has been the lowest on record. The wages rose by about 5 per cent in the first quarter, and the unions have settled for about 6 in the public sector.

Regional figures were mixed. The inflation in North Rhine-Westphalia was about 6.3, and the one in Bavaria about 6.5, and the one in Berlin about 6.6. The differences are smaller than a year ago, which the economists read as the sign of the broad pressure. The inflation in the eastern states was about 6.9 per cent, which is higher than the west for the first time in a year.

This release sets up the eurozone figure. A national rate of 6.4 per cent with the food at 13.7 is the arithmetic that keeps the bank on course for July, and the July meeting is the one that will decide the autumn. The eurozone figure is published on 30 June, and the forecast is about 5.6 per cent.
