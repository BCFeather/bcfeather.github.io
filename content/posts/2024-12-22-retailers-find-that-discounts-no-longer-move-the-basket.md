---

Retailers entered December with discounts that had started in October, and the season's results show a customer who has learned to wait. A promotion that runs for six weeks is a promotion that trains a shopper to expect the price to fall again, and the shopper who waits is a shopper who buys less at full price. A season that starts in October is a season that has no opening day. A season that begins in October is a season with no opening day.

Inventory is the reason the discounts began early. A supply chain that normalized after several disrupted years left stores with stock they had ordered when they expected shortages, and a store with excess stock cuts the price before the season rather than after it. A discount in October is a discount that protects a margin in December. A store that ordered for a shortage is a store that owns the mistake.

Mix has shifted towards cheaper goods. A shopper who is paying more for housing and for food is a shopper who trades down on everything else, and a retailer that sells both a branded item and a private label is a retailer that sees the shift in its own data. A basket that is the same size but worth less is a basket that a store has to sell more of. A private label that is twenty per cent cheaper is a label that a shopper tries once.

Online channel is where the discount lives permanently. A marketplace that runs a price comparison on every listing is a marketplace that pushes the cheapest seller to the top, and a brand that wants to be visible has to match a price that a discounter sets. A brand that refuses is a brand that disappears from the search results. A brand that is visible only when it discounts is a brand that has rented its shelf. A brand that discounts to stay visible is a brand that is renting the shelf it once owned.

Loyalty programs are the response. A retailer that collects data on its customers can offer a personalized price, and a personalized price is a discount that does not train the whole market to wait. A scheme that rewards a member is a scheme that hides the discount from the casual shopper. A member price is a price that the shopper cannot compare across stores. A member price is a price that cannot be compared across stores, which is the point.

What the season shows is that promotion has become a permanent feature rather than an event. A customer who has been trained to expect a sale is a customer who will not pay the listed price, and the retailer that survives is the one whose cost base allows it to sell at the discounted level. A cost base that allows a permanent discount is a cost base that a competitor cannot match.
