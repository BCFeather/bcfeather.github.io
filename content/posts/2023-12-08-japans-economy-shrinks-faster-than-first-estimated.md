Japanese output fell at an annual rate of 2.9 per cent in the third quarter, the revised figures said on 8 December, worse than the fall of 2.1 per cent in the first estimate. The revision was about 0.8 points worse than the forecast, and the economy has now contracted in two of the last three quarters. The figure was below the forecast of a fall of 2 per cent, and the economy has contracted in two of the last three quarters.

Inventories were cut. The firms had built fewer stocks than the first data showed, and the contribution of the inventories turned negative. A stock that is smaller is a quarter that is weaker. The inventories had added about 0.3 points in the first estimate, and the revision turned the contribution to about minus 0.2. The retail inventories fell by about 1 per cent, and the wholesale held as the distributors waited for the orders.

Capital spending was cut too. The business investment fell by about 0.4 per cent rather than the small gain in the first estimate, and the machinery orders have been falling for three months. A machine that is not ordered is a capacity that is not added. The construction spending rose by about 1 per cent, and the public works were the part that held as the private building slowed. The machinery orders have fallen for three quarters, and the survey of the large manufacturers expects a decline in the winter.

Consumption was weak. The household spending fell by about 0.6 per cent as the wages lagged the prices, and the shoppers cut the durable goods. A basket that shrinks is a recovery that waits. The real wages have fallen for about twenty months, and the household spending has fallen in four of the last six quarters. The durables fell by about 1.5 per cent, and the services were flat as the households cut the discretionary spending.

Its central bank is in a corner. The economy is shrinking while the inflation is above the target, and the exit from the negative rates depends on the wage talks in the spring. A corner that is narrow is a decision that is deferred. The bank has said the policy will stay until the wages rise sustainably, and the economists expect the first move in the summer. The core inflation was 2.8 per cent in October, and the wage growth is about 1 per cent in the small firms and about 3 in the large ones.

What the revision shows is that the Japanese economy has stalled while the prices have not, and the stall is the argument for the delay. A contraction that is revised deeper is a contraction that the bank must explain, and the explanation is the spring wage round. The government is preparing a package of the stimulus, and the bank has said the fiscal support will not change the case for the exit.
