Consumer prices rose 7.9 per cent in the year to August, the statistics office reported on 30 August. The figure was above the 7.8 forecast, and it was up from 7.5 in July. The harmonized rate, which is used for comparison, was 8.8, and it had been 8.5. Consumer prices rose 7.9 per cent, above the 7.8 forecast and up from 7.5 in July.

A fuel subsidy had ended, and that was the cause. The government had cut the tax on fuel by 30 cents a litre in June, and the cut expired at the end of August. The price of fuel rose 20 per cent in the month, and the office said it had added 0.6 points to the rate. A fuel tax cut of 30 cents a litre expired at the end of August, and fuel rose 20 per cent.

Food rose 16 per cent in the year, and the price of bread rose 20. Energy rose 35 per cent, and the price of gas was 10 times its level of 2019. The office said the relief packages had softened the effect, and that the poor households were the most exposed. Food rose 16 per cent, and bread 20, while energy rose 35.

Inflation was expected to peak in the autumn, the bank said. It said the figure would reach 10 per cent in October, and that it would fall to 7 in 2023. Its president said the bank would raise rates until the inflation fell, and that the process would take two years. The bank expected 10 per cent in October, and 7 per cent in 2023.

A 65 billion euro package was announced in September. It capped the price of gas at 40 cents a kilowatt hour from October, and it paid the difference. It also capped the price of electricity, and it said it would finance the plan with a windfall tax. The economy grew 0.1 per cent in the second quarter, and the institute said it would contract in the third. The chemical industry had cut output, and the car makers had moved production abroad. The government said the country would avoid a deep recession. Gas was capped at 40 cents a kilowatt hour, and a windfall tax paid for it.

Inflation reached 11.6 per cent in October, which is the highest since 1951. It fell to 10 in November and to 8.6 in December, and the cap on gas prices took effect in January. The bank raised to 2 per cent by December, and to 4 in 2023. The rate fell to 7.4 per cent in March 2023 and to 3.8 in October. The economy contracted 0.4 per cent for the year, and the bank raised to 4 per cent in September 2023. The rate was 3.2 per cent at the end of 2023.
