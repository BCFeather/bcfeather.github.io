Its deposit rate was raised to 3.5 per cent on 15 June, a quarter-point increase and the eighth since the summer of 2022. The main refinancing rate rose to 4 per cent. The statement said the inflation was set to stay above the target for too long, and the council would keep the rates at the restrictive levels. The council also said it would stop reinvesting the bonds it holds from the asset purchase programme from July. The deposit rate has risen by 400 basis points since the summer of 2022, which is the fastest cycle in the history of the euro.

Forecasts changed. The staff now expects the inflation to be about 5.4 per cent this year instead of 5.3, and about 3 per cent next year. The forecast for the growth was cut to about 0.9 per cent this year from 1.0, and the one for the next year to about 1.5. The staff expects the inflation to fall to about 2.2 per cent in 2025, which is the first time the target has been in sight. The forecast for the core was raised to about 5.1 per cent this year, and the one for the services to about 4.8.

Wages are the concern. The staff said the negotiated pay was rising by about 4.5 per cent, and the unit labor costs had grown by about 5 as the productivity fell. The services inflation was about 5.2 in May, and the bank has said it is the measure that the policy has to change. The unemployment rate was about 6.5 per cent in April, and the employment has grown by about 2 per cent over the year.

Officials also signalled July. The president said another increase in July was the base case unless the data changed, and she declined to say what would happen after the summer. The market had expected the guidance, and the euro rose by about 0.5 per cent against the dollar. The market priced about a 60 per cent chance of a rise in July, and about 4 per cent for the peak of the rate.

Hawks were quiet. The members who had wanted a 50 basis-point rise in the spring accepted the quarter point, and the statement said the council would decide the pace and the size meeting by meeting. The split is now about the autumn rather than about the summer. The German members had been the most vocal about the need for a larger increase, and the council compromised on the quarter point.

This decision is the one the bank had promised in May. A bank that raises the rate and cuts the growth forecast is a bank that has decided the prices matter more, and the decision is the one the governments will have to live with. The bank has been criticized for being late to the inflation, and the criticism has faded as the rate has caught up.
