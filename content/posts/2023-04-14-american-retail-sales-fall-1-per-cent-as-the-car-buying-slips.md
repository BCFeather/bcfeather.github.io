American retail sales fell by 1 per cent in March, the second decline in three months, as the households spent less on the cars and the petrol. The economists had expected a fall of about 0.4 per cent. The sales were up by 2.9 per cent over the year, which is below the pace of the prices. The control group has been flat or lower in four of the last five months, and the trend is the one the forecasters have marked down.

Vehicles did the damage. The sales at the car dealers fell by about 1.6 per cent, and the petrol stations fell by about 2.4 as the pump prices came down. The two categories account for about a quarter of the total, and both are the ones that the rates hit first. The furniture fell by about 2.4 per cent, and the electronics by about 1.2, as the households stopped replacing the appliances. The building materials fell by about 0.3 per cent, and the garden equipment by about 1, as the spring season started slowly in the colder states.

Sales in the control group were flat. That measure strips the cars, the petrol, the building materials and the restaurants, and it feeds the calculation of the consumer spending in the GDP. The economists had expected a rise of about 0.3 per cent, and the miss is a drag of about a tenth of a point on the quarter. The online sales were helped by the spring promotions, and the restaurants by the Easter weekend.

Online sales rose by about 1.9 per cent, and the restaurants added about 0.1 per cent as the bookings held. The department stores fell, and the clothing was flat as the discounts continued. A shopper who buys the services and skips the goods is a shopper who is trading down. The credit card balances are growing at about 5 per cent a year, and the delinquency rate on the cards has started to rise from the low of the pandemic.

This report fits the picture of a consumer who is still spending but has less room. The saving rate has fallen to about 4.6 per cent from the peak of the pandemic, and the credit card balances are rising. The wage growth is running behind the prices for the lower paid. The retail shares fell about 0.5 per cent on the day, and the discounters outperformed the department stores again.

Traders treated the number as a sign of a slowing economy rather than of a recession. The ten-year yield fell by about 5 basis points, and the dollar weakened. A retail sector that falls 1 per cent while the jobs report adds 236,000 jobs is a consumer that is cautious rather than absent. The consumer is the part of the economy that has carried the growth, and a flat quarter from the shops is a warning rather than a verdict.
