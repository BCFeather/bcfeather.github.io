Two years have been spent adjusting to a technology that can produce a competent undergraduate essay in a minute, and the responses have diverged sharply. Some departments have banned the tools in examinations, some have moved assessment to oral defence, and some have decided that the tool is part of the subject and teach it accordingly. A policy that varies between departments is a policy that a student experiences as arbitrary.

Variation is explained by the subject. A mathematics department can set problems whose answers cannot be guessed by a language model, and a history department can set an essay that a model can draft. The disciplines with a tradition of closed examination have adapted least, because their assessment was already the least exposed. A closed examination is an assessment that a model cannot sit, which is why the sciences adapted first. A seminar that requires a student to defend a claim is a seminar that tests understanding.

Detection has largely failed. Tools that score a text for machine authorship produce false positives, and a student who is accused on the basis of a score has no way to prove the negative. An institution that relies on detection is an institution that will eventually punish the wrong person. A false positive is an accusation that a student cannot disprove, and the cost falls on the accused. A score that a vendor's tool produces is a score that a disciplinary panel cannot cross-examine.

Labour question is the one that administration avoids. A model that can draft feedback on a thousand essays is a model that saves a teaching assistant's weekend, and a department that bans the tool for students while using it for marking has a rule that will not survive scrutiny. A lecturer who marks with a model is a lecturer who has decided the tool is acceptable for employment. A teaching budget that is cut is a budget that makes the model attractive to the people marking.

Deeper issue is what a degree certifies. A degree that certifies the ability to write an essay certifies something that a machine now does cheaply, and a degree that certifies the ability to judge an argument certifies something that is scarcer. The institutions that rethink the certificate will be the ones whose graduates are still legible to employers. A certificate that names a skill is a certificate that employers price, and the price moves with supply. A skill that is scarce commands a wage, and a wage is a signal that students read.

What the technology has exposed is that much assessment measured compliance with a format rather than understanding. A task that a model can complete is a task that was testing the format, and the reform that follows is overdue for reasons that have nothing to do with the model. A format that a model can produce is a format that measured compliance rather than thought.
