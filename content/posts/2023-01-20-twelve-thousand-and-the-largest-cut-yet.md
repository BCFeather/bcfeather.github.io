Alphabet said on 20 January that it would cut about twelve thousand jobs, which is roughly six per cent of its workforce and the largest reduction in the company's history. The chief executive, Sundar Pichai, announced the decision in an email to staff that was published within the hour, and employees in the United States were told first, with the rest of the world following by the end of the quarter. The reductions were to be completed by the end of March, and the company said it would contact affected employees country by country over the following weeks.

The company had spent two years hiring as if the advertising market would grow forever. It had added tens of thousands of people to cloud, hardware and research divisions, opened offices in cities where it had never had one, and expanded a set of perks that had become a recruiting tool in their own right. It had also built a hardware business and a cloud business on the assumption that the advertising surplus would pay for them, which is the assumption the cut tested.

Advertising did not grow forever. Growth in search advertising slowed in the second half of 2022 Growth in search advertising slowed in the second half of 2022 as advertisers cut budgets, and the company's shares fell by more than a third over the year, which made it the worst performer among the large technology firms and put pressure on a management that had never faced an activist shareholder campaign. The headcount had almost doubled since 2019, and the recruiting freeze announced in July had slowed the growth without stopping it.

The email described the cut as a decision to become leaner and to focus on a smaller number of priorities, and it named artificial intelligence as the area where hiring would continue. The severance package was sixteen weeks of pay plus two weeks for each year of service, with health coverage extended for the same period, which was more generous than the American minimum and less generous than what some of the company's rivals had offered. It also closed several experimental projects, and the reorganization that followed in the spring merged two of its artificial intelligence units. The shares had fallen by about thirty-nine per cent in 2022, and the cut was read as an attempt to stop the fall.

By then the pattern was a sequence. Microsoft had cut ten thousand two days earlier Microsoft had cut ten thousand two days earlier, Amazon had announced eighteen thousand, Meta had removed eleven thousand in November, and Salesforce had cut ten per cent at the start of the month. The industry that had defined the previous decade of white-collar employment was now defining its first correction.

A company that once hired for ideas now budgets for margins. The twelve thousand were told in an email that thanked them for their contributions, and the shares rose by three per cent on the day. The three per cent was the market's verdict on the cost of the severance, and it was a generous one.
