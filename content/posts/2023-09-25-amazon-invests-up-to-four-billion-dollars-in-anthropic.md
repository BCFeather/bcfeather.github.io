Amazon said on 25 September that it would invest up to four billion dollars in Anthropic, the startup behind the Claude models, as part of a partnership that makes the company a preferred supplier of computing. The first tranche of 1.25 billion dollars converts to equity, and the rest follows on milestones. The company said the investment does not include a seat on the board, and the two firms will collaborate on the chips and the software that runs them.

Money that arrives as computing is the shape of the deal. Anthropic has agreed to run its training on the Amazon cloud and to use the custom chips that the company designs, and a large part of the investment will come back as revenue. A round that is spent on the landlord is a round that the landlord books. The partners did not disclose the price of the computing, and the analysts noted that the credits are valued at the list price rather than at a discount.

Model makers need the capacity. Training a large model consumes thousands of the graphics processors, and the startups have been competing for the scarce supply with the established firms, which is why the cloud credits have become a currency. A credit that buys chips is a currency that the supplier issues. The company has said it will spend about 50 billion dollars on the data centers this year, most of it in America, and the capacity is being built for the customers it has signed.

Anthropic has been the smaller of the two leading labs. The company has about 500 employees and a valuation of about 20 billion dollars after the round, and it has positioned itself as the careful alternative to OpenAI, with the clients in the banks and the law firms. A lab that sells to the cautious buyers is a lab that sells trust. The revenue is reported to be about 500 million dollars a year, small against the valuation, and the investors are paying for the growth rather than the sales. The company has said it will keep the ownership below 20 per cent, which is the level at which the accounting would change.

Cloud share is the strategic prize. Amazon holds about a third of the market, and Microsoft has taken share by bundling its own models with its cloud, which is the competition the deal is meant to answer. A partnership that locks a model to a cloud is a partnership that locks the customers too. The regulators have begun to look at the ties between the clouds and the model makers, and the deals have been structured to avoid the notification thresholds.

What the deal shows is that the compute has become the scarce asset in the industry, and the firms that own it can buy the models with the credits they print. A market where the models are bought with the compute is a market where the infrastructure sets the price, and the price is the part the startups cannot control.
