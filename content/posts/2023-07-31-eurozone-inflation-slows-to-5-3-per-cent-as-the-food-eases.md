title: "Eurozone inflation slows to 5.3 per cent as the food eases"

description: "The July flash estimate showed the headline falling from 5.5 per cent, while the core held at 5.5 as the services and the wages stayed firm."
---

Eurozone consumer prices rose by 5.3 per cent over the year to July, according to the flash estimate, down from 5.5 per cent in June and in line with the forecast. The core measure, which strips the energy, the food, the alcohol and the tobacco, was about 5.5 per cent, unchanged from June, which is the number the bank watches. The figure is the lowest since January 2022, and the headline has fallen by about half from the peak of 10.6 per cent in October. The unemployment rate was about 6.4 per cent in June, which is the lowest on record for the euro area.

Energy eased again. The energy prices fell by about 6.1 per cent over the year, and the base of the summer of 2022 will keep the annual rate negative through the autumn. The gas in the wholesale market was about 30 euros per megawatt hour, which is about 90 per cent below the peak of last summer. The electricity fell by about 10 per cent over the year, and the fuels by about 4 as the base dropped out. The storage was about 85 per cent full at the end of July, which is well above the target of 90 for the winter.

Food slowed. The prices of the food, the alcohol and the tobacco rose by about 10.8 per cent, down from 11.6, and the one of the fresh food by about 8. The easing has been faster in the vegetables than in the meat, and the processors say the costs of the feed are still high. The prices of the oils and the fats fell by about 12 per cent over the year, and the one of the dairy by about 5.

Services held. The prices of the services rose by about 5.6 per cent, and the one of the industrial goods by about 4. The negotiated wages have been rising by about 4.5 per cent, and the bank expects the services to hold around 5 into the winter. The air fares rose by about 8 per cent, and the one of the hotels by about 7 as the summer bookings held.

National figures were mixed. The inflation in Germany was about 6.5 per cent, and the one in France about 5.0, and the one in Spain about 2.1. The Baltic states were above 8, and the Netherlands about 5.1. The inflation in Italy was about 6.4 per cent, and the one in Portugal about 3.5.

This report leaves the September meeting open. A headline at 5.3 with the core at 5.5 is the split that has defined the year, and the council has said it will decide the pace meeting by meeting. The council meets on 14 September, and the market sees about a 40 per cent chance of a pause.
