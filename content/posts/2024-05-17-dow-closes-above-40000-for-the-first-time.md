Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 40,000 for the first time on 17 May, having first traded through the level the day before, and it has risen about six per cent during the year. The index of thirty large companies took five months to travel from 38,000 to the new level. The level was first crossed during the session on 16 May, and the close a day later was the one that the record books count.

Composition is the reason the milestone matters less than it looks. The index is price-weighted, which means a hundred-dollar share moves it twice as much as a fifty-dollar one, and the companies in it are older and more industrial than those in the index that most funds track. A benchmark that is not the one people own is a benchmark that measures sentiment rather than returns. The index's thirty companies include several that have split their shares and several that have been replaced, which means the level is not comparable across the decades that the chart covers.

Rotation is the story of the year. The shares that had been bought for their defences in 2022 fell behind in 2023 and have led in 2024, and the interest-rate expectations that changed in the winter are what moved the money. A rotation is a market that is repricing the path of policy rather than the prospects of the firms. The shares that led were the industrial and financial ones that had lagged during the years of near-zero rates, and the technology names that had carried the previous rally contributed less.

Round number is a sentiment indicator. Round numbers draw attention from people who do not follow the market, and the historical data show that crossing them predicts nothing about the following year. A milestone is a story that gets written because it is a round number. Interest rate expectations moved more than earnings during the month, and the report on prices that arrived two days earlier was the immediate cause of the buying.

Risks are unchanged by it. Earnings have grown, and the valuations that the market pays for those earnings are still above their long-run average, which means part of the gain is a multiple rather than a profit. A gain that is a multiple is a gain that can be given back without any bad news. The number is quoted in every news bulletin and it is owned by nobody in particular, which is why it survives as a symbol while the funds that people actually hold track something else.

What the crossing shows is that the stock market's recovery from the sell-off of 2022 is complete in nominal terms and that the divergence between the price-weighted index and the wider market has never been wider. The gap between the record and the experience of an investor is the gap between the index and the portfolio, and the milestone is a story about the first rather than the second.
