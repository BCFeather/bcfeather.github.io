---

One day a year, a business can publish something false, attach a joke to it, and count the attention as a success. The genre has been growing since the early 2010s, when the technology companies discovered that a press release with an absurd product in it would be shared further than a real one, and it now runs from the breweries to the airlines to the banks.

Nobody writes the rules of the genre down, and yet they are consistent. The joke has to be obviously false to anyone who reads to the second paragraph, it must not touch a regulated claim, it must not be about a safety feature or a price, and it must be retracted by the end of the day. A company that breaks one of those rules spends the next week explaining itself to a regulator, which is why the legal departments now read the drafts that the marketing departments used to send on the afternoon of the thirty-first.

What the best examples do is use the day to say something true. A railway that announces a carriage with a library in it, a supermarket that advertises a loyalty scheme for cats, a software company that offers a keyboard with one key, these are jokes about the product that the company actually makes, and the audience understands that the joke is affectionate. The worst examples are the ones that mock the customer, and those are the ones that are remembered.

What has become difficult is the timing. A prank competes with the news, and a day on which a real event happens is a day on which a joke looks tasteless. The newsrooms dislike the first of April for this reason, because they have to check every item that arrives and they cannot check it fast enough. A story that is a joke and a story that is a rumour look identical in an inbox, and the cost of getting it wrong is paid by the outlet.

In China the tradition has taken a local shape. The companies that participate are the platforms and the consumer brands, the form is a short video rather than a press release, and the audience expects the joke to be gentle. A prank that involves a queue, a fake discount or a fake product launch will produce a customer service problem within an hour, and the brands that have learned this now shoot the video and hold it until the evening.

What the day measures, in the end, is the confidence of a company. A business that can make a joke about itself is a business that expects to be understood, and a business that cannot is one that is managing a reputation rather than a product. The pranks that work are the ones that come from the product team, and the ones that fail are the ones commissioned from an agency with a brief about engagement, which is a brief that produces a joke about nothing.
