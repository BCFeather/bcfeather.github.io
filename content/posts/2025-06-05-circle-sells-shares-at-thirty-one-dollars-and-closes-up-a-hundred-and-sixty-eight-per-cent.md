A stablecoin issuer is a company whose product is a promise that one dollar is worth one dollar, and the market on 5 June valued that promise at a large premium. Circle Internet Group priced its initial public offering at 31 dollars a share, above the range it had marketed, and the stock closed its first session at 83.23 dollars, which is a gain of 168 per cent and one of the strongest debuts of the year.

Business model is simple and unusual. Circle earns interest on the reserves that back USDC, which are held in money market funds and in short Treasury bills, and it passes some of that income to the partners that distribute the token, Coinbase chief among them. A company whose revenue is a share of the federal funds rate is a company that makes more money when rates are high.

Supply of the token is the growth metric. USDC in circulation had grown to more than 60 billion dollars by the spring, and each dollar of circulation produces interest income, which means the revenue line moves with adoption rather than with a price. A stablecoin is a utility with a balance sheet, and the balance sheet is the product.

Regulation is the reason the deal could be priced at all. The GENIUS Act, which the Senate had advanced in May, would create a federal framework for dollar-backed tokens with reserve, disclosure and audit requirements, and a company that has been lobbying for those rules for years is a company that benefits from being the compliant one. A rule that raises the cost of entry raises the value of the incumbent.

Risks are the ones that a stablecoin has always had. A run on the token would force the sale of reserves, a failure of a banking partner would break the redemption promise, and a rival token issued by a larger platform could take share without taking on the same compliance cost. The prospectus lists all three, and the market read them as distant.

Listing is also a signal about the sector. Crypto companies had spent two years avoiding public markets after the collapse of FTX, and a successful debut at a premium reopens a route that venture investors had written off. A window that opens for one issuer usually opens for several.

What would settle the valuation is whether circulation keeps growing after the listing and whether the interest income survives a cutting cycle. A token whose revenue falls when the Federal Reserve cuts is a token that needs volume, and volume is the number that the next two quarters will report. A stablecoin issuer is a narrow bank without deposit insurance, and the market has decided that a licensed narrow bank is worth more than the interest it collects. A company that earns its margin from the level of rates is a company that the market has valued on a growth story it has not yet delivered.
