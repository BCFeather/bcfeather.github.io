A team that wins one Cup can be a team that had a good spring, and a team that wins two in a row is a team with a method. The Florida Panthers beat the Edmonton Oilers 5-1 in Game 6 at Amerant Bank Arena on 17 June, closed the series 4-2, and lifted the Cup on home ice for the second straight year against the same opponent.

Reinhart produced the game of a century. He scored four goals, matching the Final record set by Maurice Richard in 1957 and becoming only the second player in league history to score four times in a series-clinching game, after Babe Dye in 1922. Two of them were into an empty net, which is what a team does when it is protecting a lead and its opponent has pulled a goaltender.

The goaltending at the other end was the quieter story. Sergei Bobrovsky made 28 saves and was beaten once, by Vasily Podkolzin, after the result had been decided, and the Panthers have now won both of their Cups in front of their own crowd, which only seven franchises had done before them.

Bennett took the Conn Smythe. He led the playoffs with 15 goals and set a league record for road goals in a single postseason with 13, which is a statistic that describes a forward who scores when the matchup is against him and the crowd is hostile. A trophy for a player who is not the most famous on his own line is a trophy for the way the team was built.

The construction is the argument. Florida has spent four seasons in the final and won two of them, with a roster built on forechecking, shot blocking and a defence that concedes the blue line and defends the slot. A model that reaches four finals in four years is a model that other general managers have already begun to copy.

Edmonton's problem is the familiar one. A team with the best player in the world has now lost two finals in a row, and its depth beyond the top two lines has been the difference in both, which is what a salary cap does to a roster built around two large contracts and a thin bottom six. Connor McDavid said the better team won, and he was right about the series and wrong about the margin.

What the repeat changes is the argument about dynasties. A third title would put this group among the handful of teams that have won three in a row, and the core is signed for long enough that the question is live in October rather than in June. A fourth final in four seasons is the number that makes this a dynasty argument and not a coincidence, and the tax code will decide whether it continues. Bennett is the fourth captain or alternate to be named the most valuable player of the postseason in five years, which says something about how this roster was assembled and nothing about how it was paid for.
