In the first ten months of 2026, social logistics handled 312.4 trillion yuan of goods, 4.8 per cent more than a year earlier, while logistics revenue rose 4.2 per cent to 12.3 trillion, according to the federation that compiles the measure, which published the figures on 28 November. Both rates were a tenth of a point slower than the nine-month readings. The 312.4 trillion is a value measure of goods moved, so it carries prices inside it.

Prices explain the gap. Services stayed cheap and recovered only mildly, and the express market shows the turn most clearly: revenue of 1,298.15 billion yuan grew 6.6 per cent, faster than parcel volume, and revenue per parcel rose 2.7 per cent to 7.69 yuan, which the federation attributes to a campaign against below-cost competition that has continued to take effect. A unit price that rises with volume is the reversal the policy was meant to produce.

As the total grew, its composition moved faster: industrial goods logistics grew 5.3 per cent, and in October equipment manufacturing demand rose 10.6 per cent while high-tech manufacturing rose 17.6 per cent, the fastest monthly rate in nearly five years. Electronic industrial equipment, computer peripherals and electronic components led, at 46.8, 44.7 and 34.8 per cent, a supply chain shifting towards machines that carry more value per kilogram. October figures are monthly and noisier than the ten-month totals, and they arrive with the year-end peak still ahead.

Where the movement reversed was imports: inbound logistics fell 0.6 per cent, with crude oil down 15.1 per cent and refined products down 20.6 per cent, while the value per unit of imported goods rose. An import figure that falls in volume terms while the value per tonne rises describes a substitution in what the country buys, and the drop in crude volumes is the largest single component of it. Refined products fell faster still, so the heavy end of the import book is shrinking twice over.

Ahead of the domestic total ran the corridors: rail moved 4.41 billion tonnes, up 1.0 per cent, container rail-water traffic rose 8.9 per cent and rail-road traffic 48.4 per cent, 18,600 China-Europe trains were dispatched, 14.6 per cent more than a year earlier, and 12,100 Central Asia trains at 1.9 per cent, while the land-sea corridor moved more than 1.13 million twenty-foot equivalent units. Cross-border trucking also logged a first shipment to combine international transit documents with green-channel treatment, which removes an inspection at the border.

Costs fell at the margin. Major operators spent 94.5 yuan for every 100 yuan of revenue, half a yuan less than in the first three quarters, supply-chain contract orders rose nearly 14 per cent and spending on digital transformation more than 20 per cent, as the largest operators move towards integrated logistics. The totals are value measures, so a price fall shrinks them while tonnage grows, and the widening gap between the two into the year-end peak is the number to watch.
