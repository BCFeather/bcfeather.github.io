On the month, prices rose 0.1 per cent. The National Bureau of Statistics published the figures on 9 September, and consumer prices rose 2.5 per cent in the year to August. The reading was down from 2.7 per cent in July and the lowest since February 2021. Food was the driver. Pork prices rose 22 per cent over the year but had begun to fall on the month, and vegetable prices fell 3 per cent as the summer harvest came in. Grain rose 3 per cent, and cooking oil rose 7 per cent because of the global shortage of sunflower oil. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, was 0.8 per cent. Pork was the largest single item in the food basket, and it had turned from a drag into a support over the year.

Producer prices rose 2.3 per cent over the year, down from 4.2 per cent in July and the slowest since January 2021. The decline reflected falling commodity prices and a weak construction market, and it was the tenth consecutive month of deceleration. Factory gate prices for steel and cement had fallen, and the property slump was the reason.

Chinese and American inflation had not been this far apart in two decades. That gap was the argument officials used to say that demand was too weak rather than too strong, and it supported the case for stimulus. It also meant that the central bank did not need to follow the Federal Reserve upwards, and it did not.

Weak consumption explained the number. Retail sales had grown 2.7 per cent in July, and household surveys showed a preference for saving. The property market had contracted for a year, and construction had slowed, which cut demand for materials. The zero-covid policy limited travel and services, and those prices rose more slowly than goods. The producer price index for mining fell 4 per cent, and for manufacturing it rose 1 per cent.

Economists said the measures were modest relative to the shortfall in demand. The government had announced 19 measures in August to support the economy, including infrastructure spending and cuts to fees for small firms. The central bank cut its main rate twice in the year, and the effect on prices was small. Prices stayed low through the autumn. Consumer inflation was 2.1 per cent in November, and producer prices turned negative in October for the first time since 2020. The reopening in December did not produce the surge in prices that some had predicted, because demand recovered more slowly than supply. Household deposits rose 15 trillion yuan in the first eight months, which is more than in the whole of 2021.

Alongside the price figures, the statisticians reported that the GDP deflator had fallen for three quarters, which is the measure economists use for the broad price level. That fall is unusual outside a recession, and it was the clearest sign that the economy was running below capacity. The debate about how much stimulus was needed continued into the following year.
