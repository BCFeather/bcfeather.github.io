description: "The quarter to July grew by 5.7 per cent, with the American stores up 6.4 and the e-commerce up 24, and the company lifted the guidance for the year."
---

Walmart reported a revenue of 161.6 billion dollars for the quarter to July, up 5.7 per cent, and a profit of 7.9 billion. The earnings per share of 1.84 dollars beat the forecast of 1.71. The shares rose about 1 per cent, and the company raised the guidance for the year for the second time. The revenue was about 2 billion above the estimate, and the operating income rose by about 6 per cent. The company said the inflation in the food had eased to about 3 per cent, and the prices were about 5 per cent higher than a year ago.

Groceries did the work. The comparable sales in the United States rose by about 6.4 per cent, and the grocery was about three quarters of the gain. The company said the customers had been buying more of the private label and the smaller packages, which is the sign of the trading down. The grocery now accounts for about 60 per cent of the American sales, and the share has been rising for two years. The average basket grew by about 3 per cent, and the number of the transactions by about 2.

General merchandise was weaker. The apparel fell by the high single digits, and the home goods by the mid single digits. The company said the inventory was down about 8 per cent, and the clearance was lighter than a year ago. The company said the toys and the electronics had been the weakest, and the seasonal goods were cleared at the discounts.

E-commerce grew by about 24 per cent. The pickup and the delivery were about 30 per cent of the online orders, and the advertising grew by about 35 per cent. The marketplace of the third-party sellers was the part the margins come from. The advertising business earned about 0.7 billion dollars in the quarter, and the margins are about three times the retail ones.

Its international segment grew by about 10 per cent. The sales in Mexico rose by about 12, and the one in China by about 18. The Flipkart in India was the largest contributor, and the company said the market would be the one it invests in. The international sales were about 29 billion dollars, and the currency effects had subtracted about 2 points.

Its guidance was raised. The company expects the earnings per share of about 6.36 to 6.46 dollars, up from 6.10 to 6.20, and the growth of about 4 per cent in the revenue. A retailer that grows the grocery by 6.4 per cent while the apparel falls is a retailer that is winning the traffic, and the traffic is the part the margins follow. The shares are up about 12 per cent this year, and the company is the best performer among the large retailers.
