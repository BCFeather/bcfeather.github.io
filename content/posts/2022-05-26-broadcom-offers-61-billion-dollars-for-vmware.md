Broadcom announced the offer on 26 May, and the price was 61 billion dollars in cash and stock. The company offered 142.50 dollars a share, which was 45 per cent above the price before the talks were reported. It also said it would take on 8 billion dollars of VMware's debt. The offer valued VMware at 4.5 times its revenue, and Broadcom's own at 6 times.

Broadcom said it would fund it with 32 billion dollars of new debt, and that it had commitments from five banks. The deal was the largest technology merger since 2015, and the second largest of the year after Microsoft's offer for Activision. The two companies' shares both rose, 5 per cent and 3 per cent. Broadcom had been acquiring software companies. It had bought CA Technologies in 2018 for 19 billion dollars and Symantec's enterprise business in 2019 for 11 billion. Its chief executive said the company would focus on VMware's largest 2,000 customers and would invest in its cloud products. Its chief executive said the company would keep VMware's products and would not sell its security unit.

VMware had been through a difficult year. It had spun off from Dell in 2021, and its revenue had grown 8 per cent to 13 billion dollars. Its shares had fallen 20 per cent in the year, and its chief executive had left in May. The company said the deal would give its shareholders 50 per cent of the combined company. CA and Symantec had cost 30 billion dollars between them, and both had grown slowly.

Broadcom had been through a review before, when its offer for Qualcomm was blocked in 2018 on national security grounds. The regulators were expected to look closely. The deal would be reviewed in the United States, in Europe and in China, and the companies said they expected it to close in 2023.
