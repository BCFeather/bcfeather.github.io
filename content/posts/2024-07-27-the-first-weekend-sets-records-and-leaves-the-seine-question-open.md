The weekend produced the largest opening television audience the games have recorded and a set of swimming finals that were postponed because the rain had raised the bacteria counts in the Seine. Both facts are part of the same story about a city that decided to hold events in its streets and its river. The two facts sit awkwardly together because the ceremony that drew the audience also celebrated a river that the athletes could not enter.

Postponement is the predictable failure. A combined sewer system that carries rainwater and sewage in the same pipes overflows when it rains, and a storm of an inch is enough to put the counts above the limit. A venue that depends on the weather is a venue whose schedule is written in pencil. The organizers had built a storage basin to hold storm water before it reaches the river, and the basin was still being commissioned when the rain arrived.

Audience is the commercial answer. A ceremony that is watched by more people than any previous one is a ceremony that justifies the format, and the broadcast contracts that run to 2032 were priced on the assumption that the games remain the largest event on television. A product that is losing its audience to streaming is a product that needs a spectacle. The American broadcaster reported its largest audience for an opening ceremony in more than a decade, and the digital streams added a share that did not exist at the last games.

Tickets were the other measure. A system that sold packages for multiple sessions produced a total above the previous record, and the demand was concentrated in the events that the host country was expected to win. A home crowd is a crowd that buys early and pays more. Hospitality packages that bundled athletics, swimming and the closing ceremony sold out first, and the cheapest tickets went to the events without a home favourite.

Athletes carried the argument. A swimmer who has trained for four years for a race that is postponed by a day is a swimmer whose preparation is disrupted by a rainstorm, and the complaints were about the uncertainty rather than the water. A schedule that is announced hours ahead is a schedule that a coach cannot plan around. Coaches had prepared for a final on a specific evening, and the postponement moved the race to a morning when the water was tested again.

What the weekend shows is that the ambition to use the city as a venue is a decision that the weather can overrule. A river that passes the test in the dry season is a river that fails in a storm, and the reserve plan is the admission that the test cannot be guaranteed. A reserve day is the admission that the venue cannot be guaranteed, and the admission was written into the schedule before the games began.
