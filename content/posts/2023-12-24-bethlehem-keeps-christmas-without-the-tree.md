description: "Bethlehem cancelled its Christmas tree, its parades and most of its midnight mass crowds on 24 December 2023, in a West Bank town whose hotels were empty and whose churches had replaced the manger scene with a figure lying in rubble."
---

Bethlehem cancelled Christmas in December, which is a sentence the town has not had to write since 2020. The municipality turned off the lights on the tree that usually stands in Manger Square, cancelled the scouts' parades and the concerts, and told the shops that sell carved olive wood that the season would bring no visitors. The Lutheran Christmas Church set up a nativity scene with the child lying in a pile of broken concrete instead of a manger, a decision the pastor explained in a few sentences that were quoted around the world.

The economics of the town explain the decision. Tourism provided about 20 per cent of the employment in the Bethlehem area before October, and the hotels that had been full in the last week of December in 2022 were empty; the buses that bring pilgrims from Jerusalem for a two-hour visit had stopped in October. The olive wood workshops that depend on the same traffic had laid off most of their carvers, and the municipality, which is small, lost the revenues from parking and from the Christmas market that pay for its services in January.

Restrictions on movement did the rest. The Israeli authorities closed checkpoints and imposed permits that made travel from the Palestinian towns to Bethlehem difficult in December, and the workers who usually came from Hebron and from the villages around the town could not reach it. The Latin patriarch, who normally enters Bethlehem through a checkpoint with an Israeli escort, made the journey as he does every year and said mass in a church with a few hundred people instead of the thousands of the year before.

The liturgical calendar continued, which is the part that the cancellation could not touch. The bells rang at the usual hours, the midnight mass was celebrated in St Catherine's, and the families that have lived in the town for centuries attended as they always have, in the same pews with the same cousins. What was missing was the audience that had made the town a stage for a season, and with it the money that the audience brought.

For the churches, the season had a different meaning this year. The heads of the churches in Jerusalem issued a statement in November asking their congregations to mark Advent without celebration, and many of them donated the money they would have spent on decorations to hospitals in Gaza. The Vatican held its own Christmas Eve mass, the pope spoke about the children of the war in his address, and the contrast between the lit tree in St Peter's Square and the unlit square in Bethlehem became an image the agencies used for the whole week.

Christmas in Bethlehem has been cancelled before, in 2000 and in 2002, during the second intifada, and in 2020 because of the pandemic. Each time the town returned to its season within a year or two, and each time the people who live there said the same thing: the pilgrims will come back, because the place is the place. What has changed in 2023 is that the war is in the strip that people can see from the ridge outside the town, and the town's own season now begins with the question of whether it is decent to celebrate at all.
