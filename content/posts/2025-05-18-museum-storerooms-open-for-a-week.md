Museums hold far more than they display. A provincial museum with 200,000 objects may have 5,000 on view at any time, and the rest sit in climate-controlled rooms, catalogued, photographed, and occasionally lent. International Museum Day, on 18 May, has become the occasion when some of them are shown. Storage is the larger part of a museum, and it is the part that visitors never see.

The logic of a store is different from the logic of a gallery. A gallery arranges objects to tell a story, and a store arranges them to be findable. What the public learns from the exercise is scale. A visitor who has seen the galleries and then walks through a store of ceramics comes away with a different sense of a collection: not a set of masterpieces but a population, with a long tail of ordinary objects that document how people lived. Curators learn something too. The objects that attract attention in a store are often the ones nobody expected, and a few museums have used the week to test display ideas before committing to them in a gallery.
