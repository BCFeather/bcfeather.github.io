title: "Biden cancels ten thousand dollars of student debt for most borrowers"

---

The president announced on Wednesday that the government would cancel ten thousand dollars of student debt for borrowers who earn less than a hundred and twenty-five thousand dollars a year, and twenty thousand for those who received grants for poor students. The relief applies to loans that the government holds, which is most of them, and the income test is based on the adjusted gross income of the year before the pandemic. About forty-three million borrowers have federal loans, and the administration said about twenty million of them would have their balance cleared entirely.

The announcement also extended the pause on repayments that began in March 2020 to the end of the year. The pause had been extended six times, and the budget office had assumed it would end in the summer. The cost of the cancellation was estimated at about three hundred billion dollars over ten years by the budget office, and the administration's own estimate was lower. The money will be charged to the deficit unless the Congress offsets it, and the Congress has not passed a bill to do so.

At the same time, the legal basis is the contested part. The administration relied on a law that was passed in 2003 to help the families of soldiers during a war, and it read the words broadly enough to cover a national emergency. The law says the secretary may waive or modify the terms of the loans during a national emergency, and the pandemic was declared one in 2020. Several Republican states sued within days, and the courts will decide whether the reading holds. The administration said it had lawyers in the department who had reviewed the question for a year.

Meanwhile, the politics are straightforward and the arithmetic is less so. The borrowers who benefit are younger and more likely to vote for the party that announced the plan, and the people who pay are the ones who did not borrow or who have already repaid. The critics said the plan transfers money from people who chose cheaper colleges to people who chose expensive ones, and the supporters said the debt had been sold to teenagers who had no way to judge the price. The polls showed that the plan was popular with the borrowers and unpopular with the rest.

In practice, the design has a gap in it. The relief applies to loans that the government holds, and the borrowers who took out the loans that were made by private banks and later guaranteed are covered, while the ones who refinanced with a private lender stay outside the scheme. The administration has said it is looking at a way to reach them and the lawyers have said the law does not allow it. The people who borrowed the least and the people who borrowed the most are both outside the cap, for different reasons.

What the plan does is make the debt a political question rather than an administrative one. The pause that was supposed to be temporary has lasted two and a half years, and the balance has grown while the payments stopped. The courts will decide whether the cancellation stands, and the answer will arrive before the repayments resume. The borrowers who expected to pay will now wait for a ruling, and the ones who expected relief will wait for the same one.
