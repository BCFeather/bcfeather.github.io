description: "The company reported a 4 per cent decline on 26 October and said losses at its virtual reality unit would grow."
---

Meta published results for the third quarter on 26 October, and revenue fell 4 per cent to 27.7 billion dollars. It was the second consecutive decline and the first back-to-back fall in the company's history. Net income fell 52 per cent to 4.4 billion, and the operating margin fell from 36 per cent to 20. The company's costs rose 19 per cent to 22 billion dollars, and it said they would rise again next year.

Advertising impressions rose 17 per cent, and the average price per ad fell 18 per cent. That pattern had held for three quarters, and it reflects the shift of advertising towards short videos, which carry lower prices. The company said Reels, its video product, had reached 140 billion daily views but was not yet earning as much as the feed. Daily active users reached 2.04 billion, and the company said the figure had grown in every region.

Reality Labs, the unit that makes the headset and the software, lost 3.7 billion dollars in the quarter and had lost 9.4 billion in the year to that point. The costs came from the bet on virtual reality. The company said losses would increase in 2023, and the shares fell 24 per cent the next day. The headset it sells cost 1,500 dollars, and the company said it had sold fewer than expected.

Headcount was 87,000, up 28 per cent over the year. The company said it would keep hiring in 2023 but at a slower rate, and it said it would reduce some teams. The chief executive told investors that the company had overinvested in some areas and that he took responsibility for it. The company said it would slow the pace of hiring and would review every team's budget.

Several analysts said the company should cut the unit or spin it off, and the chief executive said the bet was long term and that he would not change course. The strong dollar took 6 points off revenue growth. The company books half its revenue outside the United States, and it said the effect would continue in the fourth quarter. It also said the change in the way Apple handles advertising tracking had cost it 10 billion dollars since 2021, and the effect was diminishing. The company had been criticized for its spending on the metaverse, and the criticism got louder after the results. The shares had fallen 70 per cent in the year, and the market value was 250 billion dollars.

Revenue returned to growth in the second quarter of 2023, and the company said it would keep spending on artificial intelligence rather than on the metaverse. The company announced 11,000 job cuts on 9 November, about 13 per cent of the workforce. It was the first large reduction in its history, and it took a charge of 4.2 billion dollars. The shares rose 20 per cent over the following two days, and they had fallen 70 per cent in the year. The unit's losses narrowed in 2023, and the company said it would cut costs by another 10 billion dollars.
