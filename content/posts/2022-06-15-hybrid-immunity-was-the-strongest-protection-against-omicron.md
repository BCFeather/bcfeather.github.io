By the end of 2021 almost every adult in Qatar had been vaccinated, infected or both, and the arrival of Omicron made the country a place where the four combinations of immunity could be compared within one epidemic. A country where nearly every adult has been vaccinated, infected or both is a country where the four combinations can be compared within one epidemic and one testing system.

A test-negative analysis of symptomatic case-control analysis of symptomatic Omicron infection from 23 December 2021 to 21 February 2022, comparing vaccination with BNT162b2 or mRNA-1273, previous infection with a non-Omicron variant, and the combination. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 15 June 2022. A test-negative design compares the odds of prior immunity between people who test positive and people who test negative, and the design is the standard for vaccine effectiveness when randomization is impossible.

Previous infection alone was 46.1 per cent effective against symptomatic BA.2 infection, two doses of BNT162b2 with no previous infection were negligible at minus 1.1 per cent, and three doses were 52.2 per cent. Previous infection plus two doses gave 55.1 per cent, and previous infection plus three doses gave 77.3 per cent. A negative estimate for two doses is a real number and not a rounding error, because the group had been vaccinated months earlier and the antibody that Omicron escapes had waned.

A negligible estimate for two doses is explained by the timing, because nearly everyone in that group had received the second dose more than six months earlier, and the number describes waning against a variant that escapes antibody rather than a failure of the vaccine. A protection above 70 per cent against severe disease in every group is the result that a health system plans around, and the waning against infection is the result that a public health message has to explain.

Against severe, critical or fatal disease, previous infection alone, vaccination alone and hybrid immunity all exceeded 70 per cent, and that is the number that a health system plans around. The distinction between protection against infection and protection against severity is the whole story. Uniform testing and a large share of sequenced cases are what make a single-country design strong, and the limitation is that the estimates apply to the variants of those two months.

A case-control design in a single country is strong when the testing is uniform, and Qatar tested widely and sequenced a large share of cases. The limitation is that the estimates apply to the variants that circulated during those two months, and the next variant resets the calculation. A policy that treats past infection as equivalent to vaccination is a policy that leaves protection on the table, because the booster adds to immunity that infection has already produced.

What the authors conclude is that hybrid immunity from infection and a recent booster is the strongest configuration, and that vaccination still adds protection to a person who has been infected.
