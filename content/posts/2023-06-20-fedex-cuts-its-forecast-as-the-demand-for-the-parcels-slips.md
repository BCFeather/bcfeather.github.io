title: "FedEx cuts its forecast as the demand for the parcels slips"
date: "2023-06-20T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The quarter to May missed the estimates on the profit, and the company said the volumes were the weakest since 2020 as the customers shipped less."
---

FedEx reported a revenue of 21.9 billion dollars for the quarter to May, down about 10 per cent, and a profit of 1.5 billion. The earnings per share of 4.94 dollars beat the forecast, but the adjusted figure of 3.99 missed the estimate of 4.30 after the charges for the restructuring. The shares fell about 3 per cent. The revenue was about 200 million below the estimate, and the constant currency growth was a fall of about 8 per cent. The company said the restructuring charge was about 350 million dollars, and the programme would cost about 2 billion over two years.

Volumes were the problem. The express volumes fell by about 4 per cent in the United States, and the international priority volumes by about 6, which reflects the weaker trade. The ground volumes fell by about 1, and the company said the e-commerce had held up better than the business-to-business. The average daily volume of the parcels was about 6 million in the express segment, which is the lowest since the autumn of 2020. The yield rose by about 3 per cent, and the fuel surcharge added about 1 point as the jet price fell.

Its cuts are working. The company has cut about 12,000 jobs over the year, and the operating costs fell by about 1.6 billion dollars in the quarter. The aircraft fleet has been reduced, and the company has parked about 20 of the wide-body planes. The savings are expected to reach about 4 billion dollars on the annual basis by the end of the next year, and the company has said the network will shrink by about 10 per cent.

Guidance was the news. The company expects the earnings per share of about 17 dollars in the year to May 2024, below the forecast of 18.5, and the revenue to be flat. The management said the peak season would be weaker than usual, and the customers had been shipping later. The company said the December quarter would be the weakest, and the peak surcharges would be smaller than a year ago.

Rival UPS is the comparison. UPS has reported the volumes falling by about 4 per cent, and the two companies have been cutting the capacity at the same time. The parcel market has been shrinking for four quarters, and the analysts say the overcapacity is the part that the pricing will have to fix. The parcel volume in the United States has fallen by about 8 per cent over the year, and the trend has not turned.

This quarter is the one that the cost cuts carried. A company that earns 1.5 billion dollars while the volumes fall is a company that is shrinking the network to fit the demand, and the fit is the part the guidance shows. The shares are up about 30 per cent this year, which is the better performance among the two large parcel groups.
