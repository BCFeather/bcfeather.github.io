Notre-Dame de Paris reopened on 7 December with a ceremony and a first mass the following morning, five and a half years after a fire destroyed the roof and the spire and came within metres of bringing down the towers. The restoration employed about two thousand people and cost roughly seven hundred million euros, most of it raised in the days after the fire. A fire that started under the roof is a fire that a medieval timber frame fed for hours. A cathedral that was within metres of collapse is a building that engineers had to stabilize before they could rebuild.

Reconstruction is a story about craft. The roof was framed in oak from trees that had to be found in forests that had not been managed for beams of that dimension, and the frames were cut by hand with axes in a technique that had almost no practitioners left. A skill that survived in a few workshops was the skill that the project needed. A tree of the right height and girth is a tree that a forest manager has to find and fell.

Lead was the other problem. The roof and the spire were covered in lead, and the fire released dust that settled across the island and into the surrounding streets. A restoration that had to remove tonnes of contaminated material is a restoration that had to protect its own workers before it could rebuild. A dust that contains lead is a dust that a cleanup has to treat as hazardous waste.

Spire was rebuilt to a design by the nineteenth-century architect who had restored the cathedral, and the decision was contested. A contemporary spire would have marked the fire as part of the building's history, and a replica marks the building as a thing that should look as it did. The choice was political as much as architectural. A replica that is judged by historians is a replica that has to match photographs and drawings. A debate about a spire is a debate about whether the building's history includes its destruction.

Visiting arrangements are now the constraint. A cathedral that expects fifteen million visitors a year needs a system for entry, and the system that has been installed reserves timed slots and separates tourists from worshippers. A building that is both a monument and a working church has to divide its hours. A timed slot is a slot that a tour operator buys in blocks and resells. A tour operator that buys slots in blocks is an operator that resells the visit as a product.

What the reopening marks is that a disaster can be answered with an institution. A state, a church and a donor base that agreed on a date and delivered it is a rare combination, and the building that resulted is the evidence that the agreement held. A donor who gave in the week of the fire is a donor who wants to see the result.
