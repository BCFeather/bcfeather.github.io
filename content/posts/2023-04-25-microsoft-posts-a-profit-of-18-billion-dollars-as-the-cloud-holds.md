---

Microsoft reported a revenue of 52.9 billion dollars for the quarter to March and a profit of 18.3 billion, both above the forecast. The shares rose about 8 per cent in the after-hours trading. Intelligent cloud, the segment that holds Azure, brought in 22.1 billion dollars. The revenue was about 2 per cent above the estimate of 51 billion, and the earnings per share of 2.45 dollars beat the forecast of 2.15. The commercial bookings, which measure the longer contracts, rose by about 14 per cent in constant currency.

Azure grew by about 27 per cent in constant currency, which was a point slower than the previous quarter and still above the guidance. The company said the AI services had added about a point to the growth, and the customers were signing the larger contracts. A workload that is moving to the cloud is a licence that is renewed. The number of the customers spending more than 100 million dollars a year on the cloud reached about 40, which is double the level of two years ago. The company said the AI platform had been used by about 250,000 of the developers, and the usage was doubling every quarter.

Productivity software, which holds Office and LinkedIn, reported 17.5 billion dollars. The seats grew by about 12 per cent, and the average revenue per user rose as the company moved the customers to the higher tiers. The advertising on LinkedIn held up better than the market. The Teams users reached about 300 million, and the monthly active users of the consumer apps were about 1.4 billion. The advertising revenue would have grown by about 5 per cent without the currency effect, and the search volume was steady.

Personal computing fell by about 9 per cent to 13.3 billion. The Windows licences to the manufacturers were the weakest line, and the devices fell by about 30 per cent as the channel cleared the stock. The gaming revenue was flat as the console cycle aged. The Xbox content and the services grew by about 3 per cent, and the hardware fell by about 30 as the consoles stayed on the shelves. The company said the personal computing segment would fall again in the next quarter, and the guidance was about 4 per cent lower than the consensus.

Costs were the other story. The company cut about 10,000 jobs in the quarter and took a charge of about 1.2 billion dollars, and the operating margin held at about 45 per cent. The capital spending rose to about 7.8 billion as the data centers were expanded for the AI work. The free cash flow was about 15.4 billion, and the company returned about 10 billion to the shareholders through the dividends and the buybacks. The headcount of about 220,000 is roughly where it was a year ago, which means the growth of the last two years has been unwound without a second round of the cuts.

This quarter answers the question the investors had asked since the winter. A company that grows the cloud by 27 per cent while the devices fall by 30 is a company whose future is the data center, and the spending shows where the money is going. The market has been watching for the moment when the AI spending starts to pay, and the quarter gave the first evidence that the customers are willing to pay for it. The shares are up about 20 per cent this year, which is a bet on the cloud rather than on the devices.
