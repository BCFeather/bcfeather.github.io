Consumer confidence fell to 106.1 in August from 114 in July, the Conference Board said, the first decline in three months. The reading was below the forecast of about 116. The measure of the present situation was about 144.8, and the one of the expectations about 80.2. The figure was the lowest since the spring, and the index has been between 100 and 115 for a year. The survey covers about 3,000 households, and the responses are collected through the month.

Expectations did the damage. The measure for the next six months fell by about 8 points, and the one for the income by about 6 as the households said the raises had been smaller. The one for the job availability also fell, and the gap between the ones who said the jobs were plentiful and the ones who said they were hard to get narrowed. The share of the households expecting the recession in the next year rose to about 70 per cent, up about 5 points. The expectation for the inflation was about 5.5 per cent, and the one for the stock prices was negative.

Its present measure held. The measure for the business conditions rose a little, and the one for the employment was about flat. The respondents said the inflation had been the largest concern, and the interest rates were the second. The respondents who said the jobs were plentiful were about 40 per cent, and the ones who said they were hard to get about 14.

Buying plans were weaker. The share of the households planning to buy a car fell to about 10 per cent, and the one planning a home to about 4.5. The appliance plans fell as the prices stopped falling, and the vacation plans held. The share of the households planning a major appliance purchase fell to about 45 per cent, which is the lowest since the pandemic. The share of the households planning a vacation fell to about 45 per cent, and the one planning to buy a vehicle held.

A partisan split is smaller than in the sentiment survey. The confidence of the Democrats was about 90, and the one of the Republicans about 120, which is narrower than the gap in the university survey. The independents were about 100. The gap between the parties has been about 30 points, which is narrower than the 50 in the university survey.

This report is the one that the spending has been contradicting. A confidence at 106 while the retail sales grow is a gap that the economists explain with the jobs and the wages, and the explanation is the part the next quarter will test. The retail sales report of 15 August is the one that shows the divergence, and the economists expect the spending to slow in the autumn. The gap has been the largest of the three measures of the sentiment, and the economists say the politics has been the part that moves it.
