Gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancers are diagnosed late and recur often, and the standard treatment has been chemotherapy followed by surgery. A paper in Nature Medicine on 8 January 2024 reports a trial that adds an immune checkpoint inhibitor to that sequence. Neoadjuvant treatment is a design choice that turns the tumour into its own vaccine, and the choice is why the timing matters as much as the drug. Chemotherapy remains the backbone, and an add-on that improves response without replacing the backbone is what the field currently accepts.

Neoadjuvant means before surgery, and the sequence is the variable the trial changes. Atezolizumab blocks PD-L1, which is the ligand that tumours use to quiet T cells. PD-L1 blockade depends on a T-cell response being present, and a tumour with few T cells is a tumour the drug cannot amplify. An add-on is a modest advance, and modest advances accumulate into a change of standard only when the survival data arrive. A tumour with few T cells is a tumour where the blockade has nothing to release, and release is the mechanism the drug depends on.

Adding the blockade before surgery exposes the tumour to an immune response while it is still in place. An in-place tumour is a source of antigen, and antigen is what an immune response needs. Pathological response is a surrogate that trials accept and regulators treat cautiously, and the caution is the gap between the two. A pathological complete response is the strongest surrogate the design allows, and the design allows no more than that before the operation.

Pathological response is the endpoint that a neoadjuvant trial can measure, and a response in the surgical specimen is a surrogate for survival. A surrogate is not a survival benefit, and the paper is explicit about the difference. Tolerability before surgery is a hard constraint, because a complication delays the operation that the regimen is meant to enable.

The combination is tolerable, which is what a neoadjuvant regimen has to be. A regimen given before a curative operation cannot compromise the operation, and toxicity is the constraint that shapes the dose. Survival follow-up is the number that decides practice, and a response rate is the number that decides whether the follow-up is funded.

Survival data are immature, and the follow-up is the limit of the claim. A trial that reports response and not survival has reported a step. Mismatch repair status selects a group that responds strongly to checkpoint blockade, and the strong responders are a minority of gastric cancers.

Biomarkers are where the next question sits, since PD-L1 status and mismatch repair determine who responds. A biomarker that selects patients is what would make the regimen standard. A minority that responds strongly is a minority a biomarker can find, and finding them is the programme the trial implies. A regimen that depends on a biomarker is a regimen that shrinks the eligible population, and a smaller population is a smaller market.
