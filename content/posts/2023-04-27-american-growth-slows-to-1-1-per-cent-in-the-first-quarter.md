Output grew at an annual rate of 1.1 per cent in the first quarter, according to the advance estimate published on 27 April, down from 2.6 per cent in the previous quarter. The forecast had been for about 2 per cent. The growth over the year was 1.6 per cent. The figure was below the estimate of 2 per cent, and the quarter was the weakest since the first of 2022. The nominal growth was 4.8 per cent, which means the real measure was held back by the prices.

Inventories subtracted. The change in the private inventories cut about 2.3 percentage points from the growth, as the businesses worked through the stock they had built in the previous two quarters. The imports rose by about 5 per cent, which also subtracts from the measure. The inventories had added about 2 points to the growth of the previous quarter, and the reversal is the arithmetic of the stock cycle. The trade deficit widened to about 1.3 trillion dollars on the annual basis, and the imports of the goods rose by about 3 per cent.

Consumers held up. The personal spending rose by about 3.7 per cent, and the spending on the services rose by about 5.4 as the travel and the restaurants recovered. The goods spending fell by about 0.1, and the motor vehicles fell by about 1.4. The spending on the health care rose by about 4 per cent, and the one on the recreation by about 8 as the cinema and the travel recovered. The saving rate fell to about 4.8 per cent from 4.5, which is the sign of the consumer drawing down the cushion of the pandemic.

Business investment was weak. The spending on the equipment fell by about 7 per cent, and the one on the structures fell by about 12 as the factories and the offices were put off. The intellectual property rose by about 6, which is the line the AI spending feeds. The spending on the factories fell by about 12 per cent, and the one on the offices by about 8 as the vacancy rate rose. The equipment spending on the transport fell by about 20, and the one on the computers rose slightly.

Housing was a drag for an eighth quarter. The residential investment fell by about 4 per cent, and the sector has subtracted from the growth since the middle of 2021. The mortgages have come down from the peak, and the builders have been more confident since the winter. The mortgage rate was about 6.4 per cent in the quarter, and the sales of the new homes have been running at about 670,000 a year. The builders' confidence has risen since the winter, and the completions are the part that has held up.

This figure is the first of three estimates. A growth of 1.1 per cent that rests on the consumer is a growth that depends on the jobs report, and the jobs report of the month was strong. The estimate will be revised twice, and the economists expect the final figure to be between 1.3 and 1.5 per cent. A growth of 1.1 per cent that rests on the consumer is a growth that depends on the jobs report, and that report has been strong all year.
