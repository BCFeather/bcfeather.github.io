The 23rd summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) concluded in Samarkand after two days of closed-door talks, with leaders of member states signing a joint declaration and a package of cooperation documents covering trade, transport, security and cultural exchanges. Held under Uzbekistan's presidency, the gathering brought together heads of state from full members, alongside leaders of observer states and invited guests.

Counter‑terrorism and regional stability remained core items on the agenda. Leaders reaffirmed commitment to jointly fight the three forces of terrorism, separatism and extremism, and strengthen information sharing and joint drills among law enforcement agencies. Delegates also exchanged views on the situation in Afghanistan, stressing the importance of supporting an inclusive political process and preventing the country from becoming a source of regional security risks.

Economic connectivity stood out as another key focus. The leaders agreed to push forward the construction of cross‑border transport corridors, streamline customs clearance procedures and expand trade in agricultural products, energy and digital services. They endorsed plans to boost cooperation within the SCO Interbank Consortium and encourage small‑and‑medium enterprises across the region to participate in cross‑border business. The joint statement also voiced support for safeguarding stable global supply chains and opposing unilateral sanctions and protectionist measures.

The summit pushed forward the organization's expansion agenda. Negotiations on the accession procedures for new members continued, while dialogue partner mechanisms were further improved. Officials said the gradual expansion helps the SCO better represent the diverse interests of Eurasian countries and strengthen its role in global governance. Observers noted that the grouping continues to operate on the principle of the Shanghai Spirit, featuring mutual trust, equality, mutual benefit and non‑alignment, rather than functioning as a military bloc.

Climate change and food and energy security also featured prominently in the final documents. Member states pledged to set up more technical exchange platforms to share experience in drought response, grain reserve management and clean energy development. Cultural and people‑to‑people exchanges were not overlooked; leaders approved new plans for youth exchanges, academic workshops and joint tourism promotion to deepen mutual understanding among societies.

As the summit closed, Uzbekistan handed over the rotating presidency of the SCO to Kazakhstan. The next heads‑of‑state summit will take place in Kazakhstan in 2024. Analysts say the outcomes of the Samarkand summit show that the SCO maintains strong momentum for pragmatic cooperation, even amid complex geopolitical shifts. For member states, the bloc offers a reliable platform to coordinate positions and deliver tangible benefits for regional development.
