Wyndham Clark won the US Open at the Los Angeles Country Club on 18 June, finishing 10 under par and one stroke ahead of Rory McIlroy. Scottie Scheffler was third at eight under. The win is the first major for Clark, who had never finished better than 75th in one before this week. He had won once on the tour before, at the Wells Fargo in May, and the form had been building for two months. The purse was about 20 million dollars, and the winner's share was about 3.6.

His last round was a grind. Clark made two birdies and two bogeys and shot 70 on a course that played firm and fast. He saved par from the bunker at the 17th and two-putted from about 25 feet at the 18th, and the second putt was about four feet. He hit about 70 per cent of the fairways, and the driver was the club he said had saved the week. The birdie at the 14th was the one that gave him the lead for good, and the approach was about 6 feet from the hole.

McIlroy could not putt. The four-time major winner hit 15 greens in regulation and took 33 putts, and he did not make a birdie after the ninth. He has now gone nine years without a major, and the putting has been the part he has changed the coach for twice. He had the lead on the Sunday of the previous major and finished second, and the experience was the one he cited afterwards. He said the putt at the 18th had been the longest four feet of his life, and the line was the one he had practiced all week.

Los Angeles Country Club was new to the tour. The Los Angeles Country Club had not hosted a major before, and the layout played to about 7,400 yards with the barranca and the small greens. The winning score of 10 under was the lowest in the championship since 2017. The greens ran at about 13 on the Stimpmeter, and the players said the speed was the fastest of the year.

Its field was the strongest of the year. The top 10 included Scheffler, McIlroy, Harris English and Cameron Smith, and about 15 players had a mathematical chance at the start of the day. The cut came at two over, which is the lowest in a decade. The television audience was about 5 million, which is up about 20 per cent on the previous year.

Victory moves Clark to about 10th in the world from 32nd. A player who wins his first major at 29 after missing the cut in half of his previous starts is a player who has found the putting, and the putting is the part that separates the field. The next major is the Open Championship at Hoylake in July, and the course has hosted the event twelve times.
