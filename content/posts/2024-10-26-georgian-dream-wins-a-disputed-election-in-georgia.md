---

Georgian Dream, the party that has governed Georgia since 2012, won the parliamentary election of the twenty-sixth of October with 53.93 per cent of the vote and 89 of the 150 seats, according to the final results published by the central election commission. Result was produced under a fully proportional system for the first time, and it gave the party a majority that it can use to govern alone. Four pro-Western opposition blocs shared the remainder, with the Coalition for Change taking 19 seats.

Dispute began before the count was complete. Independent exit polls published by American and Georgian research organizations put the parties much closer together than the official count did, and the difference was larger than sampling error could explain. President Salome Zourabichvili, who has broken with the ruling party, said the election had been falsified and called for protests, and she refused to recognize the result. Opposition leaders said they would not take their seats in a parliament they described as illegitimate.

Observers found serious problems without endorsing the language of a stolen election. Mission organized by the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe said the campaign had been marked by pressure on public employees, the misuse of administrative resources and a climate in which the ruling party's message dominated the media. It also reported cases of ballot secrecy violations and intimidation of voters, and it said the result could not be described as fully free. Ruling party rejected the criticism as politically motivated.

Campaign itself was fought largely on a single theme. Georgian Dream presented the election as a choice between peace and war, and it accused the opposition of wanting to open a second front against Russia, which its opponents deny. Opposition campaigned on the country's European path, which stalled after the government passed a law on foreign funding that critics compared to Russian legislation. European Union had frozen the accession process and withdrawn support for the government's budget, and several member states had warned that the vote would decide whether the door remained open.

Context that shaped the vote is regional. Georgia has been under pressure from Russia since the war in Ukraine began, and the government has balanced between the European Union and Moscow while blaming the West for pushing the country into conflict. Ruling party's founder, Bidzina Ivanishvili, has accumulated wealth and influence for a decade, and he returned to the political foreground before the election. Economy has grown at a reasonable rate, and the party drew support from voters in provincial towns who credit it with stability.

Consequence for the country's alignment is the question that the vote was about. If the result stands, Georgia will have a parliament that can pass the legislation the government wants and an opposition that refuses to sit in it, which is a combination that has ended badly in other countries in the region. Protests outside the parliament building in Tbilisi continued for several nights. European officials said they would wait for the investigation of the complaints before deciding how to treat the new parliament, and that decision will not be quick.
