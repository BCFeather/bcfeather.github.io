A franchise that has been in the same city for 17 years and never won anything has a particular relationship with a Game 7, and the Oklahoma City Thunder played one at home on 22 June and won it 103-91. The series had gone the distance, the Pacers had stolen Game 1 on the road, and the last game was decided in the second half by a defence that had led the league all season.

Indiana lost its point guard in the first quarter. Tyrese Haliburton tore an Achilles tendon and did not return, and a team that had built its run on his creation played the remaining 43 minutes without it, which is the kind of event that ends a series and explains a score.

Gilgeous-Alexander finished with 29 points and 12 assists, a playoff career high, and took the Finals MVP after averaging 30.3 points in the series, which he had entered as the regular season MVP. A player who wins both in the same year joins a short list, and the list is the argument for his place among the best guards of his generation.

The defensive numbers tell the game. Oklahoma City forced 21 turnovers and converted them into 32 points, which is a margin of 32 against a team that scored 91, and the Thunder pulled away in the third quarter with a run built on deflections and transition. A defence that generates offence is the cheapest kind of scoring in a sport with a salary cap.

The Pacers were the story of the tournament and not of the final. They had beaten the Knicks in six, had come back from 15 points down in the fourth quarter of Game 1, and had won two games on the road in a series in which they were the smaller market and the younger roster. A team that pushes a 68-win opponent to seven games has already answered the question about its ceiling.

Oklahoma City's rebuild is the model that half the league is now trying to copy. The franchise traded two superstars for draft capital in 2019, drafted a roster that grew together, and reached the final with the youngest average age of any champion in decades, which is what a general manager means when he says a timeline.

What happens next is the question that every champion faces and few answer. A core this young should be back, and the second apron of the tax makes retaining five good players harder than winning with them, which is the constraint that will decide whether this is one title or several. A champion built on draft picks is a champion whose best players are cheap for two more seasons, and that window is the one that general managers dream about. The 68 regular season wins had suggested a team that would win comfortably, and the series suggested a team that had learned to win badly, which is the more useful lesson for a young roster.
