Broadcom reported quarterly revenue of about 14 billion dollars and told investors that its artificial intelligence business had reached 12.2 billion for the year, a figure that describes chips designed for specific customers rather than for a catalogue. Custom accelerators built for a few hyperscale buyers have become a business comparable in size to the merchant market. A design that a customer commissions is a design that the customer's engineers have to live with for a generation.

Model differs from a catalogue chip. A customer brings a workload, the chipmaker designs an accelerator around it, and the customer commits to a volume before the design is taped out. A design that is paid for in advance is a design that carries less risk than a product that has to find a market. A catalogue chip is a chip that a thousand customers buy, and a custom one is a chip that one does. A chip that is designed for one customer cannot be sold to another, which is the whole point of the arrangement.

Custom approach is a response to the merchant vendor's margin. A buyer that spends billions on accelerators has an interest in a design that avoids the software licence and the margin, and a chipmaker that offers a design service is a partner rather than a supplier. The buyer keeps the architecture and the vendor keeps the manufacturing. A margin that a buyer avoids is a margin that a vendor has to replace with a design fee.

Networking is the other half. A cluster of accelerators is limited by how fast the chips can talk, and switching silicon and optical links are the products that determine whether a cluster runs at the utilization its owners expect. A vendor that sells both the compute and the fabric has a position that is hard to displace. A fabric that is slow is a fabric that leaves the accelerators waiting.

Risk is concentration. A business built on a handful of programmes is a business whose revenue arrives in a few large orders, and a customer that pauses a generation is a customer that removes a tenth of the year. A custom chipmaker is a bet on a small number of engineering teams. A programme that is cancelled is a cancellation that arrives with a year of warning and no revenue. A cancellation that arrives with a year of warning is a cancellation that still leaves a hole in the revenue.

What the quarter shows is that the accelerator market has split in two. One half sells a standard product with a software ecosystem, the other sells a design service with a customer's name on the die, and both are growing fast enough to be measured in billions rather than in units. A market that is split in two is a market where the same foundry serves both halves. A customer that owns the design also owns the schedule, which is the part the vendor cannot control.
