title: "OpenAI releases a model it calls a preview"
date: "2025-02-27T20:40:00+08:00"

description: "OpenAI published GPT-4.5 on 27 February as a research preview, describing it as a larger model trained for a better understanding of language rather than for a new capability, and restricting access to a limited set of developers."
---

The company's releases had followed a pattern of capability jumps: a model that could do something the previous one could not, sold on the strength of the new ability. The announcement on 27 February described a different kind of improvement, which the company framed as a model that is better at the ordinary work of language, at following a long instruction, at holding a conversation and at producing text that a reader finds coherent.

Technical description was thin by the standards of the company's earlier releases. The model is larger than its predecessor, it was trained on more data with a revised pipeline, and the company published benchmark scores that show gains on reasoning tests rather than on knowledge tests. No architecture, no parameter count and no training data description accompanied the announcement.

Access was limited to a research preview for a set of developers and to a waiting list, and the model was not offered to the consumer product at the launch. That staging is the pattern that the company used for earlier previews, and it reflects a decision to gather feedback on a model whose behaviour is different from the one that its users have learned to expect.

The claim that the model is safer was part of the announcement. A model that follows instructions better is a model that refuses better as well, and the company said that the improvements in instruction following applied to the refusals. The counter-argument, which the company did not make, is that a model which is more compliant is also more persuadable, and the evaluations that measure the first do not measure the second.

Price was the part that the developers noticed. The model costs more per token than the one it sits above, which is the opposite of the trend in the industry, and the company justified it as a premium for quality and not for speed. A model that costs more and does not add a capability is a model that a developer buys for a specific task, and the tasks that it suits are the ones where the quality of the text is the product and the cost of a token is a rounding error in the budget.

Competition sets the context. The models that other companies released in the same month were smaller, cheaper and aimed at the same market of chat and of code, and the strategy that OpenAI chose was to differentiate on quality at the top of the range. Whether that works depends on whether the buyers who care about quality are numerous enough to sustain a premium price.

What the release shows about the industry is that the frontier has become a matter of degree. A model that is better at the same tasks is a model that competes on craft and not on capability, and the marketing has to carry a claim that a benchmark cannot fully demonstrate: that the text is better to read.
