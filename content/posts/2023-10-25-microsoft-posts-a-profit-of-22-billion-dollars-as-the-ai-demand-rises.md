Microsoft reported a profit of 22.3 billion dollars for the quarter to September on 24 October, up from 17.6 billion a year earlier, on the revenue of 56.5 billion, which rose 13 per cent. The shares rose about 4 per cent after the results. The revenue was above the forecast of 54.5 billion, and the operating margin was 48 per cent, about a point higher than a year earlier. The figure was above the forecast, and the revenue grew at the fastest pace in six quarters as the cloud and the office renewals both accelerated.

Cloud was the engine. The Azure revenue rose by about 29 per cent in constant currency, above the guidance, and the company said about 3 points of it came from the AI services. A cloud that sells the models is a cloud that grows faster than the market. The company said the AI services contributed about 3 points to the Azure growth, and the analysts expect it to double next year as the capacity arrives.

Office business is the base. The commercial products and the cloud services rose by about 15 per cent as the companies renewed the contracts at higher prices, and the seat count grew by about 8 per cent. A subscription that renews is a revenue that is predictable. The number of the seats in the commercial office products passed 400 million, and the company has raised the prices of the bundles this year.

Capital spending is the bet. The company spent 11.2 billion dollars on the property and the equipment in the quarter, up by about 70 per cent, and it said the spending would keep rising as the data centers are built. A building that is paid for is a capacity that arrives in a year. The spending is the largest in the company's history, and the finance chief said the depreciation would rise by about 50 per cent next year.

Gaming is the acquisition. The deal for Activision closed on 13 October after the regulators in Britain approved it, and the company said the unit would add to the revenue next quarter. A studio that is bought is a content that the subscription can sell. The gaming revenue rose by about 9 per cent in the quarter before the acquisition closed, and the company has said it will add about 4 billion a year.

What the quarter shows is that the AI services have begun to show in the revenue rather than only in the marketing, and the showing is what the shareholders have been paying for. A company that spends 11 billion a quarter on the buildings is a company that believes the demand, and the belief is the risk the shareholders are underwriting. The demand that the company reports is the demand of the customers who are already paying, and the paying is what distinguishes it from the marketing.
