Microsoft published results for the quarter to September on 25 October, and revenue rose 11 per cent to 50.1 billion dollars. Net income fell 14 per cent to 17.6 billion, and the figure included a 3 per cent hit from the strong dollar. The cloud business grew 35 per cent, down from 40 in the previous quarter. Azure revenue was not disclosed in dollars, and the company reports growth in constant currency.

Guidance was the problem. The company said revenue for the next quarter would be 52.4 to 53.4 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 56. The finance chief said the strong dollar would take 5 points off growth, and she said customers were taking longer to sign contracts. The shares fell 7 per cent the next day.

Commercial bookings, which measure new contracts, fell 20 per cent. That number is volatile and the company said the comparison was against a strong quarter a year earlier. The remaining performance obligation, which is the value of signed contracts not yet billed, rose 20 per cent to 222 billion dollars. The personal computing segment did better than feared. Revenue was 13.3 billion, down 3 per cent, and Windows licences fell 15 per cent as the personal computer market contracted. The company said shipments had fallen 15 per cent in the quarter, and it expected a further decline in the next. The company said it had signed a record number of large contracts, and the average size rose.

Microsoft books half its revenue outside the United States, and the euro and the yen had fallen against the dollar. The company had begun cutting costs in the summer. It slowed hiring, cut some roles, and said it would take a charge of 800 million dollars in the quarter for severance. The chief executive said the company would focus on profitable growth and that headcount would stay flat for the year. The dollar was a large part of the story for every American technology company. The company said the currency effect would ease in the following quarters if the dollar stabilized. The gaming business grew 1 per cent, and the company said console sales had fallen.

Shares fell 7 per cent on the day and 25 per cent over the year. The company had been the most valuable in the world in 2021, and it lost that position to Apple in the autumn. Its Azure business was the second largest cloud provider, behind Amazon and ahead of Google.

Azure growth bottomed at 26 per cent in the quarter to March 2023, and it recovered as the dollar weakened and as customers resumed projects. The slowdown was short. The company's revenue rose 7 per cent for the year ended June 2023, and it announced 10,000 job cuts in January. The company had 220,000 employees at the end of the quarter, and it said the number would fall. Its shares had risen 50 per cent in 2021, and they gave back most of that gain in 2022.
