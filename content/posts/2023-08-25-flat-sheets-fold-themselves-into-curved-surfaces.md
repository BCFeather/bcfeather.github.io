A flat sheet is a two-dimensional object and a curved surface is a three-dimensional one, and a sheet folding itself into a curve is a manufacturing route for structures otherwise moulded. In *Science* in August 2023, the principle is supplied. The authors pattern a sheet with cuts and hinges, and the pattern's geometry determines the curvature adopted when the sheet is actuated.

Geometry is the principle. A network of hinges has a set of configurations, and the analysis identifies patterns whose configurations include a target surface. The inverse problem is the method, since a target surface is given and the hinge arrangement producing it is computed. Actuation is the mechanism. A sheet can fold by swelling, by a magnetic field or by thermal contraction, and one route is demonstrated; uniformity is the practical issue, because a sheet swelling in an uneven bath folds unevenly while a magnetic drive is more even.

Specification is the target surface. A dome, a saddle and a shell are different curvatures, and the input is the surface with the cut pattern as output. Scalability is the appeal. A sheet is made by a planar process and the curved structure assembled from it, and the planar process is what industry does well.

Accuracy is the claim. A folded structure's shape is set by hinge geometry, and the measured error of the assembled surface is the test; the cut pattern's density sets the amplitude, because a dense pattern of small hinges bends more smoothly than a sparse one. Material sets the limitation. A hinge bending many times needs fatigue resistance, and the demonstration is a single fold. A shell that carries load is not attempted, because a folded shell carrying weight needs stiffness and the hinge's compliance is the weakness.

Engineering gains a computable design space, since a folding pattern is an inverse problem and the method turns a target shape into a pattern. Origami offers the frame, because a traditional fold has a crease pattern while these cuts specify a fold differently, and a deployable solar array is the application, flat in transport and curved in use.

Load-bearing folds would need a locking mechanism, since a hinge that bends stores energy and returns, and a surface that must hold its shape cannot rely on elasticity alone. Tolerance is the manufacturing metric, because a pattern cut by a laser has a kerf and the kerf's width sets the error the assembled surface inherits, so the design's accuracy claim depends on the cutter as much as on the geometry.

Whether the inverse method generalizes would be shown by a second target surface, and a shell with negative curvature is the case where a naive pattern would fail. A cyclic test on a hinge would give the fatigue life, and that number rather than the assembled shape is what decides whether a folded shelter or array could be deployed more than once.
