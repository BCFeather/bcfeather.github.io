A flat sheet is a two-dimensional object, and a curved surface is a three-dimensional one, and a sheet that folds itself into a curve is a manufacturing route for structures that are otherwise moulded.

The design in *Science*, August 2023 supplies the principle. The authors pattern a sheet with cuts and hinges, and the pattern's geometry determines the curvature that the sheet adopts when it is actuated.

The principle is geometric. A network of hinges has a set of configurations, and the authors' analysis identifies the patterns whose configurations include a target surface.

The actuation is the mechanism. A sheet can fold by swelling, by a magnetic field or by a thermal contraction, and the authors demonstrate one route.

The target surface is the specification. A dome, a saddle and a shell are different curvatures, and the design's input is the surface and its output is the cut pattern.

The scalability is the appeal. A sheet is made by a planar process and a curved structure is assembled from it, and the planar process is the one that industry does well.

The accuracy is the claim. A folded structure's shape is set by its hinge geometry, and the authors' measurement of the assembled surface's error is the test.

The limitation is the material. A hinge that must bend many times needs a material with fatigue resistance, and the authors' demonstration is a single fold.

What the paper does not establish is a load-bearing structure. A folded shell that must carry weight needs stiffness, and the hinge's compliance is the weakness.

For engineering, the value is a design space made computable. A folding pattern is an inverse problem, and the paper's method turns a target shape into a pattern.

The hinge's geometry is the design's variable. A cut that leaves a narrow ligament bends along a line, and the ligament's length sets the fold's angle.

The inverse problem is the method. A target surface is given, and the pattern that produces it is computed by solving for the hinge arrangement that the curvature requires.

The actuation's uniformity is the practical issue. A sheet that swells in a bath folds unevenly if the bath is uneven, and a magnetic actuation is more uniform.

The comparison with origami is the frame. A traditional fold has a crease pattern, and the paper's cuts are a different way to specify a fold.

The application is deployable structure. A solar array or a shelter that unfolds from a flat pack is a structure whose transport is flat and whose use is curved.

The error's source is the material's elasticity. A hinge that bends stores energy and returns, and a surface that must hold its shape needs a locking mechanism.
The cut pattern's density is the curvature's amplitude. A dense pattern of small hinges bends more smoothly than a sparse one, and the authors' discretization is what sets the surface's fidelity.
The assembly's tolerance is the manufacturing metric. A pattern cut by a laser has a kerf, and the kerf's width sets the error that the assembled surface inherits.
