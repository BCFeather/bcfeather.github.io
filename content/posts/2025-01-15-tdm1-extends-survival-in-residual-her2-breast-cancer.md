description: "Seven years of follow-up from the KATHERINE trial show that trastuzumab emtansine, given to women with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, reduced the risk of death by a third compared with trastuzumab alone."
---

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus trastuzumab shrinks HER2-positive tumours before surgery, and the response predicts what comes next. A woman whose tumour disappears at pathology has an excellent prognosis. A woman with residual invasive disease after the preoperative course has a worse one, and the question for a decade has been what to give her afterwards.

The KATHERINE trial answered that question with an antibody-drug conjugate. Trastuzumab emtansine, known as T-DM1, attaches a microtubule poison to the same antibody that targets HER2, so the drug delivers its payload to the cells the antibody binds. The trial randomized women with residual disease to T-DM1 or to continued trastuzumab, and the primary analysis, reported in 2019, showed a large gain in invasive disease-free survival that changed the standard of care within a year of publication.

The paper in *The New England Journal of Medicine* on 15 January reports the mature survival analysis. Seven-year overall survival was 89 per cent in the T-DM1 group, and the hazard ratio for death was 0.66, with a confidence interval of 0.51 to 0.87 and a *P* value of 0.003. The absolute difference is smaller than the relative one, because HER2-positive breast cancer kills slowly, because salvage treatments work in both arms and because the trial enrolled women whose disease had already been reduced by chemotherapy.

Two readings follow. The first is that the benefit of the conjugate is durable, which matters because a survival gain that appears at three years and evaporates at seven would have been an artefact of sequencing. The second is that the gain is limited by the treatments given after progression, and the trial cannot separate the effect of the drug from the effect of what came later, which is a limitation that any long follow-up of an active comparator carries.

Toxicity is the price of the mechanism. T-DM1 causes thrombocytopenia, peripheral neuropathy and liver enzyme elevation, and the trial's safety profile matches the clinical experience that has accumulated since approval. A drug that is given for fourteen cycles after surgery has to be tolerable to women who have just finished chemotherapy, and the discontinuation rate in the trial is the number that a clinic reads first, because a drug that cannot be completed is a drug whose benefit is theoretical.

Practice has moved past the trial in one respect. Trastuzumab deruxtecan, a conjugate with a different linker and a different payload, has shown activity in the same setting and in patients whose disease progressed on T-DM1, and the question of which conjugate to use first is now open. The KATHERINE data remain the benchmark against which any successor has to be measured, because seven-year survival is the endpoint a new drug must beat and not a surrogate that a trial can assume, and because the women who received it are now old enough to have had recurrences that a shorter follow-up would have hidden.
