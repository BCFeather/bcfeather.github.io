India and Australia began the first test of a five-match series in Perth on a pitch that had more grass and more bounce than the venue usually offers. India were bowled out for 150 in their first innings, a total that looked like the start of a long tour for a batting order that had been rebuilt after the retirement of two senior players. A total of 150 is a total that a fast bowler can defend on a surface with bounce.

Surface is the story of the series. Australian pitches have been getting faster and bouncier for several years, and a batting line-up that grew up on slow turning tracks has to adapt within an innings rather than across a tour. A batsman who is late on a ball that rises is a batsman who is out, and the technique that works at home does not work here. A batsman who plays back to a ball that climbs is a batsman who is caught behind.

Response was the part that mattered. India's second innings was built around a partnership that lasted most of a day, and the total that followed was more than three hundred, which set a target that Australia could not chase. A team that is bowled out cheaply and then bats for a day has made an adjustment within the match. A partnership that lasts a day is a partnership that takes the surface out of the equation. A target of more than three hundred on that surface was a target that the fourth innings could not reach.

Scheduling is the commercial frame. A five-test series between these two countries is the richest contest in the sport, and the broadcast revenue depends on the matches being competitive for as long as possible. A series that is decided in three days is a series that the broadcaster has paid for and cannot fill. A broadcaster that pays for five tests is a broadcaster that needs the series to reach a fourth day. A test that ends in three days is a test that the host board has to refund.

Tour is also a workload problem. A fast bowler who sends down twenty overs on a hard surface is a bowler who is not available for the next test, and the rotation that follows is a rotation that decides the series as much as the batting does. A rotation that rests a bowler is a rotation that gives a debutant a test.

What the first test established is that the venue can neutralize a batting order that is otherwise the best in the world. A pitch is a decision that a curator makes and a captain requests, and in a series of this length the surface becomes a variable that both sides plan around. A curator who leaves grass on the pitch is a curator who has been asked for a result.
