description: "The September report showed the headline unchanged and the core slowing to 4.1 per cent, with the shelter costs still contributing about half of the monthly increase."
---

American consumer prices rose 3.7 per cent in the year to September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said on 12 October, the same as in August. The core measure fell to 4.1 per cent from 4.3. The figure was a tenth above the forecast, and the three-month annualized pace of the core is now about 3.1 per cent, the slowest since the spring of 2021. The figure was above the forecast of 3.6 per cent, and the Fed's preferred measure, which is published later, has been running below the consumer index.

Shelter is still the largest contributor. The rents rose by about 0.5 per cent on the month and 7.2 over the year, and the owners' equivalent rent, which is the imputed cost of owning, rose by about the same. A rent that is measured monthly is a measure that lags the leases by a year. The market rents on the new leases have been rising by about 3 per cent, half the pace of a year ago, and the gap to the official measure is what the economists expect to close through next year. The rent of the primary residence rose by 0.5 per cent, and the measure has been above 7 per cent for a year, which is the longest run since the 1980s.

Petrol eased. The price at the pump fell by about 2 per cent on the month after the summer peak, and the energy contributed a small negative to the monthly change. A fuel that falls is a headline that stops rising. The diesel price rose even as the petrol fell, and the freight rates have begun to pass the cost into the prices of the goods. The electricity prices rose by about 2 per cent as the summer demand peaked, and the gas fell as the supply reached the storage.

Used cars fell again. The prices of the second-hand vehicles dropped by about 2.5 per cent, the fifth monthly decline, as the supply of the off-lease cars returned. A car that is cheaper is a core that falls. The new vehicle prices were little changed, and the dealers have been offering the discounts as the inventories on the lots reached their highest since 2021. The new cars rose by about 0.2 per cent as the incentives were withdrawn in the autumn, and the used ones fell as the rental fleets sold their stock.

Services other than shelter were firm. The transport services rose with the airline fares, and the insurance rose by about 19 per cent over the year as the repair costs climbed. A premium that is repriced is an inflation that the household cannot avoid. The medical care services rose by about 0.3 per cent, and the hospital prices have been rising at about 3 per cent, slower than the wages of the staff. The insurance for the motor vehicles rose by about 19 per cent over the year, and the carriers have said the repair costs and the litigation are the reason.

What the report shows is that the inflation has stopped falling at the headline and is falling slowly at the core, and the difference is the shelter that the market rents have already begun to cool. A core at 4.1 per cent is a core that the Fed will not cut against, and the holding is the policy that the market has been forced to accept. A shelter measure that is falling is a core that will follow, and the follow is what the Fed has said it is waiting for.
