Supply chains eased a little. The index for supplier deliveries fell to 62.9 from 66.2, which means deliveries were slower but less so. The survey said 40 per cent of firms reported shortages of parts, and that 25 had reported them in the autumn. Freight rates from Asia had fallen 10 per cent since October. It beat the 57.5 forecast, and new orders rose fastest since September. The output index was 55.4, and the backlog had grown for a tenth month.

Prices were still high. The output price index was 74.2, and it had been 75.5. Firms said the price of energy had risen 4 times in a year, and that the price of steel had risen 25 per cent. The survey said the inflation would peak in the first quarter, and that it would fall slowly. The deliveries index fell to 62.9 from 66.2, so delays eased but did not end. Forty per cent of firms reported shortages, against 25 in the autumn, and freight from Asia was 10 per cent cheaper.

Germany led the region. Its index was 59.8, and France 55.5, and Italy 58.3. Spain was 56.2, and the Netherlands 59.1. The survey said the German car makers had reported more orders, and that the French firms had reported fewer shortages. The output price index was 74.2 against 75.5, and energy had risen four times in a year. The survey expected inflation to peak in the first quarter and to fall slowly.

Employment rose for a seventh month. The index was 54.4, and firms said they had added staff despite the wave. The survey said the hotels and the restaurants had added 400,000 jobs in the quarter, and that the unemployment rate had fallen to 7 per cent. Germany was 59.8, France 55.5, Italy 58.3, Spain 56.2 and the Netherlands 59.1.

Bond buying was to end in March, with a first rate rise in July in 11 years. Its president said the inflation would peak at 7 per cent in the first half, and that the growth would slow in the second. The survey's author said the war in Ukraine had cut orders in March. Employment rose for a seventh month, and the index was 54.4.

New orders fell in March as sanctions hit, and the price index rose to 78.5. The index fell to 55.5 in March, and to 54.6 in April, and to 54.6 in May. The survey said the factories had reported shortages of steel and of gas. By July the index was 49.8, and by September 48.4. The survey said output had fallen for four months, and that the price index had fallen to 60. Energy costs were 10 times their level of 2019.
