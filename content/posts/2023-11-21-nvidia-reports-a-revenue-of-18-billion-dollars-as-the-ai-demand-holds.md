NVIDIA reported a revenue of 18.12 billion dollars for the third quarter on 21 November, up 206 per cent from a year earlier, and a profit of 9.24 billion dollars, which was far above the forecast. The revenue was above the forecast of 16.2 billion, and the company said the sales would keep growing into next year. The revenue was the third consecutive quarter above the guidance, and the shares fell about 1 per cent as the China warning offset the beat.

Data centers are the whole story. The revenue from the segment was 14.51 billion dollars, up about 279 per cent, and it now accounts for about four fifths of the total. A chip that trains a model is a chip that the buyers queue for. The gaming revenue rose by about 80 per cent to 2.86 billion, and the professional visualization was flat at about 0.4 billion.

Guidance was above the estimate. The company said the next quarter would be about 20 billion dollars, and the supply of the newest chips will be the limit rather than the demand. A supply that is short is a price that holds. The company has been allocating the chips to the largest customers, and the cloud providers take most of the supply. The company said the supply of the newest chips would improve next year, and the contract maker has been adding the capacity in America.

China is the caveat. The company said the export controls announced in October would cut the sales in the current quarter, and the share of the revenue from China has fallen from about a fifth to about a tenth. A rule that is tightened is a market that is closed. The rules restrict the sale of the most advanced chips, and the company has designed a version for the Chinese market that meets the limits. The company said the sales in China had been about 20 to 25 per cent of the data-center revenue in the previous quarters, and the current quarter would be well below that.

Its margin was extreme. The gross margin was 74 per cent, and the operating margin was about 57, which is the consequence of the pricing power that the shortage gives. A margin that wide is a competitor that will be tempted. The inventory rose to about 5.3 billion dollars, and the company said the purchases are commitments for the next two quarters. The company returned about 3.8 billion dollars to the shareholders in the quarter, and the cash rose to about 18 billion.

What the quarter shows is that the demand for the AI chips has not yet met a ceiling and the policy has begun to cut the market, and the two will decide the next year. A company that grows by 206 per cent is a company that will be asked to keep growing, and the asking is the risk in the price.
