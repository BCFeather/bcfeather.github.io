On 17 February the leadership met a group of private entrepreneurs in Beijing, and the guest list was the message. Founders of the largest platform companies sat beside the heads of battery, car and chip firms, and the seating arrangement put manufacturing and technology at the centre of the room. The meeting was reported as a symposium on private enterprise, and it came after several years in which the sector had been regulated more than celebrated. Several of the founders had not appeared at an official event for years, and the presence of one of them was read as a signal in itself.

The background is a sequence of corrections. A campaign against anticompetitive behaviour in platforms began in 2021, an education sector was restructured in the same year, and a data security law changed what firms could do with user information. Each of those measures had a stated purpose and an unstated cost, which was a discount applied to any business whose value depended on regulatory tolerance. A firm that lost a licence in 2021 learned that compliance is a cost of doing business rather than a formality.

Private investment responded as expected. Fixed asset investment by private firms grew more slowly than total investment for most of 2023 and 2024, and the share of projects financed by private capital fell. A firm that expects a policy to change does not commit capital to a ten-year project, and the statistics recorded that hesitation. Local governments also slowed payments to private contractors, and receivables became a financing problem for construction and equipment firms.

The meeting is an attempt to change the expectation. A signal from the top that private firms are partners rather than suspects is cheap to send and expensive to reverse, and its value depends on whether the ministries that issue permits behave differently afterwards. The visible test is in the details: licensing timelines, court rulings in disputes between firms and local governments, and access to credit. A court that enforces a contract between a private firm and a state-owned buyer is the institution the signal depends on.

Manufacturing is the part of the private sector that the state now needs most. Batteries, electric vehicles, solar equipment and mature-node chips are industries where private firms are the largest investors, and those industries are where the export growth has come from. A policy that protects them is a policy that protects the growth model. Export demand for those products has been strong enough to offset weak domestic consumption, and that has made the sector a policy priority.

What the symposium does not settle is the position of platform firms. Advertising, e-commerce and games are profitable and politically sensitive, and the balance between the two has moved several times in five years. A founder who attends a meeting is not a founder who has been promised anything, and the market's reaction at the end of February was cautious for that reason. What was on offer was recognition, which is cheaper than a subsidy and less durable.
