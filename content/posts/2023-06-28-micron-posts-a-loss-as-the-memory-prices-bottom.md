---

Micron reported a revenue of 3.75 billion dollars for the quarter to June, down about 57 per cent, and a net loss of 1.9 billion. The loss per share was 1.73 dollars, and the adjusted loss of 1.43 was better than the forecast of 1.58. The shares rose about 4 per cent after the report, and the company said the prices had bottomed. The revenue was about 200 million above the estimate, and the gross margin was negative 17.8 per cent for the quarter. The company said the inventory write-down was about 300 million dollars, down from about 700 in the previous quarter.

Prices were the reason. The average selling price of the DRAM fell by about 10 per cent on the quarter, and the one of the NAND by about 15, which is the fourth quarter of decline. The revenue from the data centre fell by about 60 per cent, and the one from the personal computers by about 65 as the inventories were cleared. The bit shipments rose by about 10 per cent on the quarter as the customers rebuilt the stock, and the prices fell by more. The revenue from the mobile fell by about 40 per cent, and the one from the automotive was about flat.

Its cuts are deep. The capital spending for the year will be about 7 billion dollars, down from 12, and the company has cut about 15 per cent of the workforce. The production of the DRAM has been reduced by about 30 per cent, and the NAND by about 20. The company had about 11 billion dollars of the cash at the end of the quarter, and the debt was about 13 billion.

A trough is the message. The management said the inventories at the customers had fallen to the normal levels, and the prices would improve in the current quarter. The guidance for the revenue was about 3.9 billion dollars, above the forecast of 3.7. The company said the gross margin would turn positive in the current quarter, and the operating profit would follow in the next year.

AI demand is the hope. The high-bandwidth memory has been the part that the company is building capacity for, and the sales are expected to grow by about 50 per cent next year. The company said the orders from the data centre customers had begun to return in June. The high-bandwidth memory is about 5 per cent of the revenue, and the company expects it to be about 15 next year.

This quarter is the bottom the industry has been waiting for. A company that loses 1.9 billion dollars and guides to a smaller loss is a company at the turn, and the turn is the one the memory cycle always makes. The shares are up about 40 per cent this year, which is the best performance in the large chip makers.
