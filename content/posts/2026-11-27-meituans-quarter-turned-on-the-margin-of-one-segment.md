Meituan booked 115.56 billion yuan of revenue in the three months to 30 September, 21.6 per cent more than a year earlier, and turned an operating loss of 19.76 billion yuan into an operating profit of 2.74 billion. Net profit came to 2.07 billion against a loss of 18.63 billion, a net margin of 1.8 per cent on the quarter's revenue, and adjusted net profit, which strips out share-based pay and investment gains, was 1.85 billion. Revenue was 10.4 per cent higher than in the June quarter.

Nearly all of the swing sits in one segment. Core local commerce, which holds food delivery, in-store dining, hotels and travel, earned 78.3 billion yuan, up 16.8 per cent, and posted an operating profit of 5.7 billion at a margin of 7.3 per cent, against an operating loss of 14.1 billion a year earlier. That turn of 19.8 billion accounts for most of the group's 22.5 billion improvement, and the segment's revenue is two thirds of the total.

New initiatives grew without widening the loss. Revenue rose 32.9 per cent to 37.3 billion, and the segment's operating loss held at 1.3 billion, with the loss margin narrowing to 3.5 per cent from 4.6. Grocery retail and its community format both added stores, and the international arm, Keeta, reports profitability in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia and operations in 32 cities, with Sao Paulo as its base in Brazil. The company attributes the narrower loss to order density in cities where the network is already built.

Costs and cash moved with the recovery. Research and development came to 7.7 billion, up 11.3 per cent, and adjusted EBITDA was 4.58 billion. Operating cash inflow was 4.8 billion, and the quarter closed with 104.1 billion in cash and 53.4 billion in short-term investments, a balance that leaves room for another year of discounting. Cash generation, more than the profit line, is what pays for the next round of subsidies.

A margin of 2.4 per cent at group level and 7.3 per cent in the core is the number that decides the shape of next year. The comparison runs against a quarter distorted by subsidy spending, so the swing measures the end of a price war as much as the strength of the franchise, and the filing reports segment profit without unit economics, which leaves a reader unable to see what a delivered order earned after riders, discounts and subsidies. No order volume is disclosed, so revenue per order cannot be computed either.

Winter is the next test. The fourth quarter carries the season's promotions, and rider injury insurance and pension subsidies now run nationwide, a cost that grows with every order. The company says it will keep investing in artificial intelligence tools for merchants, with its CatPaw workbench serving two million of them, and whether the 7.3 per cent margin survives a promotional quarter is what the next filing will show. A quarter of discounts is the test the recovery has not yet passed.
