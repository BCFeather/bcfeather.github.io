Gross output fell 0.2 per cent in the first quarter, the cabinet office reported on 18 May. The annualized figure was minus 1 per cent, and the forecast had been minus 1.8. The fall followed a 0.1 per cent decline in the fourth quarter, and the two quarters together were the weakest since 2020. The forecast had been a fall of 1.8 per cent, so the figure was better than expected.

Consumption was flat. Household spending rose 0.1 per cent, and spending on services rose 2 as the restrictions lifted. Spending on durable goods fell 6, and car sales fell 10 because of the shortage of semiconductors. The savings rate was 34 per cent, which is high for the country. The state of emergency in three prefectures ended in March, and restaurants reopened.

Imports were the arithmetic problem. They rose 3.4 per cent in volume, and exports rose 0.4. The gap subtracted from growth, and the trade deficit was 3 trillion yen in the quarter. Energy imports cost 40 per cent more than a year earlier, and the yen had fallen 15 per cent. The yen averaged 118 to the dollar in the quarter, and it had been 110 a year earlier.

Business investment fell 0.7 per cent, and it had fallen for two quarters. Firms said the uncertainty about the supply chain was the reason, and the machine tool orders had fallen 5 per cent. Construction fell 3 per cent, and housing starts were 10 per cent below 2021. The government had spent 10 trillion yen on stimulus in the autumn, and it announced another package in the spring. The measures included subsidies for fuel and for electricity, and payments to households with children. Economists said the effect would show in the second quarter. Machine tool orders fell 5 per cent, and they had been rising for two years.

Keeping rates at minus 0.1 per cent, the central bank also held its yield cap. The governor said the weak yen was helping exports and that the bank would not tighten. The bank bought 10 trillion yen of bonds in the quarter, and the yen fell to 130 to the dollar.

Economists said the quarter was a pause rather than a recession. The reopening of the borders in October was expected to bring tourists, and the government said it would lift the cap on arrivals. Vaccinations had reached 80 per cent of the population, and the booster campaign had begun. The economy grew 0.6 per cent in the second quarter and 0.8 in the third. Growth for the year was 1 per cent, and the yen fell to 150 to the dollar. The bank raised its cap in December, and inflation reached 3.8 per cent in November. Economists said they expected growth in the second quarter as the borders reopened.
