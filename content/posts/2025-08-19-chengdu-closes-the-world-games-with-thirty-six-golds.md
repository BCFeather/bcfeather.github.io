The twelfth World Games closed in Chengdu on the evening of 17 August, after eleven days in which about 4,000 athletes from 116 countries and regions competed in sports that mostly sit outside the Olympic programme. The closing ceremony was held at the Chengdu International Friendship Hall in the Expo park, and the flame was put out at 20:50.

China finished with 36 gold, 17 silver and 11 bronze, 64 medals in all. It was the country's best result at the Games and the first time it has led both the gold and the medal tables. The final gold came on the last afternoon, when Qi Xiangyu won the men's breaking competition; Liu Qingyi took silver in the women's event, which Guo Pu won. The breaking team took two golds and a silver from three events, its first medals at the Games, in a discipline that only joined the programme in 2022.

Three results stand out beyond the count. The women's speed climbing relay and the women's 4x50 metres fin-swimming relay set world records. Deng Lijuan won the women's speed climbing individual in 6.40 seconds, with her teammates second and third, a sweep that the team had not managed before. Shang Chunsong won the women's parkour freestyle, completing a career set of titles in that discipline.

The Games as a whole produced 18 world records and put athletes from 81 countries on the podium, more than at any previous edition. Fifty-two delegations won at least one gold. That spread is the point of the event: a country with no swimming programme can still be excellent at life saving, and a city with one climbing wall can produce a world record. The inclusion of life saving, finswimming and dragon boat also gave the host a set of events in which local clubs already compete.

Chengdu organized the event around existing and temporary venues rather than a single park, and the rowing and dragon boat events were held on a reservoir outside the city. Tickets were sold by session rather than by day, which produced full stands for the popular finals and thin ones for the morning heats, a pattern familiar from other multi-sport events. Organizers said ticket revenue covered a larger share of the operating budget than at previous editions, though not the capital cost of the venues.

The next edition goes to Karlsruhe in 2029, the first German host, and the city has said it will use a mix of new arenas and converted industrial buildings. For the athletes, the four-year gap is the same gap that governs everything else in their careers; for the sports themselves, the question is whether a fortnight of attention every four years is enough to build a base of participants. The evidence from Chengdu is that the attention is real, and that it lasts about as long as the flame. The sports that grew in Chengdu will be the ones that kept their clubs open in September.
