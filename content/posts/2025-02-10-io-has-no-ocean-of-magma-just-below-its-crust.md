title: "Io has no ocean of magma just below its crust"
date: "2025-02-10T20:40:00+08:00"
categories: [science]
description: "Microwave measurements of Io reported in *Nature* find no global layer of molten rock beneath the surface, which contradicts the model in which a shallow magma ocean supplies the moon's volcanoes."
---

Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system. Tidal heating from the resonance with Europa and Ganymede flexes the moon by hundreds of metres twice every orbit, and the energy that the flexing deposits melts rock and drives plumes that reach three hundred kilometres above the surface. The moon's surface is repaved so quickly that it carries no impact craters older than a few million years, and its geology is a matter of weeks, not of aeons.

The standard model put a global ocean of magma at a depth of a few kilometres, trapped beneath a cold lid, and the ocean explained two observations: the heat flow measured from the surface, which is larger than a conductively cooling body could supply, and the volcanoes, which are distributed across the whole surface instead of at a few hot spots.

Measurement that tests the model is the microwave emission of the subsurface. A material's ability to pass a microwave through it depends on its temperature and on whether it is molten, and a radiometer that measures the emission at several wavelengths sees to several depths. A global melt layer would show up as a bright signature at the wavelengths that penetrate to its depth.

Analysis in *Nature* on 10 February reports no such signature. The emission at the wavelengths that would reach a shallow ocean is consistent with cold, solid rock, and the brightness temperatures imply that any melt is confined to the plumes and to the conduits that feed them rather than spread across the moon. The heat flow that the surface radiates is then supplied by localized volcanism, with no global layer involved.

What replaces the model is a partially molten asthenosphere, a region of rock that is above its solidus in a fraction of its volume and that deforms and rises where the tides concentrate the stress. That region supplies the volcanoes without being a liquid ocean, and it is the kind of mantle that a body heated from within and flexed from outside would have.

This result matters for the interpretation of the moon's magnetic field, which is induced by Jupiter and which depends on how conductive the interior is. A global magma ocean would be a highly conductive layer, and a partially molten mantle is less so. The induction signal is the measurement that a spacecraft can make from orbit, and the interior structure that it implies has to match the microwave data.

The Juno spacecraft has been flying past Io and has measured the tides directly, and the combination of gravity, topography and thermal emission will constrain the interior more tightly than any single dataset. A model that survives those three is a model that a future lander could test, and the proposals for a mission to Io have been written around exactly that question, with instruments chosen to measure the heat flow at the surface and the tides from orbit.
