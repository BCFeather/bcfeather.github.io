Policy rates in America stayed at between 5.25 and 5.5 per cent after the meeting on 12 June, and the committee's projections showed a median of one quarter-point cut before the end of the year, down from two in the previous set. The decision came hours after the report that showed inflation at 3.3 per cent. The hold was unanimous in the vote and the change was in the language, which dropped the reference to inflation remaining elevated and replaced it with a description of progress.

Each dot plot is a signal that the market reads. A median that moves from two cuts to one is a statement that the committee expects the economy to stay strong and inflation to fall slowly, and the members who moved were the ones who had been closest to the middle. A projection that shifts by one dot is a message about patience. Seven members projected one cut, eight projected two or more, and four projected none, which is a spread that shows how little agreement there is about the second half. The projections are the committee's own forecast, and the market treats them as a commitment even when the chair says they are not.

Growth is the reason for the patience. The staff raised its forecast for the year, and the chair said the labour market was still tight enough that a premature cut could require a reversal. A committee that has been wrong about inflation before is a committee that would rather be late. The unemployment rate in the projections rose slightly to 4.1 per cent, which is the level the committee treats as consistent with full employment rather than as a warning.

Shrinking the balance sheet is the second instrument. The programme that shrinks the holdings of bonds is still running, and the chair said it would continue until the reserves reach a level that the committee judges ample. A balance sheet that is shrinking is a tightening that the rate does not measure. The runoff has been slowed twice, and the committee has said the pace will depend on the level of reserves rather than on a calendar.

Election-year politics is unavoidable. The meeting was two weeks before the first debate of the campaign, and the chair was asked about the election and said the committee would not respond to politics. A central bank in an election year is a central bank whose every word is parsed for a preference. The chair has been criticized by both campaigns for different reasons, and he has responded by describing the committee's independence as a procedure rather than as a claim.

What the meeting shows is that the last mile of disinflation is being fought with projections rather than with rates, and the committee has chosen to say less and wait longer. A projection that is revised each quarter is a forecast that the market reprices four times a year, and the repricing is the transmission that the committee has come to rely on.
