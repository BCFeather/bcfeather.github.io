Counting the cost of a disease is a way of arguing about where money should go, and the count has to be built from local prices rather than borrowed from another country. A burden study for Argentina does that for lung cancer, one of the country's leading causes of cancer death. A figure in dollars is a claim about the present, and it moves with the exchange rate and with the price of drugs.

The epidemiological inputs came from national statistics, the Global Cancer Observatory, the Global Burden of Disease study and a literature review, and costs were estimated by micro-costing, according to a preprint posted on 10 October 2023. Prices are for April 2023, at 216.38 pesos to the dollar. Micro-costing builds the total from quantities and unit prices instead of from aggregate spending, which is why the result can be decomposed by stage.

Cases are numerous enough that the arithmetic matters. About 10,000 deaths, 12,000 incident cases and 14,000 people alive within five years of diagnosis form the base for the calculation, which is the pool that generates cost in any given year. Prevalent cases matter because treatment continues after diagnosis, so the annual bill is driven by people at every stage.

The direct medical bill comes to US$556.20 million, with an uncertainty interval from 396.96 to 718.20 million, which is about 1.4 per cent of total health expenditure. An interval that wide is honest about the inputs, and it also means the point estimate should not be quoted alone. A second-order Monte Carlo simulation produces the interval, so the range reflects parameter uncertainty and not a guess.

Composition is the part a policymaker can act on. Cost rises with stage at diagnosis, which is the usual pattern for cancer, and drug acquisition accounts for 80 per cent of the total, which puts pharmaceutical pricing at the centre of any attempt to reduce it. Drug spending concentrates where the newest agents are used, so the share is also a statement about their price.

Burden in disability-adjusted life years came to 179,046, or 10 per cent of the total attributable to cancer, and the figure equals 19 per cent of an earlier estimate covering 29 cancers. Those comparisons are the ones that let lung cancer be ranked against other claims on the same budget. A disability-adjusted life year combines years lost to death with years lived in poor health, and the weighting is a judgement.

What the model does not include is indirect cost, such as lost productivity and the unpaid care that families provide, and a single exchange rate for April 2023 is a snapshot in a country where prices move. Screening programmes and procurement negotiations are the two levers the numbers point to. None of the omissions invalidates the direct-cost figure, and each would raise the total if it were included.
