Chinese exports fell 8.8 per cent in August from a year earlier, the customs administration said on 7 September, the third monthly decline. Imports fell 7.3 per cent, and the surplus widened to about 68 billion dollars. The figures cover August and were published a week after the purchasing managers' survey showed the export orders shrinking for a fifth month. The customs office also reported that the trade with the Association of Southeast Asian Nations fell for the first time this year, which the analysts read as a sign that the factories there are buying fewer components.

Much of the fall is about prices. The volumes have held up better than the values, and the prices of the goods China sells, from the electronics to the steel, have fallen from their peaks. A value that falls with the volume is a deficit of price rather than of demand. The prices of the solar panels and the lithium batteries have fallen by a third in a year, and the volumes have grown, which is the pattern of a market that has matured.

Destinations matter. Shipments to the European Union fell by about 20 per cent and to the United States by about 9, while those to Southeast Asia held up and to Russia grew. A trade that shifts east is a trade that has lost its richest customers. The shipments to Russia have grown by about half this year, and the trade with the belt and road countries now exceeds the trade with the west.

Imports show the domestic side. The purchases of the machinery and the components fell, which is the effect of the weak investment in the factories and the property. An import that falls is a factory that buys less. The imports of the integrated circuits fell by about 15 per cent by value, and the imports of the iron ore rose, which reflects the steel output that has held up.

Its surplus is the political number. A widening surplus with Europe has drawn complaints from the manufacturers there, and the European Commission has been investigating the electric cars. A surplus that grows in a slowdown is a surplus that invites a tariff. The commission has said it has evidence of the subsidies, and the investigation is due to conclude next year with the duties that could reach 25 per cent. The ministry of commerce has said it will appeal, and the carmakers have been shifting the production to the plants in Europe.

August shows an export machine that has lost its price advantage and kept its volume, and volume is the part that trade partners can measure. The customs data are the earliest measure of the external demand, and the economists watch them for the sign of whether the factories will hire. A surplus that widens in a slowdown is a surplus that the trading partners will cite, and the citation is the start of a negotiation.
