Core inflation was 6.3 per cent, down from 6.6, and the monthly increase was 0.3 per cent, the smallest since August. The Bureau of Labor Statistics published the report on 10 November, and consumer prices rose 7.7 per cent in the year to October. The forecast had been 7.9, and the reading was down from 8.2 in September. It was the lowest since January and the fourth consecutive decline. Shelter rose 0.8 per cent and was still the largest contributor, and it accounted for half of the monthly increase. The statisticians said rents would keep rising into 2023 because leases are signed a year in advance. The monthly increase was 0.4 per cent, and the figure was 0.1 below the forecast.

Goods prices fell. Used vehicles fell 2.4 per cent and new vehicles were flat, and apparel fell 0.7. Energy rose 1.9 per cent on the month after three declines, and gasoline was 5 per cent higher than in September but still below its June peak. Food rose 0.6 per cent, the slowest since December 2021. Services inflation was 6.7 per cent, and it had not fallen as much as goods. Medical care services rose 0.5 per cent, and transportation services fell 0.9. The category the Fed watches, services excluding shelter, rose 0.2 per cent, which is the smallest monthly increase in a year. Rent rose 0.7 per cent, and owners' equivalent rent rose 0.6, and the two are 30 per cent of the index.

Futures cut the probability of a 75 basis point increase in December to 20 per cent. The market reaction was large. The S&P 500 rose 5.5 per cent, its best day since April 2020, and the Nasdaq rose 7.3. The two-year yield fell 0.3 points to 4.3 per cent, and the dollar fell 2 per cent against the euro. Gasoline was 3.76 dollars a gallon, and it had been 5 dollars in June.

Economists said the decline in goods prices was the result of supply chains healing, and the services component was the part that would take longer. The report changed the inflation debate. The Fed's officials said in speeches afterwards that the number was welcome and that one report was not a trend. Airline fares fell 1.1 per cent, and they were still 40 per cent above their level of 2019.

Rates went up by 50 basis points on 14 December, the step down that the report had made possible. The chairman said the committee would need more evidence before pausing, and it raised twice more in 2023. The October report was the first one that came in below forecast in the cycle.

Inflation kept falling. The annual rate reached 6.4 per cent in January and 3 per cent in June 2023. Shelter inflation peaked in March at 8.2 per cent and then declined, which is the lag that the statisticians had described. The Fed stopped raising in July and held until September 2024.
