The last week of the term began with a test that nobody had revised for, because the notice had come on Friday afternoon. Teachers said the paper would be short, and it was short in the way that a short paper is still an hour of writing. The classroom was cold in the morning and warm by the third period, because the heating in the building came on late and then stayed on. Windows were opened at the break, and the cold came back in through them. The paper had two questions that nobody could answer and one that everybody could, and the marks would be published after the holidays.

Saturday was a school day that month, because the holidays had been rearranged to give a longer break at the new year. The timetable had been rewritten twice, and the copy pinned to the door of the staff room had corrections in three colours of ink. Half the class arrived with the wrong books, and the teacher lent out the spare set that she kept in the cupboard. Nobody minded very much, because the week was already running on the understanding that it would end. The staff room door carried a second notice about the arrangements for the new year, and somebody had drawn a face beside it.

Between the lessons there were tasks that belonged to no subject. The classroom had to be cleared of the old posters, the lockers had to be emptied, and the class photograph had to be paid for. Somebody had to return the library books, and the fines for the ones that were late were collected in a hat. The corridors smelt of the cleaning liquid that the caretaker used before the holidays, and the caretaker himself was in a better mood than usual.

The afternoon was given to a meeting that was half a lesson and half a conversation. The head teacher came to talk about the examinations in June, and she brought a sheet with the dates and the subjects printed on it. She said that the next term would be shorter than it looked and that the count of weeks was smaller than the count of things to be done. Then she asked whether anybody had a question, and nobody did. She also said that the school would open for revision sessions in the last week of January, and that attendance would be voluntary.

Afterwards a few of us walked to the shop at the corner and bought hot drinks with the money that was left after the photograph. The road was busy with cars taking people home early, and the sky was already the colour it gets at four in December. We stood outside the shop for a while because the conversation was better than the walk, and then we went our separate ways. The shopkeeper knew most of us by name and asked whether the term had ended, and we said that it nearly had.

What I remember of that Saturday is the sound of the building when it emptied. The doors banged, the caretaker's radio played in the corridor, and the lights went off in the rooms one by one. The holidays had not started yet, and already the school felt like a place that had been left.
