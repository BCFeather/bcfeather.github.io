China's economy grew by 4.5 per cent in the first quarter compared with a year earlier, well above the forecast of about 4 per cent and up from 2.9 per cent in the previous quarter. The rebound was the first full quarter after the reopening, and the services did most of the work. The economy had grown by 3 per cent in 2022, and the quarter is the first read on the year that the government has set at about 5 per cent.

Retail sales rose by 10.6 per cent over the year in March, and the catering rose by about 26 per cent as the restaurants filled again. The travel and the hotel bookings recovered to the level of 2019 in the holiday weeks, and the cinema box office had its best month since the pandemic. The box office takings in the first quarter were about 60 per cent above the level of the same period in 2022, and the domestic films took most of it. The catering revenue rose by about 26 per cent, and the hotels reported the highest occupancy since 2019 in the holiday weeks.

Factories were slower. The industrial output rose by 3.6 per cent in March, and the exports fell by about 13 per cent as the demand in Europe and America weakened. The manufacturing survey has been below 50 for several months, and the new orders are the weakest line in it. The electricity consumption rose by about 5 per cent in March, which is a better guide to the factory activity than the survey.

Property remains the problem. The investment in the real estate fell by about 5.8 per cent in the quarter, and the new starts fell by about 19 per cent. The sales of the new homes fell by about 1.8 per cent in March, which is an improvement on the winter but not a recovery. The completed floor space fell by about 17 per cent, and the land sales by the local authorities fell by about a third.

Stimulus has been kept modest. The infrastructure spending rose by about 9 per cent, and the local authorities have been slowed by the debts of the last cycle. The consumer spending is the part that has to carry the year, and the households are still cautious. The youth unemployment rose to about 19.6 per cent in March, and the number is the one the analysts cite when they question the strength of the recovery.

An official target of about 5 per cent for the year leaves the rest of the year with a lower bar, and the economists expect the base effects to slow the headline in the second quarter. A rebound that is led by the restaurants is a rebound that has to spread to the factories. The base of comparison turns harder in the second quarter, and the services will have to hold the pace of the first.
