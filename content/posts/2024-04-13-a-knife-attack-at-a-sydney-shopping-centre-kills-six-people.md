---Man armed with a knife entered the Westfield shopping centre at Bondi Junction in eastern Sydney on the afternoon of the thirteenth of April and stabbed people on two levels of the complex. Six people died, five of them women, and at least twelve were taken to hospital with wounds. Police identified the attacker as Joel Cauchi, a forty-year-old from Queensland, and said he had been known to mental health services and had no connection to any organized group.

Course of the attack was recorded by security cameras and by witnesses. He entered through a main entrance, moved through shops and towards a children's area, and was confronted by shoppers who tried to hold him back with whatever they could find. Police inspector Amy Scott, who was nearby on an unrelated matter, entered the centre alone, followed him and shot him when he refused to drop the weapon. She was later praised for her action and for the restraint she showed in not shooting earlier.

Victims included a first-time mother, Ashlee Good, whose nine-month-old daughter was wounded and survived, the daughter of a well-known businessman, and Faraz Tahir, a security guard who had arrived in Australia as a refugee a year earlier. Several of the injured were in critical condition for days, and the hospital that treated them said the wounds were consistent with a person attacking at close range without a plan.

Motivation became the central question of the investigation. Police said Cauchi had a history of mental illness and had been diagnosed with schizophrenia, and they said they had found no evidence of terrorism or of any ideological motive. His family issued a statement through the police saying they were devastated and that he had been struggling for years, and they said they had no idea what had driven him.

Political consequence was a debate about access to weapons and about mental health services. The state government said it would review the regulation of knives and would examine whether the police needed more powers at large venues. Mental health organizations said the case showed that community services had failed a man who had been asking for help for years, and they criticized the way the attack was used in the following weeks to argue for more involuntary treatment.

A coronial inquiry into the deaths was opened later in the year, and it is expected to examine the attacker's contact with mental health services. The centre reopened six days later with a memorial service at the entrance and with counsellors available to staff.

The Meaning of the thirteenth of April is a country that has built its identity around the absence of this kind of violence. Australia has strict gun laws and a low rate of homicide, and the attack produced the kind of shock that the Port Arthur massacre did in 1996. Two days later a separate stabbing at a church in western Sydney was declared a terrorist act, which complicated the picture, and the two events together produced a conversation about what can be prevented and what cannot.
