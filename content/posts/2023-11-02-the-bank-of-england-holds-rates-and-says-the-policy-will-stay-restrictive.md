Policy rate stayed at 5.25 per cent after the Bank of England meeting on 2 November, the second consecutive hold, and the committee said the policy would need to stay restrictive for an extended period. The decision was the first in which the committee did not raise the rate since the end of 2021, and the governor said the bank was not forecasting a cut.

Committee members split six to three. Three members wanted another quarter point, and the minutes recorded that the majority had seen enough evidence in the fall of the services inflation to wait. A vote that narrows is a committee that is near the turn. The services inflation was 6.9 per cent in September, and the majority said the fall from the spring was enough to justify the pause.

Its forecast is the new element. The bank projected the inflation to fall to about 4.5 per cent by the end of the year and to about 2 per cent in two years, and it said the recession would be shallow. A forecast that is lower is a rate that can be cut. The bank expects the inflation to reach the target in the second half of 2025, which is later than the government has promised. The bank also said the average rate on the fixed mortgages will rise to about 4 per cent by the end of next year, from about 2 per cent now.

Growth is the drag. The economy has stagnated for a year, and the bank expects the output to be flat next year, which is weaker than the population growth. A stagnation that is forecast is a demand that is absent. The unemployment rate is about 4.2 per cent, and the bank expects it to reach 5 per cent by the end of next year as the firms cut the hiring. The private sector pay has been rising by about 8 per cent, and the bank expects it to slow to about 5 next year as the unemployment rises.

Traders moved the cut earlier. The traders priced the first reduction for the summer, and the two-year yield fell to its lowest since the spring. A hold that is expected is a currency that does not move. The pound fell about 0.4 per cent against the dollar, and the ten-year yield fell to the lowest since the summer. The bank's own forecast assumes three cuts over the next two years, and the governor said the market was ahead of the committee.

What the meeting shows is that the bank has ended the increases and is managing the fall of the inflation in a flat economy, and the flat is the part that decides the timing. A bank that forecasts a shallow recession is a bank that has chosen to accept the stagnation rather than the inflation, and the choice is the policy.
