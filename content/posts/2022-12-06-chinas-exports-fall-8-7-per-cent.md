Imports fell 10.6 per cent, and the trade surplus widened to 69.8 billion dollars. The customs administration published the figures on 7 December, and exports fell 8.7 per cent in the year to November. The forecast had been a fall of 4 per cent, and the reading was the steepest since February 2020. The decline was broad. Exports to the United States fell 25 per cent, and to the European Union 11. Shipments to Southeast Asia fell 3 per cent, which was the first decline in two years. Electronics fell 20 per cent, and textiles fell 15. The customs figures are published in dollars, and the exchange rate moved little in the month.

November 2021 had been a record month, when shipments were catching up after the port closures of the previous year. The comparison was difficult. The two-year comparison was a rise of 6 per cent, which is weaker than the trend but not a collapse. The customs administration said the base effect explained part of the figure. Exports of laptops fell 30 per cent, and of smartphones 20.

Factories in the Pearl River Delta reported that they had cut production in November, and the ports had reported delays. The restrictions were the cause. The survey of purchasing managers had shown export orders at 46.7, and the customs figures arrived two days later. The global cycle was the other cause. Retailers in America and in Europe were clearing inventory, and they had ordered less for the spring. The container freight rate from Shanghai to Los Angeles had fallen 80 per cent from its peak, which reflects the drop in volumes. The shipping companies had begun to cancel sailings. The port of Ningbo reported that 20 per cent of its truck drivers were absent in the last week.

Imports fell for a different reason. The volume of imported iron ore fell 5 per cent, and of crude oil 3, and the prices of both had fallen. The value of imports from Japan fell 20 per cent, and from Korea 15. The weak domestic demand was the reason, and the property slump was the largest part of it. The freight rate had been 2,000 dollars a container in 2021, and it was 400.

Still large was the surplus. For the year it reached 850 billion dollars, the highest on record and 5 per cent of output. That surplus was a support for growth, and it was the reason the current account was in surplus even as the economy slowed. The surplus was 5 per cent of output, which is the largest share since 2007.

Exports fell again in December, by 9.9 per cent, and they turned positive in April 2023 as the reopening took effect. The recovery was weak, and exports fell 5 per cent for the year. The surplus stayed above 800 billion dollars, and it became a political issue in Europe and in America.
