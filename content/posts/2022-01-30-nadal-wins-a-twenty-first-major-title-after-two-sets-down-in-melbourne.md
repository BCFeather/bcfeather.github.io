Rafael Nadal beat Daniil Medvedev in the final of the Australian Open on 30 January, coming back from two sets down to win 2-6, 6-7, 6-4, 6-4, 7-5 in five hours and twenty-four minutes. The title was his second in Melbourne and his twenty-first at a major tournament, which put him one ahead of Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic in the tally that has defined the men's game for two decades. The fifth set alone lasted more than an hour, and the games at four-all and five-all included nine deuces and a break point saved with a second serve that clipped the line.

Match turned in the third set, when Nadal broke serve and then held through a series of long games that lasted more than ten minutes. Medvedev, the US Open champion and the second seed, had won the first set in twenty-eight minutes and had served for the match at 5-4 in the fifth; Nadal broke him twice in the last four games, and the second break ended the match. He had also changed his service motion in the off-season to take pressure off the foot, and the change showed in the free points he won in the last two sets.

Circumstances make the result stranger than the score. Nadal had not played a competitive match for five months because of a foot injury, he had been ill with the coronavirus in December, and he arrived in Melbourne saying that he expected to play and would see how the foot held. He had also been close to retiring in 2021, and he spoke afterwards about the limits of what his body would allow.

Medvedev's tournament had been eventful before the final. He had complained about the crowd after his semi-final win, and he had been on the receiving end of the booing that followed, and the final crowd was loudly behind his opponent. He was also the man who had lost the 2019 US Open final in five sets after winning the first two, which made the pattern of this final familiar in an unkind way.

Result changes the arithmetic of the great rivalry without settling it. Twenty-one majors is a lead that Federer, who was recovering from knee surgery, could not close at his age, and Djokovic's refusal to be vaccinated had kept him out of Melbourne, which meant that the man most likely to catch up had spent the tournament at home. He had also spent the fortnight between matches practising for four hours a day, which is more than most players attempt at his age.

Records in tennis are counted in titles because that is what the sport can measure, and the count now has a leader. What the final showed is that the three players who built the era are still the ones deciding it, and that the difference between them is being made by bodies rather than by strokes. He had also said before the tournament that he would consider retirement if the foot failed him again, and the win postponed that decision for another season.
