Any figure above 50 signals expansion, and the survey had been below the mark for seven of the previous eight months. The National Bureau of Statistics published the purchasing managers index on 1 March, and the manufacturing reading was 52.6. That was up from 50.1 in January and the highest since April 2012. The non-manufacturing index rose to 56.3, its best since 2012. The bureau also revised the January manufacturing figure up from 49.6, which changed the shape of the winter.

Production and new orders both improved, and the sub-index for new export orders crossed 50 for the first time since 2021. That matters because exports had been the weakest part of the economy through the autumn. Input prices stayed below 50, which means factories were still paying less for materials than a year earlier. Finished goods inventories fell, which suggests the orders were being met rather than stored. Import orders were the weakest component, and they stayed below 50 for the eighteenth month.

Both series had been distorted by the lunar new year, and the February figures were the first clean read of the year. The survey covers 3,000 manufacturers and is compiled by the statistics bureau with the logistics federation. Its seasonal adjustment is less refined than that of the private index published by Caixin, which came in at 51.6 a day later. The two surveys differ because the official one covers state-owned firms more heavily and the private one covers exporters.

Small and medium manufacturers were still below 50, and the employment sub-index stayed at 49.3, which means hiring continued to fall. The improvement was concentrated in the largest firms. That gap is the reason the official read looked stronger than the anecdote from the coastal provinces, where factories reported fewer orders than the index implied. Employment in manufacturing had fallen for eight months, and the sub-index improved by only 0.3 points. Construction activity rose, and the sub-index for building reached 60.8, its highest since 2011. Infrastructure projects funded by bonds issued in the autumn had started on site, and the property sector was completing homes that had been started before the downturn. Steel rebar inventories fell for the third month, which is consistent with the construction reading. Cement output rose 5 per cent in February, which is the first increase since the summer of 2021.

A reading above 52 for two months is usually followed by a rise in industrial production, and the January output figure due in March was expected to confirm it. The index is watched because it is the first monthly indicator of the year and it is published before the trade and industrial output figures. The next release was scheduled for 31 March. Seasonal adjustment for the lunar new year is why the bureau also publishes a comparison with the same month a year earlier.

Economists said the survey had overstated the recovery twice in 2022, when the spring and autumn rebounds faded within a quarter. The difference this time was that the reopening was national rather than local, and that the property measures had been widened. Whether that is enough to hold the reading above 50 through the summer was the question the March figures would answer. Industrial production for January and February was published together on 15 March and showed growth of 5.6 per cent.
