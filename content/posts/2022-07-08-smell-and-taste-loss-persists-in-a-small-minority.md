Early reports of COVID described smell loss in a majority of patients, and the reports were written when nobody knew how long the loss would last. A meta-analysis with reconstructed individual patient data now supplies the fraction that never recovers. A symptom that fades for most patients is a symptom that is easy to dismiss, and a symptom that never fades for a few is a symptom that needs a service.

Searches of Medline, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Library and medRxiv to 3 October 2021 for observational studies of adults with COVID-related smell or taste dysfunction, and reconstructed time-to-event data from published curves. The paper appeared in *The BMJ*, 27 July 2022. A reconstructed individual patient dataset is derived from published curves rather than from raw records, and the derivation is the reason the review could pool studies that reported different summary statistics.

Eighteen studies with 3,699 patients were pooled, and parametric cure modelling estimated persistent self-reported smell dysfunction in 5.6 per cent, with an interval from 2.7 to 11.0, and persistent taste dysfunction in 4.4 per cent, with an interval from 1.2 to 14.6. A confidence interval describes the mean of the pooled estimate and a prediction interval describes a new study, and the two are different quantities that a reader often confuses.

Prediction intervals are much wider than the confidence intervals, from 0.7 to 33.5 per cent for smell, which reflects the heterogeneity between studies. A prediction interval describes where a new study would fall, and the width is the honest statement about how much the estimates vary. A clinic-based series recruits the people who have not recovered, which is why the early reports put the figure in the majority and not in the single digits.

The pooled analysis follows the whole cohort through time, and the difference between 5.6 per cent and a majority is the difference between a prevalence and an incidence. The minority who never recover is large in absolute terms because the number of infections is large, and a health system needs a clinic for them.
