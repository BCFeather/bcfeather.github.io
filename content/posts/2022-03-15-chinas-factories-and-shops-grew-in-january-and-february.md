Factories carried the gain. The office said the car output had risen 7 per cent, and that the steel had risen 3. Machinery rose 9 per cent, and the electronics 12. The office said the exports had risen 10 per cent in the two months, and that the ports had cleared their backlog. Retail sales rose 6.7 per cent, and fixed investment 12.2. Property investment rose 3.7 per cent, and infrastructure carried the gain. The figures were combined for two months because of the holidays.

Retail sales rose 6.7 per cent, and the office said the online sales had risen 10. The office said the restaurants had risen 8 per cent, and that the cars had risen 5. It said the retail had been cut by the closures in January, and that the sales had recovered in February. Car output rose 7 per cent, steel 3, machinery 9 and electronics 12. Exports rose 10 per cent in the two months as ports cleared backlogs. The statistics office said factories ran at 76 per cent of capacity.

Property was weak. The office said the sales had fallen 10 per cent in the two months, and that the starts had fallen 12. The office said the developers had been cut by the borrowing limits, and that the government had eased them in February. The investment rose 3.7 per cent, and it was the slowest since 2020. Online sales rose 10 per cent, restaurants 8 and cars 5. Closures cut January trade, and sales recovered in February. The office said retail had been cut by closures in January.

Unemployment reached 5.5 per cent in February, and the office said 10 million people had looked for work. The office said the youth unemployment was 15 per cent, and that the services had hired less. Property sales fell 10 per cent and starts 12 in the two months. Borrowing limits squeezed developers, and the government eased them in February. Property investment was the slowest since 2020.

Lockdowns cut the figures in March. The office said the retail had fallen 3.5 per cent, and that the industrial output had risen 5. The office said the Shanghai lockdown had cut the ports, and that the exports had fallen 3 per cent in April. Ten million people looked for work, and youth unemployment reached 15 per cent.
