A fissure opened on the Reykjanes peninsula in south-western Iceland on 22 August, the sixth eruption in the area in three years, and the lava flowed away from the town that had been evacuated in previous events. The Blue Lagoon, a geothermal spa that is the region's largest tourist employer, closed for a week. The fissure opened near a previous eruption site and the lava moved across a lava field rather than towards the harbour.

Geology is a change in the peninsula's behaviour. A system that had been quiet for eight centuries began erupting in 2021, and the episodes have become more frequent and shorter, which is the signature of a magma body that is being refilled from below. A region that had no volcanic history in the record is now the country's most active. Geochemists have traced the magma to a reservoir at a depth of several kilometres, fed by a plume that the peninsula sits above.

Town is the reason the eruptions matter politically. A fishing community of a few thousand people has been evacuated several times, and the state has built barriers to divert lava, which is an engineering response to a geological process. A barrier that works is a barrier that buys years, not decades. The barriers are earthen walls built in a few months, and they have diverted flows in three of the eruptions so far.

Infrastructure is exposed. A geothermal power plant and a pipeline that supplies hot water to the region are inside the area that lava has threatened, and the plant supplies electricity to a large share of the country. A utility that is built on a volcanic system is a utility that has to plan for the system's behaviour. The pipeline that carries hot water is buried and cannot be moved, and a flow across it would leave the region without heating in winter. The utility has drilled backup wells and moved some equipment, and those measures are cheaper than relocating a plant that supplies the capital.

Economy of the peninsula is tourism and fish. A spa that closes for a week loses revenue that the surrounding hotels also lose, and the government has compensated the businesses in previous eruptions. A compensation scheme is a recognition that the state has chosen to keep the town where it is. The lagoon's closure is a decision about the road that leads to it, and the road was rebuilt after the last flow crossed it.

What the eruption shows is that a country can live with a volcanic system and cannot predict its next move. The barriers, the monitoring and the evacuation plans are the terms of the arrangement, and the arrangement is renegotiated at every fissure. An arrangement with a volcano is a series of temporary measures, and the measures are renewed each time the ground opens. The state has decided to keep the town where it is, and that decision is renewed every time the lava comes close.
