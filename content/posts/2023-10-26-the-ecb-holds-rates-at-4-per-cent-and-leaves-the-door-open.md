Deposit rate stayed at 4 per cent after the European Central Bank meeting on 26 October, its first hold after ten consecutive increases, and the statement said inflation was expected to stay above the target for too long. The statement added that the rates would be set at restrictive levels for as long as necessary. The decision was unanimous in the vote but not in the argument, and the president said the discussion had been about how long the rates would stay at this level.

Wording is the compromise. The bank dropped the reference to the future increases and declined to declare the peak, which is the language of a committee that is split between the hawks and the doves. A sentence that is ambiguous is a decision that is deferred. The president said the bank had not discussed the cuts, and she declined to repeat the guidance that the rates would stay high for as long as the inflation is above the target.

Weak growth is the case for the doves. The economy contracted in the third quarter, the surveys show the services weakening, and the credit is shrinking as the banks tighten the terms. A recession that arrives is a cut that the bank will have to consider. The lending survey showed the demand for the credit falling for a fifth quarter, and the banks expect to tighten the standards further by the end of the year.

Wages are the case for the hawks. The negotiated pay has been rising by about 5 per cent, and the bank has said the services inflation will not fall until the settlements for next year are known. A wage that is agreed is a cost that the prices will follow. The settlements for this year have been about 4.5 per cent, and the bank's staff expects about 4 per cent next year as the contracts are renewed.

Its balance sheet keeps shrinking. The bank said it would keep the run-off of the bonds at the current pace, which removes about 30 billion euros of the support a month. A balance sheet that shrinks is a tightening that happens without a vote. The bank has been reducing the portfolio by about 30 billion euros a month since the summer, and the reinvestment will stop altogether next year. The run-off is the automatic tightening that continues even when the rates are held, and the bank has said it will review the pace next year.

What the meeting shows is that the bank has stopped raising and is not ready to say so, and the gap between the two is the uncertainty that the market must trade. A hold that is ambiguous is a hold that the market will test with the next forecast, and the forecast is in December. A meeting that changes nothing is a meeting that sets the terms for December, and the terms are the forecasts.
