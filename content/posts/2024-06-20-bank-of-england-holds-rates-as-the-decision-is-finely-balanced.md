Bank Rate stayed at 5.25 per cent after the meeting on 20 June, and the minutes described the decision as finely balanced, with some members saying the case for a reduction had been established and others saying the services data did not yet support one. The rate has been at this level since August, the longest pause in the cycle, and the balance sheet has been shrinking throughout. The vote count was unchanged from the previous meeting, and the new element was the language that the minutes used about the balance of risks.

Seven members voted to hold and two to cut. The two who wanted a cut had argued for one at the previous meeting as well, and their reasoning was that policy was more restrictive than it needed to be at the current level of inflation. A minority that grows is a majority that is expected to arrive. The minutes described the labour market as loosening, with vacancies falling and the unemployment rate rising, which is the condition the members had set for a cut. The members who wanted a cut argued that the real rate had risen as the inflation fell, which is a tightening that nobody had decided on.

Wages are the obstacle. The committee said settlements in the private sector were running at a rate consistent with inflation above the target, and the data that would settle the question arrive in the autumn. A decision that waits for data is a decision the calendar sets. The settlements the committee examines are reported with a lag, and the figures for the second half of the year will not be available before the August meeting.

Campaign conventions are the constraint. The committee has a convention that it does not announce a change during a campaign unless it is necessary, and the meeting fell three weeks before the vote, which the members said they had taken into account. A central bank that observes a convention accepts a delay. The governor has said the committee would not be influenced by the campaign, and the timing of the meeting was fixed a year in advance.

Traders are now pricing August. The swaps that settle on the next meeting imply a probability close to two thirds, and the economists who follow the committee expect the first cut then. A path that is priced is a path the committee can follow without surprising anyone. The two-year yield fell after the release and the pound was little changed, which is the reaction of a market that had expected the hold.

What the meeting shows is that the last quarter point of a tightening cycle is the hardest to remove, because the evidence that justifies it arrives after the decision that needs it. A pause described as balanced is a pause the next meeting will end, and the only question is whether the data or the calendar decides the date.
