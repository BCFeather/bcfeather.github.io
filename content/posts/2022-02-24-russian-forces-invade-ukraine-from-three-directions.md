Vladimir Putin announced on the morning of 24 February that he had ordered a special military operation in Ukraine, and within an hour missiles were falling on cities across the country. Explosions were reported in Kyiv, Kharkiv, Odesa, Dnipro and Kramatorsk, and the air-raid sirens sounded over the capital for the first time since 1941. Columns of armour crossed the border from the north through Belarus, from the east across the Donbas, and from the south out of the Crimea that Russia had annexed in 2014.

Fighting opened at the airport of Hostomel, twenty kilometres north-west of Kyiv, where Russian helicopters landed a force meant to hold the runway for transport aircraft. Ukrainian troops retook part of the airport the same day, and the battle for the capital began along two roads that the armoured columns used. A separate advance moved west from Kharkiv towards the Dnieper, and a third moved north from the Crimea towards Melitopol and Mariupol, along the coast that runs to Odesa.

Martial law and a general mobilization followed that morning, and men between 18 and 60 were ordered to stay. President Zelensky recorded a video in the street outside his office and said his government was still there; when an American offer of evacuation came, he was reported to have said that he needed ammunition rather than a ride. The parliament passed a law on mobilization the same evening, and the first territorial defence units were handed weapons.

Casualties on the first day were uncertain and disputed. The Ukrainian authorities said more than a hundred people had been killed, including soldiers and civilians, and the Russian defence ministry said it had struck only military infrastructure. The Chernobyl plant, sixty kilometres north of Kyiv, was taken by Russian troops after a fight that the Ukrainian nuclear agency said had damaged nothing important, and its staff continued to work under armed guard.

Western governments announced sanctions within hours. The European Union agreed a package that froze the assets of the president and the foreign and defence ministers and cut several banks from the financial messaging system, and the United States, Britain and Canada imposed their own measures. The German chancellor said the invasion marked a turning point, and the leaders of the alliance agreed to reinforce the eastern members that bordered Russia and Ukraine.

Refugees began to arrive at the Polish, Slovak, Hungarian and Romanian borders on the first day, and the United Nations agency that counts them put the figure at a million within a week. Trains from Kyiv to Lviv and from Lviv to Poland were full, and the queues at the crossings reached twenty kilometres. Most of the people leaving were women and children, because the mobilization kept the men behind. The war that began that morning was expected by the intelligence services and by the governments that had spent February warning about it, and it came as a surprise to the Ukrainian public, which had been told by its own president that an attack was possible and that the warnings were hurting the economy. The sirens became the sound of the spring in every city, and the map of the war changed for the first time since 2014.
