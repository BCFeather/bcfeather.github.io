Benedict XVI died on the last morning of 2022, in the small monastery inside the Vatican gardens where he had lived since his resignation in 2013. He was ninety-five. The Vatican announced the death on 31 December and said that the funeral would be simple, as he had asked, and that it would be held on 5 January in St Peter's Square.

Pope Francis presided at the mass, which began at half past nine in the morning under a grey sky. The Sistine Chapel Choir sang, the readings were in Latin and in several other languages, and the dean of the College of Cardinals celebrated at the altar. The Vatican said that about sixty thousand people were in the square and that more than three thousand priests concelebrated. The mass was the ordinary requiem, with the readings the Church uses for every pope, and the difference was in the two popes standing at the altar.

Between the death and the funeral, sixty-five thousand people filed past the body as it lay in state in the basilica. The queue stretched along the side of the colonnade and around the square, and the Italian police opened extra routes for it on the second day. The body was dressed in the red vestments of a pope, and the face was the face of the man who had taught for eight years and then gone quiet. The basilica stayed open through the night on the last day, and the final visitors were admitted after midnight.

The funeral of a pope who had resigned had no recent precedent to follow. Benedict had invented the title of pope emeritus, had kept the white cassock, and had lived a few hundred metres from the office he had left, which meant that the ceremony had to mourn a pope without electing one. Francis, who was seventy-six when he was elected, spoke in his homily of the faithfulness of a servant, and the cardinals knelt in the order that the protocol required.

The German president, Frank-Walter Steinmeier, attended, and so did delegations from a long list of states, some of them represented by heads of government rather than by heads of state. The ceremony was watched in Germany and in Bavaria in particular, where Benedict had been archbishop of Munich before he was cardinal, and where the abuse reports of the previous year had made his record a matter of public argument. Italy sent its president, and the United States sent a delegation rather than its president.

He was buried in the Vatican crypt, in the niche that had held the coffin of John Paul II before that coffin was moved upstairs after the beatification. The grave is a few steps from the tomb of the apostle, which is where the line of popes has ended for centuries, and where the man who ended it had chosen to spend his last decade being quiet. He had asked to be buried in the ground rather than in a monument, and the simplicity of the stone was noted by everyone who described it.
