Typhoon Ragasa, the eighteenth named storm of the season, came ashore at Hailing Island in Yangjiang at about five in the afternoon on 24 September. Winds at landfall were force 13, forty metres per second, with a central pressure of 955 hectopascals, according to the provincial meteorological service. The storm had peaked at 62 metres per second earlier in the week, the highest of any tropical cyclone anywhere this year.

Its history was unusual from the start. Ragasa formed on 18 September in the western Pacific, in the same stretch of ocean where Mitag and Neoguri appeared within hours of it; three storms spinning at once in the same basin is uncommon but not rare in September. Warm water and weak vertical shear let it intensify from a tropical storm to a super typhoon in about 48 hours, and it kept that strength for more than three days, with a clear eye and a wind circle at force 7 nearly 500 kilometres across that shrank to about 250 kilometres as the centre approached the coast.

Preparations began well before the eye reached the coast. Guangdong suspended classes and work in Shenzhen, Yangjiang, Foshan, Shantou and Chaozhou from 23 September, shut down the provincial rail network the following day, and closed all 1,038 shelters in Yangjiang. By six in the evening on 24 September, the province reported moving 2.22 million people out of hazardous areas; Yangjiang alone accounted for 167,343, with 9,970 fishing boats back in port and every crew member off the offshore wind platforms.

Damage came in the usual forms. Storm surge arrived on a spring tide, and seawater backed up through drainage in Hong Kong, Macau and Zhuhai. More than twenty hours of gale-force gusts along the coast broke station records at eleven national observatories in Guangdong and Guangxi, four of them all-time records rather than September records. Rain of 300 to 482 millimetres fell in parts of Shenzhen, Maoming, Yangjiang and Jieyang, and 600 to 800 millimetres in Hualien and Taitung on Taiwan, where the barrier lake at Matian'an Creek overtopped on 23 September and took out a bridge downstream.

Ragasa crossed the coast twice more, at Beihai before dawn on 25 September and at Fangchenggang at noon, both at tropical storm strength, and was retired from the numbering that evening over Guangxi. The central government released 150 million yuan in emergency relief and 200 million in reconstruction funds for Guangdong. Power was restored in parts of Yangjiang within a day; in the villages nearest the landfall, the count of damaged roofs will take longer.

A storm that hits three times tests the same systems twice more than a storm that hits once. The warnings worked, the trains stopped, and the shelters held; the losses were in crops, in aquaculture and in the older houses that every typhoon finds, and in the fishing gear that was left on the rafts because the season had two weeks to run.
