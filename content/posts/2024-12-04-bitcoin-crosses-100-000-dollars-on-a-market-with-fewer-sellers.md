Bitcoin traded above one hundred thousand dollars for the first time on 4 December, a rise of about forty per cent from the level it held before the American election, and the move was attributed to flows into exchange-traded funds and to a market in which the supply of coins for sale has shrunk. A forty per cent move in a month is a move that a fund cannot explain to a client who expects bonds.

Supply story is arithmetic. The network issues a fixed number of coins per block, the reward halved in April, and the funds that buy for investors have absorbed more than the new issuance in most months. A market where the buyers take the new supply and some of the old is a market where the price has to rise to persuade a holder to sell. A halving that cuts issuance is a halving that arrives on a date the market knew years in advance.

Demand story is about the wrapper. A spot fund that trades on an exchange and holds coins with a custodian is a product that an institution can own without opening an account at a crypto exchange. A pension fund that could not hold an unregulated asset can hold a fund share, and the share brings a new class of buyer. A share in a fund is a claim on coins that a custodian controls, and the custodian is a counterparty.

Volatility is not reduced by the wrapper. The same asset that rose forty per cent in a month fell more than seventy per cent in 2022, and the funds that make the asset easier to own also make it easier to sell. A product that is available to everyone is a product that can be abandoned by everyone. The mining economics have decoupled from the price. Miners sell coins to cover electricity, and the consolidation that followed the halving left the industry with fewer, larger operators. A miner that has locked in a power price is a miner that can survive a price fall that would close a rival. A market that can be entered through an app is a market that can be exited through the same app in the same hour.

Comparison with gold is the one the buyers make. A store of value that is issued by a protocol rather than by a state is a store of value whose supply cannot be increased by a government, and that property is the whole thesis. Whether it holds is a question that a decade of volatility has not answered. A store of value that a government cannot issue is a store of value whose supply is a protocol parameter.

What the milestone marks is that an asset created to avoid institutions has been adopted by them. The adoption is what produced the price, and the price is what makes the adoption self-reinforcing until it is not. A milestone that attracts attention is a milestone that attracts buyers who are not sure why they are buying.
