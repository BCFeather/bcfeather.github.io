American employers added 187,000 jobs in July, below the forecast of about 200,000 and the second month of the slowdown. The unemployment rate fell to 3.5 per cent from 3.6, and the participation slipped to 62.6. The two previous months were revised down by about 185,000. The figure was the smallest since December 2020 apart from the revisions, and the three-month average is about 218,000. The revisions were the largest since the spring of 2020, and the economists said the picture of the spring was weaker than it had looked.

Hiring has cooled. The health care added about 63,000 jobs, the leisure about 17,000, and the construction about 19,000. The temporary help fell for a sixth month, and the manufacturing shed about 4,000. The government added about 15,000, which is the slowest since the winter. The retail shed about 8,000 jobs, and the transport and the warehousing about 10,000 as the parcel volumes stayed weak. The job openings were about 9.6 million in June, and the ratio of the openings to the unemployed was about 1.5.

Wage growth held at 4.4 per cent over the year, and the monthly change was 0.4. The measure for the production workers was about 4.7, and the one for the managers about 4.0. The pace has been between 4.3 and 4.5 for five months, which is the plateau the economists have been expecting. The wage growth for the leisure was about 5 per cent, and the one for the financial services about 4.8.

A second survey was stronger. The employment rose by about 270,000, and the number of the unemployed fell by about 110,000. The prime-age participation was about 83.4, and the share of the people working part-time for the economic reasons fell to about 3.6 per cent. The number of the people working from home fell to about 12 per cent, which is the lowest since the spring of 2022.

Markets took it as a sign of the cooling without the break. The two-year yield fell by about 12 basis points, and the traders cut the odds of a September increase to about 20 per cent. The dollar fell against the basket. The committee meets on 19 and 20 September, and the market expects the rate to be left at 5.25 to 5.5 per cent.

This report is the one that closes the summer. A job market that adds 187,000 jobs with the wages at 4.4 is a market that is landing rather than crashing, and the landing is the part the committee has been trying to arrange. The inflation report of 10 August is the other half of the case, and the core at 4.7 is the number the committee wants to see fall.
