---

Guidelines divide atrial fibrillation into risk bands, and the band in between is the one clinicians argue about. A CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 in a man or 2 in a woman sits at the threshold where the benefit of anticoagulation is real but small, and where the bleeding risk of a drug taken for years is not negligible. Most trials enrolled patients at higher risk, so the intermediate group has been treated by extrapolation. The threshold exists because trials enrolled older and sicker patients, and a guideline has to say something about everyone else.

The SINGLE-AF trial, conducted in South Korea as an open-label, adjudicator-masked superiority study, randomised 1,803 patients with atrial fibrillation and that intermediate score to a direct oral anticoagulant or to no anticoagulation. Mean age was 60.4 years and 23.7 per cent were women, which describes a population younger than the trials that set the guidelines. Results appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine* on 28 August.

At 24 months the primary composite of stroke, systemic embolism, major bleeding or cardiovascular death occurred in 4 patients on the drug, a cumulative incidence of 0.5 per cent, and in 13 without it, 1.5 per cent. The difference was 1.0 percentage points, with an interval from 2.0 to 0.1, *P*=0.03, and the hazard ratio was 0.31 with an interval from 0.10 to 0.94. Cumulative incidence is a kinder measure than a raw count, since it accounts for the different times patients spent in the trial.

Absolute numbers are what this trial turns on, and they are small in both arms. Three strokes occurred on treatment against 10 without it, which is the component that carries the argument, since major bleeding did not differ and no cardiovascular deaths occurred in either group. A composite that moves only through one component is a real result and a narrow one, and the paper reports serious adverse events at 8.9 and 9.3 per cent respectively.

Two features of the design limit how far the result travels. It was open-label, so neither patients nor investigators were masked to assignment, and an adjudicator-masked endpoint does not remove the possibility that surveillance differed between arms. The trial was also run in one country with a homogeneous population, and stroke risk in atrial fibrillation varies with ancestry and with the prevalence of hypertension. A single-country trial also fixes the background therapy, and the use of renin-angiotensin blockade or beta blockade differs across health systems.

The decision this informs is the one made in a clinic with a patient of sixty whose score is one. A 0.31 hazard ratio sounds decisive and translates into roughly one stroke prevented per hundred patients over two years. The trial makes that arithmetic explicit rather than implicit, which is the contribution a well-designed intermediate-risk study can make to a guideline argument.
