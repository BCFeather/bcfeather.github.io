Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 4.9 per cent, down from 5.2. The Commerce Department published the report on 27 May, and its price index rose 6.3 per cent in the year to April. The figure was down from 6.6 in March and it was the first decline in 18 months. It is published a month later than the consumer price index, and it is revised twice. The report is the last before the June meeting of the Federal Reserve.

Goods inflation slowed. The index is the one the Fed watches, and it is broader than the consumer price index. It covers all consumption, including what households pay for health care through insurance, and it weights housing differently. The two measures had diverged by 2 points in the year. Durable goods rose 6 per cent, and they had risen 12 in February. The price of used cars fell 2 per cent, which was the first decline since 2020, and the price of clothing fell. Services rose 5.5 per cent, and they were the part that was still rising. Housing was 40 per cent of the index, and rents had begun to rise.

Energy contributed 1.2 points, and food 0.9. The price of petrol was 4.10 dollars a gallon at the end of April, and it reached 5 in June. Food prices rose 10 per cent, and the price of eggs rose 30 because of the bird flu outbreak.

Consumer spending rose 0.9 per cent in the month, and it was stronger than expected. The saving rate fell to 4.4 per cent, which is the lowest since 2008, and households were using credit to keep spending. Real spending rose 0.7 per cent, and it had grown for four months. The price of natural gas rose 30 per cent, and electricity 12. The price of services rose 5.5 per cent, and they were the part that was still rising.

Incomes rose 0.4 per cent, and wages rose 0.5. The gap between spending and income was filled by savings, and economists said it could not last. The stimulus payments of 2021 had been spent, and the excess savings were estimated at 1 trillion dollars. The report supported two more half-point increases. The Fed raised by 50 basis points in June and by 75 in July, and it said the inflation data had not improved enough. The chairman said the committee would keep going until it saw a series of declines.

Inflation peaked at 7 per cent in June on this measure and fell to 5.5 per cent in November. The core rate peaked at 5.4 per cent in February 2023, and it fell to 4 per cent in December. The Fed's target is 2 per cent, and the index reached it in 2024. Excess savings were estimated at 1 trillion dollars, and they were falling.
