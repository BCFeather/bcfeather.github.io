description: "A narrowing interest-rate gap has unwound the largest carry trade in a decade over six months, and the unwinding has been orderly. The difference from previous episodes is the composition of the positions, mostly funds rather than leveraged banks."
---

A carry trade borrows in a low-yielding currency and lends in a high-yielding one, and its return is the interest differential until the exchange rate moves. The gap between two major currencies has narrowed by 1.8 percentage points over six months, and the positions built on it have been unwound without the disruption the same process caused twice in the past.

Size of the position is the first variable. Estimates from futures and options data put the net speculative position at 90 billion dollars at its peak, against 240 billion in the previous cycle. The smaller number reflects the differential, which was never as wide.

Composition is the second and more important. Banks once ran this trade on their own balance sheets with leverage; today the position sits in funds that report daily and post collateral. A fund that loses money is marked down and can be closed, which is a mechanism for absorbing a move rather than amplifying it.

Trigger was the currency's move. A 7 per cent depreciation over six weeks made the trade unprofitable for anyone who entered in the past year, and the unwinding happened through the futures market in three waves rather than in one.

Spillovers were contained. A carry unwind transmits through the funding currency's money market, and the relevant rate rose 40 basis points without a liquidity squeeze. Two central banks issued statements, neither of which intervened.

What made the difference is disclosure. Daily reporting of fund positions and margin requirements mean the leverage is visible to supervisors, and a supervisor who can see a position can act before it becomes a run. That was not true in the earlier episodes.

The trade is not gone. A differential of 2.4 points still exists, and some positions have been rebuilt at the new level. The question is whether the next unwind happens during a period of calm or during a stress event that forces sales elsewhere.

The funding currency's liquidity is the variable that made the difference. Its central bank widened collateral eligibility three years ago, so a fund that needs cash against a position can borrow overnight without selling. That backstop changes the unwind from a forced sale into a financing decision.

Data gaps remain. The futures positions are visible and the over-the-counter positions are not, and the estimate of 90 billion dollars is a floor. Supervisors in two jurisdictions publish aggregate exposures with a lag, and the lag is a month.

The rate differential is the trade's reason to exist and its reason to end. A 400-basis-point gap between two policy rates funds the position, and a narrowing to 250 removes most of the return. The gap narrowed by 120 points over six weeks, which is the arithmetic behind the exit.

For a reader, the lesson is about plumbing rather than policy. The same trade can be destabilizing or orderly depending on who holds it and how it is financed, and the change here was a shift from bank balance sheets to margined funds. That shift has made several markets less fragile and a few, the leveraged ones, more so.
