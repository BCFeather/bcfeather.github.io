description: "The June figures showed the new construction falling by about 8 per cent, with the single-family homes the weakest, while the permits for the future slipped more gently."
---

American housing starts fell by about 8 per cent in June to an annual rate of 1.43 million, below the forecast of 1.49. The single-family starts dropped by about 7 per cent, and the multifamily by about 10. The permits fell by about 3.7 per cent to 1.44 million, which is the lowest since the spring of 2020. The figure was about 4 per cent below the level of a year ago, and the economy has been short of about 1 million homes by the estimates of the developers. The starts have been falling for three months, and the level is about 20 per cent below the peak of 2021.

Rates are the reason. The average rate on the 30-year fixed mortgage was about 6.8 per cent in June, up from about 6.1 in the spring, and the payment on the median home was about 2,300 dollars a month. The builders have been buying down the rates for the buyers, and the cost is about 1 per cent of the price. The median price of the new home was about 420,000 dollars, down about 3 per cent from a year ago, which is the first annual decline since 2012. The builders have said the buydowns cost about 5 per cent of the price, and the large companies are using them more than the small ones. The median payment on a new mortgage was about 2,300 dollars, which is about 60 per cent higher than the level of 2021.

Completions rose. The number of the units finished was about 1.43 million, which is the highest since 2007, and the builders have been working through the backlog of the houses sold in 2021. The inventory of the new homes was about 430,000, which is the highest since 2009. The single-family completions were about 1 million, and the multifamily about 450,000 as the rental projects finished.

Regional figures were mixed. The starts fell by about 20 per cent in the Northeast and by about 8 in the South, and rose by about 4 in the West. The South is about half of the market, and the region has been the most sensitive to the rates. The permits fell by about 15 per cent on the year, which is the leading sign of the slower construction in the autumn.

Builders are cautious. The sentiment index of the builders fell by about 10 points in July, and the traffic of the prospective buyers dropped. The companies have been offering the rate buydowns and the price cuts, and the margins have been falling. The sentiment index was about 56 in July, down from 66, and the traffic of the buyers was the weakest since the winter.

This report is the one the committee will read with the growth numbers. A housing market that starts 1.43 million homes while the rates are at the highest in two decades is a market that has absorbed the shock, and the absorption has been the part the new homes inventory hides. The mortgage rate is expected to stay above 6 per cent into the autumn, and the builders say the demand will not recover before the spring.
