S&P Global published the flash survey on 16 December, and the composite index was 48.8 for December. The reading was up from 47.8 in November and below the 50 that separates growth from contraction. It was the sixth month below 50, and the improvement was the first in five months. The figure was the highest since August, and the survey said the decline was slowing.

Manufacturing was 47.8, and services were 49.0. The new orders index was 45.0, which is the weakest since 2012 apart from the pandemic, and the export orders index was 44.8. Companies said customers were delaying decisions because of the energy price and because of the war. Germany was 47.4, and its manufacturing index was 47.4. France was 48.1, and Italy 48.6. The survey said the decline was broad, and that the service sector had held up better because of the reopening of travel. The tourism-dependent economies were the strongest. The manufacturing index was 47.8, and the services index was 49.0.

Prices were still falling. The output price index was 59, down from 62, and the input price index was 60, down from 66. The composite input price measure had fallen for six months and was at its lowest since 2021. The bank wanted to see that before it slowed the pace of increases. Employment was growing. The index was 51.3, and it had been above 50 for two years. Firms said they were holding on to staff because of the difficulty of hiring, and the unemployment rate was 6.5 per cent, the lowest on record for the currency area. The Spanish index was 49.6, and the Irish index was 51. The German services index was 49.0, and the manufacturing index was 47.4.

Rates went up by 50 basis points on 15 December, and the bank said it would raise again in February. The survey supported a smaller increase than the 75 points of October, and the bank's president said in an interview that the pace would slow. The market priced a peak of 3.75 per cent for the summer. The output price index had fallen for five months, and it was at its lowest since 2021.

Chemical output in Germany fell 12 per cent in the year, and the survey said some plants had closed permanently. The survey's weakness was concentrated in energy-intensive manufacturing. The service sector was supported by the return of tourism, and the Spanish index was above 50 for the third month.

Output fell 0.1 per cent in the fourth quarter and again in the first, and growth returned in the second quarter of 2023. The index rose through the winter as the gas price fell. It passed 50 in April and reached 54 in May, and the recession the survey had predicted was a shallow one.
