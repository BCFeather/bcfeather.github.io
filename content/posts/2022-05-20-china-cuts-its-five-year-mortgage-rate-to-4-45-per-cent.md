Cutting the five-year loan prime rate to 4.45 per cent from 4.6 on 20 May, the central bank acted for the first time since January. The cut was the first since January and the largest since the benchmark began in 2019. The one-year rate was left at 3.7 per cent, which is unusual because the two usually move together. The cut was 15 basis points, and analysts had expected 5.

Most home loans are priced against it, and a cut of 15 basis points reduces the payment on a 1 million yuan loan by 90 yuan a month. The five-year rate is the one that matters for mortgages. The cut came as sales of new homes fell 30 per cent in the year and as 30 developers had defaulted. For a 30-year loan of 3 million yuan, the payment falls by 270 yuan a month.

Sales of floor space fell 21 per cent in the first four months, and investment fell 2.7. The property market had been contracting for a year. Land sales fell 30 per cent, which cut the revenue of local governments by 1 trillion yuan. The government had relaxed the rules on purchases in 60 cities since the autumn.

Economists said a cut of 100 basis points would be needed to move sales, and they said the bank was constrained by inflation and by the exchange rate. The cut was small relative to the problem. Consumer prices rose 2.1 per cent, and the yuan had fallen 6 per cent against the dollar. The bank also cut the reserve requirement for some banks and told them to lend more. It had cut the ratio by 1 point in April, and it said it would encourage lending to developers and to households. The total of the measures was 1 trillion yuan of liquidity.

Mortgage rates were still high relative to incomes. The average rate was 5.5 per cent for a first home, and the price of an apartment in a large city was 20 times the annual income. The government said it would not repeat the stimulus of 2015, and that it wanted the market to stabilize rather than to grow. An apartment in Shanghai cost 6 million yuan, and the payment was 34,000 a month.

Sales fell again in May and in June, and the decline reached 30 per cent in the year by the summer. The market did not respond. The government set up a fund of 200 billion yuan to complete unfinished projects, and it cut the rate again in August to 4.3. The crisis deepened in the summer. Sales fell 28 per cent for the year, and investment fell 10. Developers defaulted on 500 billion dollars of debt, and the government's measures did not restore confidence. The rate was cut to 4.2 per cent in 2023, and sales fell again.
