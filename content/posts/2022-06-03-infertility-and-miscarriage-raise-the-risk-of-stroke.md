Reproductive history is recorded in every obstetric file and used in almost no cardiovascular assessment, and the question is whether that history carries information about the risk of stroke decades later. A pooled analysis of individual participant data says that it does. A history that is recorded in every obstetric file is a history that a cardiologist rarely reads, and the paper quantifies what is being missed.

An international consortium pooled 618,851 women aged 32 to 73 at baseline from seven countries, with data on infertility, miscarriage and stillbirth, and with fatal and non-fatal stroke ascertained through questionnaires, hospital records and death registers. The paper appeared in *The BMJ*, 22 June 2022. A pooled analysis of individual participant data is the design that allows a rare outcome to be studied across countries with different coding practices, and the harmonization is the work behind the result.

Infertility was associated with non-fatal stroke at a hazard ratio of 1.14, with an interval from 1.08 to 1.20. Three or more miscarriages carried a hazard ratio of 1.35 for non-fatal and 1.82 for fatal stroke, and stillbirth carried 1.31 for non-fatal stroke and 1.26 for fatal stroke after recurrent stillbirth. A hazard ratio of 1.82 for fatal stroke after three or more miscarriages is the largest estimate in the paper, and it is also the one with the most follow-up behind it.

Median follow-up stood at 13 years for non-fatal stroke and 9.4 years for fatal stroke, and 2.8 per cent of the women had a first non-fatal stroke while 0.7 per cent had a fatal one. The absolute risks are therefore small and the relative risks are the quantities that a clinician would use. A relative risk of 1.14 is small for an individual and large for a population, because infertility is common and the outcome is severe.

Hypertension is the variable that the analysis stratifies on, because pre-eclampsia and other hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are themselves risk factors for stroke, and a reproductive history may be a marker for a vascular susceptibility that persists. A pooled cohort is an observational design, and residual confounding by socioeconomic position, smoking and access to care is possible even with adjustment. The consistency across seven countries with different healthcare systems is the argument that the association is not a local artefact. Seven countries with different healthcare systems and different coding of stroke is the strongest argument that the association is not a local artefact, and the adjustment for education and smoking is the weakest part of the defence.
