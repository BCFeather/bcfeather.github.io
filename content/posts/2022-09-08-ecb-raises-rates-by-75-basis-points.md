description: "The governing council delivered its largest increase on record on 8 September and raised its inflation forecasts again."
---

Meeting in Frankfurt on 8 September, the governing council lifted the deposit rate to 0.75 per cent from zero. The main refinancing rate went to 1.25 per cent. It was the largest increase in the bank's history and the second in two meetings, after a half-point move in July ended eight years of negative rates.

Inflation in the currency area was 9.1 per cent in August, and the bank's staff projections raised the forecast for the year to 8.1 per cent from 6.8. The projection for next year went to 5.5 per cent, and for 2024 to 2.3, which is still above the target. Growth was cut to 0.9 per cent for next year from 2.1, and the bank said a recession could not be excluded.

Front-loading the increases was the council's decision, the statement said, because inflation was likely to stay above the target for a long period. It dropped the language about gradual moves that had appeared in July. The president said in the press conference that the bank would raise rates at the next two meetings as well, without saying by how much.

Several members had wanted a smaller step. The accounts published in October showed that the decision was not unanimous and that the debate was about the risk of recession against the risk of losing credibility. The president said afterwards that the council was united on the direction and divided on the pace. Bond markets moved before the meeting. The spread between Italian and German ten-year yields widened to 2.4 points in early September, and the bank had created a tool in July to buy bonds of countries under stress. The tool had not been used, and the president said it was there for a situation the council would have to judge.

Gas prices had risen again in August, and the terms of trade had moved against the currency area more sharply than against the United States. The euro fell to 0.99 dollars on the day, its lowest in twenty years, and it stayed below parity for a month. The bank said it was watching the exchange rate without targeting it. The next meeting was on 27 October, and the council raised by another 75 basis points to 1.5 per cent. A third move of the same size followed in December. The deposit rate reached 3 per cent in March 2023, and the bank paused in July.

A decision to shrink it was postponed to 2023, when the council said it would allow 15 billion euros a month to run off. The bank's balance sheet was still growing when the rates rose, because it was holding bonds bought during the pandemic. The combination of rising rates and a large balance sheet was the subject of most of the questions at the press conference.
