Brooks Koepka won the PGA Championship at Oak Hill on 21 May with a final round of 67, three under par, and a total of nine under. Viktor Hovland and Scottie Scheffler finished tied for second at seven under. The win is the fifth major for Koepka, and the third PGA Championship. He had won the championship in 2018 and 2019, and the third title puts him level with the players of the era before the war. The purse was about 15 million dollars, and the winner's share was about 2.7.

His round was steady. Koepka made four birdies and one bogey, and he hit about 70 per cent of the fairways on a course that punished the misses. He took the lead with a birdie at the second and never gave it back, and the gap was two strokes for the last six holes. He made the turn in two under for the day, and the birdie at the 13th restored the lead after the chasing group closed. The driver was the club that did the work, and he averaged about 310 yards on the measured holes.

Oak Hill was the test. Oak Hill played to about 7,300 yards with the rough grown to about four inches, and the field averaged about 72.5 on the first day. The winning total of nine under was the lowest at the course since the championship of 2013. The greens were firm and fast, and the approach shots had to be played from the fairway to hold them. The par fives played about half a stroke over the field average, and the players who could carry the ball 300 yards had the advantage.

Hovland made the run. The Norwegian birdied the 13th and the 14th to reach eight under, and he lipped out a putt for the ninth at the 16th. He said the putter had been the difference, and the result was his best in a major. He had finished second at the Masters in April, and the form had been building since the winter.

Scheffler could not putt. The world number one hit the ball better than anyone in the field and lost about three strokes on the greens. He said afterwards that the reads had been the problem, and the statisticians noted he had gained about six strokes from the tee. The putting statistic was the worst of the top ten, and he said the speed of the greens had changed through the week.

Victory moves Koepka to 15th in the world ranking from 44th. A player who wins a fifth major after the injuries of the last two years is a player who has rebuilt the game around the power, and the power is what Oak Hill rewards. The next major is the US Open in June, and the course is the Los Angeles Country Club.
