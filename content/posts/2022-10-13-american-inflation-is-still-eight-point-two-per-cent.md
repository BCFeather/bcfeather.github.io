The Labour Department reported on Thursday that consumer prices rose 0.4 per cent in September and 8.2 per cent over the year, and that the index excluding food and energy rose 0.6 per cent in the month. Core inflation at 6.6 per cent is the highest since 1982. The monthly figure was a tenth above what the forecasters had expected, and the details were worse than the headline. The annual rate has now been above eight per cent for seven months.

The composition explains the disappointment. Energy prices fell, with petrol down 4.2 per cent in the month, and used cars fell as well. The increases came from shelter, which rose 0.7 per cent and carries the largest single weight in the index, and from services such as medical care and transport. Food rose 11.2 per cent over the year, the fastest since 1979, and the price of eggs rose 39 per cent. Airfares rose sharply again after falling through the summer, and the index for car insurance reached its highest level in two decades.

Shelter is measured with a lag, and the rents in the index reflect leases that were signed months earlier. That means the cooling in the housing market takes about a year to reach the numbers, and the officials who set rates know it. The Federal Reserve meets in November and is expected to raise its benchmark by three quarters of a point for the fourth time in a row. Several governors had said in the preceding week that they would rather stop too late than too early.

Markets spent the morning falling and the afternoon rising. Futures dropped two per cent within minutes of the release, then reversed on a report that some European officials were discussing a softer approach to energy prices, and the Dow closed 827 points higher. Traders described the move as one of the largest intraday reversals in years, and the bond market ended the day almost where it started. The ten-year yield closed lower than it opened, which several strategists found harder to explain than the rally in shares.

For households the report is a statement about wages. Average hourly earnings have fallen about three per cent over the year once inflation is counted, and the social security administration announced a cost-of-living increase of 8.7 per cent for January, the largest since 1981. Mortgage rates reached 6.9 per cent, the highest in twenty years, and applications for new loans fell again. The rate on a thirty-year loan has doubled in a year, and the number of homes for sale has fallen towards the lowest level on record.

Politically the numbers arrive three weeks before the midterm elections, and both parties used them within the hour. The president said the fight against inflation was his first priority and that the report showed progress on the month. Republicans said the core figure proved the opposite and that the spending bills had made things worse. The next rate decision falls two days after the vote, and the officials have said the decision is already made.
