Berkshire Hathaway reported a net profit of 35.5 billion dollars for the first quarter, against 5.6 billion a year earlier, as the investment portfolio gained on the stock market. The operating profit, which excludes the investment gains, was 8.0 billion, up about 12 per cent. The cash and the treasury bills reached about 130 billion dollars. The figure includes about 27 billion dollars of the gains on the stocks and the bonds, and the company has said the number should be ignored. The Class A shares were about 0.5 per cent higher in the session after the report.

Insurance did the work on the operating profit. The underwriting profit was about 1.1 billion dollars, helped by the reinsurance business and the absence of the large catastrophes. The investment income rose by about 40 per cent as the yields on the short-term paper climbed. The float from the insurance business was about 165 billion dollars, and the cost of it was negative for the quarter. The company has been buying the short-term treasury bills, which yielded about 4.5 per cent at the end of the quarter.

Its railroad was weaker than the peers. The BNSF reported an operating income of about 1.3 billion, down about 9 per cent, as the volumes fell on the lower grain and the coal shipments. The company said the costs had risen with the wages, and the margins were below the peers. The volumes fell by about 10 per cent, and the company said the consumer goods and the containers were the weakest.

Its energy unit earned more than the utilities. The Berkshire Hathaway Energy reported an operating income of about 0.7 billion, up about 15 per cent, as the utility rates rose and the wind and the solar projects came on line. The company has been spending about 4 billion dollars a year on the grid. The company said it would spend about 10 billion dollars on the grid over the next five years in the western states.

Buybacks slowed. The company repurchased about 4.4 billion dollars of its own shares in the quarter, down from about 6 billion in the previous three months, and the pace has been the slowest since 2021. The chief executive said the price was the reason. The company bought about 7 billion dollars of the shares in the previous year, and the investors have been asking for a larger return of the capital.

An annual meeting followed the results. The shareholders gathered in Omaha on 6 May, and the questions were about the succession, the banks and the Japanese trading houses that the company has bought into. A company that holds 130 billion dollars of the cash is a company that has found nothing to buy at the price, and the price is the message. The vice chairman said the Japanese trading houses were a long-term holding, and the stake in each of the five was about 5 per cent.
