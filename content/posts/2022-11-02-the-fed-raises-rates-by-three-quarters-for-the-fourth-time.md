Raising its target range by 75 basis points on 2 November, the Federal Reserve took it to 3.75 to 4 per cent. It was the fourth consecutive increase of that size and the sixth of the year. The rate had been near zero in March, and the cumulative rise was the fastest since 1980.

Officials had said the same thing in speeches over the previous two weeks, and the market had priced it. The statement added a sentence about the lags of policy. It said the committee would consider the cumulative tightening and the delay with which it affects the economy, and that language was read as a hint that the pace would slow. Two officials had said in speeches that the committee would discuss a slower pace at this meeting.

Futures priced a peak of 5 per cent in the spring. The projections published in September had shown a peak of 4.6 per cent, and the market expected that figure to rise. The chairman said in the press conference that the terminal rate would be higher than the September estimate, and he said it was premature to think about pausing. Inflation was the reason. The September report had shown 8.2 per cent, and the core measure was 6.6. The labour market was still adding more than 250,000 jobs a month, and the unemployment rate was 3.5. The committee said it would need a period of below-trend growth to bring prices down. The median projection for 2023 was 4.6 per cent, and the market expected it to be revised to 5.

Traders said the market had expected a more hawkish statement and had been relieved. The reaction was a rally and then a fall. The S&P 500 rose 1 per cent during the press conference and closed 2.5 per cent lower, its worst day since the previous January. The ten-year yield fell to 4.1 per cent from 4.2, and the dollar weakened. Job openings were 10.3 million, and there were 1.9 vacancies for every unemployed worker.

Mortgage rates had passed 7 per cent for the first time since 2002. The average thirty-year rate was 7.08 per cent, and applications had fallen to their lowest level since 1997. The housing market was the first part of the economy to respond, and sales of existing homes fell 28 per cent in the year.

Officials said the process had not caused disruption, and the reserves in the banking system remained above the level they considered ample. The committee met again on 14 December and raised by half a point to 4.5 per cent. That was the step down that the statement had anticipated, and officials said further increases would be smaller. The terminal rate reached 5.5 per cent in July 2023, and the first cut came in September 2024. The balance sheet began to shrink in June at a pace of 95 billion dollars a month, and the reduction reached 1.5 trillion dollars by the end of 2023. The rate had risen from 3.1 per cent in December, and the monthly payment on a new loan had doubled.
