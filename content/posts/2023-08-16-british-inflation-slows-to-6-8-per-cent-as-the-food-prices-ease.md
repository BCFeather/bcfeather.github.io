British consumer prices rose by 6.8 per cent over the year to July, down from 7.9 in June and in line with the forecast. The monthly change was a fall of 0.4 per cent, and the core measure was about 6.9, unchanged. The figure is the lowest since February 2022. The figure is the lowest since February 2022, and the measure has fallen from the peak of 11.1 per cent in October. The retail prices index rose by about 9.0 per cent, and the one used for the indexation of the pensions was about 8.9.

Food did the work. The prices of the food rose by about 14.8 per cent, down from 17.3, and the one of the milk fell by about 5 on the month. The bread and the cereals rose by about 12, and the vegetables by about 8 as the harvest arrived. The prices of the eggs fell by about 4 per cent on the month, and the one of the cheese by about 3. The grocery inflation has been the highest in the rich countries, and the discount chains say the customers are trading down.

Energy eased. The fuel fell by about 1 per cent on the month and about 20 over the year, and the one of the gas by about 3 as the cap fell in July. The electricity rose by about 1, and the effect of the cap change will show in the August figure. The petrol was about 1.43 pounds a litre at the end of July, down about 20 per cent from the peak of the autumn.

Services held at about 7.4 per cent. The restaurants rose by about 8, and the one of the rents by about 6 as the landlords passed on the mortgage costs. The wages have been rising by about 7.8 per cent in the private sector, which is the fastest since 2001. The private rents rose by about 5.8 per cent over the year, which is the fastest since the survey began in 2016. The bank has raised the rate fourteen times, and the market expects the peak at about 5.75 per cent.

Its core is the problem. A core at 6.9 per cent is about three times the target, and the bank has said it would need to see it fall before it stops. The market had priced a peak of about 6 per cent on the policy rate, and the report did not change it. The committee meets on 21 September, and the decision will be the one after the pause of August.

This release is the one before the September meeting. A headline at 6.8 with the core at 6.9 is the split that has defined the year, and the split is the part that keeps the bank's options open. The wage report of 12 September is the other half of the case, and the private sector pay is the number the committee watches.
