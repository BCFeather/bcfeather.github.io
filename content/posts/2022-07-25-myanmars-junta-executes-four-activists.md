The military government of Myanmar carried out four executions on Monday, according to the state media, and the men who were hanged included a former member of parliament and a prominent activist. It is the first use of the death penalty in the country in more than thirty years. The men had been convicted in a closed court on charges of terrorism and of murder, and their trials were held in a prison. The families learned of it only after the executions had taken place.

All four belonged to the resistance that formed after the coup in February 2021. One of them, Ko Jimmy, was a well-known figure in the democracy movement and had been arrested in October. Another, Phyo Zeya Thaw, was a former member of parliament from the party that won the election in 2020. The two others were a writer and a young man who had been arrested with weapons. The military said the four had been convicted of ordering attacks that killed civilians, and that the sentences had been carried out in accordance with the law of the country.

The region reacted more sharply than usual. The foreign ministers of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations said in a statement that the executions had damaged the credibility of the peace plan that the group agreed with the military last year. The Malaysian government said it would not attend meetings with the junta, and the European Union announced sanctions on the officials who ran the prisons. The Chinese and Indian governments said they were concerned.

Inside the country the point was to frighten the resistance into silence. The military has been fighting the resistance for eighteen months and it has been unable to hold the countryside in several regions. The courts that tried the men were part of a system of military tribunals that the lawyers have described as a form of intimidation, and the verdicts were announced with the sentence of death in the same hearing. The state media published the executions before the diplomatic reaction could form.

Opposition groups have promised an answer. The government-in-exile called the men martyrs and said the resistance would continue, and the armed groups that have been formed in the border areas said they would not stop. The fight has become a civil war in the places where the army is weakest, and the cities have been quiet since the crackdown of last year. The next phase will be fought in the villages, and the executions will be used by both sides as a reason.

Little doubt about the character of the government survived Monday. It came to power by force, it has held elections that nobody accepted and it has now killed prisoners whose trials were held in secret. The men who were hanged had become symbols in the eighteen months since their arrest, and the military chose to make them martyrs rather than to keep them in prison. The region has responded with words and with sanctions, and the words have not changed the situation on the ground.
