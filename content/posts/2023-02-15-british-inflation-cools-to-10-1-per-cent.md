title: "British inflation cools to 10.1 per cent"

description: "The January reading was the third monthly decline and the lowest since September, with food still rising at 16.7 per cent."
---

Consumer prices rose 10.1 per cent in the year to January, the Office for National Statistics said on 15 February. The figure was below the 10.3 forecast and down from 10.5 in December, which was itself down from the 11.1 peak of October. The monthly change was -0.6 per cent, the first fall on the month since 2021. The statistics agency said the monthly fall was driven by transport, restaurants and clothing.

Food and drink rose 16.7 per cent over the year, the highest since 1977, and it contributed more than any other category. Bread and cereals rose 20 per cent, milk 30 per cent and eggs 25 per cent. The war in Ukraine and the cost of energy for greenhouses were the explanations the statisticians gave. Food inflation peaked at 19.2 per cent in March, and it stayed above 15 per cent until the autumn. Supermarkets said their own price rises had peaked in December, and they cut milk prices in February.

Energy was the reason for the fall in the headline. The cap on household bills had been set at 2,500 pounds from October, and the government's guarantee replaced a much higher market price. Petrol fell 5 per cent over the month, and electricity and gas contributed -0.2 points to the annual rate. The guarantee was extended in the spring, and the cap was raised to 3,000 pounds from July. Gas prices on the continent had fallen to 50 euros a megawatt hour, down from 300 in the summer.

Core inflation, excluding food and energy, was 5.8 per cent, slightly below the forecast and down from 6.0. Services inflation was 6.8 per cent, and it has become the measure the bank watches most closely. Goods inflation fell to 12 per cent, and the gap between goods and services is now the widest since records began. Services inflation at 6.8 per cent was above the level the bank considered consistent with its target.

Nine increases since December 2021 had taken the policy rate to 4 per cent. Two members of the committee had voted for a larger increase at the February meeting, and the minutes showed a split. Markets priced a peak near 4.5 per cent, and the report did little to change that. The committee met again in March and raised the rate to 4.25, with one member voting for a hold.

Labour market tightness explained the persistence. Pay growth in the private sector was 7.3 per cent, and vacancies, while falling, remained above a million. The bank said it expected inflation to fall below 4 per cent by the end of the year, which requires energy prices to stay where they were in February. The unemployment rate was 3.7 per cent in December, and employment rose by 74,000 in the quarter. The bank's forecast in February was for inflation to reach 2 per cent only in the second half of 2024. Mortgage approvals fell to 25,000 in January, the lowest since 2009 outside the pandemic.
