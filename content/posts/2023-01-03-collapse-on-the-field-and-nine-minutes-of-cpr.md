Play in the game between the Buffalo Bills and the Cincinnati Bengals stopped at 8.55 in the first quarter on 2 January. Damar Hamlin, a safety in his second season, made a tackle on a receiver and stood up, then fell backwards. He was unconscious before he reached the ground, and the medical staff who ran onto the field began CPR within a minute. The game had been scheduled for a Monday night and was the last of the regular season, which is why the standings question mattered.

Emergency crews worked on the pitch for about nine minutes, twice using a defibrillator. Players from both teams stood in a loose circle, and many were in tears. Coaches and officials decided to suspend the game, and the league later announced it would not be resumed, a decision that had no precedent in the modern era. The league's chief medical officer said afterwards that the response had followed the emergency plan drawn up in 2019.

Hamlin was taken to a hospital in Cincinnati and then to one in Buffalo, where he stayed for a week. The cause was described as commotio cordis, a blow to the chest at a particular moment in the heartbeat that stops the heart without damaging it. It is rare, and it is the reason defibrillators are now required at most organised sporting events. Two other players were treated for shock at the stadium, and both were cleared to travel with the team. The ambulance took eleven minutes to reach the stadium gate, and the hospital is a mile away.

Support poured in from outside the sport. Donations to Hamlin's foundation, which had raised a few thousand dollars before, passed 8 million dollars in a week. Players across the league wore his number on their helmets, and the Bills' next game opened with a moment of silence that lasted longer than scheduled. Donations came from across the country, and the foundation later said it would pay for defibrillators in schools.

Medical protocols came under review, and the review found that the response had worked as intended. It also found gaps at some college and school level events, where defibrillators are not always on site. Several states announced funding for equipment in the weeks that followed, which is the kind of change that follows a broadcast collapse. The review recommended that every high school programme carry an emergency plan and a trained responder at games. The players' association asked for the emergency plans to be published, and the league did so in March.

Hamlin was discharged on 11 January and attended a playoff game three weeks later. He did not play again that season, and whether he plays at all remains open. The game that was abandoned was eventually replayed in a neutral stadium, and the league changed the rules for how a suspended game affects the standings. The replay was played in a neutral stadium in March, and the Bills won it without Hamlin on the field. His first public appearance was at a charity event in February, and he spoke for four minutes.
