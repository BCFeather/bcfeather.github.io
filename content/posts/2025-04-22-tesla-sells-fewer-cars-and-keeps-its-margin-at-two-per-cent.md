Deliveries are reported before earnings, which means the market knows the volume line a week early. The number for the first quarter was 336,681 vehicles, 13 per cent below the same quarter a year earlier and the lowest total in three years. Analysts had expected worse in some forecasts and better in others; the earnings call on 22 April was going to be about price, cost and the chief executive's attention.

Financials were bad across the board. Total revenue fell 9 per cent to 19.34 billion dollars, and automotive revenue fell 20 per cent, because volume fell and average selling prices fell with it. Operating income was 399 million dollars against 1.71 billion a year earlier, a fall of 66 per cent, and net income was 409 million, down 71 per cent. The operating margin narrowed to 2.1 per cent from 5.5 per cent, the lowest in six years.

Two segments carried the quarter. Energy revenue rose 67 per cent to 2.73 billion dollars, on storage deployments and not on solar, and services and other revenue rose 15 per cent to 2.64 billion. Cash stood at about 37 billion dollars, up 38 per cent, which means the company is not in a liquidity crisis even while its car business is earning almost nothing. The balance sheet is the reason the quarter reads as a problem and not an emergency.

The explanation the company gave was trade policy. In the release Tesla said uncertainty in the automotive and energy markets was increasing as rapidly evolving trade policy affected the global supply chain and the company's cost structure, and that changing political sentiment could affect demand in the near term. It declined to give second-quarter guidance, which is unusual and is itself a statement about how little visibility the company has.

Cost per vehicle is the number that matters and the number the release did not lead with. A 20 per cent fall in automotive revenue with a 2 per cent operating margin implies that the cost reductions the company has been promising for two years have not kept pace with the price cuts it has used to hold volume. Cheap models were promised for the first half of the year; the quarter passed without a launch date.

The chief executive's attention became a financial variable. Elon Musk said on the call that from May he would spend much less time at the Department of Government Efficiency and return to the company, after a quarter in which his political activity had been linked by analysts to boycotts and to a fall in orders in Europe. A founder who is the brand is a founder whose calendar is a risk factor, and the call treated it as one.

What would settle the year is the second half. A cheaper model that reaches volume, a fall in cost per vehicle, and a stabilization of deliveries in Europe would each show that the quarter was a policy shock and not a structural decline. A second quarter at a 2 per cent margin would show the opposite, and the cash balance would buy only so much time.
