A vaccine designed against a virus that has changed is a vaccine that has to be updated, and the update is a second antigen added to the first rather than a replacement. The bivalent booster is the first test of whether that addition helps. Whether a second antigen helps or merely dilutes is the question the trial was designed to answer.

Adults were randomized who had already received two primary doses and one booster of mRNA-1273 to a second booster of 50 micrograms of mRNA-1273.214, which encodes the ancestral and BA.1 spike, or of the original mRNA-1273. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 16 September 2022. A ratio of about 1.6 against BA.1 and 1.5 against BA.4/5 is the size of the improvement, and the size is modest enough that the immunology has to carry the argument.

In participants with no previous infection, neutralising antibody geometric mean titres against BA.1 were 2,372.4 after the bivalent booster and 1,473.5 after the original, and against BA.4/5 they were 727.4 and 492.1.
