Walmart reported a revenue of 160.8 billion dollars for the third quarter on 16 November, up 5.2 per cent, and raised its forecast for the year. The shares fell about 8 per cent as the margin disappointed. The revenue was above the forecast of 159 billion, and the operating income rose by about 3 per cent. The revenue was above the forecast of 159 billion, and the company said the number of the transactions rose as the basket size fell. The earnings per share were about 1.53 dollars, above the forecast of 1.52, and the company said the year would be at the top of the range.

Grocery did the work. The sales of the food rose by about 5 per cent in the American stores, and the company said the shoppers were buying the private label and the smaller packs. A shopper who trades down is a till that rings. The private label now accounts for about a quarter of the grocery sales, up from about a fifth two years ago.

Online grew. The e-commerce sales rose by about 15 per cent, and the pick-up from the stores is now the largest channel. A bag that is collected is a delivery that is not paid for. The number of the delivery orders rose by about 20 per cent, and the company said the fees had been cut in some markets. The advertising business grew by about 20 per cent, and the marketplace added about 30 per cent more sellers.

General merchandise was soft. The sales of the clothing and the home goods fell by about 5 per cent, and the company said the customers were delaying the bigger purchases. A sofa that is postponed is a category that is waiting. The inventory fell by about 3 per cent, and the company said the stores were cleaner than at the start of the year.

Margins disappointed the shares. The gross margin fell by about 0.4 points as the mix shifted to the grocery, and the finance chief said the prices of the food had come down faster than the costs. A mix that shifts is a profit that moves. The company has been cutting the prices of about 1,000 items, and the finance chief said it was investing in the price rather than the promotion. The company said the prices of the food would fall further in the next quarter as the commodities eased, and the margin would follow.

What the quarter shows is that Walmart is winning the shoppers who are cutting back and losing the margin that the win costs, and the trade is the story of the year. A retailer that grows by taking the trade-down is a retailer that must keep the price, and the price is the whole position. The company has raised the forecast twice this year, and the third raise is the one that the market did not reward.
