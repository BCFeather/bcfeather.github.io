---NVIDIA reported revenue of 26.04 billion dollars for its first quarter, which ended on the twenty-eighth of April, an increase of 262 per cent from the same period a year earlier and of 18 per cent from the previous quarter. Data centre revenue reached 22.6 billion dollars, of which the great majority came from servers built for artificial intelligence. Net income on a GAAP basis was 14.88 billion dollars, and diluted earnings per share were 5.98 dollars, both far above what analysts had forecast.

Guidance for the current quarter was higher again. Company said it expected revenue of about 28 billion dollars, above the consensus of 26.6 billion, and it said that demand for its newest processors exceeded supply. Finance chief said the company was sold out of the Blackwell systems that it began shipping in the quarter, and that the constraint was in manufacturing capacity rather than in orders.

Split announced with the results was the second in the company's history. Ten-for-one division of the shares, with a record date of the sixth of June and trading on a split-adjusted basis from the tenth, was presented as a way to make the stock accessible to employees and retail investors. Company also raised its quarterly dividend from four cents a share to ten cents on a pre-split basis, a small payout relative to its earnings and a signal that management intends to keep returning cash.

The Reaction in markets was positive but not euphoric, which was itself a change. Shares rose in after-hours trading and then gave up part of the gain, and several analysts noted that expectations had become so high that beating them by a wide margin produced a modest move. The stock had already risen by more than eighty per cent since the start of the year, and the company's market value had passed two trillion dollars, which made it the third largest in the world.

Position of the company in the industry is the subject of an argument that will run for years. Its graphics processors and the software that runs on them have become the standard for training large models, and its customers include every large cloud provider and most of the start-ups that build models. Competitors are trying to break the dependence, including Advanced Micro Devices, which has a new accelerator, and the cloud companies themselves, which are designing their own chips. None has yet produced a substitute that works as well at scale.

Company also said its gross margin had reached 78 per cent, a level that reflects both the pricing power of a near-monopoly and the cost of the memory that its systems require, and analysts said that margin is the figure to watch as competitors enter the market.

The Consequence for the wider market is the concentration of returns in a single company. NVIDIA accounted for a large share of the gain in the S&P 500 over the past year, and its customers are spending on its products in part because their earnings allow it. If demand slows, the effect would be felt across the index. That is the risk that the results did not address, and that the guidance, which points to another quarter of growth, postpones rather than settles.
