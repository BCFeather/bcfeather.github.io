Consumer confidence fell to 100.4 in June from 101.3, according to the Conference Board's survey published on 25 June. The measure of expectations for the next six months fell for a third month, and the share of households that described jobs as plentiful declined. The figure was above the forecast, and the decline came from the expectations component rather than from the assessment of current conditions. The present situation index rose, which is the reason the headline did not fall further. The reading was the lowest since November, and it came in the same week as the retail sales that showed the shops still busy.

Expectations are the part that predicts spending. The component has fallen below the level that the researchers associate with a coming slowdown, and the gap between how people feel about today and how they feel about the winter is the widest since 2022. A survey that is pessimistic about the future records a delay in purchases. The difference matters because the spending that the economy depends on is financed by expectations of income rather than by the income already received.

Labour market explains the change. The number of people who said jobs were hard to get rose for a third month, and the hiring rate has fallen to the level of 2019. A worker who believes that a job is harder to find is a worker who saves more. The quit rate has fallen to the lowest since 2020, and the workers who stay put are the ones whose raises are smallest.

Prices remain the complaint. Households named food and rent as their largest concerns, and the inflation expectation at the medium horizon rose slightly, which the committee watches as a sign of credibility. An expectation that drifts up is a policy that has to work harder. The one-year expectation was unchanged at 5.3 per cent, which is well above the level the committee considers consistent with its target.

Spending data are the test. Retail sales have grown more slowly than the confidence index has fallen, which is the pattern of households that complain in surveys and buy in shops, and the gap is the reason the economists do not trade on the index alone. A statement and a transaction are different forms of evidence. The survey covers about three thousand households and is weighted to match the population, and it has a record of turning about two quarters before the official data. The retail sales in May were stronger than the survey would suggest, and the two have disagreed before.

What the month shows is that the mood of the consumer has turned before the spending has, and the survey is the earliest place where a slowdown appears. A consumer who is worried and still spending is a consumer with savings, and those savings are the buffer that decides how long the gap lasts.
