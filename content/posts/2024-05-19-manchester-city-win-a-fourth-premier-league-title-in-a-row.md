Manchester City won the Premier League on the final day, 19 May, finishing two points above Arsenal, and became the first English club to take four consecutive top-flight titles. It was the eighth league championship since the club was bought by an Abu Dhabi consortium in 2008. The club has now won six of the last seven titles, and the one it lost was by a single point to a side that has since fallen away.

Margin is the story of the run-in. The club won nine of its last ten matches and lost none, and the one draw came against a side that was fighting relegation and defended for ninety minutes. A run that long is a run that leaves the chasing club no way to close a gap that was once five points. The goal difference was decisive in the end, and the club's habit of scoring late in matches that were already won is the part of the record that the analysts point to.

Squad depth is the mechanism. The club rotates more than any of its rivals and has two players of similar quality in most positions, which means the eleven that starts in May is not the eleven that started in August. A squad that can absorb injuries is a squad that wins the matches that decide a season. The manager has changed the shape of the team twice during the run, and the players who were signed for one system have been converted to another without a fall in performance.

Financial rules are the argument that follows. The club faces a case brought by the league over payments to sponsors and to staff, and the outcome will decide whether the titles are treated as untainted. A case about accounts is a case that could remove the records rather than the trophies. The case covers more than a hundred allegations and has been running for years, and the tribunal's timetable suggests a verdict after the next season has begun.

Challenger's season is the contrast. Arsenal spent more than it had in any previous year and improved again, and it lost the title by the width of a single result in a match it dominated. A project that improves every year and wins nothing is a project that has to explain the difference. The challenger's spending has been financed by its own revenues and by a loan against future tickets, and the interest on that loan is now a constraint on the transfer budget.

What the season shows is that a league with the largest revenues in the world has produced a concentration of titles not seen since the 1980s, and the argument about whether that is excellence or a failure of the rules is now the argument about the sport. A league that is won by the same club every year is a league whose broadcasting value depends on the drama that the other clubs provide, and the balance is the open question.
