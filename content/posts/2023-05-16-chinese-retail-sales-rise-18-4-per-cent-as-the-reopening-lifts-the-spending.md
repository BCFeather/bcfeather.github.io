title: "Chinese retail sales rise 18.4 per cent as the reopening lifts the spending"
date: "2023-05-16T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The April figures beat the forecast on the consumption and the services, while the industrial output and the property investment stayed weak."
---

Chinese retail sales rose by 18.4 per cent over the year in April, above the forecast of about 15 per cent, as the comparison with the lockdowns of 2022 flattered the number. The month-on-month change was about 0.5 per cent. The catering revenue rose by about 43 per cent, and the sales of the cosmetics by about 24. The figure was the fastest since the middle of 2021, and the restaurants and the hotels led the gain.

A base effect explains much of it. The lockdown of Shanghai began at the end of March 2022 and lasted through May, so the April of last year was the weakest month of the year. The two-year average growth is about 4 per cent, which is the cleaner measure and still below the pace of 2019. The retail sales in the first four months rose by about 8.5 per cent, and the catering by about 20.

Industrial output rose by 5.6 per cent over the year, below the forecast of about 8 per cent. The manufacturing of the cars rose by about 40 per cent, and the one of the solar cells by about 30. The steel fell by about 1, and the cement by about 8 as the construction stayed weak. The output of the electric vehicles reached about 600,000 in the quarter, and the exports of the cars were about 400,000. The output of the industrial robots rose by about 9 per cent, and the one of the chips fell by about 5 as the export orders weakened.

Property investment fell by about 6.2 per cent over the year in the first four months, and the new starts fell by about 21. The sales of the new homes fell by about 0.4 per cent in April, which is the smallest decline since the crisis began. The prices in the largest cities are still falling. The floor space sold fell by about 0.4 per cent in the first four months, and the land sales by the local authorities fell by about 20.

Youth unemployment rose to about 20.4 per cent in April, which is the highest on record. The number of the graduates looking for the work is about 11.5 million this year, and the mismatch between the degrees and the jobs is the structural part of the problem. The government has promised the support for the graduates, and the state-owned enterprises have been told to hire more of them.

These figures show a recovery led by the restaurants and the travel. A retail sector that grows by 18 per cent on a weak base is a sector that has recovered less than the headline suggests, and the comparison of the next months will show it. The consumption is about 55 per cent of the economy, and the target for the year depends on it.
