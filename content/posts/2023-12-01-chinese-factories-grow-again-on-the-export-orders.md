Caixin manufacturing activity measured 50.7 in November, up from 49.5 in October, the private survey said on 1 December, back above the line between growth and contraction. The figure was above the forecast of 49.8, and the reading is the first above the line since the spring. The survey is published by the research firm Markit and is watched more closely than the official one by the foreign investors.

Export orders did the work. The new export orders rose above 50 for the first time in six months, and the survey said the shipments to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations and to the emerging markets had grown. A shipment that is booked is a line that runs. The share of the exports to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations has risen to about 15 per cent, and the one to the United States has fallen below 15. The export orders index has been below the line for most of the year, and the reading is the first clear improvement since the spring.

Production rose as well. The output index was about 51, and the backlog of the work rose for the second month as the factories caught up. A backlog that grows is a capacity that is used. The employment index was about 49, and the survey said the factories were hiring for the skilled roles while cutting the temporary staff.

Prices were still soft. The output prices were about 49, and the input prices about 50, which is the deflation that the producer data have shown through the year. A price that is flat is a profit that is thin. The producer prices fell 3 per cent in October, and the profit of the industrial firms fell by about 8 per cent over the year. The input prices were helped by the cheaper oil and the freight, and the analysts say the margin of the exporters is the part to watch.

Divergence is the puzzle here. The official survey covers more of the state-owned firms and fell to 49.4, and the private one covers more of the coastal exporters and rose. A gap that is wide is a recovery that is uneven. The official survey covers about 3,000 firms, and the private one covers about 500 with a larger weight on the small exporters. The small firms have been the slowest to recover, and the survey says the confidence is still below the level of last year.

What the survey shows is that the export side of the industry has turned while the domestic side has not, and the turn is the part the policy has been waiting for. A divergence at the turn of the year is a divergence that the statisticians will explain later, and the explanation will not settle the policy. The official data for the month are due in the next week, and the divergence will be the number the analysts argue about.
