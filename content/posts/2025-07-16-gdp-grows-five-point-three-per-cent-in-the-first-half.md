The economy grew 5.3 per cent in the first half of the year, according to data published on 15 July, with gross domestic product of 66.05 trillion yuan. The first quarter grew 5.4 per cent and the second grew 5.2 per cent, so the momentum slowed slightly as the year went on. The figure was slightly above the target set in March and slightly below the consensus forecast.

Sectoral split was ordinary. Agriculture grew 3.7 per cent, industry 5.3 per cent and services 5.5 per cent, and the services number is the one that matters for employment, because services absorb most of the new entrants to the labour market. Services grew 5.5 per cent, industry 5.3 and agriculture 3.7, and the gap between them is the story. A rate above five with falling producer prices is the combination that has puzzled forecasters all year.

The demand side was less balanced. Consumption contributed about half the growth, investment a little less, and net exports a share that has grown since last year. A trade surplus that widens while domestic demand is soft is a familiar pattern, and it is the pattern that invites tariffs. Industrial production grew 6.4 per cent, retail sales 5 per cent, and fixed investment 2.8 per cent.

Industrial output grew 6.4 per cent in June, faster than in May, and the growth was concentrated in equipment manufacturing and in the electrical machinery that goes into grids and batteries. Prices remained soft. Consumer prices rose 0.2 per cent in the first half, and producer prices fell 3.6 per cent, which means that the deflator is negative and that nominal growth is lower than real growth. An economy that grows 5.3 per cent in volume and 4 per cent in value has a pricing problem rather than a demand problem. A deflationary reading of the price data sits awkwardly beside a growth rate above five.

What the half-year number does is set the baseline for the second half. The government's target for the year is about 5 per cent, and a first half of 5.3 gives the second half room to slow. The room will be used, because the export growth that carried the first half depends on a truce that ends in August. A target of about 5 per cent for the year now looks achievable rather than aspirational. Household consumption is the part that has not recovered to its pre-2020 share.
