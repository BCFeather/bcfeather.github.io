Israeli troops crossed into southern Lebanon in the early hours of the first of October, opening a ground campaign that the army described as limited, localized and targeted at Hezbollah positions along the border. Announcement was made by the military spokesman shortly after midnight, and it said the operation would remove infrastructure that the group had built close to Israeli towns. Armoured columns and infantry moved into villages that residents had largely abandoned over the previous fortnight of air strikes.

Background is a year of exchanges across the border and a collapse of the arrangement that had held since 2006. Hezbollah began firing rockets in support of Hamas after the attacks of the seventh of October, and Israel evacuated about sixty thousand people from towns within range. Air campaign that began in the middle of September killed the group's leader, Hassan Nasrallah, and destroyed much of its command structure, and the ground phase was presented as the next step of the same plan.

Weeks before the incursion had been the deadliest for Lebanon since the civil war. Health ministry figures put the death toll above a thousand, with a fifth of them children, and the government said more than a million people had left their homes. Schools turned into shelters, and hospitals in the south were evacuated or reduced to emergency care. Lebanese army units withdrew from positions near the frontier, which removed the only force that could have separated the two sides.

Hezbollah response was immediate but limited. Group said it had fired rockets at military sites and had engaged Israeli units that crossed the border, and its statements claimed several ambushes. Analysts noted that the group's arsenal is smaller than it was a year ago and that its command has lost most of its senior figures, which limits what it can do in a ground fight. Fighting was concentrated in a strip a few kilometres deep, and the Israeli army imposed a closed military zone along the border.

Diplomatic position of the government in Beirut was weak before the invasion and weaker after it. Prime minister said the country would defend itself and called for a ceasefire, and he accused Israel of dragging Lebanon into a war it had tried to avoid. Iran, which arms and funds the group, said it would not leave Lebanon to fight alone and then did little in the first days. American officials said they had been informed of the operation and had urged it to be short.

Consequence for the region is the part that no one can yet predict. Israeli officials have spoken of creating conditions for the return of evacuated residents and have not said how long the operation will last or how deep it will go. Lebanon has no mechanism to push the army back, and the United Nations force in the south is unable to act. What has changed in a single night is that a year of exchange across a recognized border has become a war fought inside one country, and the ceasefires that diplomats have been discussing for months now depend on terms that neither side has accepted.
