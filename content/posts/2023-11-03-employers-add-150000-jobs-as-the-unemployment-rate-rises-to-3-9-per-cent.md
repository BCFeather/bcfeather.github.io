Employers added 150,000 jobs in October, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said on 3 November, and the unemployment rate rose to 3.9 per cent from 3.8. The gain was below the forecast of about 180,000. The participation rate fell to 62.7 per cent, and the number of the people in the workforce fell by about 200,000, which is part of the reason the jobless rate rose. The gain was the smallest in five months, and the unemployment rate is the highest since the start of last year.

Strikes cut the number. About 30,000 car workers were on strike for part of the month and were counted as not employed, and the manufacturing payrolls fell by about 35,000. A strike that is counted is a payroll that is short. The strike was settled in the last week of October, and the workers will be back on the payrolls in the November report. The manufacturing payrolls would have added about 5,000 without the walkout, and the analysts said the underlying pace in the factories is flat.

Revisions took more. The two previous months were revised down by about 101,000, and the three-month average is now about 200,000, which is above the pace that the population growth requires. A revision that is negative is a report that is weaker than it first looked. The gains in the previous two months were revised to 165,000 and 141,000, and the analysts said the trend is now about 150,000 a month.

Wages are cooling. The average hourly pay rose by 0.2 per cent on the month and 4.1 per cent over the year, the slowest since the summer of 2021. A wage that is slower is a services inflation that follows. The pay for the production workers rose by about 4.5 per cent, and the union contracts signed with the carmakers will add about 25 per cent over four years.

Sectors were mixed. The health care added about 58,000 jobs and the government about 51,000, while the leisure and the hospitality added fewer as the summer ended. A hiring that is concentrated in the public sector is a hiring that the rates do not reach. The construction added about 23,000 jobs, and the retail lost about 6,000 as the holiday hiring starts later than in previous years. The retail hiring usually starts in October for the holiday season, and the delay suggests the chains are cautious about the demand.

What the report shows is that the labour market is cooling at the pace the Fed wants, and the cooling is the reason the committee has been able to hold. A report that is below the forecast and revises down is a report that the market has learned to read as good news, and the reading is the reaction.
