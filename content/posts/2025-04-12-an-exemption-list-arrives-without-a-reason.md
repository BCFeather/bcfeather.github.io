Reciprocal tariffs were built as a schedule with few exceptions, and the exceptions were carved out for goods the administration did not want to tax: books and informational material, products already covered by Section 232 duties, and goods from Mexico and Canada that meet the trade agreement's rules of origin. Electronics were not on that list when the schedule took effect on 5 April. A week later they were, and the mechanism was a customs notice published late on a Friday.

Customs and Border Protection issued the notice on the evening of 11 April, and the White House followed with a memorandum titled as a clarification of exceptions under the April executive order. The excluded headings cover smartphones, computers and their peripherals, graphics processors and printed circuit assemblies, machinery for making semiconductors, and networking equipment. Goods entered or withdrawn from a warehouse after 5 April qualify, and duties already paid on them can be refunded.

What the document does not contain is a reason. No economic analysis, no national security finding, no statement about which firms asked or which supply chain was judged indispensable. A reader who wants the rationale has to reconstruct it from the categories: these are the imports that pass through consumer prices quickly, that no domestic plant can replace within a year, and that accounted for a large share of the tariff base. Refunds also imply the government accepted that the first week's collections were wrong.

The timing carries information of its own. The schedule had been in force for six days, the bond market had moved violently, and the administration had already paused the highest rates for ninety days. An exemption issued on a Friday evening, with no press conference and no spokesperson, is a step taken to stop a specific harm, not a policy announced. That is how tariff schedules usually change, in the technical annex rather than the speech.

Industry read it as relief with a expiry date. Analysts noted that semiconductor manufacturing equipment and chips were now outside the reciprocal schedule but remained inside the Section 232 investigation that had been opened in April, and the president said over the weekend that semiconductors would be handled in a different way. An exemption from one instrument is not an exemption from the next, and firms making capital decisions noticed the difference.

The legal shape matters more than the immediate saving. A customs notice that grants an exclusion can be withdrawn by another notice, and the refund provision creates an administrative record that importers will use in court if the exclusion is reversed. Tariff policy conducted through the customs code produces a paper trail that is harder to reverse than a statement, which is an argument for reading the Federal Register and not the briefing.

For the year, the lesson is about how a tariff regime actually behaves. Rates announced as a single number turn into a schedule of headings, exclusions, refunds and effective dates, and the effective outcome depends on which of those the administration is willing to write down. A schedule with a refund clause is a schedule that admits it made a mistake, and a mistake admitted in a customs notice is a mistake that will be measured.
