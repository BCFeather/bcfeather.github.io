DNA replication doubles the genome, and the new duplex must be wrapped into nucleosomes. Deposition is done by a chaperone handing histones to the DNA, and that hand-off has been a black box. In *Science* in August 2023, the box is opened. The authors determine the structure of the chromatin assembly factor CAF-1 with its histone cargo, and the arrangement shows how a tetramer is held for delivery.

The grip is the finding. The chaperone grips the histone pair in a conformation preventing premature association with DNA, and the grip is released at the right moment. That grip is protection, because a pair meeting DNA too early forms an aberrant particle. Coupling to replication is the design. CAF-1 travels with the replication machinery through an interaction with a processivity factor, and the structure shows where that contact sits; the factor is the address, since a chaperone bound to the clamp is present where the fork is.

Ordered deposition follows. A new nucleosome needs a tetramer first and two dimers after, and the cargo is the tetramer the first step requires. Epigenetic inheritance supplies the consequence. A new nucleosome must carry the marks of the old, and a chaperone delivering histones is a node where marks can be re-established; the mark's re-establishment is the open epigenetic question, because a new histone arrives unmodified.

Comparison with other chaperones frames it. A nuclear chaperone and a cytoplasmic one bind the same proteins for different purposes, and the structures show the difference. Dynamics set the limitation. A hand-off is a motion and a structure is one state. Whether the marks are copied is not addressed, since the chaperone delivers histones and the modifying enzymes are a separate system.

Molecular biology gains a step in a cycle described at atomic resolution, since chromatin inheritance has been inferred from genetics and the structure makes the inference a mechanism. Cancer connects plausibly, because an overexpressed chaperone supports proliferation and an inhibitor would be a candidate drug.

A deposited particle must also be placed at a defined site, because the sequence's preference for a histone octamer sets the lattice the chaperone fills, and a chaperone that delivers without positioning leaves the lattice to chance. The cell cycle's constraint is supply, since a dividing cell must double its histone content, and a chaperone delivering at the fork is what matches supply to demand.

A structure of the chaperone in complex with the clamp would show how the address is read, and that interface is the part of the mechanism the present work leaves as an inference. A time-resolved version of the same complex would show the order in which the grip opens and the DNA enters, and that order is what distinguishes a chaperone from a passive carrier. A reconstitution with recombinant histones and a defined DNA template would let the deposition be followed one step at a time, and that assay rather than a structure would show whether the tetramer is handed over before or after the fork passes.
