German industrial production fell 0.8 per cent in July from the previous month, the statistics office said on 7 September, the fourth monthly decline. Total output was about seven per cent below its level a year earlier. The July figure followed a revised fall of 1.4 per cent in June, and the output has now fallen in five of the last six months. The economy ministry said the outlook had stabilized at a low level, and the institutes have cut their forecasts for the year to a contraction of about half a per cent.

Construction led the decline. The builders cut output by about three per cent as the mortgages became expensive and the orders dried up, and the permits have fallen to their lowest in more than a decade. A building site that stops is a demand for steel that stops with it. The construction output was about nine per cent below its level of last year, and the government has promised a subsidy for the housing that has not yet been legislated.

Chemicals are the second source of weakness. The energy-intensive producers have kept a fifth of their capacity idle since the gas prices rose, and the output of the chemicals is about a fifth below its level of 2021. A plant that is idled is a cost that is permanent. The chemical industry has cut about 15 per cent of its jobs since the gas crisis, and the rest of the plants run at about 70 per cent of capacity.

Carmakers are the exception. The production of the vehicles rose as the supply of the chips improved, and the orders are full into next year. A car that is delivered is a number that flatters the average. The car output rose by about five per cent on the month, and the exports of the vehicles reached their highest since 2020.

Surveys point lower. The purchasing managers' index has been below 50 for a year, and the new orders fell again in August, which suggests the third quarter will be worse than the second. A survey below the line is a forecast that has been written. The orders for the machinery fell by about 10 per cent in the quarter, and the backlog is the lowest since 2021. The chemical association has said a quarter of its members are considering moving production abroad, which would make the fall permanent rather than cyclical.

What the month shows is that the largest industry in Europe has settled into a lower level, and the level is the measure of the energy shock that the subsidies have softened and not reversed. The government has set aside about 200 billion euros for the energy support, and the support expires at the end of the year. The subsidies have kept the lights on, and the output is the measure of what the lights are producing.
