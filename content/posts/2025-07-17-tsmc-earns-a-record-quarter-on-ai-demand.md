A foundry earns a record when its customers cannot get enough of one product, and TSMC's second quarter is that record: revenue of about 30 billion dollars, up 44 per cent on the year, net profit of 398 billion New Taiwan dollars, and a gross margin above 58 per cent that the company had guided to and then beaten.

High performance computing is now the majority. The segment that serves accelerators and networking accounted for 60 per cent of revenue, smartphones fell to about a quarter, and the mix has inverted in three years, which is why the company's results now track the capital budgets of five customers and not the phone cycle. A gross margin near 60 per cent in a capital-intensive industry is a margin that reflects scarcity at the leading edge, and it is also a margin that the company has said it will defend by raising prices rather than by cutting costs.

Advanced nodes carry the margin. Three nanometre and five nanometre output ran at full utilization, the company raised prices for some processes, and the cost of converting a fab from one node to the next is being recovered from a customer base that has no alternative at the leading edge.

Capital spending is the tell. The company kept its plan for the year at 38 to 42 billion dollars and said the spending would be weighted towards the second half, which is a schedule that implies confidence in 2026 demand and a willingness to build capacity before the orders are signed.

Geographic risk is the part the numbers do not show. The Arizona fab is ramping, the Japanese fab is producing, and the European project is in planning, and each of those sites costs more per wafer than a Taiwanese one, so a company that is being asked to diversify is a company whose long-run margin is being taxed by policy. A fab that is being built in Arizona while the leading process stays in Taiwan is a hedge against a risk that nobody wants to name, and the hedge costs a few percentage points of margin per wafer, which the company has been willing to pay.

Export controls shape where the demand lands and not how much of it there is. The company cannot ship the most advanced chips to some customers in China, and the customers who can buy are ordering more, so the net effect this year has been a shift in the customer list and not a fall in the total.

What would settle the year is whether the fourth quarter holds the third, because a foundry that is sold out through December has already reported next year's first half. A hold would confirm the cycle, and a softening would mean that the accelerator inventory that everyone has been building is finally enough. A fourth quarter that matches the third would mean the foundry has sold its output for a year in advance, and a fourth quarter that falls short would mean the inventory that customers have been building is finally large enough to make them pause.
