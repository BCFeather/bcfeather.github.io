GameStop raised 933 million dollars by selling shares during the rally that began in May, according to a filing on 24 May. The stock had risen by more than a hundred per cent in the preceding week, after an investor who had led the original squeeze posted again. The filing did not name the buyers, and the company said the proceeds would be used for general purposes, which in practice means the working capital that the stores consume.

Sale is the rational move inside an irrational episode. A company whose shares trade at a price unrelated to its business can turn that price into cash by issuing new stock, and the cash can be spent on things the business needs. A valuation that is a gift is a gift that a board should accept. The board has done this before, and the last sale was made at a fraction of the current price, which is the argument the executives make when shareholders ask why the company is issuing into a rally.

Structure of the trade explains the rally. The float is small, a large share of it is held by individuals who coordinate on social media, and the options market amplifies the moves because dealers have to hedge. A stock with a small float and heavy option activity is a stock that can move far from anything. The shares that trade are a small fraction of the total, and the rest are held by the founders and by the funds that have been short, which is why the price moves so far on so little volume.

Company's business has not improved. The stores sell physical games in a market that has moved online, and the revenue has fallen every year for five years, which is why the rally is described as a vote against the short sellers rather than a bet on the firm. A business that is shrinking is a business whose shares are a token in someone else's argument. The company has been closing stores and cutting costs, and the cash from the sale buys it time rather than a new strategy, which is the distinction the analysts keep making.

Regulators have been watching. The clearing house raised the deposit it requires for the options, and the brokers restricted some order types during the most volatile sessions. A market that needs the plumbing to be adjusted is a market where the risk has left the participants and reached the infrastructure. The brokers have restored the order types they restricted, and the episode has produced a set of rules that the regulator is still drafting, six months after the first squeeze.

What the episode shows is that the price of a share can become a political object, and the company that owns the shares can use that price to fund itself while the argument continues. A company that uses a bubble to repair its balance sheet is a company that has been given a second chance by people who did not intend to give it one.
