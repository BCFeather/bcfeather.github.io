Spain won the European Championship final in Berlin on 14 July, beating England 2-1 with a goal in the closing minutes, and the victory gave the country its fourth title and its first since 2012. The team won all of its matches in the tournament and beat Croatia, Italy, Germany and France on the way. A squad that wins seven matches is a squad whose substitutions were as important as its starting eleven.

Style was the talking point. A side that kept the ball and pressed high was a side that made the tournament into a demonstration of a method, and the youngest players in it were the ones who carried the method. A team that plays a system rather than a collection of stars is a team that can replace a star. A method that is copied is a method that stops being an advantage, and the copying usually takes one tournament. A side that presses high is a side that needs seven substitutes who can run, and the squad was built for that.

England's tournament was the other story. A squad with more expensive players reached a second consecutive final and was criticized throughout for cautious football, and the criticism was about the relationship between talent and tactics. A team that wins on individual moments is a team that is judged on them. A manager who is criticized throughout a tournament that he nearly wins is a manager whose replacement is debated in public. A squad that is valued at more than a billion pounds is a squad that the public expects to win without a method.

Winning goal came from the bench. A player who came on late scored, and the decision that put him on was the manager's. A final decided by a bench is a final that rewards a squad rather than a starting eleven. A substitute who scores is a player who was chosen for a reason that the manager had explained in training.

Economics are becoming the argument. A tournament spread across ten German cities is a tournament whose ticket revenue and broadcast income are distributed through the governing body, and the clubs that supply the players receive a share. A competition that is a product is a competition whose calendar the clubs want to control. A calendar that is expanded is a calendar that the players' union has already described as unsafe.

What the final shows is that the gap between the top dozen national teams is small and that method is what closes it. A young side that plays together for a month can beat a collection of better-known individuals, and the next tournament will be organized around that lesson. A lesson that the next tournament will test is whether a young squad can repeat a method once opponents have studied it. The method has already been adopted by the Spanish federation's youth teams, which is how a style becomes a system.
