---

British inflation fell from a peak above 11 per cent in 2022 to 2.5 per cent in the fourth quarter of 2024, and the Bank of England has been withdrawing restriction at a pace of one cut per quarter since August. The February decision took Bank Rate from 4.75 to 4.5 per cent, which is the third reduction in the cycle and the first of the year.

The vote was 7 to 2. Two members preferred a half-point cut to 4.25 per cent, and the argument for the larger move was that the labour market had loosened faster than the committee expected and that the real rate, at 4.5 per cent against 2.5 per cent inflation, was more restrictive than the target required.

Projections in the Monetary Policy Report explain the caution. The committee expects inflation to rise through the year to a peak of about 3.7 per cent, driven by energy prices, by regulated prices such as water and by the effect of the Budget on employer costs, and then to fall back to 2 per cent. A central bank that expects a temporary rise while it cuts is a central bank that has decided the rise is not a reason to wait.

Growth was the other side of the argument. The economy had stagnated through the second half of 2024, business surveys pointed to a weak start to 2025, and the increase in employer national insurance contributions announced in the October Budget was expected to raise hiring costs and to weigh on employment. The committee revised its growth forecast down and its inflation forecast up, which is the combination that makes a central bank uncomfortable.

Sterling moved little, and the gilt market took the decision in stride. A cut that had been priced at close to certainty produces no reaction, and the interesting part of the day was the guidance, where the Governor said the committee would take a gradual and careful approach, a phrase he had used before and now paired with an explicit expectation that further cuts would follow if disinflation continued.

Mortgage rates had already fallen in anticipation. The average two-year fixed rate had come down from its peak above 6 per cent, and lenders were repricing before the meeting, which means the decision reached borrowers through the expectations channel before the announcement arrived. A household remortgaging in the spring pays less than one that remortgaged last summer, and the transmission is the part of monetary policy that a household actually experiences.

What the committee is managing is a trade that has no good outcome. Inflation above target and growth below trend is the position in which every central bank has to choose which error it prefers, and the committee has chosen to cut slowly and to accept a higher peak in inflation instead of holding rates and risking a recession. The next meeting is in March, and the Budget, the energy price cap and the wage settlements will have arrived by then.
