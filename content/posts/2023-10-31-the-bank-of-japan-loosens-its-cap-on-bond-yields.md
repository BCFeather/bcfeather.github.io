A 1 per cent cap on the ten-year bond yield became a reference rather than a strict limit at the Bank of Japan meeting on 31 October, and the short-term rate stayed at minus 0.1 per cent. The yen weakened past 151 to the dollar before it was pushed back. The decision was made at the end of the two-day meeting, and the bank also raised its forecasts for the inflation of this year and next.

Small in the text and large in the market is the change. The bank has been buying the bonds without limit to hold the yield, and the reference gives it room to let the market set the price within a band. A cap that becomes a reference is a policy that is loosening without a rate cut. The yield rose to about 0.95 per cent on the day, below the reference, and the bank said it would buy the bonds when the moves become rapid.

Inflation is the reason. The core measure has been above 2 per cent for a year, and the wage talks in the spring are expected to deliver about 5 per cent, which is the evidence the bank has said it needs. A wage that is agreed is a policy that must change. The bank has said it needs to see the wage growth accompanied by the services prices, and the governor repeated that the exit will be gradual. The governor said the bank would keep the purchases flexible, and he repeated that the end of the negative rates depends on the wage talks in the spring.

Yen weakness is the pressure. The currency has fallen by about 12 per cent against the dollar this year, and the import prices have risen, which the households feel at the pump and in the shops. A currency that falls is an inflation that arrives from abroad. The import prices rose by about 15 per cent over the year in the yen terms, and the energy bill for the households has risen by about a fifth.

Its officials are watching. The finance ministry has said it is ready to intervene, and it did so in the autumn of last year when the yen fell past 150. A market that tests the line is a market that the ministry must answer. The ministry spent about 9 trillion yen on the interventions last year, and the reserves have fallen by about a tenth since.

What the meeting shows is that the bank has chosen to loosen the control on the long end before it raises the short rate, and the order of the two moves is the signature of a gradual exit. A bank that loosens the long end first is a bank that is preparing the market for the end of the negative rates, and the preparation is the point. The exit is now a question of the order rather than of the timing, and the order is what the bond market is watching.
