Commerce officials finalized a grant of 7.86 billion dollars to Intel under the chip incentive programme, money intended for fabrication and packaging projects in several states. The award had been announced in the spring and took months to convert into a contract, and it arrives at a company whose foundry business has lost money for several consecutive quarters. A grant that is paid against milestones is a grant that a delayed project receives late.

Foundry is the strategic idea. A company that designs its own chips and also makes chips for others is a company that can fill a fab with its own volume while it builds a customer base. That was the model before the industry split into designers and manufacturers, and Intel is trying to reverse the split with capital that its rivals spent decades accumulating. A fab that is built for a process node is a fab that cannot be converted to another node without new tools. A foundry that is built before it has customers is a bet on a market that does not yet exist.

Customers are the missing piece. A leading-edge fab needs a few large customers to run at a utilization that pays for the equipment, and the large customers that exist are designing products for a competitor's process. A foundry that wins a customer has to win it years before the product is defined, and the sales cycle is longer than the grant process. A customer that commits to a process is a customer that has to validate a design against a new set of rules.

Construction schedule has slipped. Some of the plants have been delayed and one has been scaled back, and a subsidy that is paid against milestones is a subsidy that arrives later when the milestones move. A government that wants capacity in its own territory is a government that has to accept the company's timetable. A subsidy that arrives after the spending is a subsidy that helps the balance sheet more than the schedule. A milestone that is tied to a building is a milestone that a company can meet with a shell.

Alternative uses of the money are the political question. The same programme is funding plants in other countries and in other companies, and a grant to the largest recipient is a grant that a smaller recipient will compare with its own. A programme designed to reduce dependence on one region has become a programme that picks winners. A programme that funds many plants is a programme that has to justify each award against the others. A smaller recipient that receives less is a recipient that will ask why scale was rewarded.

What the award buys is a plant that can make chips. What it does not buy is a customer list, and the gap between those two is the difference between capacity and a business. A business that needs a customer list is a business that a grant cannot supply.
