Chinese consumer prices fell 0.8 per cent in the year to January, the fourth consecutive monthly decline and the sharpest since 2009, according to the figures published on 8 February. Deflation of this kind is a demand problem rather than a supply one. The prices of food fell as the pork cycle turned, and the prices of the goods that the factories make fell as the capacity outran the orders. A price that falls because nothing is bought is a price that tells the producers to stop. The food prices fell because the pork supply recovered, and the energy prices fell because the government capped the fuel at a lower level.

Debt makes it worse. A household with a mortgage that is fixed in nominal terms sees the burden rise as the prices fall, and it responds by saving more, which pushes the prices down again. A circle that feeds itself is the one the central bank has been trying to break. Policy has been aimed at the supply. The government has cut the rates and the reserve requirements, and the banks have been told to lend to the developers, and the result has been more capacity in the industries that were already full. A stimulus that builds is a stimulus that deflates. The central bank has cut the reserve requirement twice in six months, and the credit that the banks have extended has gone to the state-owned firms.

Comparison with Japan is the one the economists make. That country spent a decade in mild deflation and needed a decade more to escape it, and the lesson was that the expectation matters more than the rate. An expectation of falling prices is a decision to wait. The wage growth has slowed in the private sector, and the graduates who cannot find work are the group whose spending the shops relied on.

What the month shows is that the cheapest money in years has not made the households spend, and the deflation is the evidence that the problem is the demand rather than the cost. A deflation that lasts a year is a deflation that changes behaviour, and the behaviour is what the policy has not yet reached. The government has said it will support the prices of the agricultural goods, which is a policy that helps the farmers and not the demand.
