Nissan and Honda signed a memorandum of understanding on 23 December to explore a joint holding company that would own both businesses, with a target of concluding the structure within a year. Mitsubishi, which is partly owned by Nissan, was named as a possible third member, and the talks follow a year in which Nissan cut its forecasts twice. Pressure is volume. A carmaker's cost per vehicle depends on how many it builds, and Nissan's factories were sized for a market that has shrunk. A company that cuts nine thousand jobs and twenty per cent of capacity is a company that has admitted its assumptions were wrong, and the admission came after a decade of trying to grow in markets where its rivals were stronger. A factory that was built for a volume that did not arrive is a factory that has to close.

Chinese market is the reason. A carmaker that earns a large share of its profit in China is a carmaker whose earnings depend on a competition with local electric-vehicle manufacturers who price below the joint ventures. Merger logic is scale in the parts that both companies buy. Obstacle is the product overlap. Two companies that sell similar cars in the same segments have to decide which factory closes, and a decision that closes a plant in a country with strong unions is a political decision.
