Security Council adopted a resolution on the twenty-fifth of March demanding an immediate ceasefire in Gaza for the remainder of Ramadan, which was due to end in about two weeks, and the unconditional release of all hostages. Resolution was passed with fourteen votes in favour and no veto, and the United States abstained rather than use its veto for the fourth time since October. Chamber applauded after the vote, which is unusual in the council.

Text was drafted by the ten elected members rather than by one of the permanent five, which is a change in the way the council has worked on the war. It demanded that the ceasefire be respected by all parties, that humanitarian access be expanded and that the barriers to the delivery of aid be lifted. It did not condemn Hamas by name, which was the reason the American delegation gave for abstaining, and it did not link the ceasefire to the release of hostages as a condition, which Israel had demanded.

Israeli reaction was immediate and personal. Prime minister cancelled a planned visit of a delegation to Washington that had been arranged to discuss an operation in Rafah, and he said the American abstention was a retreat from the position the two governments had agreed. Foreign minister said the resolution encouraged Hamas to hold out and that Israel would not be bound by it. American officials said the abstention did not change their support for Israel's security and that the cancellation of the visit was an overreaction.

Dispute over whether the resolution is binding followed within a day. American ambassador said the resolution was non-binding, which is a position that the United States has taken on other resolutions and that other members reject. Russian and Chinese diplomats said the resolution was adopted under chapter seven of the charter and that it is therefore binding. Argument matters because a binding ceasefire would oblige Israel to stop, and the practical effect of the ambiguity was that nothing changed on the ground.

Situation in Gaza that produced the resolution had become a famine. Report by the integrated food security phase classification published in March said that famine was imminent in the northern part of the territory and that 1.1 million people faced catastrophic conditions. Aid deliveries had fallen to a fraction of what agencies said was needed, and the number of trucks entering through the Rafah and Kerem Shalom crossings was in the dozens per day.

Consequence of the twenty-fifth of March was a resolution that was obeyed by nobody. Fighting continued through Ramadan and afterwards, Israel pressed its operation in Rafah, and Hamas did not release hostages except in the November agreement and in later exchanges. The American abstention was the most significant shift in the council's position since the war began, and it produced no change in the war. What it did was to establish that the United States was willing to let the council act without it, which is a signal that the government's tolerance for the campaign had narrowed.
