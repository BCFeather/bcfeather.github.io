title: "American inflation slows to 4 per cent as the petrol falls"
date: "2023-06-13T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The May consumer prices rose by 4 per cent over the year, the smallest increase since March 2021, while the core measure eased to 5.3 per cent."
---

American consumer prices rose by 4 per cent over the year to May, down from 4.9 per cent in April and below the forecast of 4.1. The monthly change was 0.1 per cent. The core measure rose by 0.4 per cent on the month and by 5.3 per cent over the year, which is the lowest since the autumn of 2021. The figure is the eleventh consecutive month of decline from the peak of 9.1 per cent last June. The measure is still about double the target, and the sum of the last three months is running at about 4.5 per cent on the annual basis.

Energy did the work again. The energy index fell by about 3.6 per cent on the month as the petrol dropped by about 4.8, and the utility gas by about 2.6. The petrol is now about 27 per cent cheaper than a year ago, and the base of the summer of 2022 will keep the headline low through the third quarter. The electricity fell by about 1.3 per cent on the month, and the fuel oil by about 2.8 as the heating season ended. The crude was about 72 dollars a barrel at the end of May, down about 10 per cent from the spring.

Shelter stayed firm. The rent rose by about 0.5 per cent and the owners' equivalent rent by about 0.4, which is the smallest monthly gain since the end of 2021. The measure is still about 8 per cent higher than a year ago, and the market rents have been falling for a year. The measure for the new tenants has been rising by about 2 per cent, and the gap with the stock is the reason the index is still high.

Used cars were the surprise. The prices rose by about 4.4 per cent for the second month in a row, and the wholesale prices suggest another gain in June. The new cars fell by about 0.1, and the car insurance rose by about 1.7 per cent as the insurers repriced the repairs. The motor vehicle insurance has risen by about 17 per cent over the year, which is the fastest of any service.

Services were mixed. The transport services fell by about 1 per cent as the air fares dropped by about 3, and the medical care services rose by about 0.6. The food away from home rose by about 0.5, and the food at home was flat for a second month. The health insurance measure fell again, and the statisticians have said the method will keep it falling into next year.

Markets took the report as a green light for the pause. The traders cut the odds of a June increase to about 10 per cent and kept the July one at about 60. The two-year yield fell by about 20 basis points. A headline at 4 per cent with the core at 5.3 is the split that the Fed has been describing, and the core is the part that decides the autumn. The committee meets on 14 June, and the pause is now the consensus among the economists at the banks.
