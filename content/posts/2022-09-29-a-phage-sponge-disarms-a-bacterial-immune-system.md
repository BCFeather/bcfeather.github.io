A bacterium that carries an immune system against phage is a bacterium that a phage has to overcome, and the overcoming is an arms race that leaves its evidence in the genome. The defence that uses a TIR domain was known to produce a signal, and the signal had no structure. The genes a phage carries against a defence are the record of the defence it met.

A family was identified of phage-encoded proteins, called Thoeris anti-defence 1, that inhibit Thoeris immunity, and showed that they act as sponges that bind and sequester the immune signalling molecule produced by TIR-domain proteins. The paper appeared in *Nature*, 29 September 2022. A sponge binds a small molecule without catalysing anything, and sequestration decouples the sensing of the phage from the activation of the effector.

A Tad1 protein can also sequester molecules derived from a plant TIR-domain protein, and a high-resolution crystal structure of the complex revealed a 1''-2' glycocyclic ADPR. The bacterial TIR proteins appear to make a closely related molecule, 1''-3' gcADPR, which activates the ThsA effector an order of magnitude more efficiently.
