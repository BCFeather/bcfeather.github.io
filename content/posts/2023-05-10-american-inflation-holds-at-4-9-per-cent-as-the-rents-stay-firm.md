title: "American inflation holds at 4.9 per cent as the rents stay firm"
date: "2023-05-10T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The April consumer prices rose by 4.9 per cent over the year, the tenth month of decline, while the core measure eased to 5.5 per cent."
---

American consumer prices rose by 4.9 per cent over the year to April, down from 5 per cent in March and the smallest increase since April 2021. The monthly change was 0.4 per cent. The core measure rose by 0.4 per cent on the month and by 5.5 per cent over the year, which is the first reading below 5.6 since the autumn. The figure was in line with the forecast, and it is the tenth consecutive month of decline from the peak of 9.1 per cent.

Energy prices were flat. The petrol prices rose by about 3 per cent on the month as the crude rebounded from the banking scare, and they are now about 12 per cent cheaper than a year ago. The electricity fell by about 0.7, and the natural gas by about 2 as the heating season ended. The crude was about 75 dollars a barrel at the end of April, up from about 70 in March after the cuts of the producers.

Shelter stayed firm at 0.5 per cent. The rent rose by about 0.6 per cent and the owners' equivalent rent by about 0.5, and the two lines are about a third of the index. The market rents have been falling since the summer of 2022, and the index follows with a lag of about a year. The forecasters expect the shelter to ease from the autumn. The rent measure has been running at about 8 per cent over the year, and the market measure that the private firms publish is about 2.

Used cars rose again in April. The prices rose by about 4.4 per cent on the month, the second increase after the declines of the winter, as the supply of the two-year-old vehicles stayed short. The new cars fell by about 0.2, and the car insurance rose by about 1.4 per cent. The wholesale prices of the used cars have been rising since February, which suggests the retail prices will follow for a few months.

Services were mixed. The transport fell as the air fares dropped by about 3 per cent, and the medical care services rose by about 0.5. The food at home was flat, and the food away from home rose by about 0.4 as the restaurants kept raising the menu prices. The medical care was the largest line in the services, and the health insurance is the part that the index treats differently.

Markets read the report as a relief on the headline and a warning on the core. The two-year yield fell by about 5 basis points, and the traders kept the odds of a June pause at about 60 per cent. A core at 5.5 per cent with the shelter still rising is a core that will not fall quickly, and the Fed has said it wants to see the services cool. The ten-year yield was little changed, and the dollar was flat against the basket of the currencies.
