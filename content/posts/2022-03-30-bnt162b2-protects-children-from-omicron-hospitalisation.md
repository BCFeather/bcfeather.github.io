title: "Two doses protect children from Omicron hospitalisation, less so from infection"
date: "2022-03-30T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [science]
description: "In a test-negative study at 31 US hospitals, two doses of BNT162b2 were 93 per cent effective against adolescent hospitalisation during Delta, and protection against Omicron hospitalisation was lower and waned faster."
---

Vaccinating children against COVID was argued on the grounds that the disease is mild in most of them and that the benefit is mainly indirect. Omicron changed the arithmetic, because it infected children in large numbers and hospitalized more of them than any earlier wave. Pediatric wards filled during the Omicron wave in a way they had not in earlier waves, and the question of whether vaccination reduced that pressure became urgent for health systems.

A case-control study at 31 hospitals in 23 states enrolled 1,185 children with COVID and 1,627 controls between July 2021 and February 2022, and estimated vaccine effectiveness against hospitalization and against critical disease. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 30 March 2022.

Of the cases, 1,043 were unvaccinated, 291 received life support and 14 died. During the Delta period, two doses were 93 per cent effective against hospitalization in adolescents aged 12 to 18, measured two to 22 weeks after vaccination, and 92 per cent effective at 23 to 44 weeks. The test-negative design compares children who tested positive with children who tested negative for the same reason, which controls for the propensity to seek testing.

During Omicron, effectiveness against hospitalization in adolescents fell, and the protection in children aged 5 to 11, who had been vaccinated more recently, was estimated separately. Waning was faster for infection than for severe disease, which is the pattern that every study of this vaccine has found. Critical disease was defined as receipt of life support or death, which is a narrow endpoint and therefore a conservative one.

Critical disease is the outcome that matters for a parent deciding whether to vaccinate. The proportion of cases who needed life support is the number that makes the argument, and a vaccine that reduces it is doing something that the background rate of pediatric hospitalization does not. The number of deaths, fourteen, is small and is the kind of figure that makes a vaccine's benefit hard to communicate to a parent who has read that the disease is mild in children.

The design cannot fully adjust for the differences between families who vaccinate and those who do not. Families that vaccinate may also be more likely to seek care, which would bias the estimate upward, or more likely to test, which would bias it in the other direction. Observed waning is also partly an artefact of the calendar, because the Omicron period began several months after most children had been vaccinated.

For a pediatrician, the result supports vaccination in adolescents and provides a quantified basis for the conversation about the smaller absolute benefit in younger children. The waning data suggest that a booster may be needed in the groups at highest risk. The argument for vaccination in children rests on a small absolute benefit and a small absolute risk, and the balance is different for a child with a risk factor than for one without.
