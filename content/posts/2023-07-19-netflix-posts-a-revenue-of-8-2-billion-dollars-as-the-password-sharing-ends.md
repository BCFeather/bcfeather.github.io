Netflix reported a revenue of 8.19 billion dollars for the quarter to June, up about 3 per cent, and a net profit of 1.49 billion. The earnings per share of 3.29 dollars beat the forecast of 2.86. The shares fell about 5 per cent as the revenue was a little below the estimate and the guidance was cautious. The revenue was about 100 million below the estimate, and the operating margin was about 22 per cent. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 1 percentage point from the revenue. The company said the operating income was about 1.8 billion dollars, and the free cash flow was about 1.3 billion.

Subscribers grew. The company added about 5.9 million paid accounts, mostly outside the United States, and the total was about 238 million. The paid sharing, which charges the households that lend the accounts, had converted about 30 per cent of the borrowers, and the company said the rest would follow. The growth in the Asia-Pacific was about 3 million accounts, and the one in the Europe about 2. The company said about 100 million households had been using the shared accounts, and the conversion had been slower in the markets with the low prices.

Prices rose. The company has raised the prices in about 30 countries this year, and the average revenue per the member rose by about 5 per cent on the constant currency. The management said the price increases had produced little churn in the markets where the content was strong. The revenue per the member in the United States and Canada was about 16 dollars a month, up about 6 per cent.

Content was thin. The quarter had few of the large releases because of the writers' strike, and the slate for the second half has been cut. The company said the strike would shift about 2 billion dollars of the spending into the next year, and the schedule would slip. The company has about 20 of the large titles planned for the second half, including the new season of the Stranger Things.

Its advertising tier grew. The cheaper plan with the ads was about 15 per cent of the new sign-ups in the markets where it was offered, and the company said the revenue per the user was about the same as the standard plan once the ads were counted. The advertising tier had about 5 million users at the end of the quarter, and the company said the number would double next year.

This quarter is the one in which the sharing rules changed the arithmetic. A company that adds 5.9 million accounts while it charges the borrowers is a company that has found a price it can charge twice, and the second charge is the part the growth now rests on. The shares are up about 40 per cent this year, and the company is the best performer in the media group.
