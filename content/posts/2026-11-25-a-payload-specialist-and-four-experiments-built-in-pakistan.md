Launching at 08:18 and docking at 11:56 was the routine part of the day. A Long March 2F carrying Shenzhou 24 lifted off from Jiuquan at 08:18 Beijing time, the spacecraft separated from the rocket about 10 minutes later, and it docked at the forward port of the Tianhe core module at 11:56, three and a half hours after leaving the ground. The manifest is where the mission departs from its predecessors: 90 experiments, four of them built in Pakistan, and a crew that includes the station's first foreign member.

Tang Hongbo commands the flight, his third, after Shenzhou 12 and Shenzhou 17, and his second as commander. Wu Yi, from the third batch of Chinese astronauts, flies for the first time as flight engineer. Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a Pakistan Air Force pilot with the rank of lieutenant colonel, flies as payload specialist, having been selected in April 2026 under an agreement on astronaut selection, training and flight signed in Islamabad in February 2025. The crew fills the three roles the programme assigns on every flight: commander, flight engineer and payload specialist.

The four joint experiments are specific. One cultures cyanobacteria in the life-science cabinet of the Wentian module to measure photosynthetic efficiency, growth and carbon fixation under microgravity and radiation. One exposes 32 samples of quinoa seed outside the station. One places a graphene hybrid composite shield beside aluminium of equal areal density, with track detectors recording particle hits over a year. One carries carbon fibre panels with pre-cut scratches, photographed in orbit to see how far the cracks close.

Each of the four answers a question that a long mission would ask. Cyanobacteria fix carbon and release oxygen, which makes them a candidate for recycling air and producing food on a station or a base. Quinoa tolerates salt, drought and cold, and its seed has to survive radiation if it is to be grown from stock carried aloft. Shielding is a mass problem, so a lighter composite that matches aluminium changes the budget. A structure that heals a crack saves a crew from a repair they cannot make.

What the mission cannot yet report is a result. The exposure runs for about a year, the samples come back with the returning spacecraft, and the imaging of the panels is a record rather than a conclusion. Ali flies a short mission and returns on Shenzhou 23, while one member of that crew stays aboard for a year-long residency study that began before this launch and continues after it. The quinoa and the composite panels will be opened on the ground, months after the crew that installed them has left.

The programme's own count is worth setting down. This is the eighth crewed flight of the station's application and development phase and the 41st launch since the project was approved, with Shenzhou 24 the 24th flight of the spacecraft and the Long March family's 680th. Fifty person-flights have now been made by 31 Chinese astronauts and one foreign one, a number that changed for the first time on 25 November. The station has been continuously occupied since 2022, and the rotation this launch begins is the pattern the programme now runs on a fixed rhythm.
