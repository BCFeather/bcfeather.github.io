Educational attainment is a heritable trait that geneticists use as a proxy for cognitive ability, and the samples that have been analysed are overwhelmingly European. A paper in Nature Human Behaviour on 5 January 2024 asks whether the architecture holds elsewhere. A shared genetic architecture is a statement about variants, and a statement about variants is weaker than a statement about prediction, which is what a score claims. A score built for one population and applied to another is a natural experiment, and the paper treats transfer as an experiment rather than an assumption. A heritability estimate from one population is a statement about that population, and treating it as universal is the error the comparison is designed to catch.

East Asian cohorts supply the comparison, and the comparison is the point of the study. Shared architecture means that variants associated in one population are associated in another, and a shared architecture is what allows a score to be transferred. Transferability is the property a policy would want, and a policy that applies a score across populations needs evidence that the score travels. Interpretation of a heritability estimate is contested even within one population, and cross-population work inherits the contest.

Transferability is the practical question behind the comparison. A score that works in one group and fails in another is a score with a limited scope. Allele frequency differences make a comparison of effect sizes noisy, and noise is the reason a null result would not prove divergence.

The results show substantial sharing, and sharing is not identity. Effect sizes differ between populations, and differences of that kind are expected when allele frequencies differ. Social outcomes are what the trait measures, and measuring schooling is measuring a pathway through institutions that differ between the populations studied.

Allele frequency is the parameter that makes a shared architecture look unshared in a small sample. Educational attainment is a social outcome as much as a biological one, and the paper is careful about what a genetic score can say. A confounder that genetics cannot remove is a confounder that limits the claim, and the limitation is stated rather than hidden.

A score that predicts schooling is a score that predicts a pathway through institutions, not a score that measures capacity. Institutions differ between the populations, and the difference is a confounder that genetics cannot remove. Smaller cohorts produce wider intervals, and wider intervals are what a comparison across ancestries has to contend with in every study of this kind. A comparison across ancestries is a comparison of environments as well as of genomes, and an environment cannot be held constant in a natural sample.

Sample size is the limitation that most such studies face, and the East Asian cohorts are smaller than the European ones. A smaller sample produces noisier estimates, and noise is what a comparison of effect sizes has to survive. Ancestry is a proxy for many things, including environment and migration history, and the proxy is what makes a genetic comparison interpretable.
