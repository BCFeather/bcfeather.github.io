A chromosome is copied from many starting points, and where those points are is a decision that the cell makes each cycle. The decision's mechanism has been clearer in yeast than in animals.

The work in *Science*, September 2023 addresses the animal version. The authors map the origins that fire in a cell cycle and correlate their positions with chromatin marks and with the binding of a licensing factor.

The correlation is the finding. An origin's activity tracks an open chromatin state and a bound licensing complex, and the two together predict which sites fire early.

The licensing is the mechanism's core. A complex that loads a helicase onto DNA marks a site as a candidate, and a candidate that is in an accessible region is one that fires.

The chromatin's role explains the variation. A region that is compacted in one cell type and open in another has different origins, and the cell type's identity is therefore written into its replication.

The timing is the second layer. An early origin and a late one are in different chromatin environments, and the authors' ordering is consistent with a model in which accessibility sets the schedule.

The disease connection is plausible. A cell that fires from the wrong origins has rearrangements, and the authors' comparison with a cancer cell line shows a shifted map.

The limitation is the method's resolution. A population of cells averages the origins, and a single cell's choice may differ.

What the paper does not show is the trigger. A licensed origin that fires needs a kinase's activity, and the transition from candidate to active is a separate step.

For molecular biology, the value is a map of a decision. The genome's copying has been studied as a machine, and the paper describes the choice of where the machine starts.

The origin's definition is a functional one. A site that a helicase loads and that a polymerase starts from is an origin, and a site that is licensed but never fires is a candidate that the cell declined to use.

The chromatin's marks are the readout. An acetylated histone and an accessible DNA together indicate an open region, and the authors' correlation of marks with firing is what makes accessibility the predictor.

The firing's efficiency is the measured quantity. An origin that fires in every cell of a population and one that fires in a tenth of them are different, and the authors' efficiency map is the paper's resolution.

The comparison with yeast is instructive. A yeast's origins are defined by a short sequence, and an animal's are defined by context, so the two systems teach different lessons about specificity.

The replication's timing is the cell cycle's programme. An early-firing region is one that the cell needs first, and the schedule's relation to gene expression is a hypothesis that the map supports.

The genome's stability is the stake. A copy that starts from too few origins leaves gaps, and a copy that starts from too many collides, and the cell's choice is a balance that the paper measures.
