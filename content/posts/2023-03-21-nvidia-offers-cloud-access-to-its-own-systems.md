Customers pay by the month for a cluster, and the company said the entry configuration had eight GPUs and 640 gigabytes of memory. The announcement came at the company's developer conference on 21 March, which was held in person for the first time since 2019. The service is called DGX Cloud and it rents access to machines built around the company's accelerators. The conference drew 15,000 people in person and 200,000 registered for the online sessions.

Selling the hardware and renting it out is an unusual combination for one company. The company said the cloud service would run with partners that already operate data centres, and it named three in the announcement. That arrangement lets the company reach customers who cannot buy the machines outright or wait for delivery. The partners operate data centres in the United States and Europe, and one of them is a telecoms group.

The conference also produced a new generation of the data centre chip, a platform for custom silicon and a set of libraries for drug discovery. The chief executive said the company's advantage was in software rather than in the chips, and that the libraries were the reason customers stayed. The new chip was the first to use a memory technology that the company had developed with a partner.

Analysts noted that the cloud service competes with the company's own customers, which include the largest cloud providers. The company said it would not compete on price with them and that the service was aimed at enterprises that wanted a smaller scale. Two of the partners said they would offer the service in their own regions. The largest cloud providers had been designing their own accelerators, which the company called a complement.

Demand had been constrained by the supply of the most advanced chips, and the company said it had booked capacity at a foundry for the year. The company's data centre revenue had reached 15 billion dollars in the previous year, and its share of the accelerator market was estimated at 80 per cent. The lead time for a large system was reported at six months. The company's revenue for the quarter was 7.2 billion dollars, and the data centre line was 4.3 billion.

He said the company employed more software engineers than hardware engineers, and that the ratio had reversed in five years. The conference had been a hardware event for a decade and had become a software event, which the chief executive acknowledged in his speech. Partners on stage included a carmaker and a drug company, which is the pattern the company wants to show. Its software libraries had been downloaded 2 million times, and the company said 90 per cent were free. Pricing was published at 37,000 dollars a month for the smallest configuration. The service was generally available in the summer, and the company reported 1,000 enterprise customers by the autumn. Competitors in the cloud market launched their own AI instances within the quarter.
