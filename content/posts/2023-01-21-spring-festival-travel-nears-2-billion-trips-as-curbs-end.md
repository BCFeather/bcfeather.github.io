Travel around the lunar new year began on 7 January and runs for forty days. Officials put the expected number of passenger trips at 2.06 billion, up about 70 per cent from the previous year and still below the 3 billion of 2019. Railways expected 497 million journeys, the highest since the pandemic began. Tickets for the busiest routes sold out within minutes when sales opened in December, and resale prices rose above face value.

Holiday travel without movement controls for the first time since 2019 changed the shape of the season. In earlier years the advice had been to stay where people worked, and local governments had paid bonuses to those who did. This year the advice was to travel safely, and the bookings followed within days. Railway officials added 100 extra pairs of trains on the busiest corridors and ran some services through the night.

Air travel recovered more slowly than rail. Domestic flights were at about 70 per cent of 2019 capacity, and international routes were at a fifth, because many countries still required tests for arrivals. Airlines added seats on the busiest domestic routes and cut prices on the ones that had been thin. International routes were limited by agreements suspended during the pandemic, and restoring them takes months of negotiation. The aviation regulator approved 1,200 weekly international flights in January, up from 800 in December.

Busiest stations handled crowds not seen for three years. Guangzhou South, one of the largest, expected 400,000 departures on its peak day, and it opened overflow waiting areas in the car park. Staff handed out numbers and separated the queues by destination, a system designed after the crush of 2008. The railways ministry said the peak day would be 21 January, when 15 million people were expected to travel. Station staff in Guangzhou used a booking system that assigned arrival times, which spread the load across the day.

Weather was the other variable. A cold snap in the north closed several highways for two days in the middle of the season, and provincial authorities organised convoys for the cars stuck on them. The ministry of transport said the number of road trips was the largest single category, as it usually is. Crews stayed on the closed highways for two days, and no injuries were reported among the stranded drivers. Highway traffic on the first day was 25 per cent above the previous year, according to the ministry.

Retail and catering figures for the holiday week, published in late January, were the first hard measure of demand. Cinema takings reached 6.7 billion yuan, above the previous year, and restaurant bookings in the largest cities were reported at 80 per cent of 2019 levels. The season ends on 15 February, and the totals are published a week later. The holiday total is compared with 2019 in most official statements, and this year reached about 70 per cent of it.
