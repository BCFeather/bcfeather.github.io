Evans Chebet of Kenya won the men's race in 2 hours 8 minutes 41 seconds. The race was held on 6 November, and 47,000 runners started in Staten Island. The field had been capped at 33,000 in 2021 and cancelled altogether in 2020. The organisers said it was the largest field in the event's fifty-year history, and 2.5 million people watched along the route. He had won the Boston Marathon in April and had not run New York before. His compatriot Albert Korir, the 2021 winner, was second, and the Moroccan Mohamed El Gourouj was third. The field had 50,000 places, and 47,000 started, which is the largest number since the event began in 1970.

Sharon Lokedi of Kenya won the women's race in 2 hours 23 minutes 23 seconds. She was 28 and was running her first marathon, and she had not been among the favorites. The Ethiopian Lonah Salpeter, who had the fastest personal best in the field, dropped out at 30 kilometres. Chebet ran the second half in 63 minutes, and he broke away at 35 kilometres.

Hug had won in New York four times, and he set a course record in 2 hours 15 minutes. The wheelchair races were won by Marcel Hug of Switzerland and by Susannah Scaroni of the United States. The prize money was 100,000 dollars for the winners, and the total purse was 900,000. Lokedi had run 2 hours 24 minutes in training, and she was the eighth fastest woman in the field.

About 10,000 runners were raising money for a charity place, and the largest teams were for cancer research and for children's hospitals. The race raised 100 million dollars for charities, according to the organisers. The entry fee for a charity place was 3,000 dollars, and the runners raised more than that on average.

Two runners were taken to hospital with heat exhaustion, and the medical team said the number was in line with previous years. The weather was warm for November, at 21 degrees, and the organizers had added water stations. The course record of 2 hours 5 minutes 6 seconds, set in 2011, was not threatened. The organisers said the charity total was a record, and it had been 90 million in 2019.

Chebet's win put him in contention for the series prize, which he won in 2023 after winning Boston and New York again. The race was the first since the death of the event's longtime director, who had led it for two decades. The organisers dedicated the race to him, and the elite field wore black ribbons. The next edition was scheduled for November 2023, and the field was expected to be the same size. The marathon was one of six major races, and the winners collected points towards the series. Lokedi's win was her only major of the year.
