NVIDIA reported a revenue of 13.5 billion dollars for the quarter to July, up 101 per cent, and a net profit of 6.2 billion. The earnings per share of 2.70 dollars beat the forecast of 2.09. The company guided the current quarter to about 16 billion dollars, which is well above the estimate of 12.5, and the shares rose about 6 per cent. The revenue was about 2.2 billion above the estimate, and the constant currency growth was about 100 per cent. The company said the professional visualization rose by about 20 per cent, and the automotive by about 15.

Data centre did the work. The revenue from the data centre was about 10.3 billion dollars, up about 170 per cent, and the one from the gaming about 2.5. The company said the demand for the AI training had been the largest it had seen, and the supply was the constraint. The H100 chip was the largest part of the data centre revenue, and the company said the orders were booked through the next year. The networking revenue was about 3 billion dollars, up about 90 per cent as the clusters were built.

Gross margin rose to about 71 per cent from 65. The company said the mix of the AI chips and the pricing had lifted it, and the margin would be about 72 in the current quarter. The operating expenses rose by about 10 per cent as the headcount grew. The company said the inventory was about 4 billion dollars, down about 1 billion, as the supply chain normalized.

Supply is the plan. The company said it had secured the additional capacity for the chips and the memory through the next year, and the lead times were about three to four months. The customers included the large cloud providers and about 100 of the AI startups. The capital spending of the customers is expected to be about 100 billion dollars this year, and the AI part about a quarter.

Export rules are the risk. The company said the sales to China had been about 20 to 25 per cent of the data centre revenue, and the export controls could cut it. The management said it would comply and the demand elsewhere would absorb the difference. The company said the new chips for China would be shipped from the autumn, and the performance would be lower than the export limits.

This quarter is the one in which the AI became the business. A company that grows by 101 per cent and guides another 50 per cent above the market is a company selling the picks in a rush, and the rush is the part that the supply cannot yet match. The shares are up about 220 per cent this year, and the company is the best performer in the large chip makers by a wide margin.
