description: "A 4,000-square-metre showroom in Beijing sells, leases and repairs humanoid robots, and the vouchers handed out with it are a test of whether a consumer market exists at all."
---

The world's first robot 4S store opened in Yizhuang in early August, in time for the World Robot Conference, and it is organized like a car dealership: a showroom floor, a service bay, a leasing desk and a parts counter. The space is about 4,000 square metres and carries more than 100 products from more than 40 companies.

The price list is the interesting document. A full-size humanoid that can walk and carry a load costs between 199,000 and 779,000 yuan depending on the model and the payload. A quadruped for inspection costs less than a small car. A cleaning robot for a shopping centre is sold as a three-year lease with maintenance included, which is how the commercial service robot market has actually worked for a decade. The lease transfers the technology risk to the lessor, who can spread it across a hundred machines and a service team.

Buying a robot is not like buying a fridge, and the store's structure acknowledges it. The service bay exists because joints wear, batteries degrade and firmware updates break things. The leasing desk exists because a factory that wants to try a machine for a season will not commit capital for a machine that may be obsolete in eighteen months.

The consumer side is being tested with subsidies. A parallel robot consumption festival, run by the district, issued vouchers worth up to 1,500 yuan for an individual buyer and up to 250,000 for a company, redeemable in the store and at a pop-up market where robots made sugar-coated haws, played board games and poured drinks. JD.com ran an online zone with close to 1,000 products in the same category. The vouchers were designed to be spent quickly, and the queue at the sugar-coated haw stall suggested that the holiday crowd was treating the store as an arcade with a cashier.

The economics of that market are uncertain. A household machine at 200,000 yuan is a luxury object, and the stores know it: the models that sell to individuals are the small ones, in the range of a good appliance, that do one thing well. The humanoids on the floor are mostly there to be looked at, and the sales staff are honest about it.

What the store really tests is the after-sales network. A robot that needs a technician from Beijing to fix it is a product for Beijing. A robot that a local dealer can service is a product for the country. The 4S model, borrowed from the car industry, is an attempt to build that network before the demand arrives, which is either foresight or a very expensive showroom. The district's bet is that the service network is the moat, and that the buyer who needs a repair on a Sunday will pay for the store that has one. If that turns out to be true, the showroom is cheap marketing and the workshop is the actual product.
