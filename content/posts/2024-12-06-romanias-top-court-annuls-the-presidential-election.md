title: "Romania's top court annuls the presidential election"
date: "2024-12-06T22:05:00+08:00"

description: "The Romanian Constitutional Court cancelled the whole presidential election on 6 December, two days before the runoff, after intelligence documents alleged a coordinated foreign campaign on TikTok in favour of the first-round winner."
---

Romanian Constitutional Court annulled the entire presidential election on the sixth of December, two days before the second round was due, and ordered the process to start again from the beginning. Decision was unanimous among the nine judges and cited the court's power to safeguard the legality of the vote. First round had been won by Calin Georgescu, a little-known nationalist who had campaigned almost entirely on TikTok and had gone from single figures in the polls to first place in three weeks.

Documents behind the ruling came from the country's intelligence services and were declassified in the preceding week. They alleged a coordinated campaign on TikTok that promoted Georgescu, that accounts had been amplified by a network of paid influencers, and that the operation had foreign direction. Prosecutors opened a criminal investigation into money laundering and into the financing of the campaign, and the platform was asked by the European Commission for information about its handling of political advertising. No finding has been made about who directed the campaign.

Timing was the part that troubled lawyers and diplomats. Cancelling a vote after the voters had cast their ballots is a step with few precedents in a European Union member state, and it was taken against a candidate who had won the first round and who had been leading in the polls for the runoff. Court's argument was that the irregularities affected the process itself, and that a result produced by an unlawfully influenced campaign could not stand. Critics inside Romania said the court had substituted its judgment for the electorate's, and that the remedy would confirm the suspicion that the establishment would not accept an inconvenient result.

Georgescu's response was to describe the decision as a coup and to call for protests. Crowds gathered outside the court in Bucharest the following evening, and the candidate said he would challenge the ruling at the European Court of Human Rights. Support in the country was concentrated among young men, in rural areas and among voters who said they trusted nobody in the established parties, which is a constituency common to nationalist movements across the continent.

Consequences are larger than one election. Romania is a member of NATO and of the European Union, it shares a long border with Ukraine, and it had been governed for a decade by a rotation of two mainstream parties that the first round had just rejected. A rerun will be held in the spring, with a new government appointed in the meantime, and the campaigns will be watched from Brussels and from Moscow. Question that has not been settled is whether the annulment removes the foreign influence or simply postpones the vote that produced it.

Precedent is the part lawyers will argue about for years. Courts in other countries have cancelled referendums and have disqualified candidates, and few have voided a presidential election after the first round had been won. European Commission said the decision was a matter for the Romanian constitutional order and that it would await the new process. Investors took it calmly, and the currency and the bond market moved by less than a point, which is a measure of how much of the country's risk had already been priced in.
