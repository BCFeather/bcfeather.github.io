Tesla reported a revenue of 24.9 billion dollars for the quarter to June, up about 47 per cent, and a net profit of 2.7 billion. The earnings per share of 0.91 dollars beat the forecast of 0.81. The shares fell about 3 per cent as the automotive margin came in at about 18.2 per cent, down from 26 a year ago. The revenue was about 300 million above the estimate, and the operating margin was about 9.6 per cent. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 1 percentage point from the revenue.

Deliveries did the work. The company shipped about 466,000 vehicles in the quarter, up about 83 per cent, and the Model Y was the best-selling car in the world in the first half. The production was about 480,000, and the inventory rose by about 14,000. The Model 3 and the Model Y were about 96 per cent of the deliveries, and the Model S and the X about 4. The company said the orders had outpaced the production in the quarter, and the wait times had lengthened in the United States.

Prices fell. The company cut the prices in the United States and in China through the quarter, and the average selling price fell by about 20 per cent over the year. The management said the cuts were the investment in the volume, and the cost per the vehicle had fallen by about 10 per cent. The cost per the vehicle was about 38,000 dollars, down about 4,000 from a year ago, and the company said the next target is about 35,000.

Its energy business grew. The revenue from the storage was about 1.5 billion dollars, up about 70 per cent, and the one from the services about 2.1 billion. The company said the Megapack was sold out through the next year, and the margins were about 18 per cent. The storage deployments were about 3.7 gigawatt hours, up about 200 per cent, and the solar was about flat.

Factories are the plan. The plant in Mexico has been approved, and the one in Texas is ramping the Cybertruck for the delivery at the end of the year. The company said the capital spending would be about 9 to 10 billion dollars this year. The company has about 5 million square feet of the factory space under construction, and the one in Mexico will start in 2025.

This quarter is the one the price war shows in. A company that grows the deliveries by 83 per cent while the margin halves is a company buying the share with the profit, and the profit is the part the market is now pricing. The shares are up about 130 per cent this year, and the company is the best performer in the large car makers.
