---

Disney reported a profit of 264 million dollars for the quarter to September on 8 November, up from a loss a year earlier, on the revenue of 21.2 billion, which rose 5 per cent. The shares rose about 3 per cent. The revenue was above the forecast, and the operating income was 3 billion dollars for the quarter, about a fifth higher than a year earlier. The quarter was the fourth in which the streaming business narrowed its loss, and the company has promised the profit by the end of the next fiscal year.

Parks did the work. The operating profit of the experiences segment rose by about 30 per cent to 1.8 billion dollars, and the attendance in the American parks was above the level of 2019. A guest who pays the higher ticket is a margin that holds. The spending per guest rose by about 4 per cent, and the cruise line was the fastest growing part of the segment as the new ships sailed. The domestic parks were helped by the higher prices and the lower discounts, and the international parks were weaker as the tourists stayed home.

Streaming narrowed the loss. The direct-to-consumer unit lost about 400 million dollars, down from 1.4 billion a year earlier, and the company said the business would break even in the next fiscal year. A loss that narrows is a plan that is working. The number of the subscribers fell slightly to about 150 million, and the company said the price rise had cost fewer of them than it had expected. The company said the average revenue per subscriber rose by about 6 per cent in the American market after the price increase.

Cost cuts were raised. The company said it would remove 7.5 billion dollars of the costs by the end of next year, up from 5.5 billion, and about 8,000 jobs have gone since the spring. A cost that is removed is a profit that appears. The company has also been reviewing the television networks, and the chief executive said the company would keep the sports unit.

Films are the worry. The box office was weaker than the studio had planned, and the company has delayed several titles as the actors' strike ended. A slate that is delayed is a revenue that moves. The studio took a charge of about 200 million dollars for the films that were written off, and the analysts expect the next quarter to be better. The company has three films in the holiday slate, and the analysts expect the box office to recover only next year.

What the quarter shows is that the parks and the cuts have repaired the profit while the streaming and the films are still the question, and the question is the one the chief executive has been answering. A company that repairs the profit by the cuts is a company that must grow to be believed, and the growth is the part that the parks cannot supply alone.
