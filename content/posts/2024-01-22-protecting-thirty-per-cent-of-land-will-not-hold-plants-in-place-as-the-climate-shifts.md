The COP15 framework commits its signatories to protect thirty per cent of land, and a protected area is usually chosen for what lives in it now. A paper in Nature Ecology & Evolution on 22 January 2024 asks whether that choice survives climate change. A boundary that does not move is a boundary that protects a place rather than a species, and the distinction is the one the target obscures. Assisted migration is the option that the paper does not endorse and that the projections make thinkable.

Plant ranges shift as the climate shifts, and a boundary that does not move leaves the species outside. Transnational planning is the response the paper tests, because a species that crosses a border escapes a national plan. National plans stop at borders, and plants do not, so coordination is the minimum that a continental network requires. Thinkable options are not recommendations, and the paper keeps the difference visible. A continental plan needs a common map, and a common map is what the European Union already has and most regions do not.

Coordination across borders is what a continental network requires, and the current network is national. Projections of future ranges are the method, and the method carries the uncertainty of the climate models behind it. Conditional projections are the honest form of a climate claim, and the conditions should be named whenever a number is quoted. A scenario is not a forecast, and a paper that projects ranges has to say which scenario it projected under.

A projection is a conditional statement, and the conditions are the emission scenarios the authors chose. The result is that the existing network protects less of the future range than of the present range. Counting protection in area is convenient and counting it in range is accurate, and the two currencies are not interchangeable. A mismatch between area and range is the technical core of the paper, and the core is stated in a form a policy reader can use directly.

A gap of that kind means that protection is being counted in the wrong currency, and currency is the unit a policy target uses. Corridors are the alternative that the paper considers, since a corridor lets a range shift rather than requiring a boundary to hold. Corridors are cheaper than reserves and depend on land that is privately held, so their cost is political as much as financial.

A corridor costs less than a new reserve and connects two that exist. Farmland is where a shifting range usually arrives, and a range that arrives at a field is a range that stops.

Land use is the constraint that no corridor can escape, since a shifting range meets farmland before it meets protection. The thirty per cent target is a political commitment with a date, and a date is what makes the mismatch between area and range urgent. A political target with a fixed date rewards area counted quickly, and a range that shifts slowly rewards the opposite.
