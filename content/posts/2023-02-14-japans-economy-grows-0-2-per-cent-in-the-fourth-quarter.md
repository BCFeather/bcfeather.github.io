title: "Japan's economy grows 0.2 per cent in the fourth quarter"

description: "The preliminary figure beat the forecast, and business spending carried the quarter while household consumption stalled."
---

Output rose 0.2 per cent in the three months to December, the cabinet office said on 14 February. Economists had expected 0.1, and the annualized figure was 0.9 per cent. The full year grew 1.1 per cent, and the fourth quarter was the weakest of the year apart from the summer. The figure was the first estimate, and the revision published in March raised it to 0.1 per cent on the quarter. The cabinet office publishes the figure with a breakdown by demand, and the revision in March cut capital spending.

Capital spending rose 7.8 per cent, the strongest component, and it was driven by investment in machinery and software. Companies brought forward orders for equipment before the end of the tax year, and the weak yen made imported machinery more expensive. Private consumption rose 0.1 per cent, and it has now been flat for three quarters. Machinery orders, a leading indicator, rose 6 per cent in the quarter and fell back in January.

Exports fell 0.7 per cent, the second consecutive decline, as demand from Europe and America weakened. Imports fell 1.9 per cent, which helped the net trade contribution, and inventories added 0.4 points. The external sector had been the engine of growth in 2022, and its turn was visible in the monthly trade figures from November. Real household income fell 1.3 per cent for the year, which is why consumption did not respond to the reopening.

Nominal output rose 1.3 per cent, which means the deflator was about 1 per cent. That is a change from the previous decade, when nominal and real growth were almost the same. Wages rose 0.8 per cent in nominal terms in the quarter, and real wages fell for the eighth consecutive quarter. The trade deficit reached 20 trillion yen for the year, the widest on record, as energy import costs doubled. The yen traded near 132 to the dollar in February, having been 115 a year earlier.

In January the bank raised its forecast and said it expected growth of 1.7 per cent for the year. The government's stimulus package, passed in the autumn, was worth 200 billion dollars and mostly took the form of subsidies for fuel and electricity. Those subsidies held inflation below the level of other economies and supported consumption at the margin. The bank's governor said in February that the wage round would decide whether policy changed in the spring. Real wages had fallen because nominal pay was rising more slowly than prices, a gap of about 4 points.

Forecasts for the first quarter were stronger, because the reopening of the border in October was feeding through. Arrivals in January were 1.5 million, still below the 2.9 million of 2019, and spending by visitors was a third of the old level. Analysts said the export turn was the risk, and that the bank would watch it before changing policy. The government had also extended a subsidy for electricity until the summer, at a cost of 2 trillion yen.
