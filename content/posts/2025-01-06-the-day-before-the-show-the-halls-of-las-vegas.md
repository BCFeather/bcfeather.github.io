The consumer electronics show opens in Las Vegas on the seventh of January, and the day before it is the day the halls are finished. The carpets go down, the screens are aligned, the demo units are charged, and the staff who will stand at a booth for four days rehearse the sentence that explains the product in nine seconds.

The show is the largest of its kind and it has been running for more than half a century, and the function it performs has changed as the industry has. In the years when a new television or a new audio format was the news, the show was where a retailer went to decide what to order. The buying is now done through the channels, and the show has become the place where a company explains a product to the people who will write about it.

The arithmetic of that is visible in the press days. The two days before the floor opens are reserved for the press conferences and the briefings, and the companies schedule them at half-hour intervals so that a reporter can attend four in a morning. The announcements are timed to the minute and the embargoes are agreed in advance, which is why the coverage of the first day looks like a list rather than a set of stories.

What the show measures is the mood of the industry rather than its sales. A year in which every booth has a robot is a year in which the robotics money has arrived, and a year in which every booth has a screen with a language model on it is a year in which the model has become a component. The mood is a leading indicator in the way that fashion is, and it is usually early by a year or two.

The cost of attending is a barrier that shapes what the public sees. A booth of a respectable size costs a sum that a start-up cannot pay, the hotel rates triple for the week, and the companies that can afford the trip are the ones that already have a product. The start-ups are in the corner of a hall that is rented by a consortium, and the reporters who walk the main floor will not reach them.

What the show leaves behind, after the four days, is a set of videos and a set of contacts, and the value of the second is the part that the industry talks about. The deal that begins at a booth is a deal that a person can start with a handshake and a demonstration, and the demonstration is the reason that the show still exists in an age when a product can be launched on a website.

For the visitor who is not in the industry, the show is a preview of the objects that will be in the shops in the autumn, and the preview is usually accurate about the category and wrong about the date. The gadget that is shown in January reaches a shelf in September, and the version that ships is the version that the supply chain allowed.
