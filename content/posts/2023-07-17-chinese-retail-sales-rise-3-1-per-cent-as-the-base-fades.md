China's retail sales rose by 3.1 per cent over the year in June, below the forecast of about 3.5 and down from 12.7 in May as the base of the reopening dropped out. The industrial output rose by 4.4 per cent, above the estimate of 3.5, and the fixed asset investment by 3.8 in the first half. The figure was the slowest since the spring of 2022, when the lockdowns shut the shops in Shanghai. The six-month growth was about 7.6 per cent on the year, and the two-year average about 3.5.

Consumption is the slow part. The two-year average growth of the retail sales was about 3.5 per cent, and the one of the services was about 5. The restaurants rose by about 16 per cent over the year, and the one of the travel bookings by about 20 as the summer holidays began. The sales of the sports goods rose by about 9 per cent, and the one of the medicine by about 8.

Cars were the bright spot. The sales of the passenger vehicles rose by about 5 per cent in June, and the one of the electric by about 25 as the subsidies were extended. The appliance sales fell by about 3, and the one of the furniture by about 5 as the housing market stayed weak. The dealers have been cutting the prices of the internal combustion cars by about 10 per cent, and the electric ones by about 5.

Factories did better than expected. The output of the machinery rose by about 8 per cent, and the one of the cars by about 10. The cement fell by about 3, and the one of the steel by about 5 as the construction slowed. The output of the semiconductors fell by about 3 per cent, and the one of the industrial robots by about 5.

Property is still the weight. The investment in the real estate fell by about 7.9 per cent in the first half, and the sales of the floor space by about 5. The prices of the new homes in the largest cities fell by about 0.2 on the month, the first decline since the autumn. The floor space sold fell by about 5 per cent in June, and the one of the new starts by about 20. The land sales fell by about 25 per cent in the first half, and the developers have been buying only the sites in the largest cities.

These figures are the ones the second half will be judged on. A retail sector that grows by 3.1 per cent once the base drops out is a sector that needs the income, and the income is the part the policy has not moved. The economists expect the consumption to grow by about 4 per cent in the second half, which is below the level the target needs.
