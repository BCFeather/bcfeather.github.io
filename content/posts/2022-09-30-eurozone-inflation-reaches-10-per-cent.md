description: "The September flash estimate published on 30 September was a record for the currency area, driven by energy."
---

Eurostat published the flash estimate on 30 September, and consumer prices rose 10 per cent in the year to September. The reading was up from 9.1 per cent in August and the highest since the series began in 1997. On the month, prices rose 1.2 per cent, and the figure was 0.3 points above the forecast. Energy was the largest component. It rose 40.8 per cent over the year, and it contributed more than half of the headline rate. Electricity and gas prices for households had been fixed in some countries and were repriced in others, and the timing of those contracts explains why the index kept rising after the wholesale price had peaked.

Food rose 11.8 per cent, the fastest since the series began, and it was accelerating. Cereals, dairy and oils were the categories that moved most, and the war had cut Ukrainian exports. Services rose 4.8 per cent, and that component had been stable for three months, which was the only comfort in the report. Core inflation, which excludes energy and food, was 4.8 per cent. That number mattered more to the central bank than the headline, because it measures the part of the price level that policy can affect. The bank's target is 2 per cent on the headline, and its projections had core falling to 2.3 by 2024.

Estonia reported 24 per cent, and France reported 6.2, because it had capped gas and electricity prices. The differences between countries were large. Germany reported 10.9, and Italy 9.5. The single figure described an average of very different experiences, and the political argument in each country was about which part of the index to blame.

Real rates were deeply negative, and the bank said it would raise again in October. The central bank had raised rates by 125 basis points by the end of September, and its deposit rate was 0.75 per cent. Economists said the bank was behind the curve and that it had been slow to end its bond purchases, and the bank's own officials conceded the point in interviews.

Wages had begun to follow prices. Negotiated pay in Germany rose 5 per cent in the autumn round, and the public sector settled at 5.8 in December. The bank's president said a wage-price spiral was the main risk for 2023, and the data in the spring showed that wage growth had reached 5.6 per cent, the fastest since the currency began.

Inflation peaked at 10.6 per cent in October and fell below 10 in December as energy prices came down. The core rate kept rising into the spring and peaked at 5.7 per cent in March 2023. The bank kept raising until September, when the deposit rate reached 4 per cent and the council paused. The bank's next meeting was on 27 October, and it raised by three quarters of a point again.
