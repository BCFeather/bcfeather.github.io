NVIDIA reported a revenue of 7.19 billion dollars for the quarter to April and a profit of 2.04 billion, both above the forecast. The guidance for the next quarter was 11 billion dollars, about 50 per cent above the consensus of 7.2 billion. The shares rose about 25 per cent in the after-hours trading and reached a market value of about 950 billion. The revenue was about 8 per cent above the estimate, and the earnings per share of 1.09 dollars beat the forecast of 0.92. The company said the demand had accelerated through the quarter as the large customers built the training clusters.

Data centers did the work on the revenue. The revenue from that segment was 4.28 billion dollars, up 14 per cent, and the company said the demand for the AI accelerators had exceeded the supply. The gaming revenue was 2.24 billion, down 38 per cent, as the consumers returned to the cheaper cards after the boom of the pandemic. The automotive revenue was 296 million dollars, up about 135 per cent as the design wins in the electric cars went into production. The professional visualization fell by about 38 per cent, and the OEM segment by about 10 as the inventory was cleared.

Gross margin was 66.9 per cent, and the company guided about 68 per cent for the next quarter. The margin has been rising with the mix of the data center products, and the company said the pricing would hold while the supply stayed short. The operating expenses rose by about 5 per cent. The company said the margin would stay high while the supply was short, and the analysts expect the pricing to hold into next year.

Supply is the constraint. The company said the production of the flagship chip was limited by the packaging capacity and the memory supply, and the lead times had stretched to several months. The customers have been placing the orders a year ahead, and the visibility is the highest the company has had. The company has placed about 1 billion dollars of the orders with the foundries for the next year, which is the prepayment that secures the capacity.

Markets around it moved. The shares of the memory makers, the server builders and the foundries rose in sympathy, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index added about 6 per cent the next day. The company's own suppliers said the orders for the components had doubled. The shares had already tripled this year before the results, and the guidance added about 200 billion dollars to the market value in one session.

This quarter changed the story of the industry. A chipmaker that guides 50 per cent growth in three months is a chipmaker that has found the demand that the rest of the sector lacks, and the found is the one the whole market is repricing. The company is now worth more than most of the large industrial groups, and the debate is whether the demand is a cycle or a new base.
