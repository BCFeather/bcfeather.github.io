American retail sales fell 0.1 per cent in October, the commerce department said on 15 November, after a revised gain of 0.9 per cent in September. The forecast had been for a fall of 0.3. The figure was better than the forecast, and the sales are up by about 2.5 per cent over the year in the nominal terms, which is below the pace of the prices. The sales fell for the first time in seven months, and the revision to the previous month added about 0.2 points to the gain.

Dealers were the drag. The sales at the car dealers fell by about 1 per cent as the strike cut the supply and the rates raised the monthly payment. A car that is not on the lot is a sale that is not made. The average rate on a new car loan is about 8 per cent, and the monthly payment has risen by about 100 dollars over the year.

Furniture fell too. The spending at the furniture stores dropped by about 2 per cent, and the building materials fell by about 1, which is the housing market showing in the shops. A house that is not sold is a sofa that is not bought. The sales of the existing homes fell to the lowest since 2010, and the pending sales have fallen for four months.

Core sales held up at 0.2 per cent. A control that is positive is a consumer that is not stopping. The control group rose by 0.2 per cent after a gain of 0.5 in September, and the economists said the trend is about 0.3 a month. The measure rose by 0.2 per cent after the gain in the previous month was revised up, and the economists said it points to a fourth quarter of about 2 per cent growth. The measure rose 0.2 per cent, and the economists use it to estimate the consumption in the output data rather than the headline figure.

Online was strong. The non-store retailers rose by about 1 per cent, and the restaurants rose by about 0.3, which is the shift of the spending from the goods to the services. A dinner that is bought is a good that is not. The spending at the department stores fell by about 1 per cent, and the clothing was flat as the shoppers waited for the discounts.

What the month shows is that the consumer has slowed but not stopped, and the difference is what the fourth quarter will settle. A consumer that trades down is a consumer that is still spending, and the still is the word the forecasters are using. The holiday season will be the test, and the forecasters expect a gain of about 3 per cent over last year, which is below the pace of the prices.
