Kansas City arrived in New Orleans chasing a third consecutive championship, a feat no team in the Super Bowl era has achieved, and left having lost 40-22 to Philadelphia. The score was closer than the game, because two late touchdowns arrived after the outcome had been decided. The decisive passage was the first half, when the Eagles' defensive line reached the quarterback without needing to blitz.

Pressure with four rushers is the structural advantage that decides modern games. A defence that can generate a sack or a hurry while dropping seven into coverage removes the space that a quarterback needs to find a second read. Kansas City's offensive line had been assembled to protect a mobile quarterback, and it was beaten on the interior, where the Eagles' tackles are the strongest part of the team. Philadelphia's front four averaged more than a yard of penetration per dropback in the first half, which is a number that ends drives.

Philadelphia's offence did what it had done all season: it ran the ball, it converted third downs, and it asked the quarterback to make a small number of high-value throws. The passing game produced two long touchdowns on plays that were designed to beat a single-high safety, and the rest of the night was ball control. A team that wins the time of possession by ten minutes has already taken the ball out of its opponent's hands. Third-down conversion kept the chains moving, and a drive that lasts seven minutes also rests a defence that had been on the field all year.

The half-time show and the advertising are the parts of the broadcast that a global audience sees, and both are sold as inventory. A thirty-second slot in the American broadcast reportedly sold for more than seven million dollars, which is a price that only makes sense if the audience is the largest live audience of the year. The game itself is a television product as much as a sporting one. Advertisers buy the audience rather than the game, and the audience peaked before the fourth quarter had started.

Kansas City's problem is the one every dynasty faces. A roster that wins repeatedly becomes expensive, and the players who leave in free agency are the ones who were paid below their value. The line that protected the quarterback in 2023 was not the line that played in February 2025, and the difference was visible in the first quarter. Kansas City restructured several contracts to keep the core together, and the restructure is what limited the money available for linemen.

What the result changes is the argument about the best team of the decade. A third title would have settled it; a loss leaves the question open, and leaves Philadelphia with a young roster and a quarterback on a contract that the team can still afford. In a league designed to produce turnover, that combination is the most valuable asset there is. A young quarterback on a rookie contract is the mechanism that lets a team afford a defensive line, and that mechanism has a four-year clock.
