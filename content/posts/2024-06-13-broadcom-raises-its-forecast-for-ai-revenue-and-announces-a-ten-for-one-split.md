Broadcom reported revenue of 12.46 billion dollars for the quarter to the end of April, up 43 per cent from a year earlier, and raised its forecast for the year. It also announced a ten-for-one share split. The shares rose by more than ten per cent the following session. The revenue beat the consensus by about a hundred million dollars, and the guidance for the current quarter was above the highest forecast in the survey. The company also said its gross margin would hold above seventy per cent, which is the number the analysts use to separate the chip business from the rest.

Sales of artificial intelligence chips are the reason. The company said its sales of chips and of the networking equipment that ties accelerators together reached 3.1 billion dollars in the quarter, and it lifted the forecast for that line to more than eleven billion for the year. A forecast that is raised twice in a quarter is a forecast that the customers have confirmed with orders. The custom chips are designed with the customer and cannot be sold to anyone else, which means the revenue depends on four budgets rather than on a market.

Revenue composition is unusual. The company sells custom processors to a small number of cloud operators, and it sells the switches and the optical components that connect them, which means it earns from the network rather than only from the compute. A business that sells the plumbing is a business that grows with the number of machines. The networking revenue grew faster than the compute, and the company said the switches that carry traffic between accelerators were sold out for the year.

Software is the annuity. The division that the company acquired three years ago sells infrastructure software on subscription terms, and it contributes a margin that is higher than the chips. A subscription that renews is revenue that does not depend on the cycle. The software division has been raising prices at renewal, and the customers have accepted the increases because the products are embedded in their operations.

Share split is the retail question. A share that trades in the thousands is a share that few individuals buy, and the split addresses the same audience that the company is trying to reach with its index membership. A price that is lower is a shareholder base that is wider. The company has said it will not buy back shares at the current valuation, which is an admission that the price reflects the forecast rather than the history.

What the quarter shows is that the demand for accelerators has created a second supply chain around them, and the companies that build the connections are being repriced as growth stocks rather than as component makers. A supplier that sells to four customers and forecasts from their budgets is a supplier whose quarter is decided a year in advance, and the gap between order and delivery is the risk that the forecast carries.
