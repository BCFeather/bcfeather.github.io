Japan's core consumer prices, which exclude the fresh food, rose by 3.3 per cent over the year to June, down from 3.4 per cent in May. The headline was 3.3 per cent, and the measure that also strips the energy rose by 4.2, which is the twenty-third month above the target. The figure was in line with the forecast, and the measure has been above the 2 per cent target for eighteen months. The Tokyo figure for July, published a week later, showed the core at about 3.0 as the subsidies took effect.

Energy did the work on the headline. The electricity fell by about 13 per cent over the year, and the gas by about 10, as the government's subsidies were paid through the utilities. The fuel rose by about 1 per cent, and the support is scheduled to be reviewed in the autumn. The subsidy is paid to the utilities and the refiners, and the government has set aside about 2 trillion yen for it this year. The wholesale electricity price has fallen by about 35 per cent since the winter, and the effect will show in the summer bills.

Food kept rising. The prices of the food rose by about 8.4 per cent, and the one of the eggs by about 15 as the bird flu cut the flocks. The restaurant prices rose by about 5, and the services outside the restaurants by about 2. The rice rose by about 3 per cent, and the one of the vegetables fell by about 2 as the harvest arrived.

A narrower measure is the one the bank watches. A rise of 4.2 per cent means the pressure is broader than the food and the energy, and the services are the part the wages feed. The bank has said it needs the core-core to settle near 2 before it moves the policy. The rents rose by about 2 per cent in the largest cities, and the one of the hotels by about 8 as the inbound travel recovered.

A soft currency has been the background. The currency was about 140 against the dollar at the end of June, stronger than the 145 of the spring, and the import prices rose by about 1 per cent. The pass-through has been slower than in the past, and the bank says the effect shows with a lag. The import prices rose by about 1 per cent in June, and the one of the food and the energy by about 3.

This report keeps the bank in its corner. A core at 3.3 with the core-core at 4.2 is the split that the governor has described as the one that lets the bank wait, and the waiting is the policy. The bank meets in September, and the market expects the cap on the ten-year yield to be widened rather than removed.
