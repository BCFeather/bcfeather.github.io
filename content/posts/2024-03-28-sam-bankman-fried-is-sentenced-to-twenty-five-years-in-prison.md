Sam Bankman-Fried was sentenced on the twenty-eighth of March in a federal court in Manhattan to twenty-five years in prison, three years of supervised release and a forfeiture of about eleven billion dollars. Judge Lewis Kaplan said the defendant had shown no remorse and had lied repeatedly, and he described the losses as one of the largest frauds ever prosecuted in the United States. Sentence was shorter than the forty to fifty years that prosecutors had requested and longer than the six and a half years that the defence had proposed.

Conviction that produced the sentence came in November after a five-week trial in which three of his closest associates testified against him. Jury found him guilty on seven counts of fraud, conspiracy and money laundering. Witnesses said he had taken customer deposits and used them to cover losses at Alameda Research, his trading firm, to make political donations and to buy property, and that he had known the exchange was insolvent for months before it collapsed.

Collapse itself happened in November 2022, within days of a report that questioned the relationship between FTX and Alameda. Exchange had been valued at thirty-two billion dollars in a funding round earlier that year, and it was one of the largest venues for trading crypto assets. Withdrawals stopped, a rescue by Binance fell through, and the company filed for bankruptcy on the eleventh of November, with a shortfall that the new management later put at about eight billion dollars.

Defence argument at sentencing was about the character of the defendant and about the conditions in prison. Lawyers said he had been socially isolated, had been diagnosed with autism and had not intended to steal, and they submitted letters from family and from people who had known him. They also said the restitution that the estate might recover would be substantial, which the judge acknowledged while saying it did not change the offence.

Restitution that has been recovered is a distinct part of the case. The bankruptcy estate, managed by a new chief executive, has recovered assets that it says may be enough to repay customers in full, which is unusual in a fraud of this size. Judge said the forfeiture would be credited against the amount that victims receive, and prosecutors said the recovery did not diminish the seriousness of the conduct that produced the loss.

Consequence of the twenty-eighth of March is a sentence that closes the criminal case and leaves the regulatory one open. Bankman-Fried's appeal was filed within weeks, and he remained free on bail until the appeal court ordered him to surrender in September. The collapse of FTX accelerated the adoption of rules for crypto exchanges in the European Union and in several other jurisdictions, and the American Congress remained unable to pass a framework. What the sentence established was that the promise of effective altruism and the political donations that came with it were not a defence, and that the money that had been given away belonged to customers.
