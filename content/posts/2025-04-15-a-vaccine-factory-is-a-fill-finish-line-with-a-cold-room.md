title: "Vaccine factory is mostly a fill-finish line"

description: "Building capacity to make doses is not the same as building capacity to fill vials, and the second step is where most of the money goes."
---

The pandemic made every government aware that vaccine manufacturing capacity is strategic, and most of the capacity that has been built since is not the kind that produces the active ingredient. A drug substance plant ferments or synthesizes the molecule, and a fill-finish plant puts it into vials, inspects them and packs them. The second is cheaper and far more numerous, and it is the step that most new programmes have funded. A government that announces a vaccine plant is announcing a building, and the building is the part that can be photographed.

Fill-finish is a sterile process, and sterility is expensive. A line runs in a cleanroom with a classified air supply, and the vials are filled by machines that are validated against a standard that regulators audit. A plant that fills a hundred million doses a year costs a few hundred million dollars, and the qualification takes several years. A cleanroom that is validated for one product cannot be switched to another without a new validation, which is the cost of flexibility.

The drug substance is the harder half. An mRNA vaccine requires a facility that handles enzymatic synthesis and lipid nanoparticles, and the equipment is specialized and the know-how is concentrated in a few firms. A country that builds a fill-finish line has capacity for someone else's product, and a country that builds a drug substance line has capacity for its own. A lipid nanoparticle line requires a mixer that produces particles of a uniform size, and the uniformity is the hard part of the process.

Utilization is the economics problem. A plant built for a pandemic runs at a fraction of capacity in an ordinary year, and a plant that is idle is a plant that cannot be maintained. Several governments have signed contracts that pay for standby capacity, and the payments are the price of insurance. A standby contract that pays for readiness is a contract that a treasury can cancel in a year when there is no outbreak.

Technology transfer is the policy instrument. A programme that licenses a manufacturing process to a partner in a lower-income country is faster than building a new process, and it requires the originator to give up some control. The mRNA hub in South Africa is the most visible example, and its progress has been slower than the announcement suggested. A partner that receives a process also receives the audits that come with it, and the audits require a quality system.

What the post-pandemic capacity will be worth depends on the next pathogen. A plant that can fill vials is useful for any vaccine, and a plant that can synthesize mRNA is useful for one class. Countries have invested in both and in different proportions, and the proportions reveal what each government thinks the next threat looks like. A plant that can fill any vaccine is a hedge, and a plant that can synthesize one class is a bet.
