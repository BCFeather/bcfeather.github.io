Parkinson disease is diagnosed by a neurologist watching a patient walk and move, and the diagnosis depends on access to a specialist. A signal that can be measured at home while the patient sleeps is a signal that could be collected before the first appointment. A diagnosis that depends on a specialist is a diagnosis that depends on access, and a home measurement would change who can be assessed.

A model was trained on nocturnal breathing data from 7,671 individuals across several US hospitals and multiple public datasets, and used radio waves reflected from the body to extract breathing without contact. The paper appeared in *Nature Medicine*, 22 August 2022. A radio reflection is a measurement that requires no contact and no device worn on the body, and the contactless quality is the practical advantage, because a sensor that a patient has to wear is a sensor that a patient will stop wearing.

Detection reached disease with an area under the curve of 0.90 on a held-out test set and 0.85 on an external one, and estimated severity and progression in agreement with the Movement Disorder Society rating scale, with a correlation of 0.94. An external test set is the test that matters, because a model that performs on data from the same hospitals has learned the hospitals.
