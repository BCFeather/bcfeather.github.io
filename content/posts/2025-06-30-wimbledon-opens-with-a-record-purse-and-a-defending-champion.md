A fortnight on grass is the part of the tennis calendar that nobody can prepare for properly, and it begins on 30 June with a draw of 128 in each singles event and a queue that forms overnight. Wimbledon is the only major played on a surface that most of the tour visits for three weeks a year, and the result is a champion list that reads unlike the rest of the season.

Alcaraz arrives as the defending champion and as the player who has won the event twice. He had beaten Djokovic in the final in 2024 for a second title, and his game on grass, with a drop shot that a hard court does not reward and a forehand that a low bounce suits, is the closest thing the tour has to a natural fit for the surface.

Sinner arrives as the top seed and as the best player of the season. He had won the Australian Open in January, reached number one in June, and had served a three-month suspension earlier in the year that cost him the spring hard court swing, which makes the seeding a statement about points and not about form. A player who has never been past the semi-finals at Wimbledon is a player with something to prove on the surface he grew up on.

Women's draw is more open by construction. The last decade has produced a different champion almost every year, and the top seed's record at the event is a record of early exits, which is what happens when a surface rewards a serve and a slice that few players practise. A draw of 128 with four or five plausible winners is a draw that a bracket cannot predict.

Money is a record again. The total prize fund is the largest in the sport, the first round pays more than a year on the lower tour, and the allocation has moved towards the early rounds as the organisers have argued that a player who loses in the first round should not lose money by attending. A prize structure is a labour policy.

Grass itself is a managed surface. The courts are sown in the autumn, cut to eight millimetres in the fortnight, and watered to a speed that the committee chooses, and the choice between a fast and a slow court is a choice between a serve-dominated event and a rally. A tournament that controls its own surface controls its own product.

What would settle the fortnight is whether the second week produces a final between two players who have served well enough to hold through the first four rounds. A champion on grass is usually a champion who has not been broken more than twice in a match, and that statistic is the one to watch from the first Monday. A tournament that pays the first round more than a Challenger title is a tournament that has decided the bottom of the draw matters to the product.
