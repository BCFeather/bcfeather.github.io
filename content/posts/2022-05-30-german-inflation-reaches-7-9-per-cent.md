Consumer prices rose 7.9 per cent in the year to May, the statistics office reported on 30 May. The reading was the highest since 1973 and it was above the 7.5 forecast. The harmonized index, which is used for comparison across Europe, rose 8.7 per cent. The figure had been 7.4 per cent in April, and it had doubled in a year.

Energy was the largest part. Households paid 38 per cent more for gas and 30 per cent more for heating oil, and the government's subsidy of 300 euros had not offset the rise. Food rose 11 per cent, and the price of flour rose 40 because of the war in Ukraine. The core rate was 3.4 per cent, and it had been 3.1. Services rose 3.2 per cent, and rents rose 2. The gap between the headline and the core is the reason the European Central Bank was slow to raise rates, and it closed in the second half. Gas cost 90 euros a megawatt hour, and it had been 20 a year earlier.

Wages had begun to rise. The government had introduced a fuel discount of 30 cents a litre at the pump, which took effect in June. It also cut the tax on petrol for three months, and it offered a 9 euro monthly ticket for public transport. The measures cost 30 billion euros. The metal workers' union had won a 6.5 per cent increase in the spring, and the chemical workers 6. The bank said wage growth was 4 per cent, and that it would feed into services prices in 2023. The core rate had been 3.1 per cent in April, and it had doubled since 2021.

Output had grown 0.2 per cent in the first quarter, and the war had cut the forecast. The government cut its growth forecast to 2.2 per cent from 3.6, and it said the gas supply was the largest risk. The chemical industry had cut output 10 per cent. Its chemical industry had cut output 10 per cent, and it said the gas supply was the largest risk.

Rates went up in July for the first time in 11 years, and again in September. Its president said the bank would go as far as needed, and the market priced a peak of 3 per cent. The actual peak was 4 per cent in September 2023.

Inflation stayed above 7 per cent through the summer and reached 8.8 in October. The government's relief package of 200 billion euros was announced in September, and it capped the price of gas for households. The rate fell to 8.6 in December and to 3.3 in June 2023. Negotiated wages had risen 4 per cent, and the bank said they would reach 5. The chemical industry had cut output 10 per cent, and it said it might close plants.
