Salicylic acid is the hormone a plant accumulates when a pathogen arrives, and most of the genes it switches on depend on NPR1, a cofactor that works through the TGA transcription factors once they sit on DNA. The hormone also has to lift a brake: NPR3 and NPR4, its other receptors, keep defence genes shut, and plants lacking both express them without waiting for a signal. Both halves of that picture were in place by 2018. How hormone binding converts NPR1 into an activator, and how it releases the brake, stayed open.

Yujun Peng and Hainan Tian, with Yuelin Zhang at Sichuan University, report the answer in *Nature* on 16 September 2026. A suppressor screen and an interaction screen both landed on MED15A, a subunit of the Mediator complex that sits between transcription factors and RNA polymerase II. The evidence runs from pull-downs of proteins made in *Escherichia coli* to an AlphaFold 3 model, transactivation assays in protoplasts, chromatin immunoprecipitation and infection tests with the downy mildew *Hyaloperonospora arabidopsidis*. Press accounts have described the two branches; the residues below and a companion structure paper are what such accounts leave out.

The interface is narrow. MED15A's KIX domain, modelled as residues 17 to 100, docks on the NPR1 core, residues 34 to 561, and two NPR1 residues carry most of the contact: D428 and R544. Replacing either one, as D428N or R544K, largely removes salicylic-acid-induced reporter activity in protoplasts given 0.2 mM hormone for 3 h (*n* = 3). Plants built on those substitutions lose induction of *SARD1* and *WRKY70*, and the infection assays, spraying \(5\times10^{4}\) spores per millilitre of *Hpa* Noco2 and counting them 7 days later, leave such plants more susceptible than wild type.

Chromatin does the braking. NIMIN1 binds NPR3 and NPR4 and also binds TOPLESS, and the TPL co-repressor recruits PRC2, which methylates histone H3 at lysine 27 across *SARD1*, *WRKY70*, *DMR6* and *NIMIN1* while acetylation stays low. Salicylic acid loosens the grip: in leaves given 0.2 mM hormone the luciferase signal from an NPR3-NIMIN1 pair falls (*n* = 6). In the insensitive *npr4-4D* background H3K27me3 stays high, and removing *TPL*, *TPR1* and *TPR4* brings it down while H3ac rises at the same four loci, against IgG controls in four independent experiments.

Three limits hold the result in place. All of it is *Arabidopsis*, with no crop and no field trial. The hormone is supplied at 0.1 and 0.2 mM, doses a seedling seldom meets during an infection, so the order in which the two branches act remains unmeasured. And the interface rests on an AlphaFold 3 model confirmed by mutation rather than on an experimental structure: the model can name the right residues and still get the geometry wrong.

A second paper published the same day in *Nature* supplies that structure: cryo-electron microscopy at 3.2 Å of the salicylic-acid-bound NPR1-MED15 complex, backed by hydrogen-deuterium exchange. There NIMIN1 takes a second job, occupying the same NPR1 surface that MED15A needs and blocking hormone binding. Read together, the two papers replace a pathway diagram with a surface, and the NPR1 patch where MED15A and NIMIN1 compete becomes the place an agricultural analogue would have to grip.
