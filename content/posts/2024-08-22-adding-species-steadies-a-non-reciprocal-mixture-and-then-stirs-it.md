description: "Random non-reciprocal couplings between many species delay pattern formation and then leave condensates that pulse or never settle."

Non-reciprocity means the influence one species has on another is not matched by the influence it receives. In a mixture described by conserved densities, that asymmetry is written into the couplings of a Cahn-Hilliard model, and it is the source of the travelling patterns such models produce. Nothing in the mixture is created or destroyed, so any structure that appears has to appear by rearranging what is already there.

Parkavousi, Rana, Golestanian and Saha ask what happens when the couplings are random and the number of species is large. Their answer steadies the mixture: pattern formation, which a single pair of species would undergo, is suppressed, and the suppression becomes an exact statement as the number of species grows without bound. Suppressed is the right word, since the instability has not vanished; it has been pushed to a longer wavelength and a later time.

Averaging supplies the mechanism. A pattern has to grow along a direction picked by the couplings, and when those couplings are drawn at random across many species the growth available to any one mode is diluted; the uniform state survives longer than the same mixture with a deliberately chosen asymmetry would allow, with the margin set by how widely the couplings are spread, and in the limit of infinitely many species the statement holds without appeal to a particular realisation of the couplings.

Numerical work on the multi-species model confirms the trend and adds a second finding: the variety of patterns grows with the number of components. Besides the familiar steady condensates, the simulations produce states that pulse in time and states that stay chaotic in both space and time, and which of those appears depends on the strength of the asymmetry rather than on the number of species alone.

Chaotic condensates are the interesting objects. A condensate that never settles is steady only in the statistical sense rather than in any snapshot, and it offers a mechanism by which a mixture of many components could sustain structure without anyone choosing the couplings that produce it. A condensate that pulses is also harder to mistake for an artefact of the initial condition, because it keeps changing after the transient has passed.

Nothing in the paper crosses into biology. Living systems metabolise, and metabolism is a plausible source of non-reciprocal coupling between chemical species, but the calculation says nothing about a particular organism, no measured coupling matrix from a real mixture is inserted anywhere, and no prediction is offered for a real mixture; the exact result applies in the limit of infinitely many species, and finite mixtures are followed numerically.

What the result changes is the intuition about disorder. Random couplings are usually treated as an obstacle to order, and here they are what keeps the uniform state in place while multiplying the patterns that appear once it does break. The preprint was posted to arXiv on 12 August 2024.
