Into the summer the artificial-intelligence industry moved with the phase shift that the year's developments had been building towards, and the shift's components were the ones that the coverage has been tracking through the spring. The models' capabilities have climbed with the releases that the frontier laboratories have shipped, the costs of the frontier training have spiralled with the scale that the releases require, and the questions that the industry has spent years deferring, the energy, the regulation, the deployment, have become the central subjects of the conferences and the earnings calls. The summer will test whether the industry can sustain the pace that the spring set, and the test will be conducted in the releases, the data centres and the regulatory hearings that the season will hold.

Industry coverage leads with the capability race, whose terms the frontier has set. The latest models have demonstrated the advances in the reasoning, the multimodal processing and the tool use that the benchmarks measure, and the advances have been followed, with the particular rhythm that the industry has established, by the claims and the counter-claims about the lead; the race's economics are the part that the analysts have been weighing, the billions that the training runs consume, the data centres that the demand requires, and the revenue that the deployment is only beginning to produce. The capability race is the industry's headline, and the economics are its substance.

Energy has moved to the centre of the industry's planning, and the movement is the summer's most consequential development. The data centres that the models require are consuming the electricity at the scale that has forced the utilities and the governments into the planning that the industry had hoped to defer, and the planning, the new plants, the grid upgrades, the approvals, has become the constraint that the frontier's growth will test; the industry's own investments in the generation, the nuclear and the gas and the renewables, are the recognition that the constraint is now the industry's problem. The energy question will shape the industry's geography and its politics, and the shaping will be visible in the projects that the summer's announcements will carry.

Regulation has moved in parallel, shaped by the year's political events. The frameworks that the institutions have been drafting have advanced through the spring, and the administration's posture towards the industry, the support for the American leadership, the scepticism towards the regulation, has set the terms of the American debate; the European frameworks, the Asian governments' approaches, the global coordination, all of it is being negotiated against the industry's growth, and the negotiations are the summer's regulatory story. The deployment of the models into the workplaces and the public services will test the frameworks, and the testing will be conducted in the incidents and the debates that the year will produce.

Conferences and releases will let the industry perform its own trajectory through the summer, and the performance will be judged against the questions that the year has raised. The capability advances will be demonstrated, the roadmaps will be presented, and the economics will be defended; the energy, the regulation and the deployment will be the subjects that the audiences will press, and the pressing will be the measure of the industry's maturity. The AI race entered the summer with a new phase, the phase where the capabilities' promise meets the costs' reality, and the meeting is the industry's central test; the summer's events will show whether the meeting can be managed, and the showing will be the technology story of the season.
