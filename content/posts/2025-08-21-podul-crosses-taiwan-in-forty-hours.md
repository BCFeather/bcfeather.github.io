Typhoon Podul, the eleventh named storm of the season, formed on 8 August east of the Philippines and was gone from the charts by five in the morning on 15 August. In between, it made two landfalls on the Chinese coast and crossed about 1,300 kilometres in roughly forty hours, an average speed of 30 kilometres an hour that is fast even for a small, tightly wound system.

Landfall came first at about one in the afternoon on 13 August at Taimali in Taitung County, Taiwan, with winds of 42 metres per second and a central pressure of 955 hectopascals. That made it the strongest typhoon to hit the country this year. Its ten-force wind radius was only 80 to 100 kilometres, which is why the damage was concentrated and why the warnings had to be precise. A storm of that size can pass between two counties and leave one of them untouched.

The second landfall came at half past midnight on 14 August at Zhangpu in Fujian, with winds of 30 metres per second and a pressure of 990 hectopascals. It was the first typhoon of the season to reach Fujian, and it arrived after crossing the strait in about eleven hours, weakened by the mountains of the island but still carrying a broad rain shield. The mountains of the island took the energy out of the circulation, and what arrived on the mainland was mostly water. Fujian's rainfall totals were highest in the south, where the terrain lifts the flow, and lowest on the coast where the storm came ashore.

Rain was the main hazard. Dahanshan in Taiwan recorded 648.5 millimetres, and the highest mainland total was 294.9 millimetres at Chancheng in Foshan. Guangdong reported more than 106,000 people moved out of vulnerable areas by the evening of 13 August; Fujian recalled 281 fishing boats and sent 9,438 officials to the coast; Jiangxi and Hunan prepared for the remnants, which is where inland flooding usually happens.

The forecasting problem with a storm like this is time. A fast, compact typhoon gives a province perhaps twelve hours between the moment the track is certain and the moment the wind arrives, which is enough for fishing boats and not much else. That is why the warnings were issued while the storm was still 500 kilometres offshore, and why the response level was raised before the first landfall rather than after it. Guangdong sent warning messages to an estimated 190 million phone numbers through the provincial emergency platform, which is the only channel fast enough for a storm moving at this speed.

Podul weakened to a tropical depression over Guangdong and was de-registered on the 15th, but its moisture kept moving west and north, producing rain in Guangxi, Guizhou and the Sichuan basin through the 17th. That tail is the part that catches people out: the wind is over, the sky is grey, and the rivers are still rising.
