China's economy grew by 6.3 per cent over the year in the second quarter, above the forecast of about 6 per cent and slower than the 6.8 per cent of the first. The quarter-on-quarter growth was 0.8 per cent, which is about half the pace before the pandemic. The first half grew by 5.5 per cent. The figure was the fastest since the spring of 2021 on the year, and it is the last quarter to benefit from the base of the lockdowns of 2022. The economists expect the growth to slow to about 4.5 per cent in the third quarter, and the government's target is about 5 for the year.

Consumption is the weak part. The retail sales rose by about 8.2 per cent in the second quarter, and the two-year average is about 4, which is well below the level of 2019. The households have been saving more and spending less on the discretionary goods, and the surveys show the confidence at the lowest since 2009. The sales of the cosmetics fell by about 3 per cent in June, and the one of the jewellery by about 5. The household saving rate was about 33 per cent in the first quarter, which is about 10 points above the level of 2019.

Property is the drag. The investment in the real estate fell by about 7.9 per cent in the first half, and the new starts by about 24. The sales of the new homes fell by about 5 per cent in June, and the prices in the largest cities fell by about 0.2 on the month. The land sales fell by about 20 per cent in the first half, and the revenues of the local governments have been the part the crisis has hit.

Factories are cooling. The industrial output rose by about 4.4 per cent in June, and the exports fell by about 12 per cent as the demand from the West weakened. The inventories of the manufacturers have been rising, and the price of the steel has fallen by about 10 per cent since the spring. The output of the electric vehicles rose by about 35 per cent, and the one of the cement fell by about 5.

Its government has been cautious. The measures announced since the spring have been small, and the central bank has cut the rates by about 20 basis points. The local governments have been told to support the property, and the spending on the infrastructure has been slow. The central bank has cut the one-year lending rate to about 3.55 per cent, and the five-year to about 4.2.

These figures are the ones the stimulus debate rests on. An economy that grows by 6.3 per cent on a weak base while the property falls is an economy that needs the demand, and the demand is the part the government has not supplied. The government has announced about 1 trillion yuan of the special bonds for the infrastructure, and the spending has been slow to start.
