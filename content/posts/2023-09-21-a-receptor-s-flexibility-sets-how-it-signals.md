A glutamate receptor opens when a ligand binds, and the opening's speed and its duration set the synapse's response. A receptor's structure is static in a crystal, and its motion is what the synapse experiences.

The analysis in *Nature*, September 2023 connects the two. The authors compare structures and dynamics of the GluA receptor's variants, and the differences in mobility explain the differences in signalling.

The mobility is the finding. A variant whose extracellular domain is more flexible opens more slowly, and the flexibility is a property that a structure alone does not show.

The gating is the mechanism. A ligand's binding closes a cleft, and the closure's transmission to the pore depends on the domain's stiffness.

The comparison across subunits is the frame. A receptor assembled from different subunits has a range of kinetics, and the authors' series maps the range onto structural differences.

The pharmacology is the application. A drug that changes the receptor's mobility would change its kinetics, and a modulator that acts on flexibility is a different kind of molecule from one that competes with the ligand.

The measurement is the advance. A structural ensemble from cryo-electron microscopy and from simulation is what the paper uses to characterize mobility, and the ensemble is the data.

The limitation is the reconstitution. A purified receptor in a detergent or a nanodisc is not a receptor in a membrane with its partners.

What the paper does not establish is the synapse's behaviour. A receptor's kinetics in a test tube is an input to a model, and the synapse's response is the model's output.

For neuroscience, the value is a link between a molecule's motion and a circuit's timing. The field's models of synaptic transmission need rate constants, and a structural basis for them is what the paper provides.

The cleft's closure is the first step. A ligand that binds across a hinge closes it, and the closure's energy is what the domain's stiffness determines.

The pore's opening is the second. A closure in the extracellular domain must travel to the transmembrane helix, and a rigid connection makes the travel fast.

The desensitization is the third state. A receptor that stays closed after the ligand binds has entered a desensitized state, and its rate is part of the response's shape.

The simulation is the complement. A molecular dynamics trajectory samples the motion, and the authors' comparison with the structures is the validation.

The subunit composition is the biological variable. A receptor with a particular combination of subunits is expressed in a particular brain region, and the kinetics match the region's needs.

The drug's design is the opportunity. A molecule that binds a flexible interface could stiffen it, and the structural ensemble is the template for the search.
The ligand's identity is the trigger. Different agonists close the cleft to different extents, and the extent's variation is another source of kinetic difference.
The membrane's role is the context. A lipid bilayer exerts a lateral pressure on the protein, and a receptor's gating in a membrane differs from its gating in a detergent.
