A console launch is a bet on a price point and on a piece of software, and Nintendo made both bets on 5 June when the Switch 2 went on sale in most markets. The machine costs 449 dollars on its own and 499 with a copy of Mario Kart World, which is the bundle that most buyers wanted, and the first allocation in Japan was gone within hours.

Hardware is a modest upgrade and a deliberate one. The screen is a 7.9 inch liquid crystal panel with full high definition resolution and support for 120 frames per second, the storage is 256 gigabytes of internal memory, the docked mode reaches 4K through a new dock, and the controllers attach magnetically and double as mice. A handheld that runs last generation's library at a higher frame rate is a handheld that does not need a new library.

Price is the change that matters. The original Switch launched at 299 dollars in 2017 and the OLED model at 349 in 2021, and the new machine arrives at a level that a tariff on Chinese and Vietnamese assembly has made harder to hold. Nintendo moved some production to Vietnam years ago, and the American duty on that country is the variable that decides the price in the second year.

Software is the reason the launch worked. Mario Kart World is a full new game rather than a port, it is the franchise that sells consoles to families, and the bundle puts it in the hands of the buyer at a discount of about 30 dollars against buying separately. A launch lineup of one system seller plus a generation of backward compatible titles is the cheapest way to fill a store shelf.

The competition is different this time. Sony and Microsoft have moved towards subscriptions and towards personal computers, and the handheld market has been invaded by Windows machines and by phones that can stream. Nintendo's advantage is that its software cannot be played anywhere else, and its weakness is that a 449 dollar device competes with a 499 dollar tablet that does more.

Supply chain is the constraint on the forecast. Nintendo said it would sell more in the first year than the original managed, which implies a number near 20 million units, and that number depends on components that are shared with phones and on shipping capacity that a tariff regime can interrupt. A company that has been burned by shortages before has been building inventory.

What would settle the year is whether the install base converts to software sales in the autumn. A console sold at cost is a customer acquired, and the profit arrives with the second and third game, which is the arithmetic that made the original Switch a success after a slow start. A launch that sells out in its home market and sells at a premium in the United States is a launch whose constraint is manufacturing and not demand, which is the better of the two problems.
