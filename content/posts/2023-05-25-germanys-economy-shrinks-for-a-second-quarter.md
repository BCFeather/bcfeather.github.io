Germany's economy contracted by 0.3 per cent in the first quarter, the statistical office said on 25 May, revising the earlier estimate of no change. The previous quarter was revised to a fall of 0.5 per cent, so the two consecutive declines meet the definition of a recession. The output was about 0.5 per cent lower than a year earlier. The revision was small in the numbers and large in the meaning, because the earlier estimate had shown the stagnation. The statistics office said the industrial production had fallen in every month of the quarter, and the construction had been hurt by the weather.

Households cut back. The consumer spending fell by about 1.2 per cent as the energy bills took a larger share of the budgets, and the retail sales have been falling since the autumn. The government's support for the bills has kept the worst of it off the balance sheets, and the spending on the services has held. The saving rate rose to about 11 per cent, which is well above the level of 2019 and the sign of the caution about the bills. The spending on the restaurants and the travel rose, and the one on the furniture and the cars fell as the consumers postponed.

Industry was the weak part of the quarter. The manufacturing output fell by about 2.2 per cent, and the construction by about 3.9 as the higher rates reached the builders. The carmakers have been the strongest, and the orders from the exporters outside Europe rose. The chemical industry cut the output by about 10 per cent, and the energy-intensive producers have been moving the capacity abroad.

Trade added a little. The exports fell less than the imports, so the net trade contributed about 0.2 percentage points to the quarter. The exports to China fell by about 12 per cent, and the demand for the machinery was the weakest. The exports to the United States rose by about 5 per cent, and the ones to the European Union fell by about 2 as the neighbours slowed.

Forecasts have been cut. The government expects about 0.4 per cent of the growth this year, and the institutes have been revising theirs down to about zero. The Bundesbank said the second quarter would be flat at best, and the recovery was not expected before the autumn. The unemployment rate was about 5.6 per cent, and the vacancies have fallen from the record of last year.

This report is the arithmetic of the energy shock. An economy that shrinks by 0.3 per cent while the unemployment stays at about 5.6 per cent is an economy with a supply problem, and the supply is not something the rates can fix. The government has set aside about 200 billion euros for the energy support, and the fund is the part the constitutional court has questioned.
