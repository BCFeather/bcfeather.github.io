title: "The European Parliament approves a migration and asylum pact"
date: "2024-04-11T23:40:00+08:00"
categories: [news]
description: "The European Parliament voted on 10 April for a package of ten laws that creates border screening, a solidarity mechanism for relocations and a common asylum procedure, with implementation due in 2026 and objections from both ends of the chamber."
---

European Parliament approved a package of ten laws on migration and asylum on the tenth of April, after almost a decade of negotiations that began with the arrival of more than a million people in 2015. Votes were taken on each file, and the main regulation on asylum procedures passed with about three hundred votes in favour and two hundred and seventy against. The package becomes law after approval by the member states, which had already agreed the texts, and member states have two years to apply them.

What the pact contains is a set of procedures that did not previously exist at the European level. People arriving without authorization would be screened at the border within seven days, with fingerprinting and checks against databases, and those who apply for asylum would have their claims examined in a procedure that should take no more than six months. Reception conditions would be harmonized, and a new regulation sets out what happens when a member state is under pressure from a sudden arrival of people.

Solidarity mechanism is the politically difficult part. Member states that do not accept relocations would have to pay twenty thousand euros per person into a fund that supports the countries at the external border, and the total number of relocations is set at thirty thousand a year. Countries such as Hungary and Poland have said they will not participate in any relocation scheme, and the rules allow them to buy their way out.

Criticisms from the left were about the effect on people who arrive. Human rights organizations said the screening procedure could amount to detention, that the use of biometric databases without adequate safeguards would put children at risk, and that the crisis regulation would allow member states to lower standards when numbers rise. Several members of the parliament voted against the whole package for those reasons, and the Greens split.

Criticisms from the right were about sovereignty and about numbers. Parties in Hungary, Poland and France said the pact would force them to accept migrants, even though the text allows payments instead of relocations, and they said it would encourage more arrivals. Several governments said they would challenge parts of the package in the European Court of Justice.

Implementation will depend on the budget that the next European Commission proposes, and on the willingness of member states to accept the relocation quotas that the regulation sets out. The commission said it would publish a first assessment of how the rules are applied in 2027. The package replaces a set of emergency arrangements that had been improvised during the arrivals of 2015 and of 2022.

What the tenth of April means is a system that replaces ad hoc responses with rules, at a moment when the political weather has turned against migration. The pact was designed to satisfy both the countries that receive most arrivals and those that refuse to share them, and it does neither completely. Whether it works depends on how the border procedures are run in practice, and on whether the funds that are meant to support them are actually spent.
