Amazon confirmed the layoffs on 16 November, and they were the largest in the company's history. The figure was about 10,000, and it was 3 per cent of the corporate workforce. The cuts fell in the devices unit, in the retail business and in human resources, and they did not touch the warehouse staff. The figure was confirmed in a memo to staff, and the company said the process would run into 2023. The memo said the company had grown faster than its business required.

A hiring freeze in the corporate roles had been announced in early November. The freeze followed a quarter in which revenue missed the forecast and in which the company gave guidance below expectations. The chief executive said the company had hired too quickly during the pandemic.

Hardest hit was the devices unit, where the Alexa voice assistant and the hardware had been sold at cost. The unit lost 5 billion dollars a year, according to documents published later. The company said it would keep the assistant and would cut the teams that developed hardware. The unit had 10,000 employees, and the company said it would keep the Alexa service running.

Warehouse space had doubled during the pandemic. It had doubled the floor space in two years, and the capacity was now underused. The company said it would sublease some of the space and would stop building new warehouses in some regions. The company's headcount was 1.5 million including warehouse staff. The layoffs were aimed at the corporate roles, which numbered 350,000. The company said it would offer severance and would help employees find jobs inside the company where possible. The company had 1,500 warehouses, and it said it would close 10 of them.

Investors said the cuts were overdue and that the company had been slow to respond to the slowdown. The shares rose 1 per cent on the day, and they had fallen 45 per cent in the year. The company's market value had fallen below 1 trillion dollars for the first time since 2020. The cuts were followed by more in January. The company announced 18,000 job reductions in January 2023, which was larger than the November figure. It also said it would close some of its physical bookstores and would slow the expansion of its grocery chain. The severance package was 90 days of pay, and the company said it would extend health cover.

Costs fell in 2023, and the retail margin turned positive in the second quarter. The chief executive said the company had emerged leaner, and he said he expected to hire again in 2024. The shares rose 80 per cent in 2023, and the company passed 2 trillion dollars in value in 2024. The company had been valued at 1.7 trillion dollars in 2021, and the figure was 850 billion in November.
