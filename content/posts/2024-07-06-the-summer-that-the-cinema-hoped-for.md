Summer is the biggest box office window of the year. It runs from the start of July to the end of August, and it carries the films that a studio has saved for a school holiday. A film released in this window can earn several times what the same film would earn in April. The distribution is fixed by a share. A cinema keeps a percentage of the ticket and the distributor takes the rest, and the percentage moves in favour of the cinema after the first fortnight. A film that opens weakly is pulled from the screen in ten days.

Price fell this year. A ticket that cost fifty yuan in a first-tier city cost forty in a promotion. Average across the country was lower than last summer's, by about a tenth. Cheaper tickets did not bring in enough audience to fill the gap that the price cut opened. The screens are the industry's fixed cost. A multiplex built in a shopping centre pays rent on eight auditoriums whether or not they are full. A film that fills two of them for a week pays the rent for the month, which is why a distributor can negotiate a better share from a cinema that is anxious.

A mixed set of films filled the schedule. A comedy about a family, an animation about a cat, a thriller set in a rainstorm. Reviews were not the deciding factor. A film with a strong opening weekend drew the crowd that decides the rest of its run. The audience has changed shape. A family with two children buys four tickets and a bucket of popcorn. That makes one visit worth more than four visits by students. Cinemas have responded by scheduling the animation at ten in the morning, when the shopping centre is empty.

Streaming takes the audience that stays home. A film reaches a platform six weeks after the cinema. A family that waits saves the price of four tickets. Twice in five years the window has shortened. The marketing has moved to the short video. A clip of a single scene is cut and pushed to a phone before the trailer is released. A film that produces a quotable line in the first week earns a second week of screens, because the line is repeated in a million videos.

The cinema is also a property. A multiplex in a shopping centre pays a rent that rises with the footfall of the mall. Footfall of the mall depends on the cinema. Two businesses are tied together, and neither of them can afford to close.

Staff who work the season are paid by the hour and go home at one in the morning. A projectionist who has eight screens to check walks four kilometres inside a building during a shift, which is the part of the job that nobody outside the trade knows about. The season closed with a total below the record of last summer. Industry read it as a sign that the habit of going out had weakened. Autumn releases were scheduled accordingly, with fewer titles and longer runs.
