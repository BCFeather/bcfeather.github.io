China closed 2022 with growth of 3 per cent, the slowest full-year expansion since 1976 apart from the first pandemic year, after a fourth quarter in which output rose 2.9 per cent from a year earlier. Gross domestic product reached 121 trillion yuan, or about 18 trillion dollars at the average exchange rate for the year. Quarterly growth was the weakest of the year apart from the second quarter, when lockdowns in Shanghai closed the port for two months.

Consumption did the damage. Retail sales fell 0.2 per cent over the year, the first annual decline on record, as restrictions kept shoppers at home for much of the spring and autumn. Restaurants and travel were the weakest lines, and the services index for December was still below its level before 2020. Online sales rose 4 per cent while sales through shops fell 3 per cent, a pattern visible since 2020 and sharper last year. Cinema takings in the holiday week were the first test of demand, and they came in at 6.7 billion yuan, above 2021 and below 2019.

Investment held up better than expected. Fixed asset investment rose 5.1 per cent, with infrastructure up 9.4 per cent as local governments pushed through projects approved in the summer. Manufacturing investment rose 9.1 per cent. Property development fell 10 per cent, and land sales fell by nearly a third, which is the number that constrains local budgets this year. Infrastructure spending was concentrated in transport and energy projects in western provinces, where the multiplier runs lower than in coastal cities.

Industrial output rose 3.6 per cent, and exports of goods fell 9.9 per cent in December from a year earlier as demand in Europe and North America cooled. The trade surplus for the year was still large, at 877 billion dollars, which helped the currency hold near 6.9 to the dollar through the autumn. Export volumes to the United States fell 17 per cent in December, and shipments to the European Union fell 12 per cent.

Population figures published the same morning showed 1.41175 billion people at the start of 2023, a fall of 850,000 from a year before and the first annual decline since the 1960s. Births were 9.56 million, the lowest since records began in 1949, and the total fertility rate is now estimated below 1.1. Deaths exceeded births by 1.4 million, and the number of people aged over 60 passed 280 million.

Policy for the year ahead was set at a target of 5 per cent, and economists read the fourth-quarter recovery as evidence that reopening alone would not deliver it. Household savings rose sharply during the restrictions, and whether that stock is spent on services in the first half is the variable most forecasts now turn on. Several provinces have published targets above the national figure, and the sum of local plans usually exceeds the centre's number.
