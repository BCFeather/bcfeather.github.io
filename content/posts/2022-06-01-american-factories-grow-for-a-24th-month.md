New orders were 55.1, and production 54.2. The purchasing managers' index for manufacturing was 56.1 in May, the Institute for Supply Management reported on 1 June. The reading was up from 55.4 in April and it was above the 54.5 forecast. It was the 24th consecutive month above 50. The survey said the sector had grown for 24 months, the longest run since 1998.

Firms said they had 100 days of orders in hand, and that they could not get components. The survey said demand was still strong, and that the problem was supply. The index for supplier deliveries was 65, which means deliveries were slower, and the index for backlogs was 58. Firms said they had 100 days of orders in hand, and that the delivery time was 90 days. The survey said the delivery time was 90 days, and that it had been 60 before the pandemic.

Prices were the concern. The price index was 82, and it had been 84 in April. Firms said the price of steel had doubled in a year, and that the price of electronic parts had risen 30 per cent. They said they had raised their own prices, and that customers had accepted them. Firms said they had never seen such a gap between orders and materials.

Employment was 49, which is below 50, and it was the second month below. Firms said they had cut jobs because they could not get the parts, and that they would hire when supply improved. The sector employed 12.7 million people, and it had 800,000 vacancies. The survey covered 400 firms, and it asks them about orders, production, employment, deliveries and inventories. Its reading is one of the first data points of each month, and it is watched as a sign of the industrial cycle. It is published by a trade group, not by the government. The sector had 800,000 vacancies, and firms said they would hire when parts arrived.

Firms said the weak dollar had helped, and that the demand from Europe was strong despite the war. The export index was 54, and it had risen for three months. The imports index was 52, and it had fallen as the lockdowns in China cut shipments. Inventories were 48, which means firms were drawing them down. Customers' inventories were 33, which is the lowest since the survey began, and firms said their customers had no spare parts. The survey said the shortage would last into 2023. The exports index was 54, and it had risen for three months as the weak dollar helped.

It stayed above 50 through the autumn and fell to 47.6 in November. It reached 46.9 in January 2023, and it stayed below 50 until March. The sector's output fell 1 per cent in the year, and the survey's author said the cycle had turned. Firms said the cycle had turned, and that they expected a slowdown in 2023.
