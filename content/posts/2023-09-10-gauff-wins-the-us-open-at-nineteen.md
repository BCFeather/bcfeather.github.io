Coco Gauff beat Aryna Sabalenka 2-6, 6-3, 6-2 in the final of the US Open on 9 September, winning her first major title at nineteen in front of a home crowd that had waited for a champion of her generation. The title is the first for an American woman at the tournament since Serena Williams won it in 2019, and the youngest since Williams in 1999. The final lasted two hours and six minutes, and the third set was over in 34 minutes as the errors came from the other side.

Serving turned the match. Gauff lost the first four games and then held serve for the rest of the second set, and she broke Sabalenka four times in the third as the Belarusian's double faults multiplied. A serve that holds is a final that turns. She served at about 70 per cent of the first balls in the last two sets, up from 45 in the first, and she won 80 per cent of the points when the first serve landed.

Home crowd was part of it. The stadium was full of the teenagers who had followed her since she reached the fourth round at fifteen, and the noise after each point rose with the score. A crowd that is with a player is a player who plays with a crowd. The tickets for the final were reselling at about 400 dollars, and the television audience was the largest for a women's final in the tournament's history.

A coaching change was the year's work. Gauff hired Brad Gilbert and Pere Riba in the summer and moved the serve, and her results improved on the hard courts she had struggled on. A coach who fixes a stroke is a season that changes. The serve had been her weakness for two years, and the change cut the double faults from about seven a match to three.

Sabalenka had won the Australian Open in January and was ranked second going into the final, and the title here would have been her second major of the year. A second major that slips away is a season that remains the best of her career. Sabalenka made a dozen double faults in the final and hit 32 winners, and she said afterwards that the occasion had got to her in the third set. Sabalenka said the crowd had been fair and loud, and she praised the level of the third set.

What the fortnight shows is that the women's game has a champion who is nineteen and a rivalry that is likely to last a decade, and the decade is what the tour has been missing. The next major is in Melbourne in January, and the two are seeded to meet in the later rounds, which the tour will be advertising. A champion of nineteen is a champion the tour can build on, and the building has already started.
