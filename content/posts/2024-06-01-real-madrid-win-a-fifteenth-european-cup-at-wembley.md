Real Madrid beat Borussia Dortmund 2-0 at Wembley on 1 June to win the European Cup for the fifteenth time, with goals from Dani Carvajal and Vinicius Junior in the last quarter of an hour. Dortmund had the better of the first hour and did not score. The goals came in the seventy-fourth and eighty-third minutes, and the second was a cross that the German defence failed to clear rather than a move that the Spanish side constructed.

Finishing is the whole difference in a final. The German side had four chances in the first half and took none of them, and the Spanish side had two in the second and took both, which is the statistic that the coaches on the losing bench described afterwards. A team that converts its chances is a team that does not need to dominate. The expected goals figure at half time favoured Dortmund by more than a goal, which is the statistic that the analysts cite when they argue that finals are decided by finishing rather than by control.

Squad is younger than the one that won the previous edition. Several of the players who started were signed in the last two seasons, and the midfield that controlled the second half had an average age of twenty-three. A rebuild that wins immediately is a rebuild that the club can afford to describe as a transition. The signings have been financed by the sale of a share of the club's commercial revenue, and the arrangement has given the squad a depth that the domestic rivals cannot match.

Coach's record is the context. He has now won the competition twice in three years and has lost one league title in the same period, and the club's supporters have generally judged him on the European record rather than on the domestic one. A club that defines itself by one trophy is a club that tolerates inconsistency elsewhere. The supporters have tolerated a league campaign that was lost to a club that had not won the title in a decade, because the European record is the one the membership votes on.

Dortmund's season is the counterweight. The side finished fifth in its league and reached the final through a route that avoided the strongest clubs, and the money from the run will fund a squad that it could not otherwise afford. A run that is unexpected is revenue that is unplanned. The revenue from the run will be worth more than the club's annual sponsorship income, and the board has said it will be spent on players rather than on the stadium.

What the final shows is that a competition played over thirteen matches rewards a team that can be ordinary for an hour and decisive in ten minutes, and the record now sits with a club that has made that habit. A competition that is won by the same club more often than not is a competition whose format is now being questioned by the others, and the questions have not produced a change.
