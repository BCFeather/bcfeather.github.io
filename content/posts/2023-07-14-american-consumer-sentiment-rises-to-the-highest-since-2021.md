---

Consumer sentiment rose to 72.6 in July from 64.4 in June, according to the University of Michigan, the fifth consecutive gain and the highest since September 2021. The reading was above the forecast of about 65.5. The measure of the current conditions was about 77.5, and the one of the expectations about 69.6. The figure was the highest since September 2021, and the index has risen by about 30 per cent from the trough of last summer. The survey has been running since 1946, and the gap between the parties is the largest it has recorded. The survey's authors said the respondents had mentioned the prices of the food and the rent as the two burdens, and the petrol as the relief.

Inflation expectations fell. The one-year expectation dropped to about 3.4 per cent from 3.3, and the five-year to about 3.1. The respondents said the petrol prices had been the reason, and the grocery bills were still the complaint. The expectation for the petrol price was about 3.20 dollars a gallon, which is about 10 per cent below the level of a year ago. The respondents said the food prices had risen by about 15 per cent, which is the highest complaint in the survey.

Buying conditions improved. The measure for the durables rose by about 12 points, and the one for the vehicles by about 15 as the prices fell and the incentives returned. The housing measure was the weakest, and the mortgage rates were the reason. The measure for the appliances rose by about 20 points, and the one for the furniture by about 10.

Income expectations rose. The respondents expected the income to grow by about 3.4 per cent, which is the highest since 2021, and the one for the younger households by about 5. The concerns about the job losses fell to the lowest since the spring of 2022. The expectation for the unemployment rate was about 4.2 per cent, and the one for the stock market was about flat.

A partisan gap remains. The sentiment of the Democrats was about 98, and the one of the Republicans about 50, which is the widest gap in the history of the survey. The independent voters were about 65. The survey of about 400 households has been the subject of the criticism for the small sample, and the economists use the trend rather than the level.

This report is the one the soft landing rests on. A consumer who expects the inflation at 3.4 and the income at 3.4 is a consumer who thinks the worst is over, and the thinking is the part the spending follows. The retail sales report of 18 July is the one that will show whether the sentiment reached the tills. The spending has held up through the year, and the sentiment and the retail sales have diverged, which is the puzzle the economists have been trying to explain.
