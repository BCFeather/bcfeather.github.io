Carlos Alcaraz beat Daniil Medvedev 6-3, 6-2, and he had not lost a set in the six matches he played. The final was played on 19 March in California and lasted 71 minutes. The title was his third at the Masters level and his first in the United States. The win returned him to number one in the rankings, a position he had held for 20 weeks the previous year. He became the youngest player to win the event since it began in 1974, and the first Spaniard since 2009.

Medvedev had won 19 matches in a row before the final, including three against top-ten opponents. He had beaten Alcaraz in their previous meeting, on hard courts in the autumn, in four sets. This time he won 12 points on his second serve and was broken five times. He said afterwards that the ball had been too fast for him to set up his defence. Medvedev had won three titles in three weeks before arriving in California, and he had played 14 matches.

Alcaraz served at 72 per cent of first serves and won 80 per cent of those points. He came to the net 24 times and won 19 of them, which is unusual for a clay-court player of his age. His coach said the plan had been to shorten the points, because the conditions were dry and the ball was flying. He won 61 per cent of his return points in the final, which is well above his average for the season.

Two of the top four withdrew with injuries, and the fourth retired in the quarter-final. The tournament had been expanded to 96 players in 2023, and the men's draw took 12 days. Attendance for the fortnight was 450,000, the highest in the event's history, and the prize money was 8.8 million dollars. The courts had been resurfaced in 2022, and several players said they played faster than before.

Alcaraz had missed the Australian Open with a leg injury and had returned in February. He won in Buenos Aires and reached the final in Rio, and the Indian Wells title was his second of the year. He was 19 years and 10 months old, and he had won six titles by then, the same number as Rafael Nadal at that age. He had withdrawn from the Australian Open in December, and he said the injury had taken eight weeks to heal.

Alcaraz took the position in January, lost it in February, and regained it in March. The rankings changed at the top three times in the first quarter. The player he displaced had held it for 24 weeks and had won the Australian Open in the same period. The rankings are calculated over 52 weeks, and the top three had changed positions nine times since January. He then won in Barcelona and Madrid, and he arrived at the French Open as the second seed. The next Masters event was in Miami, where Alcaraz lost in the semi-final to a player ranked 50th. Medvedev did not win another title on hard courts until the autumn.
