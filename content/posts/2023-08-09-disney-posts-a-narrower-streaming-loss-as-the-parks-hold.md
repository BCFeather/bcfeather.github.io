---

Disney reported a revenue of 22.3 billion dollars for the quarter to June, up about 4 per cent, and a net loss of 460 million. The loss per share was 0.25 dollars, and the adjusted earnings of 1.03 beat the forecast of 0.95. The shares rose about 2 per cent, and the company announced the price rises for the streaming plans. The revenue was about 300 million above the estimate, and the operating income was about 3.6 billion. The company said the restructuring charge was about 200 million dollars, and the total for the programme is about 4 billion.

Streaming lost less. The direct-to-consumer business lost about 512 million dollars, down from about 1.1 billion a year ago, and the company said the loss would be about 400 million in the current quarter. The Disney+ subscribers fell by about 11.7 million to 146 million, which is the fourth quarter of decline. The Hulu subscribers were about 48 million, and the ESPN+ about 26, and the bundle was the part that grew. The average revenue per the Disney+ user rose by about 5 per cent, and the one of the ad tier by about 3.

Its parks held. The operating income from the parks and the experiences was about 2.4 billion dollars, up about 13 per cent, and the revenue about 8.3 billion. The domestic parks were the strongest, and the international ones were about flat as the French park was hit by the strikes. The cruise line was the fastest part of the experiences, and the one of the hotels was about flat. The company said the per capita spending in the parks rose by about 4 per cent, and the attendance was about flat.

Its studio was weaker. The revenue fell by about 5 per cent, and the operating income was about 240 million dollars as the releases were smaller. The company said the strike would delay the films of 2024, and the slate would be thinner. The company said the studios had about 1 billion dollars of the content spending pulled into the next year.

Prices rose again. The company raised the price of the ad-free Disney+ by about 27 per cent to 13.99 dollars a month, and the one of the Hulu by about 20. The management said the advertising tier had been growing, and the revenue per the user would rise. The company said the streaming business would be profitable in the quarter to September, which is a year earlier than planned.

This quarter is the one in which the streaming business reached the turn. A company that cuts the streaming loss by half while the subscribers fall is a company that has chosen the price over the volume, and the choice is the one the investors have demanded. The shares are down about 5 per cent this year, and the company is the worst performer in the large media group.
