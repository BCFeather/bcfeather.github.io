Apple has spent a decade arguing that a phone should be one slab of glass, and the argument held while the slab got better every year. The foldable category grew anyway, first as a curiosity with a visible crease, then as a product that Chinese manufacturers refined while Western brands watched. A folding iPhone is therefore a concession to a market that exists and a statement that the company now intends to compete in it. The concession is easier to make now that the mechanical problems have been solved by other companies over five generations of hardware.

iPhone Duo was announced on 9 September and opens to a 7.6-inch inner display with a nano-textured surface intended to cut glare, closing to a 5.4-inch cover screen that offers about 90 per cent of the display area of an iPhone 18 Pro. Both screens share an aspect ratio so that content scales between them without reflow, which is the detail that decides whether an app feels native on either surface.

The hardware is built around the hinge and the heat. A polished grade 5 titanium body carries a precision hinge, and the chip is the same A20 Pro used in the Pro phones, paired with a vapour chamber and a dual-cell battery architecture. A folding phone has less room for a battery and more surface to cool, so the two constraints interact, and Apple's answer is to split the cells across the two halves. Dual cells also let the phone balance weight across the hinge, which is the ergonomic problem that ruins most foldable designs.

Software is where the form has to earn its price. iOS 27 is described as redesigned to accommodate the range of interactions a foldable allows, and the camera system uses the hinge to enable positions a slab cannot hold. Multi-tasking is the obvious use, and the honest question is whether a 7.6-inch screen changes what people do or merely enlarges what they already did.

Price sets the audience. The device starts at 15,999 yuan with 256 gigabytes of storage and runs to two terabytes, which places it above every other phone in the lineup. Pre-orders open on 16 October and sales on 23 October, a month after the Pro phones, which suggests the supply chain is still being qualified rather than that the product is an afterthought.

The strategic risk is that the category's economics are unproven. Foldables sell in small volumes at high margins, repairs are expensive, and the hinge is a wear item on a device people open hundreds of times a month. Apple's entry will be judged on durability as much as on the screen, and the first year of warranty data will say more than the launch reviews. Repair networks are the hidden cost in this category, since a hinge replacement can approach the price of a mid-range phone.
