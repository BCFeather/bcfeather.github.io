Roger Federer announced his retirement on 15 September in a letter posted on social media, and he said the Laver Cup in London would be his last event. The letter cited his knee, which had been operated on three times since 2020, and the difficulty of returning at 41. He had not played a competitive match in fourteen months. The farewell came in a doubles rubber on the second night of the team event. Federer partnered Rafael Nadal against the Americans Jack Sock and Frances Tiafoe, and the pair lost 4-6, 7-6, 11-9 in the match tie-break. The score mattered less than the crowd, which stayed for the ceremony that followed and sang until the lights went down.

His record was 20 Grand Slam singles titles, the most of any man when he retired and the third most now. He won Wimbledon eight times, the Australian Open six, the US Open five and the French Open once. He reached 31 major finals and won 103 titles, second only to Jimmy Connors. He held the world number one ranking for 310 weeks, including 237 in a row.

He played with one hand on the backhand, and he moved without noise, and he made the court look larger than it is. The style was the reason he was liked beyond tennis. Coaches copied his forehand and his serve, and the players who followed him said he had changed what was possible. His rivals said the same thing in different words.

His business interests were unusual for a tennis player. He had a long contract with Nike that ended in 2018 when he moved to Uniqlo for 300 million dollars over ten years. He held a stake in On, the Swiss shoe company, which was worth more than 300 million dollars at its listing. He earned 130 million dollars in endorsements in 2022, more than any active player.

Television audiences for the final were the highest in the event's history. The retirement changed the calendar. The Laver Cup had been his idea, and it was run by his management company, and the event sold out within hours of the announcement. The other three members of the big four were in the building, and the tournament said it would keep the format without him.

He said afterwards that he would stay in tennis in some form and that he did not want to coach. He played exhibition matches in 2023 and appeared at Wimbledon as a spectator. The Swiss federation said it would name a court after him, and the offer was accepted in the spring. The numbers are unlikely to be repeated. A player who wins 20 majors across three surfaces and stays in the top ten for eighteen years requires a kind of durability that the modern schedule makes difficult. His last match was a doubles loss, and he said that was the way he wanted it, because the result had never been the point.
