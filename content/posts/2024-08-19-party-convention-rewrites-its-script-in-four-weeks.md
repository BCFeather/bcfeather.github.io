The Democratic convention opened in Chicago on 19 August, four weeks after the party's candidate had changed, and the programme had been rewritten around a nominee whom the delegates had not chosen in a primary. The outgoing president spoke on the first night, and the party treated the event as both a farewell and a launch. The hall had been booked and the delegates had been elected for a candidate who was no longer running. The programme had already been printed when the candidate changed, and the reprint was the first sign of how much would be rewritten.

Logistics of the rewrite were considerable. A hall that had been built around a different candidate's biography was reconfigured, and the speakers were resequenced so that the transition from the old ticket to the new one was the story of the week. A convention is a television production, and the running order is the script. A speech written for one nominee was rewritten to praise the record and then to move past it.

Party's divisions were visible and managed. A faction that had opposed the outgoing president's policy on one war was given a speaking slot and no vote on the platform, and the compromise was a plank that described the war in language that both sides could accept. A plank that satisfies both sides is a plank that satisfies neither. One faction had threatened to interrupt the proceedings, and the speaking slot was the price of its silence.

Mood was relief rather than enthusiasm. Delegates who had spent the spring defending an unpopular incumbent described the new ticket as a chance to compete, and the polling that was released during the week showed the race tightening. A party that has replaced its candidate is a party that has to reintroduce itself. Polling released during the week showed the new ticket ahead in states the party had written off in June.

Chicago was also a security decision. A city that had hosted a convention in 1968 that became a riot prepared with a perimeter and a protest zone, and the demonstrations outside were smaller than the ones that had been expected. A convention that is prepared for its own history is a convention that has learned. The designated area for demonstrators was placed a mile from the hall, and the crowd that used it was smaller than expected.

What the week shows is that a party can hold a convention for a candidate it did not nominate and make the improvisation look deliberate. The script was new and the machinery was old, and the machinery is what will be tested in the autumn. The field operation that will be tested was inherited from the president's campaign, and it was built for a different candidate. A party that has spent a year building one operation is a party that discovers how much of it was built around a person.
