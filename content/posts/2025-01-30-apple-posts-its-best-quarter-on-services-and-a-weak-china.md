Apple's fiscal first quarter covers the holiday season and it is the quarter in which the company sells the most hardware. The results published on 30 January were for the three months to 28 December, and they set records for revenue and for earnings per share, which is what the company had guided the market to expect.

Revenue was 124.3 billion dollars, 4 per cent above the same quarter a year earlier. Diluted earnings per share were 2.40 dollars, 10 per cent higher, and the company returned more than 30 billion dollars to shareholders in the quarter through dividends and buybacks. The margin story is the one that analysts watched, because a mix that shifts towards services raises the gross margin and a mix that shifts towards hardware lowers it.

Services were the record. The segment that includes the App Store, subscriptions, advertising and payments grew at a rate above the company average and reached an all-time high, and it is the segment that carries a margin near 70 per cent, which is why a percentage point of services growth is worth more than a percentage point of iPhone growth. A company that sells a phone once and a subscription every month has a revenue base that is more predictable than the hardware cycle, and the market has valued that base at a multiple it never gave the hardware.

Greater China was the weakness. Revenue in the region fell 11 per cent, which the company attributed to competitive pressure and to the absence of Apple Intelligence in Chinese, a feature that requires a partnership with a domestic provider and that had not launched. The Chinese market is the one where the local manufacturers have caught up on hardware and where the software differentiation matters most.

iPhone revenue was close to flat, which in a quarter with a new model is a sign that the upgrade cycle has lengthened. The installed base is above two billion devices, and a company that earns from services on that base does not need the hardware to grow quickly; it needs the base to stay and the services revenue per user to rise. That is the model the results describe.

The guidance for the March quarter was modest, and the stock moved little. Apple does not give a revenue range any more, and the commentary on the call pointed to currency and to the timing of product launches. A company of this size is judged on the direction of the services margin and on the Chinese share, and both were mixed in this quarter.

What the quarter shows is a company in transition without a visible break. The hardware business is mature, the services business is growing and the intelligence features that are supposed to drive the next upgrade cycle were not available in the largest market in the report. The next two quarters are where the answer will appear.
