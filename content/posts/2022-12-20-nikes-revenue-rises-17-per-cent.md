Nike published results for the quarter to November on 20 December, and revenue rose 17 per cent to 13.3 billion dollars. The figure was above the 12.5 billion forecast, and it was the fastest growth in a year. Net income was 1.3 billion, and the gross margin was 42.9 per cent. The quarter was the second in the company's fiscal year, and revenue for the half was 26 billion.

Inventory was the number the market watched. It was 9.3 billion dollars, up 43 per cent over the year but down from the 44 per cent reported in September. The company said it had cleared part of the excess through discounts, and it said the rest would be cleared by the spring. The shares rose 12 per cent the next day. The company said 40 per cent of the inventory was in its own stores and the rest was with partners.

Sales in North America rose 30 per cent, and in Europe 17. China fell 3 per cent, which was an improvement on the 16 per cent fall in the previous quarter. The company said the lockdowns had ended and that stores had reopened, and it said it expected growth in China in the next year. The company's gross margin was 42.9 per cent, down 3 points, and it said discounts cost 2 points. It said the shift would take two years and would cost 1 billion dollars in severance.

Discounts cut 2 points from the gross margin, and freight costs cut 1. The direct-to-consumer business grew 25 per cent and was 43 per cent of revenue. The company had been cutting wholesale accounts, and it said the shift would continue. It also said its membership programme had 160 million members, up 30 per cent. The margin was the weak part. The company said it had raised prices 5 per cent in the year and that further increases were planned. It said the margin would recover in the second half of 2023.

Guidance was for revenue growth in the high single digits for the year, and the market had expected 10 per cent. The company said the strong dollar would take 4 points off reported growth. It said earnings per share would be 3.20 to 3.30, and the forecast was 3.30. The company's shares had fallen 35 per cent in the year before the results, and they rose 12 per cent after them. The inventory problem had been the largest concern for investors, and the improvement in the number was the reason for the rally. The company said the discounts would continue through the spring.

Revenue for the year was 51 billion dollars, up 10 per cent, and the margin was 43 per cent. It announced a $4 billion buyback in 2023 and raised the dividend 15 per cent. The inventory was back to normal by the summer, and the shares ended 2023 up 10 per cent.
