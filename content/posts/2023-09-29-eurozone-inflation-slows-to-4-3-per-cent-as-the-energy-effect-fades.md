title: "Eurozone inflation slows to 4.3 per cent as the energy effect fades"

description: "The flash estimate for September was the lowest in two years, and the core measure fell to 4.5 per cent as the services and the food prices cooled."
---

Eurozone consumer prices rose 4.3 per cent in the year to September, the flash estimate said on 29 September, down from 5.2 per cent. The core measure fell to 4.5 per cent from 5.3. The figure was in line with the forecast, and the measure is now less than half its peak of 10.6 per cent a year earlier. The figures cover the month of September and were collected before the latest rise in the crude prices reached the pumps.

Energy is no longer the story. The line fell by about 4 per cent over the year as the gas prices settled below the peaks of last summer, and the contribution has turned from a large positive to a small negative. A base effect that has passed is an inflation that returns to the services. The gas prices are about 40 per cent below their level of last summer, and the electricity has followed, which is the reason the energy line is negative. The energy prices are now a drag on the measure for the first time since the war began, and the analysts expect the effect to persist into the winter.

Services are now the engine. The prices rose by about 4.7 per cent, and the holidays and the restaurants were the strongest, which is the part that follows the wages. A service that is priced by the hour is a price that the labour market sets. The wages have been rising by about 5 per cent under the collective agreements, and the bank has said the services will not fall until they slow.

Food is cooling. The food, alcohol and tobacco rose by about 8.8 per cent, down from 9.8, and the fresh vegetables came down as the harvest arrived. A grocery line that falls is a measure that the households notice before the statistics do. The food prices are still about twice the pace of the target, and the analysts expect the line to keep falling through the winter as the input costs ease.

Countries diverge. The inflation in Germany was 4.3 per cent, in France 5.6 and in Spain 3.2, and the differences reflect the energy contracts and the weight of the tourism in the basket. An average that hides a range is a rate that fits none of the members exactly. The eurozone economy is expected to grow by about 0.5 per cent this year, and the surveys show the industry in a recession while the services hold up. The largest four economies account for about three quarters of the index, and the divergence between them has widened this year.

What the month shows is that the disinflation has reached the core, and the pace from here will be set by the wages rather than by the fuel. A core at 4.5 per cent is a core that the bank cannot ignore, and the ignoring is what the market has been asking it to do.
