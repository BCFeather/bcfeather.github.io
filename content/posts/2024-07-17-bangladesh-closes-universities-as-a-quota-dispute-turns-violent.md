Bangladesh closed its universities indefinitely in mid-July after clashes between students and security forces over a quota of government jobs reserved for the descendants of freedom fighters. The dispute had been running for weeks and turned lethal when the police used tear gas and, in some districts, live ammunition. A dispute that begins on a campus is a dispute that has been building in the labour market for a decade.

Quota is a small share of a large prize. Government employment is scarce and secure in a country where the private sector does not offer either, and a reservation that reduces the open competition is a reservation that the majority of graduates experience as a personal loss. A job that is allocated by inheritance is a job that the exam cannot win. A reservation that covers a third of the posts is a reservation that a graduate experiences as a rival with a shorter path. The share reserved for the descendants of fighters had been reduced and then restored by the courts, and the uncertainty is what the students objected to.

Politics is the part that the students named. The ruling party had abolished the quota and a court had restored it, and the students argued that the restoration was a decision taken for the party's base rather than for the country. A court that intervenes in an allocation is a court that becomes a political actor. A court that restores a policy is a court that has taken a decision the government had already made.

Economy is the background. Growth has been strong and employment for graduates has not kept pace, and a large cohort that has done everything the system asked and has no job is a cohort that has nothing to lose by protesting. A demographic bulge is a bulge that arrives in the labour market at a fixed date. A growth rate that is celebrated in a statistical bulletin is a growth rate that a graduate cannot spend. The garment industry employs millions of young women, and the graduates who were protesting were the ones who had expected a different path.

Response hardened the dispute. A shutdown of campuses removes the place where a compromise could be negotiated, and a curfew and a communications restriction turn a student argument into a national one. A government that closes the universities is a government that has decided the argument cannot be contained. A curfew that is imposed on a city of twenty million is a curfew that closes the businesses the students were protesting about.

What the episode shows is that a quota is a question about who the state belongs to. A country that reserves posts for a founding generation is a country that has not decided how the next generation enters, and the students were asking that question with rocks. A question about who the state belongs to is a question that a quota answers badly in either direction.
