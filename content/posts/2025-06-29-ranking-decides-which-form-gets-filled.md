The application form asks a candidate to list universities and programmes in a sequence, and the province's matching algorithm assigns a place by working down the list until it finds one where the score clears the cutoff. The algorithm is published, deterministic and unforgiving. Rankings are published as a table of cumulative numbers, and a candidate reads a column rather than a sentence. The number of places is fixed months in advance, so the table is a map of a queue rather than of a market.

Key documents are not brochures but ranking tables. A province publishes the number of candidates at each score, and the cumulative table converts a score into a position. A university's cutoff in the previous year is a position, and the position is what a family compares with its own.
