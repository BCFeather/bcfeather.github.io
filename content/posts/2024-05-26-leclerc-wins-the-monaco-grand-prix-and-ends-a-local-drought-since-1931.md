Charles Leclerc won the Monaco Grand Prix on 26 May from pole position, leading every lap of a race that was interrupted once by a crash. He is the first driver from the principality to win the event since 1931. The win came at the sixth attempt from pole, and the driver had retired from the race twice in the previous four years while leading.

Circuit is the reason the race looks like a procession. The track is narrow, the walls are close and there is no run-off, so a driver who leads into the first corner has an advantage that the car behind cannot use its speed to overcome. A circuit that cannot be overtaken on is a circuit that is decided on Saturday. The teams also use the weekend to test the low-speed aerodynamic packages that only two circuits require, and the data they collect is worth more than the points.

Strategy is a gamble every year. A safety car that arrives at the wrong moment can hand the lead to a driver who has not yet changed tyres, and the teams spend the race calculating which window protects them. A race that is decided by chance is a race that the strategists treat as a probability problem. The tyre change that decides the race is usually made under the safety car, and the teams that have the faster pit crew gain a place without overtaking anyone.

Cars are too large for the track, which is the argument the sport has been having for a decade. The drivers have said the circuit has become a place where contact is unavoidable, and the organizers have resisted changes because the setting is the product. A venue that is unique is a venue that can refuse to adapt. The organizers have considered extending the track into the harbour, and the engineering has never been agreed, which is why the layout has barely changed in forty years.

Championship is now close, and the win moved the driver within a handful of points of the leader, which is the context in which the result will be judged at the end of the year. A single win at a circuit that suits the car is worth less than it looks in the table. The championship has six drivers within forty points, and the next circuits reward different cars, which is why a single result is treated as provisional.

What the afternoon shows is that a race can be decided before it starts, and the sport sells the spectacle of a place where the penalty for a mistake is the wall rather than the position. A race that is decided by the grid is a race that the sport keeps for its setting, and the setting is the only part of the weekend that nobody has tried to change. The circuit has changed less than any other on the calendar, and the race that produces no overtaking is the one that the drivers still want to win.
