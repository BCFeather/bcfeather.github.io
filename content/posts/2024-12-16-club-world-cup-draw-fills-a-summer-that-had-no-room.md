FIFA drew thirty-two clubs from six confederations into a tournament that will run for a month in the United States next summer, at a ceremony in Miami in early December. The competition is the first of its kind at this size, and the calendar it occupies is the part that has drawn objection. A tournament that a governing body invents is a tournament that the clubs did not ask for and will enter. A competition invented by a governing body is one that the clubs did not request and will enter anyway.

Slots are the argument. A player at a leading European club already plays a domestic league, a domestic cup, a continental competition and international matches, and a month-long tournament in June removes the only extended break in the year. A union that represents those players has said the schedule is unsafe, and the union's case is built on workload data. A break that is removed is a break that a physiotherapist cannot put back. A break removed from the calendar is a break that a physiotherapist cannot restore.

Money is the reason the tournament exists. A thirty-two team event with a global broadcast deal and sponsorship packages is a product that FIFA can sell separately from the World Cup, and the clubs that participate receive a share. A club that earns a payment for appearing is a club that will appear. A broadcast deal is a contract that pays whether or not the stadiums fill. A payment for appearing is a payment that a club budgets in advance.

Qualifying rules spread the places across confederations. Europe receives twelve, South America six, and the rest are distributed among Asia, Africa and North America, which means a club from a smaller league can qualify by winning its continental competition. A tournament with a wide entry is a tournament that sells in more markets. A club from a smaller league is a club that brings a market the sponsor wants. A club from a smaller league brings a market that the sponsor wants to reach.

American venue is a decision about the next decade. A country that will host the World Cup in 2026 is a country that is testing stadiums and transport a year early, and a tournament that fills those stadiums in June is a rehearsal that pays for itself. A stadium that is used a year early is a stadium whose defects are found in time. A stadium used a year early is a stadium whose defects are found before the tournament that matters.

What the draw establishes is that the football calendar is now a negotiation between governing bodies rather than a schedule. A competition added by one body imposes costs on leagues and players represented by others, and those bodies have begun to litigate the conflict in public. A conflict between bodies is a conflict that the courts will eventually price.
