Intel published results on 27 October, and revenue fell 20 per cent to 15.3 billion dollars. Net income fell 85 per cent to 1 billion, and the gross margin fell from 56 per cent to 46. The company had warned in July that the quarter would be difficult, and the figures were worse than the warning. The company's revenue had fallen in every quarter of the year, and the decline accelerated in the last two.

Guidance was cut again. The company said revenue for the next quarter would be 14 to 15 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 17. It also said the gross margin would be 45 per cent, and it withdrew the full-year forecast. The shares fell 8 per cent the next day, and they had fallen 50 per cent in the year.

Shipments fell 19 per cent in the quarter, and Intel's client business fell 17 per cent. The personal computer market was the main cause. The company said inventories at computer makers were still high and that the holiday season would be weak. It expected the market to shrink 10 per cent for the year. The data centre business fell 27 per cent, and that was the number analysts focused on. The company had lost share to AMD and to the chips that cloud companies design themselves. Its new server chip had been delayed by a year, and it shipped in the quarter, and the ramp was slow. Its server market share fell 4 points in the year, and AMD gained all of it. Its client segment was 60 per cent of revenue, and the server segment was 30.

Building factories for other companies was the company's new strategy, and it is a capital-intensive one. It said it would spend 20 billion dollars on plants in Ohio and in Germany, and it delayed both projects. The chief executive said the plan was unchanged and that the timing would depend on demand. The cost cuts were announced with the results. The company said it would cut 3 billion dollars a year by 2025, and it would reduce its workforce by 20 per cent in some units. It also said it would stop paying dividends above the minimum, and it suspended buybacks.

Its rivals had overtaken it in market value: 120 billion dollars at the end of the year against 400 billion for NVIDIA. The company said it would report a loss in the next quarter, which it did, and the shares fell to their lowest level since 2009 in the autumn.

Recovery took two years. Revenue fell 16 per cent in 2023, and it returned to growth in 2024 as the personal computer market recovered and as its new server chips shipped. The foundry business signed its first large customer in 2024, and the company said the plan was on schedule. Its shares recovered 30 per cent in 2023 as the personal computer market stabilized.
