Three interstellar objects in eight years is a rate that the surveys can now resolve, and the third, designated 3I/ATLAS after the asteroid impact warning system that found it, was announced on 1 July with a path that is hyperbolic from the first orbit.

A hyperbolic orbit is the proof of origin. An object bound to the Sun travels on an ellipse, an object passing through travels on a hyperbola with an eccentricity greater than one, and the measured eccentricity here is well above that threshold, which means it came from the interstellar medium and will leave it. A path that is hyperbolic on the first orbit is a path that cannot be explained by a perturbation from a planet, and the measurement of that eccentricity is the entire basis for calling the object interstellar.

Physical measurements are early and provisional. The nucleus is small, the activity is modest, and the coma shows gas that includes water and carbon compounds, which is the composition that makes a comet a comet and that also makes it a sample of the chemistry of another planetary system.

Timing is the part that decides how much can be learned. Perihelion falls in the autumn, the closest approach to Earth comes in December at a distance of about two astronomical units, and every instrument that can point at it will be pointed at it through that window. A nucleus that small is a nucleus that a survey can see only when it is close and active, and the window in which the composition can be measured is therefore a few weeks on either side of perihelion.

The comparison with the first two is the scientific value. 'Oumuamua in 2017 was silent and oddly shaped, Borisov in 2019 was an ordinary comet from another system, and this one is a comet with a measured composition at a distance where a telescope can resolve it, which fills the gap between the first two objects and gives the statistics a third point.

Surveys are the reason the rate has risen. ATLAS, Pan-STARRS and the forthcoming Rubin observatory scan the sky often enough that a fast, faint object is caught within days of brightening, and a survey that finds three in eight years implies that the population is large and that the next decade will find dozens. A survey that finds three interstellar objects in eight years implies a population of perhaps a few thousand per cubic astronomical unit, and the uncertainty in that number is the uncertainty in the size distribution of objects that nobody can see. A third object also changes the vocabulary, because a category that once held two members now holds a population and a population can be modelled rather than described one specimen at a time.

What would settle the composition question is spectroscopy through perihelion. A spectrum that matches the ratios measured in solar system comets would suggest that the chemistry of planet formation is common, and a spectrum that does not would suggest that the solar system is unusual in a way that nobody has yet modelled.
