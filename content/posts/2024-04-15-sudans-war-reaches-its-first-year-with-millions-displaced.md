title: "Sudan's war reaches its first year with millions displaced"

description: "A year after fighting began on 15 April 2023, Sudan's war has killed tens of thousands of people, displaced about eight million and pushed parts of the country towards famine, with mediation stalled and both generals still in place."
---

War between Sudan's armed forces and the Rapid Support Forces reached its first anniversary on the fifteenth of April, with no sign of a settlement and with the country divided between two governments that each claims to be legitimate. Fighting began in Khartoum in April 2023 over the integration of the paramilitary force into the army, and it spread within weeks to Darfur, to Kordofan and to the east.

Human cost of the year is the largest of any current conflict. The United Nations says about eight million people have been displaced inside the country or across its borders, which is the biggest displacement crisis in the world, and that tens of thousands have been killed. Counts of the dead are unreliable because most of the fighting has happened in cities where records were destroyed, and researchers who used satellite imagery and hospital data estimated a toll substantially higher than the official figures.

Famine is the consequence that the aid agencies have warned about since the winter. Fighting has closed markets and blocked roads, and the two sides have restricted access to areas held by the other, which is a tactic that both have used. The World Food Programme said in March that it could not reach parts of Khartoum and of Darfur, and the classification that measures food crises warned that several areas were at risk of famine in the coming months.

Diplomatic effort has produced agreements that were not implemented. Talks in Jeddah sponsored by the United States and Saudi Arabia produced a declaration on protecting civilians in May 2023 and a ceasefire that both sides violated within hours. The African Union and the regional body IGAD have tried to mediate, and the two generals, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, have each refused to meet the other except on terms that would amount to surrender.

Regional involvement has made the war longer. The United Arab Emirates has been accused by United Nations investigators of supplying the Rapid Support Forces with weapons through Chad, which it denies. Egypt supports the army, and Eritrea and Ethiopia have been drawn in by the flow of refugees. Several outside governments have interests in Sudan's gold and in the Red Sea coast, and those interests have kept the belligerents supplied.

What followed from the fifteenth of April is a state that may not be recoverable in its present borders. The army has withdrawn from most of Khartoum and from parts of Darfur, and the Rapid Support Forces controls the capital's streets and much of the west. A ceasefire would still leave two armed movements that have been enriched by war, and an election becomes conceivable only if they are demobilized.

What the year established was that a dispute between two generals could destroy a state, and that the countries with the influence to stop it chose to watch. Aid agencies said the response was funded at a fraction of what the appeal required, and that the gap had widened since the winter.
