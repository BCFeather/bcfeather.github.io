UPS reported a revenue of 22.1 billion dollars for the quarter to June, down about 11 per cent, and an operating profit of 2.8 billion. The earnings per share of 2.54 dollars beat the forecast of 2.50. The shares fell about 4 per cent after the call, and the company cut the revenue forecast for the year. The revenue was about 300 million below the estimate, and the adjusted margin was about 12.7 per cent. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 1 percentage point from the revenue.

Volumes were the problem. The average daily volume fell by about 9.4 per cent, and the one of the domestic parcels by about 9. The company said the customers had been trading down to the cheaper services, and the returns had been weaker than in the previous year. The revenue per the piece rose by about 2 per cent, and the fuel surcharge added about 1 point. The international volume fell by about 10 per cent, and the one of the supply chain by about 15. The company said the volume from the large retailers had fallen by about 12 per cent, and the one from the small ones by about 6.

Costs came down. The company has cut about 12,000 jobs this year, and the one of the aircraft fleet by about 10 per cent. The operating expenses fell by about 12 per cent, and the productivity was the best in the quarter since 2020. The company said the capital spending would be about 5 billion dollars this year, down from about 5.4.

labour talks are the risk. The contract with the Teamsters covers about 340,000 workers, and the union has threatened a strike at the end of July. The company said it had reached a tentative agreement, and the cost of it would be about 3 per cent of the wage bill. The agreement includes the wage rises of about 4 per cent a year, and the part-time pay would reach about 21 dollars an hour.

Its guidance was cut. The company expects the revenue of about 93 billion dollars for the year, down from 97, and the operating margin of about 11.8 per cent. The market had expected about 95 billion. The company said the peak season would be weaker than a year ago, and the surcharges would be lower. The company said the dividend would rise by about 5 per cent, and the buyback was about 3 billion dollars.

This quarter is the one in which the parcel market shrank. A company that earns 2.8 billion dollars while the volumes fall by 9 per cent is a company cutting the network to the demand, and the cut is the part that the guidance reflects. The shares are down about 5 per cent this year, and the company is the worst performer among the large transport groups.
