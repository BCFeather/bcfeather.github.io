Starbucks reported a four per cent fall in comparable sales for the quarter to the end of March, with declines in every region it trades in, and it withdrew the forecast it had given for the year. Same-store sales in China fell eleven per cent. Revenue was 8.56 billion dollars, two per cent below the same quarter a year earlier. The withdrawal of the forecast is the detail that the market punished, because it means the company cannot yet see the bottom of the decline it is describing.

American number is the surprise. The company had raised prices for two years without losing customers, and the quarter showed that the price had reached a level where the occasional visitor stops coming. A customer who visits twice a month rather than three times is a customer who still says the coffee is good. The company's own research shows that the customers who left were the ones who came for a treat rather than for a habit, and those customers return when the treat is cheaper or the day is warmer.

Promotion is the response that the company has chosen. Discounts through the loyalty programme and cheaper combinations at the counter have been expanded, and the executives said the discounts were aimed at the afternoon hours when the stores are half empty. A discount that fills a quiet hour costs less than a price cut that applies all day. The loyalty programme gives the company a way to reach those customers directly, and the offers it sends to a phone cost less than a poster in a window that everybody sees.

China is the harder problem. The local chains sell coffee at half the price, deliver it in twenty minutes and open outlets in the same streets, and the number of stores in the country has grown faster than the number of customers. A market that is saturated with outlets is a market where the next store cannibalizes the last. The outlets in China are owned by the company and by a partner, and the partner has been the one pushing the expansion that the same-store numbers now show to be counterproductive.

Franchise question is the constraint. The company owns its stores in most markets, which means the rent and the labour are on its own books when volume falls, and the alternative, licensing to partners, is a slower and less controlled route. An owned estate is an estate that cannot be trimmed quickly. The company has said it will close some stores and renovate others, which is a slower route to lower costs than the franchise model that its competitors use.

What the quarter shows is that a premium price can be defended only while the product is visibly better than the cheaper alternatives, and the company's problem is that the difference has become harder for a customer to taste. A premium brand that discounts is a brand that has decided the price was too high, and the customers who notice that decision are the ones who were paying it without complaint.
