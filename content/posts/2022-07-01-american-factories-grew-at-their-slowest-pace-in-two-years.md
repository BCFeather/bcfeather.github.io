New orders were 49.2, which is below 50 for the first time since 2020. The purchasing managers' index for manufacturing was 53 in June, the Institute for Supply Management reported on 1 July. The reading was down from 56.1 in May and it was below the 54.5 forecast. It was the lowest since June 2020, and it was the third monthly fall.

Production was 54.2, and it had held up because firms were working through backlogs. Employment was 47.3, and it had fallen for two months. Firms said they had stopped hiring and that they would cut shifts in the autumn. The sector employed 12.7 million people. Prices were still rising. The price index was 78.5, and it had been 82.2 in April. Firms said the price of steel had fallen 20 per cent from its peak, and that the price of freight had fallen. They said they expected the price of components to fall in the second half.

Deliveries were faster. The index for supplier deliveries was 57, and it had been 65 in January. Firms said the ports had cleared their backlog, and that the freight rates from Asia had fallen 30 per cent. The index for backlogs was 50, and it had been 58. Firms said the price of steel had fallen 20 per cent from its peak, and freight rates had fallen 30.

Inventories were 56, which means firms were adding stock. Customers' inventories were 35, and they had risen from 33. Firms said their customers had enough parts, and that they had stopped ordering. The survey said the inventory build would cut production in the third quarter. Export orders were 50.8, and they had fallen for three months. Firms said the strong dollar had made their goods dearer, and that the demand from Europe had slowed. The imports index was 51, and it had fallen as the lockdowns in China ended. Its customers' inventories were 35, up from 33, and firms said orders had stopped. The inventory build was expected to cut production in the third quarter.

Published on the first working day of each month, the survey covers 400 firms. Its reading is watched as a sign of the industrial cycle, because it reaches the market before the official data. It had peaked at 63.1 in March 2021. The index stayed near 50 through the summer and fell to 50.9 in September. It reached 47.6 in November and 46.9 in January 2023. The sector's output fell 1 per cent in the year, and the survey's author said the cycle had turned.

Recovery came in the spring of 2023 as the supply chain eased. It reached 49 in March and 50.2 in June. The sector added 200,000 jobs in the year, and the price index fell to 50. The survey's author said the shortage had ended.
