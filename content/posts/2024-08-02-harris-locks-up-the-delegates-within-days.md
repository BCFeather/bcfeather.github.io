Kamala Harris secured pledges from enough delegates to win the Democratic nomination on the first ballot, the party announced at the beginning of August, eleven days after the president withdrew from the race. The nomination was to be ratified in a virtual roll call before the convention in Chicago. The virtual roll call was scheduled before the convention to satisfy a deadline in Ohio, which is the kind of rule that shapes a nomination more than the politics does.

Speed was the point. A party that had been planning a contested convention within a week had instead settled on a candidate, and the settling removed the argument that the other side was preparing to exploit. A nomination that is decided in eleven days is a nomination that denies an opponent a month of footage. A convention that would have gone to a second ballot would have been covered as a party in crisis, and the coverage is what the leadership was buying off.

Money was the other measure. The campaign reported a record sum raised in the days after the announcement, much of it from small donors, and the total was described as the largest single-day figure in a presidential cycle. A donor who gives twenty dollars is a donor who can give again, which is the difference between a cheque and a list. The list of donors is a list that the campaign can use in October, and the small donations are the ones that can be repeated.

Coalition is the open question. The candidate had been unpopular in her own primary campaign four years earlier, and the enthusiasm that greeted the transfer was concentrated among groups that the party needs and does not always turn out. A surge in money is not the same as a surge in voters. The groups that were most enthusiastic are the ones that vote least reliably, and the campaign's first task was to convert a crowd into a field programme.

Convention was being rebuilt around a candidate rather than a programme. The platform that had been drafted for the president was adopted with adjustments, and the speakers were being chosen for a message about the future rather than about the record. A convention that is written in three weeks is a convention that reveals what the party thinks it needs to say. The speakers were chosen to describe the candidate's biography as an argument about opportunity, which is the theme that the polling had identified.

What the fortnight shows is that a party can replace a candidate without replacing its coalition. The delegates moved, the money moved and the argument about whether the president should stay ended, which is the fastest that American politics has reorganized itself in decades. The reorganization took a party that had been built around one man and made it a party built around an idea of the future, and that is the change that the election will test.
