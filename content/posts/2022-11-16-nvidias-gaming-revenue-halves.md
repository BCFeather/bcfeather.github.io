NVIDIA published results for the quarter to October on 16 November, and revenue fell 17 per cent to 5.93 billion dollars. Net income fell 72 per cent to 680 million, and the gross margin fell from 65 per cent to 54. The gaming business was the reason, and its revenue fell 51 per cent to 1.57 billion. The company's revenue had grown 50 per cent in the previous quarter, and the fall was the first since 2020.

A warning in August had said gaming revenue would fall by about half, and the figure matched it. It said the problem was inventory at retailers and at its own partners, and it took a charge of 1.3 billion dollars to write down stock. It also said it would slow purchases from its supplier for two quarters.

Revenue was 3.83 billion, up 31 per cent, and it was the largest segment for the first time. The data centre business held up. The company said sales of its chips for artificial intelligence had grown, and it said the demand was from cloud companies and from research institutions. Its data centre chips were used in the language models that had been announced during the year.

Cryptocurrency mining had been a large part of the demand, and it had disappeared. Its gaming cards had been bought by miners in 2021, and the used market was now full of cards sold at half price. The company said it had underestimated the effect of the collapse. The automotive business grew 86 per cent to 251 million, and it was the smallest segment. The company said it had design wins with several car makers, and the revenue would grow as those models entered production. The professional visualization business fell 7 per cent. The company said it had sold 10 million gaming cards in the year, down from 15 million.

Shares rose 2 per cent on the day, and they had fallen 50 per cent in the year. The company's value was 350 billion dollars, and it had been 800 billion in November 2021. The market was pricing the end of the gaming cycle and the beginning of the artificial intelligence one.

Revenue was 6 billion dollars, and the data centre business grew 40 per cent. The next quarter was better than expected. The company said in February that it had seen demand for its new chips from companies building language models, and the shares rose 40 per cent in the first half of 2023. The gaming business recovered in the second half of 2023 as the inventory cleared and as new cards shipped. The data centre business became 70 per cent of revenue by 2024, and the company's value passed 2 trillion dollars. The quarter in November was the trough of the gaming cycle. The shares had fallen 50 per cent in the year, and they were still 30 per cent above their level of 2020.
