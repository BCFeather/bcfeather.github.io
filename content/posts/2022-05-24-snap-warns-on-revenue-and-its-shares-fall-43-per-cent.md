Snap told investors on 23 May that revenue growth in the current quarter would miss its forecast. The company had said in April that growth would be 20 to 25 per cent, and it said the figure would be 13 to 15. The shares fell 43 per cent on 24 May, and the company lost 16 billion dollars of market value. Revenue in the quarter was 1.06 billion dollars, and the company had lost 360 million in the first quarter.

Advertising was the reason. The company said advertisers had cut spending because of inflation and because of the war in Ukraine. It also said the changes to the privacy rules on Apple's phones had made it harder to measure advertising, and that its tools had not adapted. Its tools measured conversions over seven days, and Apple's change let users opt out of tracking.

Hiring would slow. It had planned to add 2,000 staff in the year, and it said it would add 500. It also said it would cut spending on content and on marketing, and that it would not pay cash bonuses to executives. The shares had fallen 70 per cent in the year before the warning, and they fell to 22 dollars from 40. The company's value was 22 billion dollars, down from 130 billion in 2021. The loss was the largest one-day fall for a large technology company since 2018. It also said it would cut spending on content and on marketing by 100 million dollars.

Other advertising companies fell with it. Meta fell 8 per cent, Pinterest 24, and Alphabet 7. The market said the warning applied to the whole sector, and the shares of the digital advertisers fell 20 per cent in the week. The sector had grown 40 per cent in 2021, and it was contracting in 2022. The advertising market had grown 40 per cent in 2021, and it contracted in 2022.

Daily users were 330 million, and the company said engagement had grown. Its revenue per user was 3.20 dollars, which is below that of its rivals. It said it would focus on its subscription product, which it had launched in June, and that it expected 4 million subscribers. The advertisers were shifting to platforms with more data. The company's tools relied on a pixel that Apple had restricted, and its measurement had become less accurate. It said it would build its own measurement, and it said the change would take a year.

Shares kept falling and reached 8 dollars in December. The company laid off 20 per cent of its staff in August, and it cut its revenue forecast twice more. Its revenue fell 2 per cent in the year, and its losses reached 1.4 billion dollars. It recovered in 2023, and its shares rose 90 per cent.
