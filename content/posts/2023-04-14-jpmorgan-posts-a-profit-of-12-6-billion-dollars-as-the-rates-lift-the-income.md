JPMorgan Chase reported a profit of 12.6 billion dollars for the first quarter, up 52 per cent from a year earlier, on a revenue of 39.3 billion. The earnings per share of 4.10 dollars beat the forecast of 3.41. The shares rose about 7 per cent on the day. The revenue was about 3 billion above the estimate, and the provision was the line that the analysts had flagged. The return on tangible equity was about 23 per cent, the highest among the large banks.

Net interest income did the work at 20.8 billion dollars. The bank earned about 20.8 billion dollars from the difference between what it pays for the deposits and what it charges for the loans, up about 49 per cent. The higher rates have been passed to the borrowers faster than to the savers, and the gap is the profit. The guidance for the year was raised to about 81 billion dollars of net interest income from 74. The finance chief said the deposit costs would rise through the year and the income was close to its peak.

Reserves rose. The bank added about 1.1 billion dollars to the provision for the credit losses, which brought the total to about 2.3 billion for the quarter. The finance chief said the consumer was still in good shape and the commercial real estate was the area being watched. The card business reported a net charge-off rate of about 2.5 per cent, which is still below the level of 2019. The commercial real estate book is about 60 billion dollars, and the office loans are the part the bank has been marking down.

Trading was mixed. The fixed income revenue fell by about 12 per cent as the volatility of the previous year faded, and the equities fell by about 3. The investment banking fees fell by about 19 per cent as the deals dried up. The pipeline has been improving, and the fees are expected to recover in the second half. The equity trading was helped by the volatility around the bank failures, and the advisory fees were the weakest line. The bank said the deal pipeline had grown in the quarter as the boards returned to the transactions.

Deposits fell by about 2 per cent. The customers moved some of the money to the treasury bills and the money market funds, and the bank has been paying more for what it keeps. The flight after the collapse of the two regional banks in March did not reach the largest institutions. The average deposit cost was about 2 per cent, and the bank expects it to rise further in the second quarter. The loans grew by about 4 per cent, and the card balances rose by about 8 as the consumers kept spending.

This quarter showed the split in the industry. A bank that earns 12.6 billion dollars while the regional lenders are losing the deposits is a bank that has the scale to absorb the higher rates. The common equity ratio was about 13.8 per cent, and the buyback was paused for the quarter to build the capital. The stress test later in the year will decide how much of the excess the bank can return.
