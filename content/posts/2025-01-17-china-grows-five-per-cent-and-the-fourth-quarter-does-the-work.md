National Bureau of Statistics released the 2024 accounts on 17 January, and the headline was the one the government had promised. Gross domestic product grew 5.0 per cent in real terms, against a target of around 5 per cent, and the level reached 134.9 trillion yuan, or about 18.8 trillion dollars at the average exchange rate for the year.

Growth by quarter is where the work happened. Growth ran at 5.3 per cent in the first quarter, 4.7 per cent in the second and 4.6 per cent in the third, a sequence that pointed to a year below target until the fourth quarter came in at 5.4 per cent. On a seasonally adjusted quarter-on-quarter basis, the fourth quarter grew 1.6 per cent after 0.9 per cent in the third, which is the strongest sequential reading of the year.

What produced that quarter is a policy story. The stimulus measures announced in September, which included a cut in policy rates, support for the property sector and a programme of trade-in subsidies for consumer goods, began to appear in the monthly data from October. Retail sales of household appliances and automobiles rose on the subsidy, and the December figures were the ones that carried the annual number over the line.

Composition of growth was unbalanced in the way it has been for three years. Final consumption contributed 2.2 percentage points, gross capital formation 1.3 points and net exports 1.5 points, which means that external demand carried a share of growth that a large economy would normally not need. The trade surplus reached a record, and the tariffs that were about to arrive in 2025 were already being anticipated by exporters who shipped ahead of them.

Deflation remained the unresolved problem. Consumer prices were close to flat for the year and producer prices fell, which means that nominal growth ran below real growth and that the debt burden of local governments and property developers grew relative to the economy that has to service it. A target measured in real terms can be met while the nominal economy stalls, and 2024 was a year in which that distinction mattered.

Labour market showed the same pattern. Surveyed unemployment held near 5 per cent, but the youth rate was higher and the number of new urban jobs was supported by the platform and service sectors that the trade-in programmes touched. Wages grew slowly, which is why consumption contributed less than the policy intended.

What 2025 inherits is a growth rate that depends on policy support and an external sector that is about to be taxed. The government has set the same target for the new year, and the arithmetic is harder, because the base is larger, the stimulus has to be renewed to keep its effect and the export channel is the one that the incoming American administration has said it will close.
