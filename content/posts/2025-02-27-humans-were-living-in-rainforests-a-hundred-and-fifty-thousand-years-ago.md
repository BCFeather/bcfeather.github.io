title: "Humans were living in rainforests a hundred and fifty thousand years ago"
date: "2025-02-27T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [science]
description: "*Nature* reports stone tools and sediments from a West African rainforest that push the earliest evidence of human occupation of humid tropical forest back to about 150,000 years ago, a habitat that was long thought to have been avoided."
---

Rainforests were once treated as a barrier in the story of human dispersal. The reasoning was ecological: a closed canopy offers little game, the soils are poor for agriculture, and the diseases that a humid forest carries are hostile to a primate that evolved on a savanna. The earliest rainforest occupations were dated to about 45,000 years ago in Southeast Asia, and the assumption that the forest was a late frontier followed from those dates.

The evidence that challenges it comes from a site where sediments preserve a sequence of occupation layers with stone tools, and where the pollen and the phytoliths in the same layers record a closed forest instead of an open landscape. A site that records both the tools and the vegetation in the same stratigraphy is the kind of evidence that the argument needs, because the alternative, a tool assemblage found near a forest but deposited in a clearing, cannot distinguish occupation of the forest from passage through it.

Dating places the oldest layers at about 150,000 years before the present, which is within the range of the earliest *Homo sapiens* fossils and well before the dispersal out of Africa that the genetic record dates to about 60,000 years ago. A species that was living in a rainforest in Africa at that time had a broader habitat range than the savanna model allowed.

Tool assemblage is not primitive. The layers contain prepared cores and flakes that were struck from them, which is a technology that requires planning several steps ahead, and the raw materials include stone that was carried from sources outside the immediate area. A group that carried stone across a forest is a group that knew where it was going and expected to need it.

Forest was not empty of resources. A humid tropical forest provides fruit, nuts, insects and small game in a density that a savanna does not match in the dry season, and the difficulty is the search, not the supply. A population that learned to exploit those resources had a diet that varied through the year, which is the kind of adaptation that a generalist primate would be expected to make.

What the site does to the map is to put a habitat on it that the models had left out. Reconstructions of human dispersal tend to draw corridors along coasts and rivers through grassland, and a dispersal that also used forest is a dispersal with different routes and different timings. The genetics of forest-dwelling populations in Africa, which show long-standing structure, are consistent with a history that the archaeology now supports.

Limits are the ones that a single site imposes. One sequence cannot establish a regional pattern, and the taphonomy of a rainforest deposit is difficult, because the same conditions that preserve pollen destroy bone. The absence of fauna from the layers is the gap that a future excavation at a comparable site would need to fill.
