title: "Stellar flares heat the interiors of close rocky planets"

description: "Magnetic flux carried by flare-driven coronal mass ejections dissipates inside a rocky planet by Ohmic heating, and a stochastic model applied to TRAPPIST-1 finds the mantle effect competitive with surface forcing."
---

Studies of stellar flares on habitable-zone planets usually stop at the atmosphere. Ultraviolet radiation and plasma are treated as surface phenomena, and the planet's interior is assumed to be unaffected because a magnetic field and a solid mantle stand between the two. That reasoning treats the field as a shield, and a shield in a conductor is exactly where a changing flux can deposit energy, since a conductor that carries an induced current is a resistor with a current in it.

Work posted to arXiv on 11 November 2022 argues that the assumption is wrong. A flare is often accompanied by a coronal mass ejection, and the ejection carries magnetic flux that reaches the planet and can penetrate far enough to dissipate inside it. Dissipation of that kind is Ohmic heating, and the authors build a physical model in which the flux drives currents in the silicate mantle. The mantle has a conductivity, the flux changes on the timescale of an ejection, and the induced current dissipates as heat.

The model is applied to TRAPPIST-1, a cool star whose flaring has been constrained by Kepler observations. Its planets are close, so the ejections arrive often and with little dilution, and the paper treats the input parameters as random variables to reflect the uncertainty in the star's activity and in the arrival geometry. A distribution of heating values is the output, and the paper reports it as such rather than as a single number per planet.

For the innermost planets the heat dissipated in the mantle is of a magnitude that matters. The authors compare it with the heating that comes from the planet's own radioactive inventory and with the surface forcing, and they report that it can be competitive, which is the claim a reader should carry away. Competitive here means a sizeable fraction of the total, not a marginal addition, and the fraction differs between the planets in the system, which is the reason the paper reports each one separately.

Interior heating has consequences beyond temperature. A warmer mantle changes convection, and convection drives the magnetic field that shields the surface, so a planet under heavy flaring can have its interior and its exterior coupled in a loop that neither model alone would show. A weaker field lets more flux reach the interior, which raises the heating, which changes the convection that maintains the field.

Limits are those of the model. The ejection parameters are estimated from the star's observed activity rather than measured at the planet, and the conductivity of the mantle is a range rather than a number. A reader should also note that the paper's heating estimates are stochastic, so they describe a distribution rather than a single value.

A test would come from an interior model that includes the feedback, since the coupling the authors describe is the part that a thermal history calculation would have to absorb.
