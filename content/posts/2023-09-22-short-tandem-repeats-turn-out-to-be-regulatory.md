description: "A genome-wide survey of repeats in promoters shows copy number grading the expression of the gene beside it, so the repeat behaves as a dial rather than a marker."
---

A short tandem repeat is a stretch where a motif is copied several times, and the copy number varies between people. That variation has long been treated as a marker rather than a mechanism. Surveyed in *Science* in September 2023, repeats in promoters across the genome turn out to change the expression of the gene they control. Effect is the finding. A promoter with more copies drives stronger expression, and the relationship is graded rather than switch-like, which is what makes the repeat a dial.

Binding supplies the mechanism, because a repeat matching a factor's motif offers more binding sites and the count of sites is the variable setting expression. Variation carries a population consequence. A repeat's length differs between people, and a length shifting expression is a variant with a measurable effect.

Disease link points to use. A repeat in a promoter controlling a growth gene is a candidate risk variant, and association analysis is the evidence. Evolution frames the whole class, since a repeat expands and contracts through replication slippage and an unstable regulatory element is one that evolves quickly.

Measurement sets the limitation, because a repeat's length is hard to genotype with short reads and long-read sequencing is what makes a survey feasible. Size of the effect in a person is what the paper does not deliver, since a promoter is one contribution among many and the effects are modest.

Genetics gains a class of variant that has been neglected, because repeats have been filtered out of most analyses and a functional role is a reason to keep them. Slippage is the mutation, since a polymerase stuttering on a repeat changes copy number and the rate rises with length. Motif identity gives specificity, because a repeat matching one factor's site is a dial for that factor and one matching several is a dial for a combination.

Scale of the survey is the discovery, since a genome-wide scan turns an anecdote into a class. Enhancers offer the frame, because an enhancer regulates at a distance while a repeat in a promoter regulates in place.

A reporter assay in which the repeat's copy number was varied one unit at a time would show whether the dial is smooth or stepped, and a stepped response would imply a threshold in the number of bound factors. A survey of repeats in enhancers as well as promoters would show whether the class extends beyond the site the authors chose to study. Long-read genotyping of a cohort would let the repeat be tested in an association study, and a significant term there is what would move the class from mechanism to medicine. A comparison of repeat lengths across primates would show whether the dials have been tuned differently in different lineages, and a conserved length at a conserved gene would suggest the copy number is under selection. A reporter construct with a mismatched motif would separate binding from structure, and a control that loses regulation when the match is broken is the cleanest evidence the mechanism offers.
