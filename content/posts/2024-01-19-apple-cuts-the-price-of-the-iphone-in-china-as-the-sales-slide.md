Apple allowed its online retailers in China to cut the price of the iPhone 15 by up to 500 yuan in the third week of January, the largest discount it has offered in the market since the launch. The promotion ran for four days and was described by the sellers as a festival offer. The discount was applied to the models that the retailers had in stock, and the newest version of the largest model was not included.

Discount is a signal about the inventory. The company does not usually reduce the price of a phone that is four months old, and the retailers that hold the stock are the ones that asked for the support. A discount that arrives early is a channel that is full. The inventory in the channel had been building since the launch, and the sellers had been offering their own reductions before the company approved one. The sellers had been cutting prices themselves since the launch, and the authorisation gave them cover to advertise the numbers.

Competition is the reason. The domestic brands have been selling folding phones and camera phones at prices well below the iPhone, and the buyers who upgrade every two years have been moving to them. A market that offers more for less is a market where the premium has to be defended. The domestic brands shipped more phones than the company did in the quarter, which is the first time that has happened in the market.

Shipment data confirm it. The data for the fourth quarter showed the company's share of the Chinese market falling, and the analysts attributed the fall to the absence of a cheaper model rather than to the quality of the phone. A share that falls is a price that has to move. The company has been investing in its own stores in the country, and the physical outlets were not part of the promotion. The company does not report the shipments for the market separately, and the analysts estimate them from the customs data and from the retailers.

Regulatory pressure is the background. The government has restricted the use of foreign devices in some offices, and the agencies have said the rules are about security rather than about trade. A restriction that is announced quietly is a restriction that the buyers notice. The rules on foreign devices apply to the staff of the agencies and of the state-owned companies, and the surveys show the effect is limited so far. The agencies have said the rules apply to the devices used for official business, and the company has said it complies with the local law.

What the promotion shows is that a premium brand in a competitive market has to choose between its margin and its volume, and the choice is now being made in the open. A price cut that is authorised rather than announced is a company that wants the volume without the headline, and the headline arrived anyway. The promotion was described by the sellers as a festival offer, and the company has not commented on it.
