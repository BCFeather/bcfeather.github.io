Samsung reported operating profit of 6.6 trillion won for the first quarter, roughly ten times the 640 billion it earned a year earlier, and its semiconductor division returned to profit after four quarters of losses. Revenue rose to 71.9 trillion won. The result was the strongest since the peak of the last cycle, and it confirmed that the downturn in memory is over for the largest producer.

Memory cycle is the whole story. Prices for the memory chips that go into servers and phones fell through 2023 as the industry built capacity it could not sell, and the producers responded by cutting output, which is what turned the price around. A commodity that is made by three companies is a commodity whose price is decided by their discipline. The three producers have said they will keep their output below the level of the previous peak, and the analysts who follow the industry describe that as the most disciplined arrangement in a decade.

Artificial intelligence changed the mix. The accelerators that train models need a particular kind of memory stacked in layers, and the demand for that product has grown faster than the industry can add capacity, which is why the most advanced memory now sells at a premium. A component that a new market needs is a component that a mature industry can price again. The stacked memory is sold before it is made, and the customers who design accelerators have been signing agreements that run into next year.

Foundry is the laggard. The contract manufacturing business lost money again, and its customers have been slow to fill the capacity that the company built for the most advanced circuits. A fab that is not full is a fab that depreciates whether or not it produces anything. The company has said it will slow the construction of new foundry capacity, which is an admission that the demand it expected has not arrived at the price it charged.

Handset business is the cash. Phones sold in larger numbers than a year earlier, and the cheaper models carried the volume, which is a mix that protects revenue and dilutes margin. A product line that competes on price at the bottom is a product line that protects share rather than profit. The handset division earns a margin that is smaller than the chip division's, and the mix is why the group's profit rose less than the memory price did.

What the quarter shows is that a cyclical industry can be turned around by cutting supply, and the discipline that produced this quarter is the same discipline that will eventually produce the next downturn. A cycle that turns on supply discipline is a cycle that depends on three boardrooms in three countries making the same decision at the same time. The next downturn will begin when one of them decides that its share matters more than the price, and nothing in the quarter suggests that decision is close.
