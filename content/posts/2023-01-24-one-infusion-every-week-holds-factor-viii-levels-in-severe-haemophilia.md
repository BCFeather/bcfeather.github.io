Haemophilia A is managed by replacing the missing clotting factor, and the schedule has been the compromise. A standard factor VIII product has a half-life of about twelve hours, so prophylaxis means infusions two or three times a week, and adherence among teenagers is poor for reasons that are easy to understand.

The product tested in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 26 January 2023 is engineered to last longer. Efanesoctocog alfa is a fusion that binds factor VIII to von Willebrand factor and to a fragment that extends its circulation, and the result is a half-life measured in days rather than hours.

The trial gave the product once weekly to patients with severe haemophilia A, some of whom had been on prophylaxis and some of whom had been treated on demand. The primary measure was the annualized bleeding rate, and the reported rates were low, in the range that prophylaxis aims for.

Joint health is the outcome that matters over a lifetime. Repeated bleeding into a joint destroys cartilage and produces the arthropathy that characterizes the disease in adults. The trial reports that a large majority of patients had no bleeding into joints during prophylaxis, and that target joints resolved.

The pharmacokinetics are the mechanism. A weekly interval requires a trough level above the threshold at which spontaneous bleeding occurs, and the fusion achieves that by slowing clearance rather than by increasing the dose. The paper's concentration-time curves show a trough that stays in a protective range.

Immunogenicity is the risk with any new factor VIII construct. Inhibitors develop in a minority of patients and neutralize the product, and a modified molecule can present new epitopes. The trial's inhibitor rate was low, and the follow-up is short relative to the lifetime of treatment.

The comparison with emicizumab is unavoidable. A bispecific antibody that mimics factor VIII is given subcutaneously every week or two and has changed the field, and a weekly intravenous product competes with a subcutaneous one. The advantage of factor replacement is that it can be dosed for surgery and for bleeding, which the mimic cannot fully replace.

Cost is the unresolved variable. A longer-acting product at a higher price per milligram may cost more per year than a cheaper product given more often, and the health-economic case depends on adherence and on the bleeds avoided.

For patients, the practical gain is a schedule that fits a life. One infusion on a fixed day, with levels that hold, is a treatment that a student can manage without rearranging a week.

The trial's design is a single-arm study with a comparison to the patients' own previous treatment, a design that has to be read carefully. Annualized bleeding rates while on prophylaxis are compared with rates on the previous regimen, and the comparison is historical rather than randomized.

The subgroup that had been treated on demand is the more informative one. Their bleeding rate fell by an order of magnitude when they moved to prophylaxis, and the comparison to a previous regimen is where the product's value shows most clearly.
