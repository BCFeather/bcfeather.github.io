Japanese core consumer prices rose 2.5 per cent in the year to November, the statistics bureau said on 22 December, down from 2.9 per cent in October. The figure was the lowest since the summer of 2022, and the measure has now fallen for three months in a row. The figure was in line with the forecast, and the measure is now about half the peak of 4.2 per cent in the winter.

Food prices did the work. The prices of the fresh food rose by about 3 per cent rather than the 8 of the summer, and the vegetables fell as the harvest arrived. A cabbage that is cheaper is an index that falls. The prices of the imported wheat have fallen from the peak, and the government has raised the price it charges the mills by about 10 per cent. The prices of the meat and the dairy rose by about 5 per cent, and the one of the fruit fell by about 8 as the imports were cheaper.

Fuel subsidies held the line. The government has been paying part of the household bill since the start of the year, and the effect is about 0.5 points of the index. A subsidy that is paid is a price that is not. The subsidy costs about 1 trillion yen a year, and the government has said it will extend it into the spring. The subsidy is paid to the refiners and the importers rather than the households, and the effect shows in the index through the fuel prices.

Underlying pressure was firm. The measure that strips the fuel and the food rose by about 3.8 per cent, and the services were about 3 as the wages rose. A service that is priced up is a wage that has arrived. The prices of the hotel and the restaurants rose by about 5 per cent, and the transport was flat as the fuel was subsidized.

Its governor is still cautious. The governor has said the spring wage talks will be the test, and the unions have been asking for about 5 per cent. A claim that is high is a policy that must move. The bank meets in January, and the economists expect no change before the spring wage round is concluded. The bank's next meeting is in January, and the economists say the decision will depend on the wage talks rather than the index.

What the month shows is that the headline is falling towards the target from the top and the underlying is holding, and the holding is why the bank has not moved. A core at 2.5 per cent is a core the bank can present as a success, and the success is what the exit needs. The measure has been above 2 per cent for twenty months, and the bank says the target is the level rather than the direction.
