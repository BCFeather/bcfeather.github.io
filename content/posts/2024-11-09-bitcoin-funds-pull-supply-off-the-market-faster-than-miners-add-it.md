description: "Exchange-traded funds have turned a speculative asset into something a pension consultant can buy, and the flow has outrun the new coins being issued."
---

Spot bitcoin funds have been buying more of the asset each month than the network produces, and the arithmetic of that flow is the simplest explanation for a price that has moved from sixty thousand dollars to above ninety in half a year. A fund buys coins in the market and issues shares against them, and the coins it buys do not come back to the market while the shares stay outstanding. A fund that holds coins in a vault has removed them from the order book, and the order book is where the price is discovered.

Supply is fixed by code. The network issues a set number of new coins per block, and the reward was cut in half in April, which reduced the daily issuance to a few hundred coins. A buyer that wants a thousand coins a day has to take them from holders rather than from miners, and holders who are not selling are holders who have to be persuaded by a price. A reward that halves every four years is a schedule that miners plan against, and the plan is a capital budget.

Demand changed character. An asset that was bought on an exchange by an individual is an asset that a fund can hold in a custody account, and a fund is a vehicle that an adviser can recommend without explaining a wallet. The buyer that arrives through a fund is a buyer with a longer horizon and a smaller appetite for self-custody. A buyer who arrives through an adviser is a buyer who wants a line in a portfolio rather than a wallet.

Leverage is the risk that the flow hides. Funds buy coins and the coins sit with a custodian, and the same custodian may lend against them to other market participants. A market where the same coin supports two claims is a market that unwinds quickly when the price falls, which is what happened in every previous cycle. A custodian that lends against the same coin twice is a custodian that has created a claim nobody can see.

Mining is the part that the price change does not rescue. A miner sells coins to pay for electricity, and a miner that has borrowed to build a facility is selling regardless of the price. The industry consolidated around the operators with the cheapest power, and the smaller sites either shut or were bought. A site with a power contract above the market price is a site that stopped mining in the last cycle.

What the funds have done is give the asset a wrapper that the financial system understands. A wrapper brings capital and also brings the obligations of a regulated product, and the day a fund has to sell to meet redemptions is the day the market learns how deep the bids are. A redemption that forces a sale is a redemption that reveals how many of the buyers were there for the momentum.
