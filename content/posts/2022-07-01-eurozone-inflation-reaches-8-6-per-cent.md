Consumer prices rose 8.6 per cent in the year to June, the statistics office reported on 1 July. The reading was the ninth consecutive record and it was above the 8.4 forecast. It was up from 8.1 per cent in May, and the monthly gain was 0.8 per cent. Core inflation was 3.7 per cent, and it had been 3.8 in May. Energy rose 41.9 per cent, and food 8.9. Services rose 3.4, and industrial goods 4.3. The figures are provisional, and the final ones were published on 19 July.

Energy was the largest part. It contributed 4 points to the total, and the price of gas was 10 times its level of 2019. The embargo on Russian oil had been agreed in May, and it took effect in December. Electricity rose 30 per cent in Italy and 40 in Spain. Governments had spent 200 billion euros on subsidies by the summer.

Estonia's rate was 22 per cent, and Lithuania's 20. The differences between countries were wide. Latvia's was 19, and Slovakia's 13. Germany's was 8.2, and France's 6.5, because of its price caps. Malta's was 6.1, and it had the lowest rate in the region. Food was rising faster than energy in the month. The price of cereals rose 30 per cent, and of milk 20. The war had cut the exports from Ukraine, and the price of wheat had doubled in the spring. The European Union said it would open corridors for grain, and the first ship left Odessa in August.

Wages had begun to follow prices. Negotiated pay rose 2.8 per cent in the first quarter, and the bank said it expected 4 by the end of the year. Germany's metal workers won 6.5 per cent, and the chemical workers 6. The bank said the risk was a wage price spiral, and that it would watch the settlements. The bank raised rates on 21 July, for the first time in 11 years. It raised by 50 basis points, and it said it would raise again in September. Its president said the bank would go as far as needed, and that the process would take two years.

Tourism had recovered, and the reopening of services had offset the fall in industry. The economy had grown 0.7 per cent in the second quarter. The bank said a recession was possible if gas supplies stopped, and that the risk had risen since June. Inflation peaked at 10.6 per cent in October and stayed above 10 until December. The bank raised by 75 points twice in the autumn, and its rate reached 2 per cent in October. Core inflation fell slowly, and it reached 5 per cent in December.

Inflation reached 10 per cent in September and 10.6 in October, and it fell below 8 in the spring of 2023. The rate fell to 8.9 per cent in July and rose to 9.1 in August. The bank said the peak would come in the autumn, and it did.
