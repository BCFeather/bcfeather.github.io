Gamma-graphyne has been a theoretical target since 1987, when a family of carbon networks with alkyne linkages was proposed. Making one in a flask took decades, and in May 2022 a group at the University of Colorado Boulder with colleagues at Qingdao University of Science and Technology reported a synthesis in *Nature Synthesis*. On 2 September 2024 the same journal published a retraction note.

A short note carries the finding. A formal investigation by the Office of Research Integrity at the University of Colorado followed concerns about the validity of some data, and it concluded that the data used to produce Supplementary Figure S10 had been fabricated, and that the procedures used to produce Figure 2b departed from standard practice. The authors state that they retract out of a sense of responsibility.

Centrality was the committee's second finding, and it cuts against the fabrication. Its conclusion, as recorded in the notice, was that the data in Figure S10 do not support the major claim of the manuscript and do not materially affect the advance reported. That combination is unusual, since fabricated data normally take the claim down with them.

What the notice does not do is name a person. Institutional findings of fabrication usually stop at the data, and the Office of Research Integrity reports to the university rather than to the public, so the reader learns that a figure was invented and not who invented it. Journals are left to publish the consequence, which is the retraction, while the investigation that produced it stays internal.

Two authors, both at Qingdao, did not respond to the editors' correspondence and are not listed among those who agree with the retraction, a silence that leaves their share of the work formally unaddressed. The rest, including the corresponding authors, do.

The field loses a milestone, not the target. The material had been sought for its predicted Dirac cones and its porosity, and the synthesis route, a dynamic covalent chemistry approach in which alkyne metathesis builds the network, remains a plausible strategy that someone will have to redo with verifiable data. Whether the reported product was that material is now an open question rather than a settled result.

Where integrity problems hide is the lesson here. A supplementary figure carries data that no reader checks against a main text claim, and a procedure described in a methods section can deviate without any figure looking wrong. Synthesis papers are particularly exposed to this, because a claimed new material is judged by a diffraction pattern and a handful of spectra, and a reader who is not in the lab cannot repeat the route. Reproducing a carbon allotrope takes weeks, and few groups attempt it for a paper they merely cite. Journals audit the headline, and the supplement is the part nobody re-derives, and the supplement is where a fabrication can sit for years without a reader noticing.
