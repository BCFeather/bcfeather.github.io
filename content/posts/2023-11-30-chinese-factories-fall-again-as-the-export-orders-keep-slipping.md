Official manufacturing activity measured 49.4 in November on the purchasing managers' index, down from 49.5 in October, the statistics bureau said on 30 November, below the forecast of 49.7. The reading was the second below the line in three months, and the index has stayed under 50 for most of the year. The reading was below the forecast of 49.7, and the index has now been under the line in nine of the eleven months.

Export orders kept slipping. The new export orders fell to about 45, the lowest since the end of last year, as the shipments to Europe weakened. A booking that is not made is a shift that is not run. The manufacturers have been cutting the prices to win the orders, and the average export price of the goods fell by about 4 per cent this year. The export orders have been under the line for nine months, and the shipments to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations fell for a third month.

Domestic demand was flat. The new orders index was about 49.4, and the production was about 50.5, which suggests the factories are filling the orders they already have. A backlog that is used is a demand that has not arrived. The inventories of the finished goods rose to about 48, which means the warehouses are filling faster than the orders. The production index was about 50.5, and the one of the imports and the raw materials was about 48 as the builders bought less steel.

Services were weaker. The non-manufacturing index fell to 50.2 from 50.6, and the construction was about 55 as the infrastructure projects continued. A crane that is working is a fiscal policy that is still spending. The services have been above the line for eleven months, and the ones for the travel were the strongest over the holidays.

Prices stayed down. The output price index was about 47, and the input price about 50, which is the deflation that the producer data have shown for a year. A price that is flat is a profit that is thin. The producer prices fell 2.6 per cent in October, and the profits of the industrial firms fell by about 8 per cent in the year. The government has been cutting the rates on the mortgages and the down payments, and the sales of the new homes have stabilized in the largest cities.

What the month shows is that the stimulus has kept the construction going while the exports and the factories weaken, and the going is not enough. A reading at 49.4 is a reading that the year-end data will confirm, and the confirmation is the base for next year's target. The government has said the target will be met, and the economists expect the growth to be about 5 per cent this year.
