---Matt Gaetz withdrew from consideration for attorney general on the twenty-first of November, eight days after the president-elect announced that he would nominate him. Statement was posted on social media and said the confirmation process had become a distraction from the work of the transition. Nomination had drawn opposition from senators of both parties within hours of being made, and the withdrawal was followed the same evening by an announcement that Pam Bondi, a former attorney general of Florida, would take the post.

Opposition rested on two things. House ethics committee had been investigating allegations that Gaetz had paid women for sex and had used illegal drugs, and had obtained a report that it had not published. Gaetz has denied wrongdoing and the justice department declined to bring charges after its own investigation. Separately, senators objected to his demand that the department be purged of officials who had pursued cases against the incoming president, which he had described as a programme rather than a threat.

Procedure of the ethics committee became a public argument of its own. Committee had been deadlocked on whether to release its report, and the dispute continued after the nomination was announced, with senators saying they wanted to see the material before voting. Gaetz resigned from the House on the fourteenth of November, which removed him from the committee's jurisdiction, and the panel ultimately released a version of the report in late December after a court filing and press investigations had made much of its content public.

The Timing of the withdrawal was a relief to the transition. Senate Republicans had been preparing for hearings that would have dominated the confirmation calendar and had privately asked the nominee to step aside. Bondi, by contrast, is a known quantity to the party: she served as Florida's attorney general for eight years, has worked on the president-elect's legal defence, and has said in interviews that she would restructure the department's national security division and review the conduct of prosecutors who investigated the former president.

Signal the episode sends is about the limits of loyalty as a qualification. Nomination was read as a reward for a congressman who had defended the president-elect during the impeachment and the criminal cases, and it was withdrawn when the cost inside the Senate exceeded what the transition was prepared to pay. Pattern repeated with two other nominees in the following weeks, and the withdrawals were all attributed to the same cause, which is that a narrow Senate majority cannot absorb a confirmation fight it does not need.

Department that the next attorney general inherits is the subject of the argument that produced the nomination in the first place. Incoming administration has promised to investigate the investigators, and the officials who would have to carry that out are the ones already in post. Bondi's hearings in January will be the first occasion on which the plan is tested against the civil service that would implement it, and the withdrawal of November has moved that test to a nominee who is expected to pass it.
