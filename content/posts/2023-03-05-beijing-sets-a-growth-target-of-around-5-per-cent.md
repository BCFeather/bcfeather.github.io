Growth of around 5 per cent was the number published on 5 March in the annual work report at the opening of the legislature. It was lower than the 5.5 per cent of 2022 and below the 5.2 per cent that most forecasters had expected. It was the lowest target since 1994 apart from the pandemic year, when no number was set. The report ran to 60 pages and was read aloud in full, which took about an hour.

Officials said the aim was to restore confidence rather than to boost demand directly. The deficit was set at 3 per cent of output, up from 2.8 per cent, and local government special bonds were raised to 3.8 trillion yuan from 3.65 trillion. That combination implies a modest increase in the fiscal impulse rather than a large one. Tax revenue had fallen in 2022 for the first time in a decade, which is why the deficit was widened.

Consumption was named as the priority for the first time in years. The report promised support for household income, tax relief for small firms and measures to make the property market work again. It also said the government would not use the property sector as a short-term stimulus, which is the phrase officials use when they mean they will not repeat 2015. The report listed 12 measures for consumption and did not put a figure on any of them.

Employment was given a target of 12 million new urban jobs, up from 11 million, and the survey unemployment rate was to be kept around 5.5 per cent. Those numbers imply output growth of about 4.5 per cent on the official elasticity, which is slightly below the headline target. Economists noted the gap and said it left room for the target to be missed without the employment goal being missed. The 12 million jobs target had been raised twice in five years, and the actual figure had exceeded it each time.

Spending on science rose 2 per cent, and education was flat in nominal terms. The military budget rose 7.2 per cent, faster than the overall spending increase, and it was the third consecutive year of acceleration. The totals were published alongside the report, and the detailed budget followed in the spring. The science budget had risen by more than 10 per cent a year between 2018 and 2021. Markets reacted mildly, because the number had been leaked in the weeks before and because it was close to the consensus. The currency strengthened slightly and the ten-year bond yield was unchanged. Analysts said the target was credible but not ambitious, and that the second half would decide whether it was met. The renminbi was 6.9 to the dollar on the day, and it weakened to 7.3 by the autumn.

First quarter output came in at 4.5 per cent, and the second quarter at 6.3, which put the year on course. The government had set a target every year since 1994 and had missed it twice, in 1998 and 2022. The 2022 miss was by 2.5 points, the widest on record, and the new number was read as an attempt to avoid repeating it. The first quarter figures, due in April, were expected to show growth of about 3 per cent on the weak base of the previous year.
