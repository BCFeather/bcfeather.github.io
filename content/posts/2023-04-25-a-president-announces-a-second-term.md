President Biden announced on 25 April that he would seek a second term, in a video of about four minutes that repeated the word freedom and promised to finish the work he had begun. The announcement came on the fourth anniversary of the launch of his previous campaign, and it named Kamala Harris as his running mate without a discussion of alternatives. The campaign filed its paperwork with the Federal Election Commission that morning. The video was made in the White House and released at seven, and the first rally was set for the following weekend in a state the party had lost narrowly.

Calendar was the first issue. At 80, the president is the oldest person to hold the office, and he would be 86 at the end of a second term. Polls have shown that a majority of Democrats, and a larger majority of voters overall, say they would prefer another candidate, and the party's attempts to find one have produced a field of two declared challengers and no serious rival for the nomination.

The record he will defend is mixed and unusually visible. Unemployment is at 3.4 per cent, the lowest in more than half a century, and the Inflation Reduction Act, the chips legislation and the infrastructure bill have put money into projects that are visible in every state. Inflation peaked above nine per cent and has fallen unevenly, and the withdrawal from Afghanistan and the border have given the opposition two arguments that do not require a briefing. The unemployment figure is real, and so is the fact that prices are about twenty per cent higher than when he took office.

Money and the machinery are in place. The campaign began with about 100 million dollars raised, much of it small donations, and the party's national committee has spent the past two years rebuilding the operation that the previous president dismantled. The Republican field was still forming, with the former president the clear front-runner and a governor expected to enter in the summer. Two declared candidates have raised little and polled less, and the party's large donors have stayed with the incumbent.

The institutional risk is the one the campaign cannot manage. The president is the subject of a special counsel investigation into the handling of classified documents, and his son is under investigation by a federal prosecutor in Delaware. Neither case has produced charges, and both will produce news during a campaign in which the opposition's own candidate may be on trial in four separate cases.

What the announcement changes is the shape of the next eighteen months. A rematch that most voters say they do not want is now the most likely outcome, and the campaign will be fought over turnout in the six states that decide the college, which is why the video was released in the morning and the travel began in the afternoon. The age question will be answered by the schedule rather than by the argument, and the schedule is lighter than it was four years ago.
