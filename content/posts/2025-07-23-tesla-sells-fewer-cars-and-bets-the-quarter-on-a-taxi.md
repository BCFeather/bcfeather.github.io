description: "Tesla reported a 12 per cent fall in quarterly revenue on 23 July as deliveries dropped and margins narrowed, and spent the call describing a robotaxi business that has ten cars on the road."
---

Deliveries fell 13.5 per cent to 384,122 vehicles in the quarter, revenue fell 12 per cent to about 22.5 billion dollars, and automotive revenue fell faster than the total, which is what happens when a carmaker sells fewer cars at a lower price and earns less from the sale of regulatory credits.

Margins told the same story from the other side. Operating income fell by more than 40 per cent, the automotive gross margin stayed in the mid teens on an adjusted basis, and the company's profit now depends more on energy storage and services than it did a year ago, when the car was the whole business. A revenue decline of that size in the quarter after a cheap model was promised is the cost of a price cut that did not produce the volume the company had forecast, and it is also the cost of a product line that has not been replaced since 2020.

Energy storage was the bright line. Deployments rose to a record and the division's margin was healthy, and Megapack has become a business with a queue and not a sideline, which is the part of the company that reads like an industrial supplier and not like a growth story.

Robotaxi is the story the call was about. The service opened in Austin in June with about ten Model Y vehicles, a safety monitor in the front seat, a geofenced area and daylight hours only, and the company described expansion to more cities as a matter of months and not years.

Comparisons to a certain competitor are unavoidable. A fleet of ten cars with a supervisor is a pilot and not a service, the regulatory environment in Texas is permissive and the one in California is not, and the gap between a demonstration and a business is measured in permits, insurance and the cost per mile. A supervisor in the front seat is the cheapest way to satisfy a regulator and the most expensive way to run a fleet, because the cost per mile stays near the cost of a taxi with a driver, which is the number that determines whether the business can scale.

Capital spending stays high and the balance sheet carries it. The company is building factories for a cheaper model and for batteries, and it is funding that from cash and not debt, which is comfortable while the energy business grows and uncomfortable if the car business keeps shrinking.

What would settle the year is whether the cheaper model arrives on schedule and whether the taxi expands beyond a geofence. A car at a lower price would restore volume, a taxi in two more cities would restore the story, and the company currently needs both. A cheaper model that ships in the first half would put volume back into a factory that has been running below capacity for a year, and it would also compress the margin further, so the company is trading profit for units in a market that has stopped paying a premium for its badge.
