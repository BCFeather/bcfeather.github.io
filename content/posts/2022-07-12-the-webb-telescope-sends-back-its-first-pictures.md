The first scientific images from the James Webb Space Telescope were released on Tuesday, and they arrived five at a time in a broadcast from the space agency's headquarters. The telescope was launched on Christmas Day in 2021 and it has spent six months unfolding and cooling and calibrating. The pictures were taken in the first weeks of operation and they were chosen to show what the instrument can do rather than to answer a single question. Astronomers had been waiting for them since the launch.

Hold a grain of sand at arm's length and the patch of sky it covers contains thousands of galaxies, in the deepest image taken so far. Some of them are so distant that their light left them within a billion years of the beginning, and the telescope's mirror resolves details that the older ones could not. The picture was built from more than a hundred exposures and it was processed in days rather than weeks. The astronomers said the number of galaxies in it was larger than the models had predicted.

Then there are the other four frames: a dying star and the gas it has thrown off, five galaxies in collision, and the spectrum of an exoplanet's atmosphere. The spectrum matters most for the search for life, because it shows the absorption lines of water and of clouds in the light that passes through the atmosphere. The planet is a gas giant that orbits a star in the constellation of Sagittarius, and the measurement was made in a few hours of observing time.

Compared with Hubble, two things matter. Its mirror is six and a half metres across and it is made of eighteen segments that were aligned in space, and it observes in the infrared, which is the part of the spectrum that carries the light of the oldest galaxies. The telescope can also measure the composition of an atmosphere from a spectrum, which the older one could do only for a few objects. The cost was about ten billion dollars and the mission is planned to last at least ten years.

Relief came first among the groups that had been waiting for this, and appetite came second. The teams that had been allocated time in the first year said the data were better than the simulations, and the ones who had not been allocated time began to write proposals for the next rounds. The images were released to the public without a restriction, and the raw data will follow in the coming days. The first papers are expected within weeks, and the first surprises are expected before that.

Five pictures amount to a promise that covers several fields at once. It will look at the atmospheres of planets around other stars, at the formation of galaxies and at the dust that hides the birth of stars in the Milky Way. The first pictures are a demonstration and the science will follow, and the questions that it was built to answer are the ones that the last thirty years of astronomy have been unable to. The next decade of the field will be shaped by what it finds in the first year.
