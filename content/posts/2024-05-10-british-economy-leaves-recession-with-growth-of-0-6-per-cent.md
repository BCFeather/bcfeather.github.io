British economy grew 0.6 per cent in the first quarter, the fastest quarterly pace since the reopening of 2021, after a shallow recession in the second half of last year. Services grew 0.7 per cent and manufacturing was flat. The figure was published on 10 May. The quarterly figure was above the 0.4 per cent that economists had forecast, and the revision to the previous quarter turned a contraction into a small fall.

Composition tells the story. Health, transport and professional services added output, while construction fell and the factories that make chemicals and metals continued to struggle with the price of energy. A recovery led by services is a recovery that depends on consumer spending rather than on investment. Retail sales recovered as the weather improved, and the households that had cut back on eating out began to spend again, which is the part of the recovery that the shops can feel.

Output per person is still below its level before the pandemic, which is the number the government has been asked about in every interview since the data appeared. A growth rate that is positive can coexist with a standard of living that has not recovered, and the two numbers are published side by side. The figure is about two per cent below where it stood at the end of 2019, and the shortfall is largest in the regions that depended on manufacturing and on public spending.

Household sector is the reason. Real wages have begun to rise again as inflation has fallen, and the saving ratio has stayed high because the households that hold mortgages have not yet met the higher payments. A saving ratio that is high is demand that is being postponed rather than lost. The saving ratio was close to ten per cent, well above its pre-pandemic level, and the households with fixed-rate mortgages have not yet met the payments that the new rates imply.

Rate-setters are watching the composition. Growth driven by services and by government spending is growth that does not relieve the pressure on prices in the way that investment would, and the committee has said it wants to see business investment before it cuts. A recovery without investment is a recovery that the central bank treats cautiously. Investment fell again in the quarter, and the surveys of the smaller firms show that the cost of borrowing is the reason they are postponing projects that would have added capacity.

What the quarter shows is that an economy can leave a recession and remain smaller than it was, and the political argument about the last two years is now an argument about the level rather than about the direction. Whether the level recovers depends on productivity, and the productivity figure has grown more slowly than at any time since the war, which is the constraint underneath the quarterly number.
