Time named Taylor Swift its person of the year on 6 December, the first musician to take the title on the strength of a touring year rather than a political one. The magazine's citation described her as the protagonist of a cultural story, and the interview that accompanied it ran to several thousand words in which she talked about the tour, the re-recordings and the machinery of fame with a directness that her songs rarely allow. The issue went to print with four covers.

The numbers behind the decision were unusual even for her. The Eras Tour played 60 dates in 2023 across North and South America, with a set list of 44 songs that ran three hours and fifteen minutes, and it became the highest-grossing concert tour ever recorded, with reported ticket revenue above a billion dollars before the 2024 legs in Asia, Europe and Australia. The film of the tour, released in October, took about 250 million dollars at the box office, and the album of re-recorded material released in July sold more copies in a week than any other record that year.

The economic effect was the part that newspapers found easiest to write about. Cities reported hotel occupancy at levels normally reserved for sporting finals, transport authorities added trains, and in Seattle a seismometer registered the audience's movement as a small earthquake. Economists at a consulting firm put the American leg alone at more than five billion dollars of spending, a figure that circulated widely and was repeated by mayors who had never been to a concert.

Her year was also a case study in control. The re-recordings existed because the masters of her first six albums had been sold twice without her consent, and the project turned a business grievance into a second catalogue that now competes with the first. The tour was managed by a team that declined sponsorship, kept ticket prices below the market clearing level and then watched a ticketing platform collapse under the demand, which produced a lawsuit, a congressional hearing and a set of state laws about fees.

Politics followed the story without ever being invited. Her Instagram post urging fans to register to vote produced a surge in registrations that the non-partisan organization tracking it called unprecedented, and her relationship with a football player turned a sport that had ignored her audience into a weekly television event. She said almost nothing about any of it, and the silence was treated as a statement by everyone who wrote about her.

What Time's editors were buying with the title is a year in which one person's labour organized the attention of a very large number of others, and did so without a war, a scandal or a campaign to explain it. The magazine has given the title to musicians before, and never to one whose year was this commercial and this self-directed. The issue closed with a sentence about the difference between being famous and being the weather, which is the kind of line that reads better in December than it will in ten years.
