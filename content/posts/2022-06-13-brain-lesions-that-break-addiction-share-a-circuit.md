Lesion locations were mapped in two cohorts of patients with focal brain damage, 67 and 62, onto a brain atlas, and computed the network functionally connected to each location with connectome data from 1,000 people. The paper appeared in *Nature Medicine*, 13 June 2022. Lesions that disrupted smoking addiction occurred in many different places, but they shared a pattern of connectivity: positive to the dorsal cingulate, the lateral prefrontal cortex and the insula, negative to the medial prefrontal and temporal cortex. This pattern proved reproducible across the two cohorts. An independent cohort of 186 patients with focal damage and alcohol addiction risk scores confirmed the generalizability, and a comparison against 37 other neuropsychological variables showed that the pattern was specific to addiction.
