A prison camp at Olenivka, in the part of the Donetsk region that Russia controls, was struck on Friday and about fifty prisoners were killed, according to the two sides. The prisoners were Ukrainian soldiers who had been captured at Mariupol, and they were held in a compound that the separatist administration runs. Russia said the camp had been hit by a rocket that the Ukrainian army fired, and Ukraine said the camp had been hit from inside to hide the mistreatment of the prisoners. The two accounts agree on the number of the dead and on nothing else.

The Russian defence ministry published pictures of the wreckage and said the barracks had been destroyed by an artillery rocket carrying a cluster warhead. The Ukrainian intelligence service said the explosion had been caused by a device that was placed inside the building and that the Russian side had moved the prisoners there to create a pretext. The Ukrainian president said the attack was a war crime and called for the international organizations to send investigators. Neither account has been independently verified.

This camp had appeared in the news before. The soldiers who were captured at Mariupol were held there after the siege ended in May, and the relatives said that the conditions were poor and that the men had been beaten. The Red Cross has said that it has asked for access to the site since the spring and has been given it only once. The prisoners included the fighters from the Azov regiment, and the Russian officials had spoken about putting them on trial.

Politically the effect came at once. The United Nations said it was ready to send a team, and the Red Cross said it would go if it was given the guarantees for the safety of the staff. The American government said it had no evidence that the Ukrainians had fired the rocket, and the European Union said the strike showed the character of the Russian occupation. The Russian government said it would invite the international organizations that it chose.

Neither statement mentions the military context, which is awkward for both. The camp is about fifteen kilometres from the front and it is within the range of the artillery that both sides use. The Ukrainian army has been striking the supply lines in the region with the rockets that it has received, and the Russian side has been moving the prisoners to the places that are farther from the fighting. The strike came on the same day that the grain corridor was being prepared.

The question of the prisoners now belongs to the international courts. The two sides will present their evidence and the organizations that are allowed in will describe what they see, and the truth will be established slowly or not at all. The families of the men who were captured at Mariupol have been waiting for news since May, and the news that arrived was the worst kind. The war has produced so many of these incidents that the world has begun to rank them, and this one will be ranked among the worst.
