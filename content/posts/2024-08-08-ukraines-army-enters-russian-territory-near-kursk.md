Ukrainian units crossed into the Kursk region of Russia on 6 August and advanced across several districts within a week, in an operation that the Ukrainian command described as an attempt to move the war onto Russian territory. The incursion was the first sustained ground operation of its kind since the invasion began. The operation used armour, electronic warfare and short-range air defence in a combination that the Ukrainian army had not shown before. The advance covered a stretch of countryside that is flat and crossed by few paved roads, which meant that the columns could be seen from a distance and still moved faster than the defence could react.

Surprise is the explanation. A border sector that had been held by conscripts and by border guards was not prepared for an armoured assault, and the Russian response took days to organize. A front that is quiet is a front that is thinly held, which is what the planners counted on. The conscripts who were captured were mostly young men on their first posting, and their presence is what made the sector weak. The border posts were manned by border guards and by young conscripts, and the units that fled left their documents and their equipment behind.

Purpose was political as much as military. A government that can show its citizens that the war has moved is a government that can argue for more weapons, and the operation was announced with that argument. A battlefield success is a currency that a government spends in capitals abroad. The government's argument in Washington and in Berlin was that the incursion showed the army could still take ground, which is an argument about weapons rather than about territory.

Cost is the redeployment. Units that were holding positions in the east were moved north, and the pressure that they had been applying on other sectors eased. A diversion is a diversion only if the enemy moves, and the enemy moved. The redeployment removed units from the direction of Pokrovsk, where the Russian army had been advancing, and the advance slowed within weeks.

Prisoner exchange was an early result. A large number of Russian conscripts were captured, and the exchange that followed returned Ukrainian soldiers who had been held for months. A capture that produces an exchange is a capture that has a value beyond the ground it holds. The exchange was mediated by a third country, and the families of the returned soldiers were the audience that the operation was also aimed at.

What the operation establishes is that the war's geography can change quickly where one side is unprepared. A border that was treated as a formality became a front in a week, and the lesson for both armies is about where the next quiet sector is. The next quiet sector will be held more densely than this one was, which is the change that the operation has already caused.
