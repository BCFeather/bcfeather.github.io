ASML published results on 19 October, and net sales were 5.78 billion euros for the quarter, above the 5.4 billion forecast. Bookings were 8.9 billion euros, the second highest on record, and the backlog reached 38 billion. The company said it could not build machines fast enough to fill the orders. The guidance for 2023 was the surprise. The company said sales would grow about 25 per cent to 7.5 billion euros a quarter, and analysts had expected 30 per cent. The chief executive said the constraint was capacity rather than demand, and he said the company was adding cleanroom space in Veldhoven and in Asia. The backlog was 38 billion euros, which is more than a year of sales at the current rate.

Printing the finest layers of a chip requires machines that only ASML supplies, and its extreme ultraviolet tools are the only ones on the market. Its customers are TSMC, Samsung and Intel, and each is building capacity for 2023 and 2024. The backlog reflected orders placed before the downturn in memory.

Memory was the weak part of the market. The company said orders from memory makers would fall in 2023 because they were cutting capital spending, and the shortfall would be made up by logic. It said the mix would shift towards the most advanced tools, which carry higher prices and better margins. Memory makers were expected to cut spending by 40 per cent, and logic makers to raise it by 20.

Export controls were the risk that analysts asked about. The United States had announced restrictions on the sale of advanced chip equipment to China on 7 October, and the rules covered some of ASML's immersion tools. The company said the direct effect was about 5 per cent of sales, and it said it was seeking clarity from the American and Dutch governments. The Dutch rules were announced in March 2023, and they covered two of the company's older tools.

Negotiations with Washington over the rules had been going on for months. The company's extreme ultraviolet machines had never been sold to China, and the dispute was about the older immersion tools. The government said in March 2023 that it would require licences for those exports as well. The shares fell 3 per cent on the guidance and recovered within a week. The company returned 8 billion euros to shareholders in the year through dividends and buybacks, and it said it would do the same in 2023. Its order book gave it visibility that most equipment makers did not have.

ASML's bookings fell 40 per cent in the first quarter of 2023 as memory customers stopped ordering, and the company said the second half would be stronger. The results were the last quarter before the downturn reached the equipment makers. Its sales rose 30 per cent for the year, and the stock ended 2023 up 30 per cent.
