Nippon Steel agreed to buy United States Steel for about 14.1 billion dollars in cash on 18 December, or 55 dollars a share, which is about 40 per cent above the price before the bid was reported. The offer is about 55 dollars a share, and the deal is the largest purchase of an American company by a Japanese one in decades. The board approved the deal on 17 December, and the two companies said the transaction would close in the second half of next year. The two boards approved the transaction at the weekend, and the companies said the closing would take about nine months.

A premium prices the assets. The American mills have been rebuilt with the electric furnaces, and the demand for the steel for the cars and the appliances is the most profitable in the world. A mill that is new is a cost that is sunk. The company has about 20 million tons of the capacity, and the combined group would be the third largest outside China.

A rival bid was higher on paper. The other suitor had offered about 54 dollars but with the structure that the union had backed, and the board chose the cash. A cash that is certain is a premium that is paid. The other bid came from a rival American mill, and the board said the cash offer was the one that the shareholders prefer.

Union leaders object to the deal. The steelworkers have said the deal was made behind their backs, and the politicians have said the American steel should stay American. A foreign owner is a review that is certain. The union has asked the regulators to block the deal, and the senators from the steel states have written to the treasury.

Regulators will look at it. The committee that reviews the foreign investment will examine the deal, and the analysts expect the conditions rather than the block. A condition that is imposed is a price that is paid. The review can take up to 90 days, and the company has said it will keep the headquarters in Pittsburgh. The treasury has said it would review the deal with the committee that examines the foreign investments, and the review can be extended.

What the deal shows is that the consolidation of the steel has moved across the borders and the politics has moved against it, and the two will meet in the review. A deal that is paid in cash and opposed at home is a deal that the politics will price, and the pricing is the risk. The company has said it will keep the American management and the plants, and the promise is the part the review will test. The combined company would be the third largest in the world outside China, and the ranking is what the buyers and the regulators both see.
