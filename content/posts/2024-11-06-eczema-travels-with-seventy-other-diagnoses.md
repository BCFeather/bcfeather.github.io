Atopic eczema is a skin condition, and the conditions that accompany it have usually been studied one at a time. A paper in *Nature Communications* on 6 November 2024 studies seventy-one of them at once and ranks their associations in a single primary care population. A national record database supplies numbers that no single clinic can, and it supplies them for outcomes that no cohort was ever designed to study prospectively.

The data are the Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum records from 1997 to 2023. Up to 3.6 million people with eczema were compared with 16.8 million without, matched on age, sex and general practice. Seventy-one outcomes invite false positives, and the authors widened intervals rather than correcting for multiplicity, which is a choice a reader is free to evaluate.

Seventy-one cohort studies were run, one per outcome, and Cox regression supplied adjusted hazard ratios with 99 per cent confidence intervals. The widened interval reflects the number of comparisons rather than a shortage of data. Food allergy is the association that clinicians already act on, and the value of the study is that it places the remaining seventy relative to it instead of in isolation.

Food allergy is the strongest association, at a hazard ratio of 4.02. Other outcomes follow at smaller magnitudes, and the ordering is the contribution, since no clinician can act on seventy-one associations at once. Eczema is treated with immunosuppressants, so some of the associations may reflect the treatment rather than the disease, and the records do not distinguish the two.

Matching is the design's defence against confounding. Age, sex and practice are the variables a record-linked cohort can control, while smoking, body mass and deprivation are not, which leaves residual confounding that the paper cannot exclude. A ranked list is actionable in a way that a folder of separate findings is not, and actionability is what a guideline committee needs before it writes anything.

The study is observational and cannot establish that eczema causes any of the outcomes. What it can do is tell a patient and a clinician which conditions are worth watching, and a ranked list is what a screening aid should offer. Record linkage cannot capture severity, and severity is the variable that would stratify the list, which is the improvement a linked clinical cohort would supply.

On the modelling side, the scale is the argument. A single association in a small cohort is a hypothesis, and seventy-one associations in a national database are a pattern that a guideline can use, provided the pattern holds in another health system. The study's scale also makes it a baseline, since a later cohort drawn from the same database can show whether the pattern is shifting as treatment changes. A pattern that is stable over two decades is evidence for a mechanism, and an unstable one is evidence for a change in practice. Whether the ordering survives in a different system is the test the next cohort would supply, and that cohort is the natural follow-up.
