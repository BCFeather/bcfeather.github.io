Chinese exports fell 6.4 per cent in October from a year earlier, the customs administration said on 7 November, after a fall of 6.2 per cent in September. The imports rose 3 per cent, and the surplus narrowed to about 56 billion dollars. The figure was in line with the forecast, and the year-to-date exports have fallen by about 5 per cent, the first annual decline since 2016.

Europe is the weak market. The shipments to the European Union fell by about 12 per cent, and the ones to the United States by about 9, while the trade with the Association of Southeast Asian Nations held. A market that is in a recession buys fewer of the goods. The shipments to Russia rose by about 20 per cent, and the trade with the belt and road countries now exceeds the trade with the west.

Imports are the signal. The purchases of the integrated circuits rose by about 2 per cent and the crude oil by about 8, which suggests the factories and the refineries are running. An import that rises is a domestic demand that is not collapsing. The imports from the European Union fell, and the ones from the Association of Southeast Asian Nations rose as the regional supply chains deepened.

Prices are part of the fall. The prices of the electric vehicles and the batteries have fallen by about a fifth, and the volumes have grown, which is the pattern of a market where the Chinese firms compete on the price. A price that falls is a value that shrinks. The car exports rose by about 50 per cent to a record, and the electric models account for about a third of the total. The solar panels and the lithium batteries have fallen by about a third in price, and the volumes have grown by about a fifth.

Base effects explain the rest. A base that changes is a rate that misleads. The trade surplus with the United States widened, and the one with the European Union narrowed as the shipments fell. The comparison is with October last year, which was strong before the demand turned, and the economists expect the fall to narrow in the winter as the base weakens. The base was unusually high because the shipments in October last year had been held back by the port congestion in the summer.

What the month shows is that the exports have become a price story rather than a volume story, and the volume is the part that the trade partners are watching. An export sector that competes on the price is an export sector that deflates its own market, and the deflation is the reason the surplus worries the partners. The customs data are the first monthly test of the export sector since the stimulus began, and the second month of the fall is what the analysts cite.
