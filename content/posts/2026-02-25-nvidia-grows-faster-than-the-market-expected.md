Going into the quarter, the question was whether the growth rate would slow, and the answer was that it accelerated. NVIDIA reported revenue of 68.1 billion dollars for the fourth quarter of its fiscal year, which ended on 25 January, an increase of 73 per cent from a year earlier and 20 per cent from the previous quarter. Analysts had expected about 66.2 billion, and adjusted earnings per share of 1.62 dollars beat a consensus of 1.54.

Data centre revenue was 62.3 billion dollars, up 75 per cent year on year, and the segment supplied 91 per cent of the quarter's total. Compute contributed 51.3 billion and networking 11.0 billion, and networking grew 263 per cent, which is a rate that reflects the shift from selling chips to selling racks, clusters and switches. Gross margin was 75 per cent on both a GAAP and a non-GAAP basis, which is a software-like margin on a hardware business.

Full-year revenue was 215.9 billion dollars, up 65 per cent, and data centre revenue for the year was 194 billion, up 68 per cent. The company describes its data centre business as having scaled by nearly thirteen times since the launch of ChatGPT in fiscal 2023, which is the kind of comparison a management team makes when it wants to argue that a growth rate is structural rather than cyclical.

Customer concentration is the risk that the numbers describe. Hyperscalers remained the largest customer category at slightly over half of data centre revenue, and the company says growth was led by the rest of its customers, which is a diversification claim that a single quarter can support and cannot prove. A business where four or five buyers account for half the revenue is a business exposed to their capital spending plans.

Gaming was 3.73 billion dollars, up 47 per cent, and the company warned that supply constraints would be a headwind in the next quarter. Professional visualization reached 1.32 billion, up 159 per cent, and automotive was 604 million, up 6 per cent, which is small enough to be irrelevant to the total and large enough to be a disappointment relative to the surrounding growth.

Guidance is where the argument moves from results to expectations. Management said it expects sequential revenue growth through calendar 2026 that exceeds what was included in the 500 billion dollar Blackwell and Rubin opportunity it described earlier, and it said it has inventory and supply commitments extending into calendar 2027. Those commitments are a statement about demand that the company believes it can see, and they are also a liability if demand turns.

Market difficulty is that a company growing at this rate at this margin has no valuation that is obviously wrong. A buyer is paying for the durability of the growth, and the quarter supplies evidence that the growth is continuing and no evidence about when it stops.
