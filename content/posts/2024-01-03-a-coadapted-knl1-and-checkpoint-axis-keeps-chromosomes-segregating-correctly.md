Chromosome segregation depends on a checkpoint that delays division until every kinetochore is attached, and the scaffold that recruits checkpoint proteins is KNL1. A paper in *PNAS* on 3 January 2024 shows that the scaffold and the checkpoint evolved as a pair. An interface is a set of contacts, and contacts that must fit together are contacts that drift together. Aneuploidy is a consequence with clinical weight, and weight is what makes a mechanistic paper interesting to oncology. A rebuilt checkpoint is evidence that the interface cannot be swapped, and swapping is what a therapeutic strategy would attempt.

Co-adaptation means a change in one partner is matched by a change in the other, and matched changes are what keep an interface functional. The paper identifies the motifs on both sides and follows them across species. Motif comparison across species is the comparative method, and the method produces a hypothesis that the substitution experiments then test. A target with a narrow window is worth pursuing when the alternative is no target at all. Structural targets are the ones a small molecule can address, and the interface is a surface rather than a pocket.

An interface that co-evolves resists substitution, since a component from a distant species does not fit. The experiments test substitution and find that it fails. A component that does not fit is a component that fails, and failure is the readout the paper uses. Co-adaptation is a general phenomenon at protein interfaces, and the checkpoint is a well-characterized case of it. A surface target is harder to drug than a pocket, and difficulty is why structural interfaces are often left to biology rather than to chemistry.

Failure of substitution is evidence for co-adaptation rather than for conservation of sequence. Sequence conservation is the weaker signal, and the functional test is what the paper adds. Sequence conservation tells that something mattered, and the functional test tells what it did. Well-characterized cases are what the field uses to teach the phenomenon, and teaching is how it becomes part of practice.

The checkpoint is the cell's fidelity mechanism, and a mechanism that depends on a matched pair cannot be borrowed across a large distance. A matched pair cannot travel, so the checkpoint's components are more constrained than their functions suggest.

Missegregation produces aneuploidy, and aneuploidy is a hallmark of cancer, so the interface is a candidate target. Narrow windows are normal for targets that are structural rather than enzymatic, and the constraint is not unique to this one. A case that teaches the phenomenon is a case that outlives its own result, and outliving is what a good model organism does.

A target that is species-specific is a target with a narrow window, and the window is the constraint a drug programme would face. A fidelity mechanism that is species-specific is a mechanism that evolution had to rebuild, and rebuilding is what the co-adaptation records.
