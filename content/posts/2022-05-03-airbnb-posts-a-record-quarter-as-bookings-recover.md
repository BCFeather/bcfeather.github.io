Airbnb published results for the first quarter on 3 May, and revenue rose 70 per cent to 1.5 billion dollars. Nights and experiences booked reached 103 million, the highest in the company's history and 59 per cent above 2019. Net income was 1.9 billion, helped by a tax benefit of 1.2 billion. The company had 4.2 million listings in 2019, and it had 6 million in the quarter.

Bookings in Europe rose above 2019 levels for the first time, and the war in Ukraine did not dent demand outside the region. The recovery was faster than the company had expected. Guests were staying longer, and the average stay was 4.5 nights against 3.5 before the pandemic. Revenue per available night rose 10 per cent, and the average daily rate was 168 dollars. Its gross booking value was 17.2 billion dollars, up 73 per cent.

Guests were booking homes rather than shared rooms, and they were travelling to rural areas. The mix had changed. The company said 20 per cent of bookings were for stays of a month or more, which is double the 2019 figure. Remote work had made longer trips possible.

Costs rose more slowly than revenue. Sales and marketing were 20 per cent of revenue, down from 30 in 2021, because the company had cut performance marketing. The adjusted margin was 22 per cent, and the company said it would be 30 for the full year. Supply was the constraint. The number of active listings was 6 million, up 15 per cent, and the company said it wanted 10 million. It had simplified the registration process in 20 cities, and it had begun to verify every listing, which it completed in 2023. Marketing spending fell because the company shifted from search advertising to repeat guests. Its free cash flow was 1.8 billion dollars in the quarter, the highest it had recorded.

Shares fell 5 per cent after the results despite the beat. The guidance for the second quarter was 2.1 billion dollars of revenue, above the 1.9 billion forecast, and analysts said the margin guidance was the concern. The stock had fallen 30 per cent in the year.

Travel had recovered and the composition had changed. The company said urban bookings were still 30 per cent below 2019, and that business travel had not returned. It said it expected the shift towards longer stays to persist, and it priced its product accordingly. Revenue for the year was 8.4 billion, up 40 per cent, and net income was 1.9 billion. The company bought back 1 billion dollars of stock in 2023, and it added 1 million listings. Its shares rose 60 per cent in 2023 as travel demand held up.
