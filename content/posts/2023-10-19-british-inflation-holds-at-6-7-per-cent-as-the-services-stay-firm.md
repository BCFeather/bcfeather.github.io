---

British consumer prices rose 6.7 per cent in the year to September, the Office for National Statistics said on 18 October, the same as in August and above the 6.6 per cent that the economists had expected. The core measure was 6.1 per cent. The figure was the second month without a fall, and the measure is still more than three times the target two years after the first increase in the rates. The figure was a tenth above the forecast, and the measure has fallen in only three of the last eight months.

Services pushed the measure up. The services inflation rose to 6.9 per cent from 6.8, and the insurance, the restaurants and the hotels were the strongest, which is the part that follows the wages. A service that is priced by the hour is a price that the labour market sets. The insurance for the motor vehicles rose by about 30 per cent over the year, and the carriers have blamed the repair costs and the thefts.

Fuel came down. The petrol price fell by about 4 per cent on the month as the crude eased, and the energy contributed a small negative to the annual rate. A tank that is cheaper is a headline that stops rising. The diesel price rose even as the petrol fell, and the hauliers have said the cost will reach the shelves by the winter. The petrol is about 5 per cent cheaper than a year earlier, and the diesel is about 2 per cent cheaper, which is the first annual fall since the winter.

Food eased more slowly than hoped. The food inflation fell to about 12.2 per cent from 13.6, and the bread and the cereals were still rising by about 14, which is the line the shoppers notice. A loaf that costs more is a statistic that the household does not need. The vegetables fell by about 3 per cent as the harvest was better than last year, and the eggs rose as the supply recovered from the avian flu.

Wages are the reason for the caution. The private sector pay has been rising by about 8 per cent, and the committee has said the services will not fall to the level consistent with the target until it slows to about 5. A pay settlement is a price that arrives in the next quarter. The unemployment rate is about 4.2 per cent, and the vacancies have fallen for a year but remain above the level before the pandemic.

What the month shows is that the disinflation has stalled at the level where the wages dominate, and the stall is why the bank has not yet declared the peak. A rate at 5.25 per cent is a rate that the committee can defend while the wages are at this level, and the level is the test.
