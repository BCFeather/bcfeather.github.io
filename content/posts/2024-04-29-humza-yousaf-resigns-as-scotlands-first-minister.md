Humza Yousaf announced on the twenty-ninth of April that he would resign as first minister of Scotland and as leader of the Scottish National Party, thirteen months after taking both posts. The decision came four days after he ended the power-sharing agreement with the Scottish Green Party, which had provided the votes that his minority government needed to pass budgets and legislation.

Chain of events that produced the resignation began with a decision about climate targets. The government had promised to reduce emissions by seventy-five per cent by 2030, and it concluded in April that the target could not be met, and it proposed to keep the interim goal while changing the way progress was measured. The Greens said they could not support a government that abandoned a commitment that the two parties had made together, and Yousaf ended the agreement rather than accept their conditions.

Arithmetic of the parliament then turned against him. The SNP had sixty-three of the hundred and twenty-nine seats, and the Greens had seven, which left the government without a majority. The Greens said they would support a motion of no confidence in the first minister, the Conservatives and Labour said they would support a separate motion in the government, and Yousaf said he would not seek a deal with a party that had just withdrawn its support.

Record of his term was short and dominated by events outside Scotland. He took office in March 2023 after the resignation of Nicola Sturgeon, and he inherited a government that had been in power for sixteen years and that was divided over the strategy for independence. He also spoke publicly about the death of relatives of his wife in Gaza, and he clashed with the British government over its refusal to grant visas to members of his family.

Succession was settled within a week. John Swinney, a former deputy first minister who had served in the cabinets of Sturgeon and of Alex Salmond, was elected leader of the SNP on the sixth of May without a contest after other candidates withdrew, and he became first minister the following day with the support of the Greens, who returned to a less formal arrangement.

The Scottish parliament then confirmed Swinney as first minister by a majority of the assembly, with the Greens voting in favour. The two motions of no confidence were never put to a vote, since Yousaf's resignation removed the first and the second fell away when the new first minister took office.

Consequence of the twenty-ninth of April is a party that has now had three leaders in eighteen months and a government that has lost the discipline that kept it together. The SNP has been in office since 2007 and has not won a majority in the parliament since 2011, and its independence strategy depends on a vote that the British government refuses to authorize. What the resignation established was that a coalition built on a shared commitment can be ended by a single disagreement, and that the party's remaining years in office will be spent managing a minority.
