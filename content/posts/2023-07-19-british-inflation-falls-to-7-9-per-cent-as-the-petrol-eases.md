title: "British inflation falls to 7.9 per cent as the petrol eases"

description: "The June figure came in below the forecast and the first below 8 per cent since the spring of 2022, with the core easing to 6.9."
---

British consumer prices rose by 7.9 per cent over the year to June, down from 8.7 in May and below the forecast of 8.2. The monthly change was 0.1 per cent, and the core measure eased to 6.9 from 7.1. The figure is the first below 8 per cent since March 2022. The figure was the lowest since March 2022, and the measure has now fallen for two months from the peak of 11.1 per cent in October. The retail prices index, which is used for the indexation of the pensions and the rail fares, rose by about 8.0 per cent.

Petrol did the work. The fuel prices fell by about 1.7 per cent on the month and about 22 per cent over the year, and the one of the gas by about 4 as the price cap fell in July. The electricity rose by about 1, and the effect of the cap is the one the statisticians will show next month. The petrol was about 1.43 pounds a litre at the end of June, down about 25 per cent from the peak of the autumn. The cap on the household bills fell to about 2,074 pounds a year in July, which is about 400 below the level of the spring.

Food slowed a little. The prices of the food rose by about 17.3 per cent, down from 18.7, and the one of the bread and the cereals by about 15. The grocery inflation is still the highest in the group of the rich countries, and the discounts at the supermarkets have been the first sign of the turn. The prices of the milk fell by about 5 per cent on the month, and the one of the eggs by about 4 as the supply improved.

Services held at about 7.2 per cent. The restaurants rose by about 8, and the one of the rents by about 6 as the landlords passed on the mortgage costs. The wages have been rising by about 7.3 per cent in the private sector, which is the number the committee has said it watches. The rents in the private sector rose by about 5.5 per cent over the year, which is the fastest since 2016.

Its core measure is the part that matters. A core at 6.9 per cent is about three times the target, and the bank's forecast of the autumn has been that it would fall to about 5 by the end of the year. The market had priced a peak of about 6 per cent on the policy rate. The bank has raised the rate thirteen times, and the market expects the peak at about 6 per cent in the winter.

This release is the one before the August meeting. A headline at 7.9 with the core at 6.9 is a better report than the market had feared, and the odds of a half-point rise on 3 August fell to about 30 per cent from about 60. The committee meets on 3 August, and the decision will be the first after the half-point rise of June.
