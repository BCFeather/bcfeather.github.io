description: "The strongest earthquake to hit Taiwan in twenty-five years struck off Hualien on 3 April, killing at least nine people and injuring more than a thousand, and it closed roads and damaged buildings while the island's chip factories kept running."
---Earthquake of magnitude 7.4 struck about twenty-five kilometres south-east of Hualien on Taiwan's east coast at fifty-eight minutes past seven in the morning on the third of April, at a depth of about fifteen kilometres. It was the strongest tremor since the earthquake of 1999 that killed more than two thousand people, and it was followed by hundreds of aftershocks, one of which reached magnitude 6.5. Shaking was felt across the island and in Taipei, where the metro stopped for more than an hour.

Damage was concentrated in Hualien, a city of about a hundred thousand people between the mountains and the sea. Several buildings tilted or partially collapsed, including a warehouse and a block of flats, and a road tunnel on the coastal highway was blocked by rockfalls. The highway that runs through the Taroko gorge, one of the island's main tourist routes, was closed by landslides, and the government said the repair would take months.

Death toll reported by the authorities began at nine and rose to seventeen as rescuers reached cut-off areas. More than a thousand people were injured, and about a hundred were trapped in tunnels or in buildings for hours before they were freed. Two dozen people were reported missing in the first days, most of them hikers on trails in the mountains behind Hualien, and search teams continued to work through the following week.

Industrial effect was smaller than the images suggested. Taiwan is the largest producer of advanced semiconductors, and its factories are built to withstand shaking, with foundations that isolate the building and with automatic shutdowns that stop the tools when the ground moves. The largest manufacturer, TSMC, evacuated some plants and said several tools had been damaged, and it resumed production within a day. Analysts said the loss of output was measured in hours rather than weeks.

Regional response was quick. Japan issued a tsunami warning for the islands of Okinawa and observed waves of about thirty centimetres, and the Philippines warned coastal communities before lifting its alert. China's government said it was willing to send rescue teams and equipment, and the offer was received through channels that the two governments use for disasters, which are among the few that remain open.

The Cause of the earthquake is the collision of two plates beneath the island. Taiwan sits where the Philippine Sea plate is pushed under the Eurasian plate, and the resulting faults produce frequent earthquakes and steep mountains. Building codes were tightened after 1999, and the performance of those codes was the subject of the engineering reviews that followed the third of April.

What the tremor showed was that preparation reduces deaths without preventing damage, and that the difference between a magnitude 7.4 and the earthquake of 1999 was mostly a matter of where the rupture happened. Rail services along the east coast were suspended for a day, and the government said more than three hundred aftershocks had been recorded in the first week. Schools and offices in Hualien were closed for two days while engineers assessed the buildings that had been damaged.
