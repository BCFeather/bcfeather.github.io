The year ends today as the warmest in the instrumental record, with the global average expected near 15.1 degrees, roughly a tenth of a degree above the previous record set in 2023. The figure that matters is the comparison with the pre-industrial baseline: the year is about 1.6 degrees above it, the first calendar year to exceed the 1.5 degree level that the Paris agreement adopted as a goal. A record that is set two years running is a record that has stopped being a surprise.

Threshold is a political number as much as a physical one. A single year above 1.5 degrees does not breach the agreement, which refers to a long-term average, and the scientists who produced the data have been careful to say so. A year is weather as well as climate, and the next La Niña will pull the average down. A long-term average is an average over twenty years, and a single year is a data point. A political threshold is a threshold that a diplomat negotiated and a physicist measures.

Pattern is not uniform. Europe warmed fastest, the Arctic warmed faster than the tropics, and the ocean absorbed most of the additional heat. A global average is a summary that hides the places where the change is largest, and the places where it is largest are not the places with the most people. Drivers were identified. The long-term trend from greenhouse gases accounts for most of the rise, and an El Niño in the first half of the year added a temporary push. A year that combines a strong natural variation with a rising baseline is a year that sets a record, and the next record will be set without the natural help. A natural variation that adds a tenth of a degree is a variation that the next year will subtract. A record set with natural help is a record that will be broken without it.

Consequences are already in the data. A warmer atmosphere holds more water, and the rainfall that arrives is more intense and less frequent, which is a pattern that a farmer experiences as drought followed by flood. A sea that is warmer expands, and the sea level rise that follows is a slow emergency. A rainfall that is more intense and less frequent is a rainfall that a soil cannot absorb.

What the year establishes is that the 1.5 degree target has been passed in practice before it has been abandoned in principle. A target that is missed is a target that still organizes policy, and the argument now is about how fast the overshoot is reversed. A target that has been passed is a target that still sets the direction of policy.
