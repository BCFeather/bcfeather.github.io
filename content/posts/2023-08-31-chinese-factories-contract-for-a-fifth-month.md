China's official manufacturing purchasing managers' index rose to 49.7 in August from 49.3 in July, the fifth month below the 50 mark. The new orders measure was about 50.2, which is the first above 50 since the spring, and the new export orders about 46.7. The production measure was about 51.9. The figure was in line with the forecast of about 49.6, and the Caixin index, published the next day, came in at 51. The new orders have been below 50 for five months before the August reading, which is the longest run since 2022. The production measure rose above 50 as the mills kept the runs high, and the one of the finished goods fell.

Demand improved a little. The orders from the domestic customers rose as the government's measures for the private firms were announced, and the ones from the exporters stayed weak. The survey's respondents said the infrastructure projects had been the part that generated the orders. The backlog of the orders rose to about 46, and the one of the imports to about 48 as the domestic demand improved. The property construction has been the largest drag, and the cement and the steel orders have fallen with it.

Prices were still weak. The output prices rose to about 49, and the input prices to about 48, as the commodity costs rebounded a little. The producer prices have fallen over the year for about ten months, and the deflation in the factory gate is still the largest in seven years. The producer prices fell by about 4.4 per cent over the year in July, which is the steepest since 2016.

Services held. The non-manufacturing index fell to 51 from 51.5, and the construction to about 50. The travel and the restaurants were the strongest, and the respondents said the summer bookings had been better than the spring. The catering and the hotels were above 55, and the transport was the second strongest. The respondents said the hotel bookings for the October holiday were about 20 per cent above the level of last year.

Employment softened again. The measure for the manufacturing rose to about 48.9, and the one for the services fell to about 46.8. The graduates entering the market have pushed the youth unemployment to about 21 per cent, and the government has stopped publishing the figure for the summer. The government has announced the measures for the private firms and the platform companies, and the effect has been small so far.

This report is the first in five months with the orders above 50. A factory sector that is still contracting while the orders improve is a sector at the turn, and the turn is the part that the fourth quarter will confirm. The economists expect the growth of about 4.4 per cent in the third quarter, and the target of about 5 is now out of reach.
