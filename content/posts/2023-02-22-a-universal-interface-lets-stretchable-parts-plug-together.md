Stretchable electronics have a common failure point, and it is not the material. A soft conductor can stretch, a soft substrate can bend, and the joint between them is where the device breaks. An interface that survives strain is the component the field has needed.

The interface reported in *Nature*, 16 February 2023 is designed for assembly rather than for a single device. The authors describe a universal interface that bonds stretchable components of different chemistries, so that a circuit can be built from modules and reconnected when a module is replaced.

The requirement is mechanical. Two soft materials in contact must transmit electrical current while their interface accommodates deformation, and a rigid solder joint concentrates stress at its edges. A soft joint distributes strain, and a distributed strain does not crack.

The requirement is also chemical. Elastomers and hydrogels bond through different chemistries, and an interface that works for one pair does not work for another. A universal interface is one whose bonding mechanism does not depend on the pair.

The assembly consequence is modularity. A device that is built from modules can be repaired, upgraded and tested in parts, and a wearable that is assembled from a sensor, a conductor and a power source can have one component replaced without discarding the rest.

The paper's demonstrations are the evidence. The authors show stretchable devices assembled through the interface and operated under strain, and the electrical performance is maintained rather than degraded.

The fatigue question is the one a product would ask. An interface that survives a hundred cycles is a laboratory result, and a wearable experiences thousands. The paper's cycling data indicate the direction and not the lifetime.

The encapsulation question follows. A soft device in contact with skin or with tissue has to be sealed, and a seal that is stiffer than the interface moves the failure to its edge. The materials have to be co-designed.

What the paper does not resolve is the manufacturing route. A laboratory assembly by hand is not a process, and a universal interface that can be printed or laminated would be the version that industry adopts.

For the field, the value is a standard part. Stretchable electronics have been a collection of bespoke devices, and an interface that is the same across devices is what turns a collection into a platform.

The medical use is the nearest application. A patch that monitors a signal has to conform to skin that moves, and a device that fails at its joints is a device that a patient notices.

The electrical resistance of the interface is a design variable. A joint that conducts well but stiffens the device is worse than one with slightly higher resistance that lets the whole assembly flex, and the paper's characterization reports both.
