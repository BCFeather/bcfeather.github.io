Kamala Harris named Tim Walz, the governor of Minnesota, as her running mate on 6 August, ending a selection process that had been conducted in public over two weeks. The governor is a former teacher and a member of the national guard, and he was chosen over several candidates from more competitive states. The shortlist had included senators and a governor from a state the party must win, and the choice of the safer option was reported as a decision about chemistry.

Geography is the calculation. A running mate from a state that the party already wins does not deliver its electoral votes, and the campaign argued that the selection was about the message rather than the map. A candidate who can speak to rural voters is a candidate who can campaign in the places the party lost. The governor's district is rural and conservative in parts, and his argument was that the party had stopped listening to those voters rather than that it had lost them.

Style was the contrast. The governor had built a reputation for describing the opposition's programme as strange rather than dangerous, and the phrase became the campaign's frame within a week. A frame that makes an opponent seem odd is a frame that avoids arguing about his policies. The phrase was picked up by the campaign's advertising within days, and the advertising that followed used it against the opposition's more extreme statements.

Record is the vulnerability. His handling of the pandemic and of a period of unrest after a killing by police in Minneapolis were examined within hours of the announcement, and the campaign had prepared answers. A governor's record is a longer record than a senator's, which is the risk of choosing one. The reviews of his pandemic record were already written by the opposing campaign, and the ticket had to answer them in the first week.

Money and the rallies followed. The campaign reported a surge in donations after the announcement and scheduled a tour of the swing states, and the ticket appeared together for the first time in Philadelphia. A tour that begins in one city and ends in another is a tour designed around the map that the campaign says is not the point. The tour was designed to introduce the pair in states that the campaign needs to hold rather than to expand the map.

What the selection shows is that the party has chosen to argue about character rather than about policy. A ticket that is presented as normal against an opponent presented as strange is a ticket that will be judged on whether it can hold the centre, and the centre is where the election will be decided. The centre is where a handful of states decide the result, and a ticket that is presented as moderate is a ticket that is asking those states to choose normal over change.
