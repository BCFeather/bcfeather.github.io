Nike reported results for the quarter to August on 29 September, and revenue rose 4 per cent to 12.7 billion dollars. Net income fell 22 per cent to 1.5 billion, and the gross margin fell 2.2 points to 44.3. The number that moved the stock was inventory, which had risen 44 per cent to 9.7 billion dollars.

Nike said it would cut prices to clear the stock, and it said the discounts would continue through the autumn. The company blamed late deliveries and a weak market in North America. It had ordered goods in 2021 when demand was strong and shipping was slow, and the goods arrived in 2022 when demand had changed. The shares fell 13 per cent on 30 September, the largest one-day fall since 2001. Several analysts cut their ratings the same morning, and one wrote that the company had lost control of its own supply chain. The chief executive said the problem would take two quarters to fix, and the market did not believe him.

Adidas had warned in August that its inventory was 35 per cent higher than a year earlier, and Puma reported a similar figure. The problem was general. The three companies had all misjudged the shift from online shopping back to shops, and all three had ordered too much of the wrong product. The discounts that followed cut prices across the sector. The company said 65 per cent of the stock was in its own warehouses, up from 50 a year earlier.

China was the other weak spot. Sales there fell 16 per cent in the quarter, and the company said lockdowns had closed a third of its stores in April. The market had been the growth engine for a decade, and the decline was the first in five years. The company said it would keep investing and that the recovery would come in 2023. Foot Locker, its largest wholesale customer, said its own stock was 30 per cent above normal.

It wanted to sell more directly, and it had ended contracts with several retailers. That shift left it holding stock that the retailers would have carried, and it made the inventory problem larger than it would have been. The quarter's numbers were the last under the chief executive who had led the company since 2020. The stock recovered in the spring as the discounts ended and as China reopened.

Nike's revenue for the full year rose 10 per cent, and the margin recovered to 44.5 per cent. The company said it had learned to shorten its lead times and to order closer to the season. The industry as a whole took two years to work through the stock it had built, and the discounts were the reason inflation in clothing was lower than in services.
