Ireland beat England 29-16, scoring four tries and conceding one, and the bonus point came before half time. The match was played at Lansdowne Road on 18 March in front of 51,000 people. It was the fourth Grand Slam in the country's history and the first since 2018. The win also secured the championship on points difference ahead of France. Two of the four tries came from the back row, and the fourth came from a turnover inside the opposition 22.

Ireland had won its first three matches by comfortable margins, and the fourth against Wales was the closest. England arrived with two wins and had beaten Ireland at Twickenham the previous year. The visitors led 3-0 after five minutes and did not score again until the last quarter, by which time the contest was decided. Ireland had beaten France by 32-19 in the second round, which turned out to be the decisive match.

Ireland's coach had taken charge in 2019 and had won 12 of his last 14 matches in the competition. The fly half kicked 14 points and was named player of the match. The pack won eight turnovers and the lineout held against a heavier opposing eight. He said afterwards that the squad had been built around players who had come through the academy together. The squad used 26 players across the five matches, and three of them made their first start in the final.

England finished third, with three wins and two defeats, and its coach was replaced at the end of the year. The team had conceded 12 tries in the tournament, the most of any side in the top half. Its captain said the gap with the leading teams had widened rather than closed, which was the remark that filled the newspapers the next morning. England had lost to Scotland at home in the opening round, which cost it the championship in the end.

Ticket prices had risen about 10 per cent, and the organisers said the revenue would be shared with the smaller unions. The tournament's attendance was 980,000 across 15 matches, a record, and the television audience in Britain was the largest for a non-final match since 2019. Italy won one match, against Wales, and finished fifth. The organisers said the average ticket price was 85 pounds, and the cheapest seat was 40.

Ireland's next fixtures were a tour of the southern hemisphere in the summer and a pool at the World Cup in the autumn. The team had never passed the quarter-final of that competition, and the coach said the Grand Slam would count for little if that did not change. Bookmakers made Ireland the favourite for the World Cup within a week of the final whistle. Ireland had won 12 of its last 15 Tests, and it was ranked first in the world before the tournament began. France finished second on points difference, having beaten England by 43 points in the opening round. The Six Nations had been played in its current format since 2000, and Ireland had won it five times, four of them with a clean sweep. Wales finished last and changed its coach in the autumn.
