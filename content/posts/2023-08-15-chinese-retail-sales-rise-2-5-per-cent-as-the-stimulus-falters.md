China's retail sales rose by 2.5 per cent over the year in July, below the forecast of about 4 and the weakest since the spring. The industrial output rose by 3.7 per cent, below the estimate of 4.4, and the fixed asset investment by 3.4 per cent in the first seven months. The figure was the slowest since the spring of 2022, when the lockdowns shut the shops in Shanghai. The seven-month growth was about 7.3 per cent on the year, and the two-year average about 3.5.

Consumption is the weak part. The two-year average growth of the retail sales was about 2.5 per cent, which is well below the level of 2019. The cars fell by about 1.5 per cent, and the one of the cosmetics by about 4 as the households cut the discretionary spending. The sales of the jewellery rose by about 8 per cent, and the one of the appliances by about 1. The household saving rate was about 33 per cent in the second quarter, which is about 10 points above the level of 2019.

Factories slowed. The output of the steel rose by about 15 per cent as the mills kept the runs high, and the one of the cement fell by about 5. The exports fell by about 14 per cent in July, which is the steepest since the spring of 2020. The output of the electric vehicles rose by about 25 per cent, and the one of the integrated circuits by about 4. The exports to the European Union fell by about 12 per cent, and the ones to the United States by about 13.

Property is still contracting. The investment in the real estate fell by about 8.5 per cent in the first seven months, and the new starts by about 24. The sales of the new homes fell by about 5 per cent in July, and the prices in the largest cities fell by about 0.3. The land sales fell by about 20 per cent, and the revenues of the local governments have been the part the crisis has hit.

Its stimulus has been small. The central bank cut the rates by about 15 basis points in August, and the government has announced the measures for the private firms. The economists say the measures are about 1 trillion yuan, which is about 1 per cent of the output. The government has cut the stamp duty on the share trades by half, and the measure was the largest of the summer. The government has also cut the tax on the home purchases in the largest cities, and the effect on the sales has been small so far.

These figures are the ones that the autumn policy will answer. An economy that grows the retail by 2.5 per cent while the property falls is an economy that needs the demand, and the demand has not been supplied. The economists expect the growth of about 4.2 per cent in the third quarter, and the target of about 5 is now out of reach.
