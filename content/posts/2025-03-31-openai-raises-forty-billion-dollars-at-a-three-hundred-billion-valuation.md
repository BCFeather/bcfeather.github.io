Round had been reported for weeks and closed at a price that valued the company at about twice the level of its previous round in October 2024. SoftBank committed the largest share, with Microsoft, Thrive, Altimeter and Coatue participating, and the structure includes a first close and a second tranche that depends on the company completing a reorganization of its corporate form. That condition is the part of the transaction that matters, because the for-profit entity that the investors are buying into does not yet exist in the shape they require.

The reorganization is the conversion of a foundation-controlled company into a structure in which a for-profit subsidiary holds the business. The foundation would retain a role, the investors would hold equity with a defined claim on returns, and the governance would change in ways that the state of California and the company's own nonprofit status have to permit. A financing conditional on that outcome is a financing that the lawyers will spend the year executing.

What the money buys is compute. The company has signed agreements with several suppliers for capacity that must be paid for over years, and the commitments are larger than the revenue that the business generates. A round of this size is a way of funding a capital programme that the company has already begun to sign, and the investors are underwriting the programme rather than the current income statement.

Usage is the number that justifies the valuation. The company said that 500 million people use its chatbot each week, a figure that it had not disclosed before in that form, and the revenue per user is the variable that determines whether the business can cover its costs. A subscription business with half a billion weekly users at ten dollars a month would be a large company, and the actual conversion rate is smaller, which is why the valuation rests on growth and not on current margins.

Competition is the risk that the investors accepted. A cheaper model from a Chinese developer showed in January that an open system could match the quality of a frontier model on many tasks, and the company's response was to spend more on scale and not less. The bet is that capability at the frontier is worth paying for, and the counter-bet is that the frontier moves slowly enough that a commodity model is good enough for most uses.

Concentration of the investor base is the governance question. A single fund supplying the largest share of a round has influence over the company's strategy, its board and its eventual listing, and the history of SoftBank's investments includes both successes and write-downs. A company that depends on one backer for its capital plan is a company whose independence is a matter of contract and not of fact.

What the round marks is the arrival of artificial intelligence at the scale of infrastructure finance. A private company raising more than the market capitalization of most listed firms, in order to buy equipment, is a company that has become a utility before it has become profitable. That is the shape of the industry in 2025, and the round is its clearest statement.
