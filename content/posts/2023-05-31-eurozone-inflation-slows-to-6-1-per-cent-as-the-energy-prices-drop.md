Eurozone consumer prices rose by 6.1 per cent over the year to May, according to the flash estimate, down from 7 per cent in April and below the forecast of 6.3. The core measure, which strips the energy, the food, the alcohol and the tobacco, eased to 5.3 per cent from 5.6. The monthly change in the headline was about 0.1 per cent. The figure is the lowest since the start of 2022, and it is the fifth consecutive month of decline from the peak of 10.6 in October. The unemployment rate was about 6.5 per cent in April, and the labor market has stayed tight through the energy shock.

Energy did the work again. The energy prices fell by about 1.7 per cent over the year, and the month was the second in a row of the annual decline. The gas in the wholesale market was about 25 euros per megawatt hour, down from about 40 at the start of the year and from the peak of 300 in the summer of 2022. The electricity fell by about 5 per cent over the year, and the fuels by about 0.5 as the base of the spring of 2022 dropped out. The governments of Germany and France have been subsidizing the bills through the winter, and the schemes are being wound down.

Food is still the highest line. The prices of the food, the alcohol and the tobacco rose by about 12.5 per cent, down from 13.5, and the one of the fresh food by about 10. The easing is slower than the headline, and the grocery bills are the part that the households notice every week. The prices of the vegetables rose by about 15 per cent, and the one of the meat by about 8 as the feed costs stayed high.

Services eased a little. The prices of the services rose by about 5 per cent, down from 5.2, and the one of the industrial goods by about 5.1 as the supply chains normalized. The negotiated wages rose by about 4.5 per cent in the first quarter, and the bank expects the services to hold above 4 through the year. The air fares rose by about 12 per cent, and the one of the package holidays by about 15 as the summer bookings opened.

National figures were mixed. The inflation in Germany was about 6.3 per cent, and the one in France about 6.0, and the one in Spain about 2.9. The Baltic states were above 10, and the Netherlands was about 6.8. The inflation in Italy was about 8.1 per cent, and the one in Portugal about 4.0, which shows the different weight of the energy.

This report keeps the bank on course for another increase in June. A headline at 6.1 per cent with the core at 5.3 is still more than double the target, and the council has said the job is not done. The deposit rate is 3.5 per cent, and the market expects another 25 basis points in June and perhaps one more in July.
