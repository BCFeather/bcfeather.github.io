---

Carcinogenesis is usually told as a sequence of mutations. A cell acquires them one at a time, and the ones that confer advantage accumulate until the cell is malignant. The story explains many cancers and fits pancreatic cancer poorly.

The analysis in *Science*, 12 May 2023 offers a different sequence. The authors trace pancreatic tumour evolution across model systems and find that epigenetic plasticity, rather than a single initiating mutation, primes progenitor-like epithelial cells for transformation.

The plasticity is a state. A cell whose chromatin is accessible at many regulatory regions can adopt several identities, and an injury that inflames the tissue pushes such a cell towards a ductal programme. The state precedes the mutation.

The microenvironment is a participant. The paper maps communication between epithelial cells and immune cells, and identifies an interleukin-33 loop that sustains inflammation and reinforces the plastic state.

The computational method is the contribution. A model that infers communication modules from gene expression assigns a role to each cell type in each stage, and the trajectory from healthy tissue to inflammation to premalignancy to malignancy becomes visible.

The chromatin evidence is the mechanism. Accessible regions near cell-cell signalling genes are remodelled as the tissue progresses, which is how a plastic state becomes a stable programme.

The clinical implication is about interception. A patient at risk has an inflamed pancreas, and an intervention that prevents the plastic state from stabilizing is a preventive strategy that a mutation-centred model does not suggest.

The limitation is the model system. Engineered mouse models and organoids approximate a human pancreas, and the timing of the stages in a patient is not the same as in a mouse.

What the paper does not establish is causality. A state that precedes cancer is not necessarily a state that causes it, and the perturbation experiments are the evidence that it does.

For cancer biology, the value is a mechanism that precedes the mutations. A field that looks for the first mutation may be looking at the wrong step, and the paper's trajectory suggests where to look instead.

The interleukin-33 loop is a therapeutic handle. A cytokine that links epithelial stress to immune recruitment is a target that an antibody can block, and the paper's perturbation of the loop is the test of its necessity.

The evolutionary comparison is instructive. Pancreatic cancer's mutational burden is low and its lethality is high, and a model in which plasticity does the early work explains why a low-burden tumour can be so aggressive.
The spatial data are the method's strength. A section of tissue preserves the neighbourhood of each cell, and an analysis that keeps the neighbourhood intact can attribute a signal to a cell type rather than to a bulk average. The paper's imaging is what makes the communication map possible.
The comparison with other tumours is the generalization. Plasticity is a feature of several epithelial cancers, and pancreatic cancer's speed makes it the clearest case. A slow tumour would blur the stages that the paper resolves.
