Employers added 390,000 jobs in May, the government reported on 3 June, and the figure was above the 328,000 forecast. The unemployment rate stayed at 3.6 per cent, which is the lowest since the pandemic began. The previous two months were revised down by 22,000 in total, and the three-month average was 408,000. The workforce grew by 200,000, and the participation rate rose to 62.3 per cent.

Hiring was broad. Leisure and hospitality added 84,000 jobs, and the sector was still 800,000 below its level of 2020. Professional services added 75,000, and health care 58,000. Construction added 36,000, and manufacturing 18,000. Retail added 11,000, and it had added 100,000 in the previous two months. Government added 20,000 jobs, and the federal payroll was still below its level of 2019.

Wages rose 5.2 per cent in the year, and they had risen 5.5 in April. The monthly gain was 0.3 per cent, which is slower than the 0.5 of the previous month. Economists said the figure showed that pay was not chasing prices as fast as they had feared, and the market took it as good news. Real wages had fallen 3 per cent in the year, and households were using savings to spend.

Participation rose to 62.3 per cent, still 1 point below the level before the pandemic. The rate for prime age workers rose to 82.3 per cent, which is the highest since 2001. The number of people working part time for economic reasons fell by 300,000.

It was the last report before the June meeting of the Federal Reserve. The chairman had said the committee would raise by 50 basis points in June and in July, and the report did not change that. The market priced a 50 point rise with 90 per cent probability after the release. The household survey, which is separate from the payroll one, showed a smaller gain of 320,000. Its measure of employment had fallen in April, and it recovered in May. The two surveys had diverged by 1.5 million jobs over the year, which is the largest gap on record. Treasury yields rose 10 basis points, and the dollar gained against the euro.

Other data suggested the labour market was still tight. There were 11.4 million job openings at the end of April, and the ratio of openings to unemployed people was 1.9. The number of people who quit their jobs was 4.4 million, and the rate was 2.9 per cent. The Fed raised rates by 75 basis points in June, more than the chairman had signalled, after the inflation report of 10 June. Hiring stayed strong through the summer, and the average monthly gain in the third quarter was 330,000. The unemployment rate reached 3.5 per cent in September, and it began to rise in 2023.
