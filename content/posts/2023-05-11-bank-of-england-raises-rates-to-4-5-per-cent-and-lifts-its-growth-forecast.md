Britain's central bank raised its policy rate to 4.5 per cent on 11 May, a quarter-point increase and the twelfth since the end of 2021. The vote was 7 to 2, with the two dissenters wanting a larger increase. The rate is now the highest since 2008. The increase was in line with the forecast, and the rate is the highest since the financial crisis.

Its forecast changed. The committee said the economy would grow by about 0.25 per cent this year instead of shrinking, and it said the recession that it had predicted in the autumn would not happen. The growth in the first quarter was about 0.1 per cent, and the services have been stronger than the models expected. The committee said the energy prices had fallen more than it had assumed, and the household incomes were therefore stronger than the forecast.

Forecasts for the near term were raised. The committee expects the headline to be about 5 per cent by the end of the year, which is higher than the 4 per cent it had projected in February, and it expects the target to be reached only in 2025. The food prices are the reason for the upgrade. The bank now expects the inflation to be about 5 per cent in the fourth quarter and about 3 per cent a year later. The committee said the food prices would fall more slowly than it had expected, and the services inflation would stay above 5 per cent through the year.

Labor market conditions are still tight. The unemployment rate is about 3.8 per cent, and the vacancies have fallen from the peak but remain above the level of 2019. The committee said the wage growth was running at about 7 per cent, which is too fast for the 2 per cent target. The vacancies were about 1 million in the spring, down from about 1.3 million at the peak, and the ratio of the vacancies to the unemployed was about 1.5.

Markets moved modestly. The traders had priced a small chance of a 50 basis-point rise, and the pound fell by about 0.5 per cent against the dollar after the statement. The two-year gilt yield fell by about 10 basis points, and the market now expects the rate to peak at about 5 per cent. The market expects the rate to peak at about 5 per cent in the autumn, and the economists at the banks are split between 4.75 and 5.25.

This is the first statement in which the bank has accepted that the economy avoided the recession it had forecast. A committee that raises the rate while lifting the growth forecast is a committee that is fighting the prices rather than the demand. The governor said the committee would watch the wage settlements of the summer, and the next meeting is in June.
