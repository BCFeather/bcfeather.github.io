Twisting two layers of graphene produces a moiré pattern, and the pattern's length scale sets the electronic behaviour. A stack of several layers adds a third dimension, and the interplay between the two periodicities is the paper's subject.

In *Nature* on 24 August 2023, that stack is built. The authors twist a thin flake of graphite on a substrate and measure transport and spectroscopy, and the data show states confined to a single layer alongside states extending through the stack. Mixed dimensionality is the finding. A two-dimensional state and a three-dimensional one coexist in the same crystal, and their coexistence produces a response neither alone would give.

Moiré supplies the tuning. The twist angle sets the moiré's period, and the period sets the energy scale of the flat bands those states occupy; a flat band whose width is small compared with its separation is one where interactions dominate, which is why the period matters so much.

Measurement tests the claim. Transport shows a series of features tracking the twist angle, and the spectroscopy shows the states' spatial extent. The transport signature is the evidence, because a resistance peaking at a series of fillings is a spectrum and the spacing of that series is the moiré's energy scale.

Fabrication is the difficulty. A thin flake must be twisted relative to its neighbour and kept clean, and assembly is what makes the system reproducible. The substrate's alignment is the second knob, since a flake's twist is set relative to a neighbour and to a substrate, and the two alignments give two periodicities controlled independently.

Theory supplies the interpretation. A moiré potential in two dimensions and a stacking potential in the third produce a band structure whose states localise differently, and the calculation reproduces the features. Disorder sets the limitation. A twist angle varying across a device blurs the features, and mapping the angle bounds that variation. Sample size limits the probes available, because a device of a few micrometres is too small for some techniques, and the choice of methods is made for that scale.

A correlated phase is the discovery this platform has yet to yield, because the flat bands are a platform and superconductivity or magnetism in them would be the discovery it enables. Condensed matter gains a design space, since the moiré has been a two-dimensional tool and this stack makes the third dimension a parameter. A device with a gate would let the authors move the Fermi level across the flat band and watch the two kinds of state respond differently, and that gate dependence is the test that would separate a genuine mixed-dimensional system from a stack whose layers merely coexist. A comparison with a stack twisted by a few degrees more would then show how the coupling between the periodicities scales with the angle, which is the number any future device would be designed around. A transport measurement in a magnetic field would show whether the flat bands carry a Berry phase, and that phase is what would tell the two-dimensional states apart from the three-dimensional ones.
