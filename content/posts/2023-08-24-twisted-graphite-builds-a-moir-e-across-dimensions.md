---

Twisting two layers of graphene produces a moiré pattern, and the pattern's length scale sets the electronic behaviour. A stack of several layers adds a third dimension, and the interplay between the two periodicities is the paper's subject.

The system in *Nature*, 24 August 2023 is that stack. The authors build a twisted thin flake of graphite on a substrate and measure transport and spectroscopy, and the data show states confined to a single layer alongside states that extend through the stack.

The mixed dimensionality is the finding. A two-dimensional state and a three-dimensional one can coexist in the same crystal, and their coexistence produces a response that neither alone would give.

The moiré's role is the tuning. The twist angle sets the moiré's period, and the period sets the energy scale of the flat bands that the states occupy.

The measurement is the test. The authors' transport data show a series of features that track the twist angle, and the spectroscopy shows the states' spatial extent.

The fabrication is the difficulty. A thin graphite flake must be twisted relative to its neighbour and kept clean, and the authors' assembly is what makes the system reproducible.

The comparison with twisted bilayer graphene is the frame. The bilayer's flat bands have produced a family of correlated phases, and the multilayer's extra dimension adds a knob that the bilayer lacks.

The theory is the interpretation. A moiré potential in two dimensions and a stacking potential in the third produce a band structure whose states are localized differently, and the authors' calculation reproduces the features.

The limitation is the disorder. A twist angle that varies across a device blurs the features, and the authors' mapping of the angle is what bounds the variation.

What the paper does not show is a correlated phase. The system's flat bands are a platform, and superconductivity or magnetism in them would be the discovery that the platform enables.

For condensed matter, the value is a design space. The moiré has been a two-dimensional tool, and the paper's stack makes the third dimension a parameter.

The substrate's role is the alignment. A flake's twist is set relative to a neighbour and to a substrate, and the two alignments produce two periodicities that the authors control independently.

The flat band is the resource. A band whose width is small compared with its separation is one where interactions dominate, and the moiré's period is what makes the width small.

The transport signature is the evidence. A resistance that peaks at a series of fillings is a spectrum, and the series' spacing is the moiré's energy scale.

The comparison with the magic-angle bilayer is the benchmark. The bilayer's correlated states appear at a specific twist, and the multilayer's richer spectrum may host more.

The sample's size is the limit. A device of a few micrometres is too small for some probes, and the authors' combination of techniques is chosen for that scale.

The control of the twist is the engineering. A stack assembled by transfer has an angle that is set by the transfer stage's rotation, and the precision is the platform's foundation.
