title: "China's retail sales fall 11 per cent as lockdowns bite"
date: "2022-05-16T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The April figures published on 16 May showed the steepest fall since the first lockdown of 2020."
---

Retail sales fell 11.1 per cent in the year to April, the statistics bureau reported on 16 May. The reading was the steepest since March 2020 and it was worse than the 6 per cent fall forecast. Industrial output fell 2.9 per cent, which was the first decline since February 2020. The figures covered a month in which Shanghai was shut for three weeks. The statisticians said the base of comparison was strong, because April 2021 had been a record month.

Restaurants and shops in the locked cities were closed. Catering revenue fell 22.7 per cent, and the sale of clothing fell 22.8. Cosmetics fell 22, and jewellery 26. Cars fell 31, and the dealers said they had 2 million vehicles in stock. Online sales fell 5 per cent, which is unusual because they had grown through every previous month of the pandemic.

Fixed investment grew 6.8 per cent in the first four months, and infrastructure grew 8.3. Property investment fell 2.7, and sales of floor space fell 21. The property sector had been contracting since the summer of 2021, and the lockdowns made the decline worse. Local governments sold 30 per cent less land in the period. Investment by state firms rose 10 per cent, and investment by private firms 4.

Industrial output was dragged down by the lockdowns. Production of cars fell 31 per cent, and of computers 15. Steel fell 4, and cement 12. The statistics bureau said 30 per cent of large firms had reported disruption in their supply chains, and that the port of Shanghai had handled half its usual volume.

Employment was the concern. The urban survey rate was 6.1 per cent, and the rate for people aged 16 to 24 reached 18.2 per cent, which is the highest on record. The statisticians said 10 million graduates would enter the market in the summer, and that the youth rate would rise further. The government responded with measures in late May. It announced 1 trillion yuan of infrastructure spending and tax cuts of 140 billion, and it told banks to increase lending. The State Council said the second quarter was critical, and it sent teams to 12 provinces to check that the measures were being applied. The rate for migrant workers was 6.5 per cent, and their numbers fell by 1 million.

Economists cut their forecasts for the year. The median estimate fell to 4 per cent from 5, and some banks said the second quarter would contract. The official target was 5.5 per cent, and the government said in August that it would miss it. The actual growth was 3 per cent. Activity recovered slowly after the lockdowns ended in June. Retail sales turned positive in June and grew 3.1 per cent in July. The youth unemployment rate kept rising and reached 20 per cent in July. The property market did not recover, and investment fell 10 per cent for the year.
