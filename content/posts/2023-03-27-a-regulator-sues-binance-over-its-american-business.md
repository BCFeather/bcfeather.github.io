title: "Regulator sues Binance over its American business"
date: "2023-03-27T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The Commodity Futures Trading Commission filed on 27 March, alleging that the exchange had evaded rules and coached customers."
---

Filing took place on 27 March in a federal court in Chicago. It names the exchange, its founder and its former compliance officer, and it alleges that the company had knowingly offered derivatives to American customers without registering. The regulator is the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, which oversees futures and swaps rather than securities. The court in Chicago was chosen because the regulator's jurisdiction over derivatives is clearest there.

According to the complaint, the exchange had instructed customers on how to hide their location and had used a corporate structure that was not disclosed. It also says the company had run an unregistered futures exchange and had failed to keep records. The regulator asked for a permanent injunction, disgorgement and a civil penalty, without specifying a sum. The complaint quoted internal messages in which staff discussed the location of customers. The exchange had applied for a licence in several countries and had withdrawn from others.

Responding to the filing, the exchange said the complaint was unexpected and that it had been cooperating with the regulator for two years. It said the allegations were a misreading of its business and that it would defend itself. Its chief executive said on social media that the company had no plans to change its structure. The company's American affiliate had been set up in 2019 and was regulated as a money transmitter.

American regulators had brought three earlier actions against the company in five years, and this was the first to name the founder personally. The Securities and Exchange Commission had opened a parallel inquiry, and it filed its own case in June. The two regulators had disagreed about which of them had authority over the tokens that trade on the exchange. The securities regulator's case was filed on 5 June and alleged 13 violations.

Customers withdrew 1.5 billion dollars in the week after the filing, according to one data provider. The company's share of the spot market was about 60 per cent at the start of the year, and it fell to 40 per cent by the autumn. The company's own token fell 6 per cent on the day. The exchange had 1.5 million American users, and it stopped serving them in June.

Timing was awkward for the industry, which had spent two years arguing that it could be regulated like other financial businesses. The failure of an exchange in November 2022 had already removed the argument that self-regulation worked. This case added the claim that a large firm had deliberately designed itself to avoid rules. The settlement required an independent monitor for three years and a compliance officer approved by the government. He was sentenced in April 2024 to four months and left the company. The case was settled in November 2023, with the company agreeing to pay 4.3 billion dollars and the founder pleading guilty to a money-laundering charge. The settlement was the largest ever imposed on a financial firm by the regulator.
