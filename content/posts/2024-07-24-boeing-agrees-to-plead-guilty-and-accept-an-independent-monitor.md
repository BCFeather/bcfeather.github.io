Boeing agreed in July to plead guilty to a charge of conspiring to defraud the regulator over the certification of the 737 MAX, and the agreement includes a fine, a period of probation and an independent monitor who will oversee its safety and compliance systems. The charge arose from the two crashes in 2018 and 2019 that killed 346 people. The agreement followed a finding that the company had withheld information from the regulator about a software system that moved the aircraft's tail without the pilots being told.

Monitor is the part that matters to the company's future. An outside auditor with access to the engineering decisions is an auditor who can stop a programme, and the company had argued that its own processes were adequate. A regulator that is embedded is a regulator that the factory has to plan around. The monitor will report to the department rather than to the board, which means the findings will not be filtered by the company's lawyers.

Families objected. Relatives of the passengers who died told the court that a fine and a monitor are not accountability, and they asked for a trial in which the decisions would be examined in public. A settlement that avoids a trial is a settlement that keeps the record closed. A jury trial would have exposed internal emails that the settlement keeps sealed, and the families said that was the point of asking for one.

Commercial context is a company already in difficulty. A production rate that has been capped, a backlog that is measured in years and a cash position that has been weakened by a strike and by the caps are the conditions in which the agreement was signed. A company that is losing money is a company that cannot afford a long legal fight. The company had already announced a slowdown in production and a cut in deliveries for the year, and the fine is small against those numbers.

Engineering culture is the underlying issue. A programme that was designed to compete with a rival's newer aircraft, and certified on the basis that pilots would react quickly to a system they had not been told about, is a programme where the schedule won. A monitor can watch the process, and a schedule that is set by a sales department is a schedule that the monitor cannot change. The engineers who raised concerns about the schedule had been overruled for years, and a monitor who can interview them is the change that matters.

What the agreement establishes is that the state has decided the company is too important to fail and too compromised to leave alone. A settlement that keeps the aircraft flying and puts an auditor inside the factory is the compromise that both sides could accept, and it will be judged by the next incident. The next test is not the settlement but the certification of the next model, which will be examined by a regulator that has been criticized for deference.
