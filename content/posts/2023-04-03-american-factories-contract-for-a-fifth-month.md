American manufacturing contracted for a fifth consecutive month in March. A survey of the purchasing managers at the factories fell to 46.3 from 47.7, and any reading below 50 marks contraction. The new orders measure dropped to 44.3, which is the weakest since the lockdowns of 2020. The index has now been below the threshold for six of the last seven months, and the average of the winter was about 47.

Employment in the survey slipped to 46.9. The survey's respondents described the demand as soft and the backlogs as shrinking, and several said the customers were working through the stock they had built during the shortages. A backlog that is cleared is an order that never arrives. The respondents in the machinery said the orders from the farms had held while the ones from the builders had fallen away. The order backlog index fell to about 43, which means the factories are working through the orders they already had rather than booking new ones.

Prices paid by the factories fell to 49.2 from 51.3 on the index. That is the first reading below 50 since the summer of 2020, and it suggests the cost of the components has stopped rising. The suppliers have been cutting the surcharges that they added during the freight crisis, and the buyers have noticed. The measure is still above the level of the commodity boom, which suggests the relief is partial rather than complete.

Weakness is concentrated in the durable goods. The machinery and the electrical equipment contracted, while the food and the furniture held up. The export orders were the weakest line in the report, and the respondents blamed the strong dollar and the slower demand in Europe. The furniture was the only category that grew, and the respondents linked it to the housing completions of the winter. The imports fell by about 2 points, and the respondents said the containers from Asia were arriving with less than a full load.

Services have held better than the factories. The separate index for the services published later in the week stayed above 50, and the gap between the two is the widest in more than a decade. A factory that is contracting while the services expand is an economy that is rotating rather than falling. The gap has widened for three months, and the last time the two diverged this far the factories were in a recession. The backlog of the orders fell to about 43, which is the lowest since the spring of 2020.

This report supports the view that the tightening has reached the goods economy first. The manufacturers have cut the shifts and the inventories, and the survey suggests the process is close to its end. A contraction that has lasted five months is a contraction that the next two reports will settle. The consumption of the goods has been flat for two quarters, and the services have taken the difference.
