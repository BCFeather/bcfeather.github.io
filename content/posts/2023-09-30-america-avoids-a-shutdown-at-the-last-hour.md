description: "Congress passed a forty-five-day spending bill late on 30 September 2023, hours before the federal government would have run out of money, and the compromise dropped new aid for Ukraine and relied on Democratic votes."
---

House passed a continuing resolution on the afternoon of 30 September and the Senate followed in the evening, and the bill was signed before midnight, which meant the government did not close. The measure funds federal agencies at their existing levels for forty-five days, extends some expired programmes including the authority to collect aviation fees, and omits the new money for Ukraine that the White House had requested. It also omits the border provisions that House Republicans had demanded, which is why it needed Democratic votes to pass.

Speaker Kevin McCarthy chose to pass the bill with a majority of his own conference against it. He had tried a more partisan version earlier in the week and lost it, after twenty-one of his own members voted with the Democrats, and he then produced a measure that could pass with Democratic support, which is the manoeuvre that a speaker uses when his right flank will not accept anything. The move cost him his job within days, when a member of that flank filed a motion to remove him.

A shutdown would have had visible effects. About four million federal employees would have been furloughed or worked without pay, national parks would have closed, and the economic data that the Federal Reserve uses to set rates would have stopped being published. Previous shutdowns have cost the economy about 0.1 per cent of annual output for each week they lasted, and the disruption falls hardest on the workers who are least able to absorb it, including the cleaners and guards who are paid by the hour and the contractors whose invoices stop being paid.

Aid for Ukraine was the political casualty. The administration had asked for more than six billion dollars in additional assistance, and the House bill left it out, which the president described as a serious mistake. Supporters of Ukraine in both parties said the money would be restored in a separate vote within weeks, and the Pentagon said it had enough authority to continue deliveries for a short period. The omission signalled that the Republican majority was losing its appetite for the war.

The deal settles almost nothing. The forty-five days end in mid-November, and the House and Senate have not agreed on any of the twelve appropriations bills, with disputes over spending levels, abortion coverage in health programmes, the border and the impeachment inquiry that the House opened against the president in September. The election year makes each of those harder, and the next deadline will arrive with a presidential campaign in full progress.

A government that was hours from closing stayed open with a bill that nobody liked, passed with the votes of the opposition and signed by a president who had asked for more. The argument has been postponed by six weeks, and the speaker who postponed it is already fighting for his position, with a removal motion that he may lose, filed by a member of his own party who wants the spending cuts he could not deliver.
