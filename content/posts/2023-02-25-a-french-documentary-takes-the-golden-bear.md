Awards were announced on 25 February at the Berlinale Palast, and the Golden Bear went to Sur l'Adamant. The film is a documentary by Nicolas Philibert, who had won a prize at the festival thirty years earlier. It is shot inside a day centre for people with mental illness that floats on the Seine in Paris. The prize is decided by a seven-member jury, and the decision was unanimous according to its president. Philibert had won a silver bear in 1992 for a film about a museum, and he had not been back since.

Jurors, led by Kristen Stewart, said the film had changed how they saw the subject. Philibert spent months at the centre and appears in the film himself, asking questions and eating lunch with the patients. Nothing in it is staged, and the camera is almost always at eye level. Reviews described it as the least sensational documentary about mental illness in years. The centre has 60 places and is open six days a week, and the film shows the staff at work as much as the patients.

Second prize went to a German film about a schoolteacher in a small town, and the director's prize to a French-Algerian filmmaker. The best actor award went to a nine-year-old in a Belgian production, and the best actress to a Spanish actress in her first leading role. The jury gave a special mention to a Ukrainian documentary about the first weeks of the war. The German film was about a teacher in a town of 3,000, and it was shot in 24 days. The jury gave no prize to the two films about the war in Ukraine, which was read as a deliberate choice.

Attendance at the awards was smaller than usual, and the festival's director used his speech to argue for public funding for cinemas. The German culture minister announced an extra 30 million euros for distribution, most of it for subtitled releases. The market had closed the day before with 600 exhibitors and 9,000 visitors. The festival sold 320,000 tickets, and the market reported 1,500 buyers.

Philibert said he had made the film because the centre was about to lose its lease, and he wanted to record it. The building was saved in the spring, and the film's success was credited by its operators with helping the campaign. It opened in France in April and sold 400,000 tickets, which is high for a documentary. The centre's lease had been renewed for five years, and the operators said the film had helped.

Next edition was scheduled for February 2024, and the directorship was extended to cover it. Two of the competition films had been bought by streaming services before the awards, and one of them was released in cinemas first. The Golden Bear winner was sold to 25 countries within a month. The prize money is 30,000 euros, and it goes to the producer rather than to the director.
