Parliament elected Srettha Thavisin as prime minister on 22 August with 482 votes, more than the simple majority required, after three months in which the party that won the election was unable to form a government. Srettha, a property developer who ran one of the country's largest conglomerates and had never held office, leads an eleven-party coalition assembled by Pheu Thai, the party that finished second in May. Move Forward, which won the most seats on a platform that included reforming the monarchy, will sit in opposition. The senate that blocked its leader in July did not block Srettha, and several of its members voted for him.

Arithmetic explains the outcome. Move Forward's leader, Pita Limjaroenrat, was blocked by the senate, whose members were appointed by the junta that ruled after the 2014 coup, when he sought a second vote. Pheu Thai then left the alliance it had campaigned with and negotiated with the parties of the outgoing government, including Palang Pracharath and United Thai Nation, which puts a coalition that was elected as a reform ticket in power with the establishment it promised to displace.

Hours earlier, Thaksin Shinawatra returned to Bangkok after fifteen years in exile. The former prime minister, who was ousted in 2006 and convicted in absentia on charges he calls political, landed at the airport and was taken to the Supreme Court and then to prison, where he was admitted to a hospital within a night. His return had been expected for weeks, and the timing, on the morning of the vote, was read as part of the bargain that produced the government.

Policies on offer are the ones Pheu Thai has campaigned on before: a ten thousand baht digital payment to households, a higher minimum wage, a freeze on energy prices for a period and a rewrite of the constitution. Economists have questioned whether the payment, which is to be financed by borrowing, is compatible with the fiscal rules, and the coalition agreement leaves the details to a government that will take office in September. Srettha, who is sixty-one and ran the developer Sansiri for years, will have to manage a cabinet of eleven parties with incompatible promises.

The losing side has stayed in parliament. Move Forward holds 151 seats and a mandate from younger voters who turned out in record numbers, and it has said it will keep pressing the issues that the senate blocked. Its supporters have described the new government as a restoration rather than an election result, which is a description that the coalition's own voters may come to share. Pita was suspended from parliament by the Constitutional Court in July while it considered a complaint about his shares in a media company.

Thailand has a prime minister again and a former one in a hospital ward, and the two arrivals on the same day describe the deal that made them possible. The country's courts, its senate and its army will decide how long the arrangement lasts, and none of them was on the ballot in May.
