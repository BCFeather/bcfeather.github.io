The two delegations met at the Dolmabahce Palace in Istanbul on 29 March, the first face-to-face talks in three weeks, and Turkey's president opened the session with a speech about the cost of the war. Ukraine's delegation came with a written proposal: neutral status, no membership of any military alliance, and security guarantees from a group of states that would be obliged to defend the country if it were attacked. The proposal set out a mechanism that would be triggered by an attack and would be backed by named countries rather than by a promise.

Russia's response was a promise about the battlefield. The deputy defence minister said the armed forces would reduce their activity around Kyiv and Chernihiv, on the ground that the talks had produced progress and that the first phase of the operation was complete. He said the two sides had agreed on the principles of a treaty and that a meeting between the two presidents could be arranged once the text was ready. The Ukrainian side said it had heard the promise and did not believe it.

The promise was tested within a week. The shelling of Chernihiv continued, the positions north of Kyiv were shelled on the day of the talks, and the withdrawal that began at the end of March was accompanied by the discovery of the bodies of civilians in Bucha, Irpin and other towns on the road west. President Zelensky said the talks had produced nothing that could be trusted, and the Western governments that had described the reduction as a regrouping were shown to have been right.

Negotiations had a structure that made progress possible and a subject that made it impossible. A treaty of neutrality is a document that lawyers can draft, and the Ukrainian proposal was detailed enough to be signed. The subjects that the talks did not touch were the ones that mattered: the ceasefire line, the status of the Crimea and the Donbas, the sanctions, and the reparations. Each of those required a decision that neither delegation was authorized to make.

Talks continued through April at a lower level, and the two sides met again in Istanbul in May without a result. The Ukrainian delegation said the negotiations had been suspended because Russia had shown no intention of stopping, and the Russian side said the draft that Ukraine had proposed in March was no longer acceptable after the change of government in Kyiv that it was still demanding. By the summer, the channels were open and the substance was gone.

What the Istanbul round established was the way in which a negotiation is used in a war of attrition. A promise to reduce activity on one front buys time on another, and a proposal for neutrality gives the side that makes it a way to describe the war as a dispute that can be settled. The talks of 29 March produced a document, a statement and a week of hope, and the ruins of Bucha were found before the ink could be read.
