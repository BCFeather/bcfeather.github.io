Molecular collisions are the elementary steps of chemistry, and a collision between two molecules with many internal states has many possible outcomes. Measuring all of them is what turns a rate into a mechanism.

In *Science* on 10 March 2023, those outcomes are measured. The authors collide nitric oxide with deuterated ammonia, resolve the quantum state of the products, and scan collision energies over nearly four orders of magnitude, from a fraction of a wavenumber to several hundred.

Mechanism changes across the scan. At low energy the scattering is dominated by the long-range dipole-dipole interaction, and at high energy the molecules approach closely enough for short-range forces to take over, with the crossover visible in the state distributions. Deuterated ammonia is a practical choice for seeing it, because deuterium's mass slows the molecule's rotation and sharpens the state structure, so the detection scheme can separate channels that hydrogen would blur together.

Because a product that has changed its rotational or fine-structure state carries a signature and a detection scheme can assign each outcome, state resolution is the technical achievement, and without it a cross-section is an average over channels. The apparatus is a merged-beam machine with velocity-map imaging, in which two beams cross at a small angle, products are ionized and projected onto a detector, and the image encodes scattering angle together with state, which is where the resolution comes from.

Cold chemistry supplies the motive. Cold molecules are a platform for quantum simulation and for controlled chemistry, and a collision understood channel by channel is one that can be used as a tool. Theory uses the same data differently, since a calculation of the scattering has to reproduce the energy dependence and the branching, and a dataset this complete is the test a potential energy surface needs.

Channel count sets the limit. Two molecules with several states each produce a large set of outcomes, and the paper resolves the dominant ones, so a complete map would be a larger experiment. Solvent stays out of the argument, because a gas-phase collision is the isolated event while a reaction in a liquid is the same event dressed by a crowd, and the connection between them is a model rather than a measurement.

Benchmark status is what the field gains. NO and ND3 are molecules that lasers can cool and manipulate, and a collision whose channels are mapped is one that can test control schemes. Astrochemistry reads the result speculatively, since dense clouds hold polar molecules at a few kelvins and the measured energy dependence is the kind that sets rates where temperature carries almost no energy at all. A ladder of energies, rather than a single snapshot, is what makes the mechanism identified rather than assumed. A further use is calibration, since a collision whose channels are known gives a reference for the state detection itself, and a new imaging scheme can be checked against it.
