Molecular collisions are the elementary steps of chemistry, and a collision between two molecules with many internal states has many possible outcomes. Measuring all of them is what turns a rate into a mechanism.

The experiments in *Science*, 10 March 2023 do that. The authors collide nitric oxide with deuterated ammonia, resolve the quantum state of the products, and scan collision energies over nearly four orders of magnitude, from a fraction of a wavenumber to several hundred.

The result is a change of mechanism. At low energy the scattering is dominated by the long-range dipole-dipole interaction, and at high energy the molecules approach closely enough for the short-range forces to take over. The crossover is visible in the state distributions.

The state resolution is the technical achievement. A product that has changed its rotational or its fine-structure state carries a signature, and a detection scheme that reads it can assign each outcome. Without that, a cross-section is an average over channels.

The cold chemistry context is the motivation. Cold molecules are a platform for quantum simulation and for controlled chemistry, and a collision that is understood channel by channel is a collision that can be used as a tool.

The theoretical benchmark is the other use. A calculation of the scattering has to reproduce the energy dependence and the branching, and a dataset this complete is the test that a potential energy surface needs.

The limitation is the number of channels. Two molecules with several states each produce a large set of outcomes, and the paper resolves the dominant ones. A complete map is a larger experiment.

What the paper does not address is the role of the solvent. A gas-phase collision is the isolated event, and a reaction in a liquid is the same event dressed by a crowd. The connection is a model rather than a measurement.

For the field of cold collisions, the value is a benchmark system. NO and ND3 are molecules that lasers can cool and manipulate, and a collision whose channels are mapped is a collision that can be used to test control schemes.

The astrochemical reading is speculative. Dense clouds contain polar molecules at low temperatures, and the energy dependence that the paper measures is the kind that sets reaction rates in an environment where the temperature is a few kelvin.

The deuterated ammonia is a practical choice. Deuterium's mass slows the molecule's rotation and sharpens the state structure, so that the resolution scheme can separate channels that hydrogen would blur together.

The energy scan is the experiment's backbone. A single collision energy gives a snapshot, and a scan across four decades gives the trend. The paper's ladder of energies is what allows a mechanism to be identified rather than assumed.
The apparatus is a merged-beam machine with velocity-map imaging. Two beams cross at a small angle, the products are ionized and projected onto a detector, and the image encodes the scattering angle and the state. The paper's resolution comes from that geometry.
