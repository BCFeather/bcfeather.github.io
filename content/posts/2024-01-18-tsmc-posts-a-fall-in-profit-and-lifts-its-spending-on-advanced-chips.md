TSMC reported a net profit of 238.7 billion Taiwan dollars for the fourth quarter on 18 January, down 19 per cent from a year earlier, on revenue that was roughly flat at 19.6 billion dollars. It said its capital spending this year would be between 28 and 32 billion dollars. The revenue was in line with the monthly reports that the company publishes, and the margin was higher than the forecast because the exchange rate helped.

Structure of the demand has changed. The chips for phones and computers were the bulk of the revenue for two decades, and the accelerators for artificial intelligence are now the fastest-growing line, which is why the company is building the plants that make them in Arizona and in Japan. A customer mix that shifts is a factory plan that shifts. The advanced nodes were close to fully used, and the mature ones ran at about two thirds of capacity, which is the split that explains the fall in the profit. The customers have been paying a premium for the capacity outside Taiwan, and the company has said the price will stay higher than at home. The computers and the phones that the company supplies have been selling less for two years, and the chips that go into them carry a lower margin than the ones the data centres buy.

Older plants were underused, which is why the profit fell. The equipment that makes the mature chips ran at less than its capacity as the customers worked through their inventories, and the depreciation on those plants fell on a smaller volume. A fixed cost that is spread over fewer units is a margin that compresses. The Arizona plant has been delayed by the shortage of the skilled workers, and the company has sent its own staff from Taiwan to install the equipment.

Guidance is the signal. The company said the demand for the advanced chips would exceed its capacity through the year, and it raised the spending rather than the dividend, which is the choice of a management that believes the cycle has turned. A capex number is a statement about the future. The customers have been paying a premium for the capacity that is outside Taiwan, and the company has said the price will stay higher than at home.

Concentration is the risk. Most of the advanced capacity is on one island, and the customers have begun to ask for the production to be duplicated elsewhere, which the company has agreed to do at a higher cost. A supply that is concentrated is a supply that the buyers want to diversify. The dividend was left unchanged, and the company said the cash would go into the new plants, which is the choice that the shareholders have accepted for a decade.

What the quarter shows is that the business of making the most advanced chips is now a business of building capacity rather than of filling it, and the spending is the measure of how long the company thinks the demand lasts. A foundry that sells capacity rather than chips is a business whose results depend on the plans of a handful of customers, and those plans are now dominated by one product.
