description: "An Israeli air strike on the Tel al-Sultan camp in Rafah on 26 May killed about forty-five people, most of them displaced families living in tents, and came two days after the World Court ordered Israel to halt its offensive there."
---Israeli aircraft struck the Tel al-Sultan camp in north-west Rafah on the evening of the twenty-sixth of May, setting fire to tents that housed displaced families and killing about forty-five people, according to the Hamas-run health ministry. Images of the aftermath circulated within an hour and showed a fire burning through a compound of shelters, with bodies being pulled from the wreckage and rescue workers using torches. More than two hundred people were wounded, many of them with burns.

Israeli military said the strike had targeted a compound where two senior commanders of Hamas were meeting, and that it had used precision munitions. Spokesman said the army was reviewing the reports of civilian casualties and that the incident was under investigation, and he acknowledged that a fire had broken out in the camp. Military released the names of the two commanders the following day and said it had killed them.

The Timing of the strike was the part that drew most criticism. It came two days after the International Court of Justice ordered Israel to halt its offensive in Rafah, and a day after Hamas fired rockets from the area at Tel Aviv for the first time in months. Israeli officials cited the rocket attack as justification. Several governments said the strike demonstrated that the government had no intention of complying with the court, and the European Union began discussing whether to reopen the association agreement that governs trade with Israel.

The Reaction from allies was sharper than at any previous point in the war. The American administration said it was assessing the reports and had asked Israel for information, and it did not repeat its earlier language of support for a limited operation. France, Germany and Canada condemned the strike, and the foreign ministers of several countries summoned Israeli ambassadors. The United Nations secretary general said the ruling of the court had been ignored and that the humanitarian system in Gaza was collapsing.

Camp itself had been designated a humanitarian area. Families had been told to move there after Rafah was declared a safe zone, and most of the people in it had already been displaced several times since the war began. Tents offered no protection against blast or fire, and the strike happened at night when families were inside them. Aid agencies said the camp had no water supply and no medical facilities beyond a small clinic that was destroyed.

Palestinian officials said the death toll could rise as rescue workers searched the wreckage, and they released a list of names that included children and elderly people, which aid groups said was consistent with the accounts of survivors.

The Consequence of the strike is a narrowing of the space for the government's remaining supporters. The American administration had said that a major operation in Rafah would cross a red line, and the strike made that line harder to define. Israel said the operation would continue and that the incident was being investigated. The case at the World Court gained another set of facts to consider, and the diplomatic cost of the war rose among governments that had been willing to give Israel the benefit of the doubt. None of that stopped the fighting, which continued through the summer.
