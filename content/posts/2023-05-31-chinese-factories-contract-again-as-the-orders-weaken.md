---

China's official manufacturing purchasing managers' index fell to 48.8 in May from 49.2 in April, the second month below the 50 mark and the weakest since the lockdowns of 2022. The new orders measure fell to 48.3, and the new export orders to 47.2. The production measure dropped to 49.6. The figure was below the forecast of about 49.5, and it is the lowest since December. The Caixin index, which covers the smaller private firms, was published the next day and came in at 50.9, which shows the split between the exporters and the domestic producers.

Demand is the problem. The orders from the exporters have been falling since the spring as the American and the European inventories were worked down, and the domestic orders have not filled the gap. The survey's respondents said the customers were buying in smaller batches and asking for the longer credit terms. The backlog of the orders fell to about 43, which is the lowest since the spring of 2020. The property construction has been the largest drag, and the cement and the steel orders have fallen with it.

Prices were weak. The output prices fell to about 46, which means the factories are cutting the prices to win the orders, and the input prices fell to about 41 as the commodity costs came down. The deflation in the factory gate has been running since the autumn. The producer prices fell by about 4.6 per cent over the year in April, and the economists expect a similar fall in May. The export orders have been below 50 for five months, and the respondents said the American buyers were placing the orders later and in the smaller quantities.

Services held up. The non-manufacturing index fell to 54.5 from 56.4, and the one of the construction to 58.0. The travel and the restaurants were the strongest, and the respondents said the holiday bookings had been better than last year. The catering and the hotels were above 60, and the transport was the second strongest.

Inventories rose. The finished goods index was about 49, and the raw materials about 47, which suggests the factories are holding more of the stock than the orders justify. The employment measure fell to about 48 as the hiring slowed with the output. The delivery times fell to about 49, which means the suppliers are delivering faster than the orders require. The finished goods inventory has risen for three months, and the respondents said the warehouses were fuller than they had planned.

This is the second report in a row below 50. A factory sector that has contracted for two months while the services expand is an economy that is relying on the reopening, and the reopening is the part that runs out. The government has been cutting the rates and the reserve requirements, and the effect on the orders has been small so far.
