Policy rate stayed at 5.25 per cent after the Bank of England meeting on 21 September, ending a run of fourteen consecutive increases. The vote was five to four, with four members wanting another quarter point. The rate is now the highest since 2008, and the increase of 5.25 points since the end of 2021 has been the fastest in the modern history of the bank. The bank also said it would sell about 100 billion pounds of the government bonds it held, which is the quantitative part of the tightening.

Timing was the news. The decision came a day after the inflation report showed a larger fall than the forecast, and the committee said it had seen enough evidence that the tightening was working. A hold that follows a soft report is a hold that the market had not expected a week earlier. The committee said the decision had been finely balanced, and the minutes recorded that the members had agreed the policy was already restrictive.

Its statement kept the warning, saying the policy would stay restrictive for an extended period, and it pointed to the wage growth of about 7 per cent as the reason. A warning that is repeated is a cut that is further away. The statement also said that the labour market remains tight and that the services inflation is the measure of how quickly the wages are passing through.

Growth is the other side of the argument. The economy has stagnated for a year, the unemployment rate is rising, and the surveys show the services slowing, which is the case the doves made in the meeting. A stagnation that deepens is a hold that becomes a cut. The output has grown by about 0.2 per cent in the quarter, and the surveys of the purchasing managers show the services slowing for a fourth month.

Traders moved the first cut earlier. The traders now expect a reduction by the middle of next year, and the two-year yield fell to its lowest since the spring. A committee that splits is a market that reprices every meeting. The pound rose briefly after the vote split was published and then gave up the gain, which the traders read as a sign of how little the decision had settled.

What the decision shows is that the bank has stopped at a level it cannot yet lower, and the level will be tested by the wage settlements of the winter. A bank that stops after fourteen increases is a bank that has used up the easy part of the tightening, and the hard part is now the waiting. The waiting is the policy that the committee has chosen, and the winter will show whether it can last. The market has priced about 50 basis points of cuts by the end of next year, and the committee has said it will not follow the market.
