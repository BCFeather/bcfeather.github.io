Chinese equities fell on Friday, led by the growth stocks that had carried the market higher this week. ChiNext index dropped 3.32 per cent to 2,426.08, two days after a 3.11 per cent rally, and the session opened higher before turning sharply lower in the afternoon. The index had closed at 2,509.43 on Thursday and 2,572.07 on Tuesday, when telecom and optical shares led the advance.

Shanghai Composite lost 0.59 per cent to 3,634.35, Shenzhen Component fell 2.05 per cent to 10,968.79 and STAR 50 shed 2.30 per cent to 1,279.66. Turnover on the two mainland exchanges came to 1.66 trillion yuan, 276 billion less than on Thursday, and decliners outnumbered gainers by 4,746 to 764, with 66 stocks unchanged. Financials and consumer staples accounted for most of the buying, while the ten-year treasury contract lost 0.07 per cent to 110.03 and the five-year shed 0.03 per cent.

Defensive sectors held up. Household appliances rose 1.47 per cent, banks 1.46 per cent and food and beverage 1.18 per cent, according to Shenwan industry indices. Telecoms dropped 4.97 per cent, electronics 3.75 per cent and building materials 3.25 per cent. Pig farming, banks and liquor were among the few concept groups in the black, while co-packaged optics, copper-clad laminates and circuit boards led the decline.

Breeding and catering names rallied on expectations that a smaller pig herd will lift prices next year, with several producers closing at the daily limit and a breeding fund tracking the sector up more than 2 per cent intraday. Film and cinema chains also climbed, a rare gain in a market where almost every growth theme fell, and thirty-year treasury futures slipped 0.2 per cent as money moved towards equities late in the day.

Selling tracked a weaker session for artificial-intelligence hardware overseas. Chip shares fell sharply in New York on Thursday, and Nebius cut its 2026 revenue guidance while raising capital spending, which sharpened doubts about the returns on data-centre construction. Two large CSI 300 exchange-traded funds drew heavy buying in the last half hour, a pattern traders associate with state-linked support. One fund turned over more than 730 million yuan, 27 times its full-day figure on Thursday, and the other more than 330 million yuan.

Money rotated into banks and consumer staples instead of leaving the market outright, and the thinner turnover points to deleveraging in the crowded hardware trade rather than a change of view on the sector. Attention now turns to NVIDIA results on 17 November, which fund managers describe as the clearest test of whether the hardware cycle still has room. The Shanghai exchange said it had taken 95 regulatory measures against unusual trading this week and was monitoring funds whose prices run well above their net asset value.
