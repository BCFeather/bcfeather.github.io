description: "The July consumer prices rose a little faster than the forecast as the comparison with last summer dropped out, while the core slowed to 4.7."
---

American consumer prices rose by 3.2 per cent over the year to July, up from 3 per cent in June and below the forecast of 3.3. The monthly change was 0.2 per cent. The core measure rose by 0.2 on the month and by 4.7 over the year, down from 4.8, which is the smallest increase since October 2021. The figure is the first acceleration in a year, and the measure has risen from the 3 per cent of June as the base of last summer dropped out. The measure is about 3.2 per cent on the three-month annualized basis, which is the number the committee has said it looks at.

Energy did the work again. The energy index fell by about 12.5 per cent over the year, and the petrol by about 20, as the base of the summer of 2022 is still high. The electricity rose by about 0.4 per cent on the month, and the utility gas fell by about 1.5. The fuel oil fell by about 15 per cent over the year, and the one of the propane by about 12. The crude was about 80 dollars a barrel at the end of July, which is about 20 per cent below the level of a year ago.

Shelter slowed again. The rent rose by about 0.4 per cent and the owners' equivalent rent by about 0.4, which is the smallest monthly gain since the end of 2021. The measure is still about 7.7 per cent higher than a year ago, and the market rents have been falling for a year. The measure for the new tenants has been rising by about 1.2 per cent, and the gap with the stock is the reason the index is still high.

Used cars fell more. The prices dropped by about 1.3 per cent, which is the second month of decline, and the wholesale prices suggest another fall in August. The new cars fell by about 0.1, and the car insurance rose by about 1.6 per cent. The car insurance has risen by about 17 per cent over the year, and the one of the repairs by about 10.

Services were mixed. The air fares fell by about 8 per cent, and the one of the hotels by about 3, while the medical care rose by about 0.4. The food away from home rose by about 0.3, and the food at home was flat for a fourth month. The health insurance measure fell again, and the statisticians have said the method will keep it falling into next year.

Markets read the report as benign. The two-year yield fell by about 5 basis points, and the traders kept the odds of a September pause at about 80 per cent. A headline at 3.2 with the core at 4.7 is the report the committee needed, and the core is the part that is now falling. The committee meets on 19 and 20 September, and the market expects the rate to be left at 5.25 to 5.5 per cent.
