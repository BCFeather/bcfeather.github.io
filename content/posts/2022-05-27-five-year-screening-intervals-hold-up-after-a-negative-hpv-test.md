Cervical screening programmes have been moving from cytology to primary HPV testing, and the interval between screens is the parameter that determines both the cost and the safety of the programme. The English pilot supplied the data to set it. The interval is the parameter that determines how many women are screened per year and how many cancers are caught between screens, and both quantities are what a programme is judged on.

The study is an observational analysis of the first and second rounds of the English HPV screening pilot, run from 2013 to 2016 with follow-up to the end of 2019, and it covers 1,341,584 women. The paper appeared in *The BMJ*, 31 May 2022. A pilot that ran two rounds on more than a million women is the closest thing to a randomized comparison that a national programme can produce, and the observational design is the price of that scale.

For women younger than 50, detection of CIN3+ at the second round was significantly lower after a negative HPV screen than after cytology: 1.21 against 4.52 per 1,000 women screened, an adjusted odds ratio of 0.26. Interval cancer risk was also lower, 1.31 against 2.90 per 100,000 woman-years. An odds ratio of 0.26 for a precancerous lesion at the second round is a large effect, and the effect is attributable to the test rather than to the population because the comparison is within the same cohort.

Women older than 50 had an even lower risk five years after a negative HPV test, 0.57 per 1,000, than younger women had at three years. That is the observation that supports a longer interval for the older group. A longer interval for the older group is supported by a risk that is lower in absolute terms, and the lower risk reflects both a slower progression and a lower incidence of new infection.

A negative HPV test is a negative test for the cause of the disease, and a negative cytology is a negative test for its current manifestation. The difference in predictive value follows from that, and it is the reason the programmes can be spaced further apart. A negative test for the cause is a test that predicts the absence of the disease for longer than a test for its current manifestation, and the difference in predictive value is what the programme is exploiting.
