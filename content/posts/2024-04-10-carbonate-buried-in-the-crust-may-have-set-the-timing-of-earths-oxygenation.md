Oxygen rose in the atmosphere long after cyanobacteria began producing it, and the delay of hundreds of millions of years has never had a clean explanation. A paper in Nature Geoscience on 10 April 2024 proposes one built on crustal carbonate. The proposal is that carbon burial removed the sink that kept oxygen low. A parameter that can be tuned to fit a record is a parameter that weakens a claim, and the paper fixes the ones the rock record constrains. A hypothesis that names its parameters is a hypothesis a field can test, and testing is the only route from a model to an explanation. A delay explained by a slow process is a delay that later work can date more precisely, and dating is what the record still permits.

Carbonate is carbon stored in rock, and storing carbon removes it from the atmosphere and from the ocean. Removal matters because oxygen and carbon cycles are coupled through organic matter, and coupling is what makes burial a lever. A lever that operates slowly is a lever that explains a delay, and delay is the phenomenon rather than the rate. Fixing is what distinguishes a model that explains from a model that accommodates, and the distinction is the scientific stake.

Crustal build-up is the process that the model represents, and representing it requires assumptions about weathering and subduction. Assumptions are where a model of the distant Earth earns or loses its plausibility. Weathering consumes carbon dioxide and supplies cations that become carbonate, and supply is what limits the burial rate. Accommodation is easy in a system with few observations, which is why Archean modelling attracts both hypotheses and scepticism.

A limit is what a model needs before it can predict a timing, and a predicted timing is the test of the hypothesis. Records of carbonate abundance are patchy, and patchiness is the uncertainty the model inherits. Inheritance is honest, and the paper says which interval its timing depends on. Scepticism is healthy here, and the paper's framing invites it rather than deflecting it.

An alternative explanation is that oxygen rose when phosphorus supply changed, and a competing hypothesis is what a paper of this kind must address. Addressing it means showing that the carbonate story predicts the same record as the alternative. A story that predicts the record is not necessarily the right one, and the paper is careful about the difference.

The difference between prediction and proof is the space in which this result lives. A model of the Archean is a model with few constraints and many parameters, and parameters are where a fit can be arranged.

Arrangement is the risk that a reviewer would name, and a named risk is a risk the paper can discuss rather than hide. More constraints are what the next decade of geochemistry will supply, and a model that survives them is one that will be cited as an explanation.
