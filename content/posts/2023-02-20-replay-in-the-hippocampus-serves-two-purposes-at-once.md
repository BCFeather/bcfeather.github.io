The hippocampus replays experience when it is not happening. A sequence of places that was walked through during the day is reactivated during sleep, and the reactivation has been interpreted as consolidation, as planning, or as both.

The study in *PNAS*, 7 February 2023 separates the interpretations. G. Elliott Wimmer, Yunzhe Liu, Daniel McNamee, Raymond Dolan and colleagues record neural activity while participants perform a task that requires model-based choices, and they identify replay events with signatures that distinguish the two functions.

The signatures are temporal. A replay that runs forward in time, in the order the experience occurred, is the pattern associated with memory; a replay that runs in a different order, or that jumps to a state relevant to a decision, is the pattern associated with planning.

The task is designed to require both. A participant who must choose between options has to retrieve what happened and to imagine what would happen, and the two demands occur in the same session, which is what allows the comparison.

The result is that the signatures coexist. Replay events of both kinds appear, and their timing relative to the decision separates them: the planning-like events cluster before a choice, and the memory-like events appear after.

The measurement is decode-based. A classifier trained on the patterns associated with each state reads the sequence out of the activity, and the decoded trajectory is the evidence for replay. The method is indirect and it is the standard in the field.

The design's strength is the within-subject comparison. Each participant provides both kinds of event, so that a difference between them is not a difference between people.

The limitation is the recording. Intracranial electrodes give access in a small number of patients, and the sample is not representative. The phenomena are robust enough to appear in that sample, and their generality is an open question.

What the paper cannot show is the causal role. A replay event that precedes a good decision may cause the decision or may be a consequence of the deliberation, and a perturbation experiment would be the test.

For memory research, the result removes a dichotomy. Consolidation and planning have been treated as competing accounts of the same phenomenon, and the paper's data suggest that the phenomenon is a family of events with different signatures.

For artificial agents, the analogy is suggestive. A system that learns from experience and one that plans from a model both use stored trajectories, and the hippocampus may be the place where the two uses are implemented in the same tissue.

The hippocampal dependence is a further clue. Replay is associated with the hippocampal formation, and the paper's recordings come from electrodes placed across that region. A signature that appears in one subfield and not another would localize the function, and the dataset permits that analysis.
