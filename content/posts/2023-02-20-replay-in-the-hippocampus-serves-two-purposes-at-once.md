title: "Replay in the hippocampus serves two purposes at once"

description: "The hippocampus produces distinct replay signatures for prospective decisions and for memory, which shows that one structure supports two different uses of the past."
---





The hippocampus replays experience when it is not happening. A sequence of places walked through during the day is reactivated during sleep, and the reactivation has been read as consolidation, as planning, or as both.

In *PNAS* on 7 February 2023, the interpretations are separated. G. Elliott Wimmer, Yunzhe Liu, Daniel McNamee, Raymond Dolan and colleagues record neural activity while participants perform a task that requires model-based choices, and they identify replay events whose signatures distinguish the two functions.

Timing carries the signatures. A replay that runs forward, in the order the experience occurred, is the pattern associated with memory, while one that runs in a different order or jumps to a state relevant to a decision is the pattern associated with planning. The task demands both, since a participant who must choose between options has to retrieve what happened and imagine what would happen, and both demands occur in one session, which is what allows the comparison.

Because replay events of both kinds appear with timing that separates them, planning-like events clustering before a choice and memory-like ones after, the signatures coexist rather than compete. Decoding supplies the measurement, since a classifier trained on the patterns associated with each state reads a sequence out of the activity, and the decoded trajectory is the evidence, a method that is indirect and standard in the field.

Within-subject comparison gives the design its strength, because each participant supplies both kinds of event and a difference between them is not a difference between people. Behaviour backs the claim, since participants whose replay shows more planning-like events choose better on the model-based trials, and that correlation is what makes the signatures more than an epiphenomenon. Localization is the step the dataset permits, as electrodes placed across the hippocampal formation let a signature appearing in one subfield and not another be mapped.

Recording sets the limit, because intracranial electrodes give access in a small number of patients and the sample is not representative, so the phenomena are robust in that sample while their generality stays open. Causality is what the paper cannot show, since a replay event that precedes a good decision may cause it or follow from the deliberation, and a perturbation experiment would be the test. Memory research loses a dichotomy, because consolidation and planning have been treated as competing accounts of one phenomenon, and these data suggest a family of events with different signatures. Artificial agents offer an analogy, since a system that learns from experience and one that plans from a model both use stored trajectories, and the hippocampus may be where the two uses share a single tissue.

Sleep is the state where most replay has been recorded, and a task performed awake may produce a different mixture of the two signatures, which is a comparison the design leaves open. Replay may also serve a third function that the paradigm does not probe, and an experiment that rewards retrieval alone would separate memory from preparation more sharply.
