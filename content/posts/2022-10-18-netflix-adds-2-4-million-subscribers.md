Netflix published results for the third quarter on 18 October, and it added 2.41 million subscribers, the most in a year. Analysts had expected 1 million. The total reached 223.1 million, and revenue rose 6 per cent to 7.9 billion dollars. The shares rose 14 per cent in after-hours trading. The growth came from Asia. The region added 1.4 million, and Japan and India were the strongest markets. Europe added 0.6 million, and Latin America 0.3. The United States and Canada lost 0.1 million, which was the third consecutive quarter of decline in the largest market. Revenue was 7.93 billion dollars, and the company said it expected 7.8 billion in the next quarter.

Losing 1.2 million subscribers in the first half of the year had sent the shares down 60 per cent. It blamed account sharing, which it estimated at 100 million households, and competition from streaming services that had launched in the previous two years. It had announced a cheaper advertising-supported tier in July.

Launching on 3 November, the advertising tier cost 6.99 dollars a month, four dollars below the basic plan. It carried about five minutes of advertising per hour, and the company said it would not appear on children's programmes. The launch was brought forward by a month to catch the holiday season. The company also said it would begin charging for account sharing in 2023. The plan was to allow users to create sub-accounts for people outside the household at a lower price. The company had tested the idea in three Latin American countries, and it said the results were encouraging. The service had 223 million subscribers, and 100 million households shared an account without paying.

Operating margin was 19 per cent, up from 18, and the company said it would reach 20 per cent in 2023. The financials were mixed. Free cash flow was 473 million dollars, and the company said it would be positive for the year. It had 17 billion dollars of debt and had stopped buying back shares. Advertisers were offered 1,000 impressions for about 65 dollars, and the inventory sold out in a week.

Included in the quarter's content slate was the second part of Stranger Things, which the company said was its most watched season ever. It also released The Gray Man, a thriller that it said had been watched by 100 million households in its first month. The company spent 17 billion dollars on content in the year, which was flat on 2021. The company said it would release fewer titles in 2023 and would spend the same amount on each.

By the end of the year the shares were 50 per cent lower than in January, despite the recovery in the quarter. The advertising tier reached 1 million users in its first month, and the company said it would be profitable by 2023. The account-sharing charge was introduced in May 2023 in more than 100 countries.
