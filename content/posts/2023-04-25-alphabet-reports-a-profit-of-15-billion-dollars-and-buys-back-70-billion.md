Alphabet reported a revenue of 69.7 billion dollars for the quarter to March, up 3 per cent, and a profit of 15.1 billion. The shares rose about 4 per cent after the close. The company also authorized a buyback of up to 70 billion dollars. The earnings per share of 1.17 dollars were above the forecast of 1.09, and the operating margin rose to about 25 per cent from 22. The free cash flow was about 9.5 billion, and the capital spending fell by about 20 per cent as the company slowed the data center build.

Search did the work. The revenue from the search and the other properties was 40.4 billion, up about 2 per cent, and the paid clicks grew while the cost per click fell. The advertisers have been buying the cheaper formats, and the volume has carried the line. The retail and the travel were the categories that grew, and the finance and the crypto advertising kept shrinking. The other bets, which include the autonomous driving unit, reported 288 million dollars of revenue and a loss of about 1.2 billion.

YouTube was the weak spot. The advertising revenue fell by about 2.6 per cent to 6.7 billion, the third decline in a row, as the brand budgets moved to the performance channels. The subscriptions grew, and the company said the bundle was the answer. The YouTube subscriptions and the music passed 2.5 billion dollars of revenue, and the company said the bundle had about 80 million subscribers. The short-video format earns less per view than the long one, and the company has been closing the gap with the new formats of the ads.

Cloud narrowed its loss. Google Cloud reported 7.4 billion dollars of revenue, up 28 per cent, and an operating loss of about 400 million against 900 million a year earlier. The backlog grew, and the company said the AI work was bringing the larger customers. The backlog of the cloud reached about 30 billion dollars, and the AI work is the part that the customers have been signing. The headcount rose by about 5,000 in the quarter, which is the slowest growth since the start of the pandemic.

Headcount was flat at about 190,000. The company had added about 16,000 people in the previous year, and the hiring has been slowed to a trickle. The operating margin rose to about 25 per cent, and the finance chief said the discipline would continue. The company said the hiring would be limited to the AI and the cloud roles, and the other teams would stay at the current size. The buyback is the largest in the history of the company, and the dividend has not been raised since 2019.

This quarter marks the turn in the mood. A year of the falling advertising and the expensive hiring has given way to the cost control and the buyback, and the shares have recovered most of the loss of 2022. The regulators have been watching the search business, and the buyback does not change the debate about the default placements. A company that spends 9.5 billion on the buyback and the same on the capital projects is a company that thinks it has the cash for both.
