---

Consumer prices rose 3.4 per cent in the year to December, the same as the month before and above the 3.2 per cent that forecasters expected, according to the report published on 11 January. The core measure rose 3.9 per cent. The figure was a tenth above the consensus, and the detail that the analysts noted was the revision to the previous months, which left the trend slightly higher.

Shelter is the largest single contributor. The rents in the index are measured from a survey that lags the market by about a year, and the slowdown in the rents that the landlords are asking has not yet reached the statistic. A measure that lags is a measure that keeps the headline above the target for longer than the market expects. The rents that the landlords advertise have been rising at about half the rate of the measure in the index, and the gap is expected to close over the year.

Services other than shelter were also firm. Car insurance rose by more than twenty per cent in the year, and the prices of the medical services that the insurers pay for were revised higher. A service whose price is set by a contract moves when the contract is renewed. The insurance increases have been approved by the state regulators, and the companies have said they expect the premiums to keep rising as the repair costs rise.

Goods were the offset. The prices of the used cars fell for a sixth month and the clothing was discounted, which is the part of the basket that reflects the supply chains that have healed. A good whose price is set by a market falls faster than a service whose price is set by a wage. The used car prices are falling because the supply of new cars has recovered, and the rental companies that bought at the peak are now selling into a weaker market.

Market reaction was restrained. Yields rose slightly and the probability of a March cut fell below a fifth, which is the reaction of a market that had already lowered its expectations after the payroll report. A report that confirms a trend changes little. The three-month annualized rate of the core measure was about 3.3 per cent, which is higher than the committee's target and lower than the peak of last year. The dollar was little changed, which is unusual for a report that beat the forecast.

What the month shows is that the disinflation of the second half of last year has slowed, and the arithmetic of the shelter index means the committee will not see the target on the headline before the summer. A basket in which the shelter is a third of the weight is a basket that the rents decide, and the rents are the part of the inflation that the rate cannot reach quickly.
