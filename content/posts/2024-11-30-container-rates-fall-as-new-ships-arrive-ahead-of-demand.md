Spot rates for container freight have fallen from the peaks reached when carriers were routing around the Red Sea, and the decline coincides with the delivery of new capacity ordered during the boom. A shipping line that ordered ships in 2021 receives them in 2024, and the timing of that delivery is the industry's persistent mistake. A rate that tripled during a diversion is a rate that carries a premium for the risk of a longer route. A premium that is earned by distance is a premium that disappears when the distance shortens.

Diversion added distance rather than demand. A route that goes around the Cape rather than through the canal takes a fortnight longer each way, and a longer route absorbs ships. A fleet that is stretched by distance is a fleet that can charge a premium, and the premium lasted as long as the diversion did. A canal that is avoided is a canal whose operator loses the transit fee that funded its maintenance.

New tonnage removes that cushion. Ships delivered this year add capacity that the market did not have when the diversion began, and a market with more ships and the same cargo is a market where the spot rate falls towards the cost of the least efficient operator. A ship that is delivered is a ship that has to be employed, and an idle ship still costs its mortgage. A carrier that has ordered ships cannot cancel them, and the delivery is a commitment to trade.

Alliance structure is being rebuilt at the same time. Two of the largest carriers are reorganizing their partnerships at the start of next year, and a change in who shares a slot with whom changes the effective capacity on a route. A network that is redrawn is a network whose schedule is unstable for a quarter. A slot-sharing agreement that ends is an agreement that has to be replaced before the first sailing of the year.

Contracts are the part that matters to a shipper. A large importer signs an annual contract at a rate that reflects the spot market of the moment, and a contract signed at the peak is a contract that the shipper will regret. A carrier that locks in a high rate for a year is a carrier that has hedged the next fall. A contract signed at the peak is a contract that a procurement team will defend for twelve months. A shipper that signs late is a shipper that pays the spot rate, and the spot rate is the market's guess.

What the market shows is that freight is a cycle driven by orders placed three years earlier. A shipyard that is full in a boom is a shipyard that delivers into the next bust, and no operator can cancel a hull that is already welded. A shipyard order book is a forecast that is written three years before the market it serves. A cycle that is driven by shipyard orders is a cycle that the industry has never learned to time.
