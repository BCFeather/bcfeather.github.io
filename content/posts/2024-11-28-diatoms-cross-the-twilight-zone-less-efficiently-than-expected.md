title: "Diatoms cross the twilight zone less efficiently than expected"
date: "2024-11-28T18:20:00+08:00"
categories: [science]
description: "Diatoms were thought to carry carbon to depth efficiently because their opal skeletons ballast them, and measurements in the subpolar Southern Ocean show that their transfer through the twilight zone is inefficient."
---

The biological carbon pump moves carbon from the surface ocean to depth, and diatoms have been treated as its principal agents because their silica skeletons sink. A paper in *Nature Geoscience* on 28 November 2024 questions the size of that role. Ballast is a plausible mechanism, and a plausible mechanism that is not quantified can persist for decades in a field that lacks the measurements to test it. A role assumed from a physical property is a role that measurement can reassign, and reassignment is what this paper performs.

The setting is the subpolar Southern Ocean, a region that regulates nutrient cycles and atmospheric carbon dioxide through the pump. It is also a region where the relevant fluxes have been measured seldom. A region with sparse sampling is a region where global terms are extrapolated, and extrapolation is the step the paper replaces with observation.

Conflicting observations had obscured the mechanism, and the paper resolves the conflict with a quantitative accounting of transfer efficiency. Diatoms turn out to be transferred inefficiently, which is the opposite of what ballast predicts. Transfer efficiency is measured as a ratio, and a ratio is sensitive to the depth horizon chosen, which is a definitional choice a reader should inspect.

A heavy skeleton should sink fast, and a fast sink should escape remineralization in the upper mesopelagic layer. The explanation lies in what happens to the aggregate rather than to the cell. The contradiction between prediction and measurement locates an assumption, and the assumption is that density determines fate rather than the aggregate's history.

Sinking particles are consumed and fragmented in the twilight zone, and a bloom grazed early delivers little carbon despite its density. Fragmentation is physical as well as biological, since a sinking aggregate can be broken by shear as well as consumed by zooplankton. A fate model without fragmentation is a model that assumes the aggregate survives its own journey.

On the modelling side, the consequence for the carbon budget is that a region's export production overstates its sequestration, and the overstatement is largest where diatoms dominate. Carbon accounting is the use of the number, and a budget that confuses production with sequestration misleads every policy that depends on it. A model term and a regional budget differ by three orders of magnitude in what they control, and the paper is about both.

Models that assign export efficiency by functional group would need to revise the diatom term. A revised term is a small change to a model and a large change to a regional budget, and the discrepancy between those two scales is what the paper exposes. Shear is a mechanical process that a model can include, and its inclusion would change the predicted fate of an aggregate. The revision is the deliverable that a modelling group can act on, and acting on it is what closes the loop between measurement and prediction.
