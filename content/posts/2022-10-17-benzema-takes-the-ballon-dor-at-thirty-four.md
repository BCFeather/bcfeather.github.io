France Football announced the award in Paris on 17 October, and Karim Benzema took the men's prize for the first time. He was 34, which makes him the oldest winner since Stanley Matthews in 1956. He had finished fourth in 2021 and had never been higher than third before. He is the fifth French player to win, and the first since Zinedine Zidane in 1998.

His season had produced 44 goals in 46 matches for Real Madrid. He scored 15 in the Champions League, including hat-tricks against Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea and a late equalizer against Manchester City in the semi-final. Real Madrid won the competition for the fourteenth time, and Benzema was its captain in the final. His hat-trick at Stamford Bridge came in 17 minutes, and it is the fastest in the competition's history.

Benzema received 549 points, and Sadio Mané was second with 193, and Kevin De Bruyne third with 175. The award is voted by journalists from 100 countries, and each votes for five players. The margin was the largest since the voting format changed in 2016. The ceremony had been moved from its usual date because of the World Cup, which started in November. That meant the award covered the 2021-22 season rather than the calendar year, and the World Cup itself would count towards the 2023 prize. France Football said the change was temporary.

The women's award went to Alexia Putellas for the second year, and she had torn a ligament in July and had not played since. The club award went to Manchester City, and the young player award to Gavi. The ceremony was held at the Theatre du Chatelet, and it was broadcast in 100 countries.

Benzema's career had been complicated. He had been left out of the France squad for five years after a scandal in 2015, and he returned in 2021 for the European Championship. He won the Nations League with France in October 2021, and he was selected for the World Cup squad in November before withdrawing with an injury. Putellas had won the award in 2021, and her second win made her the third player to repeat.

Lionel Messi had won it seven times and Cristiano Ronaldo five, and the voters had been criticized for rewarding reputation over the season. The prize was read as a correction. Benzema's win, and Mané's second place, were taken as evidence that the voting had changed. He played one more season at Real Madrid and moved to Al-Ittihad in 2023. His last act in Europe was to lift the Copa del Rey in May, and he left as the club's second-highest scorer of all time. The 2023 award went to Messi after the World Cup, and the argument about the criteria continued. He finished the season with 44 goals, his best return in fourteen years at the club.
