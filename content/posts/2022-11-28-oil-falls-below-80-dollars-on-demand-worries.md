Brent settled at 78.30 dollars a barrel on 28 November, and West Texas Intermediate at 73.60. Both were the lowest since January and 30 per cent below their level of June. The fall came as China reported a rise in cases and as the European Union debated a price cap on Russian crude. The fall began in June, and it accelerated in November as the restrictions in China widened.

Demand forecasts had been cut for the sixth month. The International Energy Agency lowered its estimate for 2023 by 500,000 barrels a day, and it said the slowdown was broad. The agency said Chinese demand would grow more slowly than expected because of the restrictions, and it said European demand would fall 3 per cent. The agency said demand in Europe would fall 3 per cent, and in China growth would be 2 per cent.

Supply was holding up. The group of producers had cut 2 million barrels a day from November, and the effect was smaller than the headline because members were already producing below their quotas. American output was 12.1 million barrels a day, close to its record, and the rig count had risen for two years. American production was 12.1 million barrels a day, which is close to the record set in 2020.

How the price cap would work was the unknown. The European Union was negotiating a level between 60 and 70 dollars, and the market was trying to work out what would happen if Russia refused to sell at that price. The cap took effect on 5 December at 60 dollars, and Russian exports fell 20 per cent in the following month. The cap was set at 60 dollars, and the European Union agreed the level on 2 December.

Traders said it was the clearest signal of the year. The futures curve had been in backwardation for a year, which means that near-term prices were higher than later ones. It moved into contango in November, which means the opposite, and that is a sign that the market expects supply to exceed demand. The physical market was still tight for diesel. The crack spread for diesel was 45 dollars a barrel, and inventories were 15 per cent below normal. The price of crude fell, and the price of refined products did not fall as fast. That gap is what kept inflation high in the winter. Traders said the contango was 2 dollars a barrel for the six-month contract.

Brent reached 87 dollars in September 2023, and it averaged 82 for the year, which is lower than in 2022. The fall helped consumers. The average price of petrol in the United States was 3.60 dollars a gallon on 28 November, down from 5 in June. The decline was worth 0.5 points off headline inflation, and it arrived in time for the holiday shopping season. The price stayed near 80 until the spring. The reopening of China raised demand in the first quarter, and the group cut output again in April.
