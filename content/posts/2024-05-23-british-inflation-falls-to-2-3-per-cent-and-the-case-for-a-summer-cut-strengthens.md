British consumer prices rose 2.3 per cent in the year to April, down from 3.2 per cent in March and close to the central bank's target, and the core measure fell to 3.9 per cent. The figures were published on 22 May and were below what the forecasters expected. The prime minister called the figure a return to normal, and the opposition pointed out that prices are still a fifth higher than they were three years ago.

Energy is the explanation for most of the fall. The cap on household bills was lowered in April, and the price of gas in the wholesale market is far below its peak, which removed the largest single contribution to the inflation of the previous two years. A price that is administered is a price that falls on a known date. The regulator sets the cap every three months, and the next change in July is expected to lower the bill again, which means the contribution will stay negative through the summer.

Services inflation is the remainder. Prices in the service sector rose 5.9 per cent, slower than the month before and still well above the rate consistent with the target, and the committee has said that this measure is what it is watching. A number that falls slowly is a number that keeps the argument alive. The wage settlements in the public sector have been larger than the private ones, and the committee has said it is watching the settlements that are funded by the taxpayer.

Market moved quickly. Traders priced a cut in June at a probability above fifty per cent for the first time, and the pound weakened against the dollar, which is the reaction of a market that had expected the bank to wait. A price that is repriced is a position that has to be closed. The cut that the market now expects would be the first since 2020, and the committee has said it will not move until it is confident that the service prices are falling for good.

Election is the complication. The government has called a vote for July, and the opposition has been arguing that the improvement in the figures is the result of a global fall in energy prices rather than of any domestic policy. An argument about causes is an argument that the voters decide. The vote is on 4 July, and a cut that arrives before it would be read as a political event even if the committee insists that it is not.

What the month shows is that the last mile of the return to target runs through wages and through services, and the two measures move more slowly than the headline that the public reads. A target that is reached while the underlying measures remain above it is a target that has been met on the headline and not yet in the mechanism, and that is the distinction the bank keeps making.
