description: "Sensory neurons that signal pain also direct dendritic cells, attracting them with a chemokine, tuning their inflammatory programme with a neuropeptide and coupling to them electrically."
---

Nerves and immunity meet in the skin, and the meeting has been treated as a coincidence of location. A nerve ending and a dendritic cell occupy the same tissue, and the assumption has been that they act in parallel rather than in conversation.

In *Science* on 31 March 2023, the conversation is documented. The authors show that nociceptors, the neurons that carry pain signals, control dendritic cells in at least three ways, each with its own molecular channel.

Recruitment is the first. A nociceptor releases the chemokine CCL21, which attracts dendritic cells to the tissue and holds them there, so a nerve that is active sets the density of immune cells around it. Programming is the second, because the neuron releases the neuropeptide CGRP when triggered and induces a poised gene programme in the dendritic cell, a state in which the cell is ready to respond but not yet responding.

Electrical coupling is the third. The neuron can drive calcium flux and membrane depolarization in the dendritic cell, so the immune cell's activity follows the neuron's, and a direct electrical link between a neuron and an immune cell is an unusual finding. Immune cells are not excitable the way neurons are, and a depolarization that changes their behaviour implies a channel the paper identifies, which is the mechanistic core of that route.

Anatomy supports the claim, since nerve endings in the dermis lie close to the dendritic cells that patrol it and the imaging shows the contact, though proximity is not proof and the three mechanisms are what turn proximity into interaction. Context decides the route, because the paper shows that which mechanism operates depends on the state of the tissue, and that dependence is what distinguishes a physiological conversation from a fixed wiring diagram.

Inflammation follows from the pain signal, since an injury activating nociceptors recruits and programs the cells that present antigen, and the response is tuned by the nervous system. Clinical use suggests a target, because if pain neurons set the tone of local immunity then blocking a neuropeptide changes an inflammatory disease, and the receptor on the immune cell is reachable without touching the neuron.

Model sets the limitation, since skin in a mouse is not skin in a person and the density of innervation and the subtypes of dendritic cells differ. Infection is what the paper does not measure, because a poised dendritic cell may present antigen more effectively or less, and the outcome for a pathogen is the test the mechanism still needs. Neuroimmunology gains a vocabulary, since recruitment, programming and coupling are three verbs where the field had one word. What a therapy would need is a schedule, since blocking a neuropeptide for weeks may change the immune cell's baseline rather than its response to an injury, and the timing is the variable no single measurement settles. Blocking one route may simply move the work to another, and the three mechanisms being context-dependent is the reason a single antagonist rarely abolishes the interaction.
