American output grew at an annual rate of 2.4 per cent in the second quarter, above the forecast of about 1.8 and up from 2 per cent in the first. The advance estimate from the commerce department was published on 27 July. The growth has now been above 2 per cent for four quarters. The figure compared with the forecast range of 1.5 to 2.0 per cent, and the year-on-year growth was about 2.6. The nominal output grew at about 4.7 per cent, and the deflator was about 2.2, which is the lowest since the spring of 2021.

Consumption carried it. The personal spending rose at about 1.6 per cent, and the services grew faster than the goods as the travel and the restaurants recovered. The durable goods fell as the car purchases slowed after the strong spring, and the spending on the food services rose. The spending on the transport services rose at about 12 per cent, and the one on the recreation at about 6. The goods spending fell at about 0.3 per cent, and the one on the motor vehicles at about 4.

Investment was strong. The business spending on the equipment rose at about 7.7 per cent, and the one on the structures at about 9.7 as the factories and the data centres were built. The residential investment fell for the ninth quarter, and the drag was about 0.2 percentage points. The spending on the computers rose at about 20 per cent, and the one on the industrial equipment at about 8 as the factories were built.

Inventories and the trade subtracted. The change in the stock took about 0.1 points, and the net trade about 0.3 as the imports fell less than the exports. The government spending rose at about 2.6 as the state and the local budgets grew. The imports fell at about 7 per cent, and the exports at about 10, which is the pattern of the weaker global demand.

Inflation measures eased. The core PCE price index rose at about 3.8 per cent, down from 4.9 in the first quarter, and the headline at about 2.6. The saving rate fell to about 3.6, which is the lowest since the autumn of 2022. The compensation per the employee rose at about 4.6 per cent, and the productivity at about 1.5.

This report is the one the committee wanted before it decides the autumn. A growth of 2.4 per cent with the core inflation at 3.8 is the combination that argues for one more rise rather than a pause, and the meeting is the one that will settle it. The committee meets on 25 and 26 July, and the market expects the rate to be left at 5.25 to 5.5 per cent until the autumn.
