NATO leaders met in Washington on 9 and 10 July for the alliance's seventy-fifth anniversary and agreed a package of support for Ukraine that commits members to a year of deliveries and to a share of the cost. The communiqué described Ukraine's path to membership as irreversible and did not name a date. A communiqué is drafted by officials and signed by leaders, and every adjective in it was negotiated.

Language is the compromise. A word that promises direction without a timetable is a word that satisfies a government that wants to join and a government that does not want a treaty obligation. A promise of irreversibility is a promise that the next summit can reinterpret. A country that is promised membership eventually is a country that reforms its courts and its procurement in the meantime. A word that is repeated in every communiqué is a word that has acquired a meaning through repetition rather than through text.

Money is the harder commitment. A pledge to fund a share of the aid is a pledge that parliaments have to vote, and several members have governments that cannot pass a defence increase without a coalition argument. A pledge made at a summit is a pledge that a finance ministry implements. A parliament that votes for aid is a parliament that has to explain the cost to a taxpayer who is also paying for energy. The pledges were counted in national contributions, which means the total depends on budgets that have not been voted.

Production is the constraint. Shells and interceptors are made on lines that were built for smaller volumes, and the orders that would expand them take years to arrive. A shortage of ammunition is a shortage of industrial capacity, and capacity is a decision about investment. A production line that runs one shift is a line that cannot be doubled without a second shift and a second supplier.

Political risk was visible in the room. A new American administration could change the commitment, and the European members used the summit to argue that the alliance should be able to act without the United States. A plan for European autonomy is a plan that needs money that the same members are unwilling to spend. A European plan that duplicates American capacity is a plan that spends money twice for the same deterrent.

What the summit establishes is that the alliance has agreed on direction and deferred the cost. A communiqué that is read as a signal to Moscow and as a promise to Kyiv is a communiqué whose meaning depends on the budgets that follow it. A summit that is remembered for a phrase is a summit that moved the argument rather than the money. A summit that was held in the city where the treaty was signed was a summit that wanted to be photographed against its own history.
