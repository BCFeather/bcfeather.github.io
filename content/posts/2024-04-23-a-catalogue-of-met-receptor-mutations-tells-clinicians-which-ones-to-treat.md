MET is a receptor tyrosine kinase that drives growth when mutated or amplified, and inhibitors against it are approved for advanced lung cancer. A paper in eLife on 23 April 2024 builds a catalogue of MET mutations. MET exon 14 skipping is the alteration with an approved indication, and a skipping event is a splicing change rather than a point mutation. Prediction of a second drug is the practical value of a resistance map, and the map is what the paper assembles. A catalogue is a reference and not a recommendation, and the difference is what keeps it useful to a clinician. Practice without a guide still treats patients, and treatment without a guide is what a catalogue is meant to replace.

A catalogue is what a clinician needs before ordering a test, since a variant of unknown significance is a result without a decision. Classification separates variants that activate the kinase from those that do not, and activation is the criterion that connects a mutation to a drug. Splicing changes require a different assay from sequencing a hotspot, and assay choice is where a catalogue meets a laboratory. Assembly from published reports inherits their biases, and the bias is that responding patients are reported more often. Usefulness depends on updates, and a catalogue that is not maintained becomes a historical document.

A mutation that does not activate is a passenger, and treating a passenger is treating nothing. Structural reasoning supports the classification, since a variant in the kinase domain can be judged against the shape of the binding pocket. Hotspot sequencing is cheaper and misses skipping, and missing a responsive patient is the cost of the cheaper test. Reporting bias is the limitation a catalogue of case reports always carries, and the paper's framing is appropriate to it. Maintenance is the hidden cost of every curated resource, and the cost is why many such resources lapse.

A pocket that a drug occupies is a pocket where a mutation can prevent binding. Amplification is a third class, and copy number is a continuous variable that a threshold turns into a category. A resource that lapses leaves practice without a guide, and the guide is what the next guideline revision will cite.

Resistance mutations are the second use of the catalogue, and a record of them is what tells a clinician why a patient stopped responding. Thresholds differ between trials, and a difference in threshold is a difference in the population a drug is approved for.

Stopping is a clinical event that a sequence can explain. Approval by threshold is how oncology works, and working that way makes the catalogue a guide to trial eligibility rather than to biology.

The catalogue is a resource rather than a trial, and a resource changes practice only when guidelines adopt it. Biology still matters for combinations, since a resistant variant predicts which second drug might work.
