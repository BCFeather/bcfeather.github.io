The fear going into the quarter was that a trade war would cut advertising before it cut anything else. Advertising is the first budget a firm trims when it cannot see the next six months, and search advertising is the most exposed part of Alphabet's business. The results reported on 24 April said the trim had not happened, at least not yet.

Revenue rose 12 per cent to 90.23 billion dollars, or 14 per cent at constant currency. Google Services brought in 77.3 billion, up 10 per cent, with search, YouTube advertising and subscriptions each growing at a double-digit rate. Cloud revenue rose 28 per cent to 12.26 billion, slightly below the consensus of 12.27 billion, which is the kind of miss that says more about the forecast than about the business.

Profit rose faster than revenue. Operating income increased 20 per cent to 30.6 billion dollars and the operating margin widened two points to 34 per cent. Net income rose 46 per cent to 34.54 billion, a figure that includes a gain on an investment, and adjusted earnings per share were 2.27 dollars against expectations near 2.01. Capital expenditure was 17.2 billion, most of it servers and data centres.

Cloud is where the margin story sits. Operating income for the division rose to 2.2 billion dollars and its margin went from 9.4 to 17.8 per cent, even as depreciation rose with the fleet. A cloud business that grows 28 per cent and doubles its margin is a business that has finished building ahead of demand and started filling the capacity it built. That is the point in the cycle when a capital-intensive division stops being a drag.

The company returned more than it spent in one sense and less in another. It raised the quarterly dividend 5 per cent to 21 cents and authorized up to 70 billion dollars of buybacks, while guiding to higher capital spending for the year. A firm that buys back stock and builds data centres at the same time is telling investors that it thinks the return on the servers exceeds the return on the shares.

Competition from chat assistants did not show up in the search line. That is the number the market wanted, because the argument against Alphabet has been that a conversational interface would take queries away from a results page. A 10 per cent rise in Services revenue in a quarter when a rival model was being adopted widely is evidence against that argument, although one quarter of advertising says little about a five-year shift in how people ask questions.

What would settle it is the next two quarters and the cost of the compute. If advertising holds while capital spending rises, the year is a story about a durable franchise. If advertising slows and spending keeps rising, the year is a story about a company defending a position, and the market will price the defence rather than the franchise.
