Officials disqualified Magnus Carlsen from the World Rapid Championship in New York on 27 December after he refused a request to change out of jeans, and the incident became the most discussed event of a tournament that had been about the clock. The governing body said the dress code applies to all players and that it had warned him before the round. A tournament that is played in a hotel ballroom is a tournament that has a sponsor to please. A rule applied in the middle of a round is a rule that a player experiences as a negotiation.

Rules exist for the broadcast. A tournament that sells its feed to a sponsor is a tournament that wants its players to look like professionals, and a dress code is the part of the contract that the organizer can enforce cheaply. A player who complies is a player who has accepted that the event is a product. A code that is printed in the regulations is a code that a player signs when entering. A player who signs the regulations is a player who has accepted a code that the organiser can change.

Player's argument was about the boundary. Carlsen said he had been told the previous day that a jacket and shirt were acceptable and that the jeans had not been raised, and he offered to change for the next round. A demand made mid-tournament is a demand that a player experiences as arbitrary. A warning given at the start of the day is a warning that a player can plan around.

Consequence was larger than a fine. A reigning champion who is removed from a competition is a champion whose absence changes the value of the title, and the winner of the rapid event is a winner who did not beat him. A sport that depends on one player's participation is a sport that has a problem with its own rules. A title that is won in the absence of the champion is a title with an asterisk. A tournament that loses its headline player loses the audience that the sponsor paid for.

Governing body's position is the harder one to defend in public. A code that is enforced inconsistently is a code that looks like a personal dispute, and the officials who applied it had a discretion that they used. A rule that is applied with discretion is a rule that invites a challenge. A discretion that is used once is a discretion that will be used again.

What the episode exposes is that chess has become a commercial sport without commercial institutions. A player who is a brand, an organizer who sells a broadcast and a federation that writes the rules are three parties with different interests, and the trousers were the surface on which they met. A player who is bigger than the federation is a player whose departure would cost the event.
