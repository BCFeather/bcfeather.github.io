The ceremony on 12 March gave seven awards to a film that had opened in a handful of cinemas a year earlier and had been made for a budget smaller than the marketing spend of most of its competitors. Everything Everywhere All at Once took best picture, best director, best actress, both supporting categories and the screenplay and editing prizes. Its directors, Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert, accepted the best picture award together and thanked the crew by name.

Michelle Yeoh won best actress, which made her the first Asian woman to hold that prize, and her speech was the evening's most quoted passage. She noted that she had been in the industry for forty years and that the award had arrived at an age when roles are supposed to shrink. Ke Huy Quan won best supporting actor, thirty-eight years after his last appearance in a film, and his speech was about the years in between.

Brendan Fraser won best actor for a performance that had required him to wear a prosthetic body, and the award was read as a return for an actor who had left the industry for a decade. Jamie Lee Curtis won best supporting actress, and the two supporting prizes from one film were a first for the ceremony in decades. All Quiet on the Western Front took four awards, including the international feature prize, and Navalny took the documentary prize.

The broadcast was the third in a row with falling ratings, and the producers had tried to fix it by shortening the speeches and by handing several craft awards before the telecast. The changes produced a shorter show and a smaller audience, and the academy's governors said afterwards that the experiment would be reviewed. The ceremony's central problem was that the films it honoured were made for adults who do not watch a three-hour broadcast, and the problem had no technical fix.

Read as a statement about what the industry had begun to fund, the wins described a shift that had been building for a decade. A film with a small budget, a cast of Asian and Asian American actors, and a plot about quantum mechanics and family grief had been the most awarded of the year, and the studios that had spent two hundred million dollars on a single picture went home with technical prizes. The shift had been building for a decade, as streaming weakened the theatrical market and the awards body expanded its membership.

What the ceremony could not do was settle the argument about the medium. The films that won were the ones that had been seen by the fewest people, and the films that were seen by most people were the ones that the ceremony ignored. The gap between the two had grown for twenty years, and the evening's answer was to celebrate the smaller films with more enthusiasm, which is what an institution does when it has decided to be a guild rather than a mirror. The guild had added hundreds of members from outside the studio system in the previous five years, and the membership was the reason the voting changed.
