American employers added 209,000 jobs in June, below the forecast of about 225,000 and the smallest gain since December 2020. The unemployment rate fell to 3.6 per cent from 3.7, and the participation held at 62.6. The two previous months were revised down by about 110,000. The figure was the first below 250,000 since the spring, and the three-month average is about 244,000. The revisions took about 0.3 percentage points off the growth of the second quarter on the annual basis, and the economists said the base was weaker than it had looked.

Hiring slowed but held. The government added about 60,000 jobs, the health care about 41,000, and the leisure about 21,000. The construction added about 23,000, and the manufacturing about 7,000. The temporary help fell for a fifth month, which the economists read as the leading sign of the cooling. The retail shed about 11,000 jobs, and the transport and the warehousing about 12,000 as the parcel volumes fell. The job openings have fallen from about 12 million last year to about 9.8 million in May, and the ratio of the openings to the unemployed is about 1.6.

Wage growth held at 4.4 per cent over the year, and the monthly change was 0.4. The measure for the production workers was about 4.8, and the one for the managers about 4.1. The gap has been closing since the winter, and the pace is still about double the level consistent with the target. The wage growth for the leisure was about 5.2 per cent, and the one for the information sector about 3.

A second survey was strong. The employment rose by about 270,000, and the number of the unemployed fell by about 140,000. The prime-age participation was about 83.5, which is the highest since 2008, and the share of the people working part-time for the economic reasons fell. The long-term unemployed, the ones out of work for more than 27 weeks, were about 1.1 million, which is the lowest since 2019.

Markets read it as a mixed report. The two-year yield fell by about 10 basis points and the ten-year by about 5, and the traders kept the odds of a July increase at about 90 per cent. The dollar fell against the basket. The committee meets on 25 and 26 July, and the market expects another quarter-point increase to 5.25 to 5.5 per cent.

This report is the one the committee wanted to see before July. A job market that adds 209,000 jobs with the wages at 4.4 is a market that is cooling without breaking, and the cooling is the part that the arithmetic needs. The inflation report of 12 July is the other half of the case, and the core at 4.8 is the number the committee wants to see fall.
