American core prices rose 3.2 per cent in the year to November, the commerce department said on 22 December, down from 3.5 per cent in October and below the forecast of 3.3. The headline measure rose by about 2.6 per cent, and the energy fell by about 4 as the petrol came down. The figure was below the forecast of 3.3, and the measure is now about half the peak of 5.4 per cent at the start of the year. The measure is the one the Fed uses for the target, and the reading is the last before the meeting in January.

Prices were almost flat on the month, with a change of 0.1 per cent, and the six-month pace was about 2.4, which is close to the target. A pace that is near is a cut that is near. The three-month pace was about 2.4, and the economists said the run rate is now consistent with the target. The three-month pace was about 2.4 per cent on the annualized basis, and the six-month was about 2.8. The energy fell by about 4 per cent over the month, and the food rose by about 0.2 as the groceries stopped falling.

Goods were flat to lower. The prices of the goods excluding the food and the energy fell by about 0.2 per cent, and the used cars and the furniture did the most. A good that is cheaper is a discount that is passed on. The clothing fell by about 1 per cent, and the recreational goods fell by about 2 as the discounts were deep.

Services were firm. The prices rose by about 0.2 per cent, and the housing and the health care were the parts that held. A service that is firm is the last mile of the measure. The housing rose by about 0.4 per cent, and the health care by about 0.2, which is the pattern of the last year. The transport services fell by about 1 per cent as the air fares dropped, and the insurance rose by about 1.

Spending was solid. The consumer spending rose by about 0.2 per cent, and the incomes rose by about 0.4 as the wages and the transfers grew. A spending that is steady is a demand that the rates have not stopped. The saving rate fell to about 4 per cent, and the real spending has grown by about 2 per cent over the year. The real disposable income rose by about 0.3 per cent, and the saving rate was about 4 per cent.

What the month shows is that the measure the Fed targets is now within a point of the goal and the services are the resistance, and the resistance is the reason the cuts are being discussed rather than promised. A core at 3.2 per cent is a core that the Fed will describe as progress, and the description is the reason the market is pricing the cuts. The market moved the first cut to the spring, and the two-year yield fell to the lowest since the summer.
