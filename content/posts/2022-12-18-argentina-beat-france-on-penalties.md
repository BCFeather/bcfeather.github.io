The match finished three goals each after extra time, and Argentina won the shootout four two. Messi had scored the first from the penalty spot and the third in the hundred and eighth minute, and Mbappé had scored all three for France. Nobody who watched the last half hour will forget the sequence of it. The stadium held nearly ninety thousand people, and the noise after the equalizer was the loudest of the tournament.

Argentina controlled the first hour without much difficulty. Di María won the penalty that Messi converted and then scored the second himself after a move that began on the right and ended with a cut-back. France had not had a shot on target until the final minutes of normal time, and its midfield was chasing shadows for long stretches. The Argentine supporters had begun to sing about the trophy before half time. Argentine midfield pressed in a shape that France could not break, and the full-backs pushed high on both sides of the pitch.

Then the match turned within two minutes. A penalty gave France its first goal, and Mbappé scored the second with a volley from inside the area less than a minute later. The French bench, which had been silent, was on its feet, and the Argentine players looked like men who had seen something impossible. The last ten minutes of normal time were played at a pace that neither team could sustain for much longer. Both benches were on their feet for most of the added period, and the fourth official had to intervene twice.

Extra time produced two more goals from the same two players. Messi scored from close range after a save, and Mbappé equalized from the spot after a handball that the video assistant confirmed. A save by the Argentine goalkeeper in the last seconds kept the score level, and the referee blew for the shootout after a hundred and twenty minutes that felt longer. Mbappé's three goals made him the first man to score a hat-trick in a final since 1966.

The shootout was settled by nerves rather than by technique. The French substitute missed wide, the Argentine goalkeeper saved the second, and the third went in. When the last Argentine penalty went in, the players ran to the goalkeeper and the bench emptied onto the pitch. Messi was carried on shoulders, and the television cameras followed him for ten minutes without cutting away.

Messi had said before the tournament that this would be his last World Cup, and he had already won the Golden Ball as the best player of the competition. His career had been measured against this trophy for fifteen years, and the argument is now settled in a way that arguments rarely are. The French president was on the pitch afterwards, and so were three generations of Argentine players, and the rain that had threatened all evening finally arrived. He had won everything else the game offers, and the one trophy missing had been the subject of a thousand television arguments.
