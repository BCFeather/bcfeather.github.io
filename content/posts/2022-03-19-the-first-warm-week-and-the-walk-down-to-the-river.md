Warmth arrived on a Tuesday and stayed for six days. It came with a wind from the south that brought the smell of wet earth up from the fields on the edge of the city, and by Wednesday the plane trees along the road had produced the small hard buds that look, from a distance, like a green mist. People who had spent the winter walking with their shoulders up around their ears began to walk with their hands out of their pockets.

Down at the river the water was still low and clear, the colour of weak tea, and the willows on the bank had gone yellow-green in a single week. A man in a padded jacket sat on a folding stool with three rods in the water and a thermos beside him, and he said he had caught nothing since morning and that it did not matter. Two women spread a plastic sheet on the grass and sorted a crate of oranges, and the peelings went into a bag that they tied to the branch of a tree.

The path along the bank had been rebuilt the year before, with a surface of red gravel and lamps every twenty metres, and the benches were new enough that the wood still smelled of the treatment. A group of old men played cards on one of them, slapping the cards down on a wooden board, and a boy on a scooter wove between them and the railing. Nobody looked at the river, which is what a river is for in a city: a thing to be next to.

I sat on the bench with a phone and read the news for an hour, which is a thing I have been doing too much of. The reports from the south of Ukraine had the quality of a serial, with the same places returning in a different order of destruction, and the numbers were becoming hard to hold. A woman walked past with a dog and said something about the weather to nobody in particular, and I realized that I had been holding my shoulders up around my ears as well.

The river does not know about any of it. It carries the same volume it carried last March, when the news was a pandemic that had begun to feel like weather, and it will carry the same volume next March. The willow roots hold the bank together, the gravel path holds the people, and neither has an opinion. There is a comfort in that which is close to indifference, and I could not decide, sitting there, which of the two it was.

On the sixth day the wind turned and the temperature fell by ten degrees in an afternoon, and the buds on the plane trees closed again. The man with the rods folded his stool and walked up the path with the thermos under his arm, and the women with the oranges covered the crate with the plastic sheet. The week had been a loan rather than a payment, and the city went back to its coat, and the river kept going the way it always had, which is the only thing in the piece that can be relied upon.
