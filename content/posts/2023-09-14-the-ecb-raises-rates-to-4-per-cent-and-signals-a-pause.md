Deposit rate rose a quarter point to 4 per cent at the European Central Bank meeting on 14 September, the tenth consecutive increase, and its president said the bank had reached the level that would bring inflation back to the target if it held long enough. The rate has risen by 4.5 points in a year, the fastest increase since the euro was created, and the deposit rate is now at its highest level.

Wording shifted as the statement dropped the sentence that said the rates would be raised as long as necessary and replaced it with a reference to the current level, which the analysts read as the end of the cycle. A sentence that disappears is a direction that stops. The bank said the inflation would stay above three per cent next year and would not reach the target until 2025, which is the reason the rates will stay high.

Forecasts were cut again. Staff lowered its growth estimate for this year to 0.7 per cent and for next year to 1 per cent, and it raised the inflation forecast for this year because of the energy. A forecast that is cut is a case for stopping. The staff now expects the inflation to average 5.6 per cent this year and 3.2 next year, and it cut the growth forecast for the two years that follow. The bank also said the fiscal support would be withdrawn next year, which would reduce the demand that the rates are meant to cool.

Decision was not unanimous. The accounts published later showed a few members wanted to wait, and the president said the discussion had been about the pace and not the direction. A split committee is a committee whose next move is harder. The governors from the smaller economies argued that the energy shock would fade on its own, and the hawks said the wages could keep it alive. The president said the decision had been taken with the full agreement that the rates should stay at this level for as long as it takes.

Traders read it as a peak. The two-year yield fell and the traders moved the first cut to the middle of next year, which is the reaction of a market that believes the statement. A market that believes a pause is a currency that weakens. The euro fell about half a per cent against the dollar, and the spreads of the Italian bonds narrowed as the investors priced the end of the increases.

What the meeting shows is that the bank has decided the cost of more tightening is higher than the cost of waiting, and the waiting will be judged by the wages. The wages are the number the bank has said it will follow, and the settlements for next year will be known in the spring. A pause that is signalled is a pause that the market will test, and the test will be the next inflation report.
