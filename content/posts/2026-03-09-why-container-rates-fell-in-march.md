---

Freight rates are a price for capacity, and capacity has been arriving. Spot rates on the Asia-Europe route fell 22 per cent between January and the first week of March, to about 1,850 dollars per forty-foot container, which is close to the level of early 2024 before the Red Sea diversions added a week to every voyage.

Supply explains the move. Thirty-eight vessels above 15,000 twenty-foot-equivalent capacity were delivered between October and February, adding about 4.5 per cent to the global fleet in five months, and the order book still holds a further 12 per cent. Ships ordered in 2021 at record prices are arriving into a market that did not need them.

Red Sea effect is unwinding in the opposite direction. Diversions around the Cape of Good Hope absorb capacity, roughly 8 per cent of the effective fleet on the affected routes, and any resumption of Suez transits releases that capacity in a single schedule change. Rates fell before the resumption, which tells you what the market expects.

Demand is not the story. Container volume in the first two months was up about 3 per cent year on year, which is consistent with a modest recovery rather than a boom, and the seasonal restocking that usually lifts rates in February was muted because retailers entered the year with high inventory.

Carriers respond by blanking sailings, cancelling a voyage to keep capacity off the market, and the announced blankings for March cover about 6 per cent of advertised capacity. That is the standard instrument and it has a cost: a shipper whose booking is cancelled rebooks at a higher spot rate or waits, and the reliability index falls.

Contract season is the next test. Annual contracts on the east-west routes are negotiated in the spring, and a spot rate near the cost floor gives the buyer leverage. Carriers will try to hold contract rates above spot, and the gap between the two is the measure of how much market power they retain.

Newbuild economics are what to watch. A ship ordered at 2021 prices needs a rate well above 2,000 dollars per container on the Asia-Europe run to service its financing, and a market at 1,850 leaves the least efficient operators covering fuel and crew only. Scrapping is rare for ships under fifteen years old, so the adjustment will come through idling and slow steaming.

Reliability is the hidden cost of blanking. A carrier that cancels a sailing forces the shipper to rebook, and the index of schedule reliability on the east-west routes fell to 52 per cent last quarter. Shippers price that risk into their choice of carrier, which is why the largest carriers with the most reliable schedules can hold rates above the spot market.

For a reader, the lesson is that freight is a capital-cycle industry wearing a spot market's clothes. Rates are set by the interaction of ships ordered three years earlier and demand that moves quarterly, and the lag between the two is where the volatility lives.
