title: "The European Central Bank cuts rates for the third time this year"

description: "The European Central Bank lowered its deposit rate to 3.25 per cent on 17 October, its third cut of the year, and said the outlook for growth had worsened while inflation was close to target."
---European Central Bank cut its three key interest rates by a quarter of a percentage point on the seventeenth of October, taking the deposit rate to 3.25 per cent, the main refinancing rate to 3.40 per cent and the marginal lending rate to 3.65 per cent. Decision took effect on the twenty-third of October and was the third reduction since June, a pace that is faster than the bank had signalled at the start of the year. Governing council described the move as unanimous in substance and said it reflected the direction of the data.

Inflation had fallen to 1.7 per cent in September, below the two per cent target for the first time in three years, and the bank's own projections had been revised down twice. Services inflation, which had been the stubborn component, eased to about four per cent from more than five a year earlier. Wage growth was still elevated, and the bank said it expected the effect of past increases in pay to fade through the winter as new settlements were agreed at lower rates.

Growth is the reason the bank moved faster than it had planned. Surveys of purchasing managers pointed to a contraction in manufacturing that has lasted more than two years, and the German economy, the largest in the bloc, was expected to shrink for a second consecutive year. Households have been saving more than before the pandemic, and construction has been weak since rates rose. Bank said the risks to growth were tilted to the downside, a phrase that had not appeared in its statements earlier in the year.

The Reaction in markets was small because the cut had been fully priced. Euro was little changed, and government bond yields in Germany fell slightly. Traders moved to price a further reduction in December, and economists at several banks said the deposit rate would reach about two per cent by the end of next year, which is a level the bank has described as neutral for the economy.

Politics intruded on the decision in a way that has become routine. Officials in several member states have complained that rates remain too high while budgets are constrained by new fiscal rules, and French and Italian governments have run larger deficits than the rules allow. Bank has insisted that it sets rates for the bloc rather than for individual countries, and its president has repeated that fiscal policy and monetary policy must pull in the same direction.

The Comparison with the American central bank is the argument that matters for markets. Federal Reserve has cut once, by half a point, and is constrained by stronger growth and firmer inflation, which leaves the two on diverging paths and pushes the euro down against the dollar. That depreciation supports European exporters and adds to import costs, which is a mild complication for the inflation outlook. Bank said it was watching the exchange rate and did not set policy for it, which is the standard formulation and does not settle how much it matters.
