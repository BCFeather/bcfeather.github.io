Consumer prices rose 3.4 per cent in the year to April, down from 3.5 per cent in March, and the core measure that excludes food and energy eased to 3.6 per cent. The report was published on 15 May, and it was the first in four months that did not surprise to the upside. The figure was a tenth lower than the previous month and in line with the forecast, and the report was read as the first sign that the spring surge had ended.

Shelter is the stubborn component. The cost of housing in the index rises with rents that were signed a year earlier, and the lag between the market and the statistic means that the measure is still reflecting the surge of 2022. A component with a lag is a component that keeps the headline high after the cause has passed. The rents in the index are calculated from a survey of tenants that is taken every six months, and the measure lags the rent that a new tenant actually pays by about a year.

Goods prices fell again, which is the part of the basket that reflects the repair of the supply chains, and the used car market continued to normalize after the shortage. A price that falls is a price that the central bank does not have to fight, and the goods side has been the easy half of the problem. The motor insurance rose by more than twenty per cent in the year, and the regulators in several states have approved the increases that the insurers had requested.

Services excluding housing rose at a slower pace, which is the number the committee watches because it tracks wages, and the improvement was small enough that the market did not change its view of the year. A number that moves in the right direction is a number that buys the committee time. The food prices rose more slowly than at any time since 2021, and the grocery chains have been reporting that the customers are switching to their own brands.

Market reaction was modest. Yields fell a few basis points, the dollar weakened, and the probability of a cut by September rose slightly, which is the response of a market that has already priced most of the good news. A report that meets expectations is a report that changes little. The report left the three-month annualized rate at about four per cent, which is the number the committee has said it wants to see fall before it cuts.

What the month shows is that the last mile of disinflation runs through housing, and the statistic that measures housing moves more slowly than the economy it describes. A measure that is stuck at three per cent while the goods prices fall is a measure driven by the services, and the services are where the wages are.
