A doctor who is exhausted, cynical and convinced that the work accomplishes nothing is a doctor who is described by a burnout inventory, and the inventory has been administered to thousands of samples. What the scores predict about a career and about patients is the question a synthesis can answer. Two decades of surveys have produced a literature large enough to pool, and the pool is what this paper builds.

A search of the literature for observational studies that assessed physician burnout against career engagement and quality of patient care, and pooled 170 studies covering 239,246 physicians with random-effects models. The paper appeared in *The BMJ*, 14 September 2022. A search of the literature for observational studies is a search that has to include the weak designs, and the inclusion is what makes the heterogeneity predictable.

Overall burnout was associated with a pooled odds ratio of 3.79 for reduced job satisfaction, 3.49 for career choice regret and 3.10 for turnover intention, and with a small but significant effect on productivity at 1.82. Direction is the same across the three outcomes, and the consistency is what makes the pattern interpretable.

Heterogeneity was high, with an I-squared above 90 per cent for the main associations, which is the statistic that says the studies were not measuring the same population in the same way. A pooled estimate across very different settings is a summary and not a law.

The authors contacted all the corresponding authors of the included studies and 84 of 170 confirmed the data, which is a step that a meta-analysis rarely takes. Verifying data with the original investigators is the practice that separates a careful synthesis from a citation count. Eighty-four of 170 corresponding authors confirmed their data, and the confirmation is a rate most reviews never report.

Observational studies cannot establish whether burnout causes a doctor to leave or whether a dissatisfied doctor reports burnout, and the cross-sectional design of most of the included work leaves the direction open. A correlation between a state and an intention is the kind of evidence that a policy has to interpret. The cross-sectional design of most included studies leaves the direction of the association open. For health systems the implication is that burnout is not only a personal problem to be managed with resilience training, because the associations run through the structure of the job. A structure that produces exhaustion in most of its workers is a structure that a manager can change. Resilience training addresses the individual, and the associations here run through the organisation.

The evidence is about physicians, and a workforce whose shortages are concentrated in nursing and primary care is not described by a review of doctors. The review covers one profession, and the step to other health workers is the reader's inference and not the paper's claim, which is a limit that a workforce short of nurses will notice.
