A producer who built one of the most successful labels in popular music was charged in New York on 17 September with racketeering conspiracy, sex trafficking and transporting people for prostitution, in an indictment that ran to dozens of pages. He pleaded not guilty and was detained after the judge refused bail. The indictment was filed after a federal investigation that had been running since the previous year, and it names the companies as well as the person.

Racketeering charge is the structure of the case. It allows prosecutors to present years of conduct as a single enterprise, and it requires them to show a pattern rather than a series of separate incidents. A charge that describes an enterprise is a charge that turns a reputation into evidence. The enterprise is described as a group of businesses and employees that acted together, which is the legal form that lets one trial cover a decade of alleged conduct.

Allegations come from people who had worked for him. Witnesses describe auditions, parties and contracts, and the indictment says that employees were required to arrange the encounters, which is the element that makes the conduct corporate rather than personal. A company that organizes the conduct is a company whose liability reaches its executives. Several of the witnesses had signed agreements with his companies and had received payments that the indictment characterizes as hush money.

His defence is that the encounters were consensual and that the witnesses are motivated by money and by publicity. The judge weighed that argument against the risk of flight and the resources of the defendant, and the resources are the reason that the bail was refused. A defendant who can charter an aircraft is a defendant who can leave. The judge cited the defendant's access to aircraft and to foreign residences, and the prosecutors had argued that no conditions could secure his appearance.

Industry has responded by distancing itself. Several companies suspended their relationships with his businesses within days, and a university returned a donation that had funded a programme in his name. An industry that acts in a week is an industry that has decided the commercial risk is larger than the loyalty. The catalogue is the asset that the charges cannot reach, and the label has continued to release records while the case proceeds. The companies that cut ties did so within days of the indictment, and none of them explained the decision beyond a statement about the conduct alleged.

What the case shows is that a business built on personal networks is a business that inherits the conduct of the person at the centre. The label will survive if its catalogue does, and the catalogue is the asset that the charges cannot reach. The trial is scheduled for next year, and the delay will be long enough for the industry to reorganize around the absence of its most prominent figure.
