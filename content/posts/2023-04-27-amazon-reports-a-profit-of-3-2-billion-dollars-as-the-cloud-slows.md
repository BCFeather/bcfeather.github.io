Amazon reported a revenue of 127.4 billion dollars for the quarter to March, up 9 per cent, and a profit of 3.2 billion. The shares fell about 2 per cent after the close as the cloud growth missed the higher estimates. The company had lost 3.8 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. The earnings per share of 0.31 dollars were above the forecast of 0.21, and the revenue was in line with the estimate. The company said the second quarter would bring about 4 to 8 per cent of the growth, which is below the forecast of the analysts.

Cloud growth slowed. AWS reported 21.4 billion dollars of revenue, up about 16 per cent, and the operating income fell to about 5.1 billion from 6.5. The management said the customers were optimizing the workloads and delaying the new projects, and the trend would last a few more quarters. The backlog of the cloud grew by about 8 per cent to 114 billion dollars, and the contracts are the promise that the growth will return. The capital spending fell to about 14.2 billion for the year, down from 16.6, as the company slowed the warehouse build.

Retail margins improved. The North America segment reported an operating income of about 0.9 billion against a loss a year earlier, and the international segment lost about 0.25 billion against 1.3. The fulfillment costs fell as the company closed the warehouses. The shipping costs fell by about 20 per cent in the quarter as the company moved the volumes to the cheaper hubs. The company has closed about 30 of the warehouses and paused about 60 projects, and the savings are showing in the delivery times.

Advertising grew by about 23 per cent to 9.5 billion. The business has become one of the largest in the industry, and the company has been putting more of the sponsored units into the search results. The subscriptions rose by about 15 per cent. The advertising revenue reached about 9.5 billion, and the company said the sponsored products were the fastest-growing part. The prime membership kept growing, and the company said the video advertising would start next year on the main platform.

Headcount fell by about 10 per cent from the peak of the winter to about 1.46 million. The company took about 500 million dollars of the severance charges in the quarter, and the finance chief said the costs would fall further. The restructuring charge of about 500 million dollars was the last of the costs of the cuts of the winter. The company said the wages for the warehouse workers would rise by about 5 per cent in the autumn, which is the part of the costs that has resisted the cuts.

This quarter is the one in which the cost control reached the retail business. A company that earns 3.2 billion dollars after a loss of 3.8 is a company that has stopped spending ahead of the demand. The shares fell about 2 per cent after the call, and the investors have been asking when the cloud would grow by 20 per cent again. A retailer that earns more than the cloud for a quarter is a retailer that has learned to run the warehouses at the size it has.
