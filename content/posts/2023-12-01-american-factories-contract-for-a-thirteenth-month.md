Manufacturing activity measured 46.7 in November on the Institute for Supply Management index, unchanged from October and below the forecast of 47.6. A reading under 50 signals the contraction. The reading has been below the line for thirteen months, the longest stretch since the downturn of 2001, and the index has not been above 50 since October of last year. The survey covers the firms that buy the materials and the parts, and the reading is one of the first indicators of the month.

Orders were the weakest part. The new orders index was about 48, and the backlog fell for a fourteenth month as the customers bought only what they needed. The number of the manufacturing payrolls has fallen by about 30,000 over the year, and the survey suggests more cuts in the winter.

Prices were flat. The index was about 49, and the respondents said the steel and the aluminium were cheaper while the labour was still the cost. A cost that holds is a margin that is protected. The prices paid index has been under 50 for eight months, and the one of the supplier deliveries was about 46 as the lead times shortened.

Industries were split. Six of the eighteen industries reported the growth, and the machinery and the transport equipment were among the ones that contracted.
