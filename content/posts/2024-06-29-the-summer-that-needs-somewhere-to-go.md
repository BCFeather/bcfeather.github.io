School holidays begin this week. For a household with two working parents, the holiday is a gap of seven weeks that has to be filled by somebody. Options are a grandparent, a paid class, a camp, or a child alone at home with a phone. The schools were asked to run a summer service three years ago. A centre that joins the programme keeps its building open for a month and staffs it with teachers on rotation. Daily fee is small enough to be covered by a lunch allowance. The programme is voluntary for school and for family.

The content is modest. A morning of reading, an afternoon of sport, a film on Friday. Supervision is what the service is meant to provide. The teachers who run it are paid an allowance that sits below their hourly rate, which is the reason the rota fills slowly in some districts. Public institutions fill within minutes. A science museum releases tickets for a holiday workshop and the page is empty by half past nine. A children's palace runs a drawing class for thirty and receives three hundred applications.

Tutoring that survives the regulation goes underground in the summer. A class of eight in an apartment, advertised by word of mouth, paid in cash. Enforcement teams patrol the buildings in July and the classes move to the suburbs. University students take the work that pays. A graduate who is waiting for a result teaches a primary pupil for two hours a day. The rate is thirty or forty yuan an hour, cheaper than a company and less reliable than a relative.

Rural children have a different summer. School is closed and the fields are not. A child of twelve helps with the harvest in the morning and swims in the irrigation channel in the afternoon. The channel is the reason the village broadcasts a warning through the loudspeaker every July. The parents' cost is measured in leave. A worker with ten days of annual leave spends most of them in July. A worker without leave pays for the camp. A camp costs more than the holiday pay would have been, and it is the only option that includes lunch.

A commercial camp market of its own has grown up. A week of football, a week of robotics, a week of English with a foreign teacher. The price ranges from a few hundred to several thousand yuan, and the expensive ones are sold as preparation for a school that the child has not yet entered.

Grandparents are the largest provider of the service and they are not counted. A grandmother who looks after a child for seven weeks has taken on a job that the statistics record as retirement. A child of nine who spends the holiday alone learns the schedule of the delivery drivers before learning anything else. A holiday with no adult in it is the part of the summer that a school cannot fix when the term begins again. By the last week of August the parents are counting the days to the first of September, and the question of who watches the child in July has been answered for another year.
