---

Plant immunity depends on a family of immune receptors that carry a TIR domain, and the domain is the same one that animal cells use in the innate immune system. What the domain does in a plant cell was an open question until the enzymatic products were identified. A receptor family that shares a domain with the animal innate immune system is a family that plant biologists borrowed from immunology, and the borrowing is where the analogy ends.

TIR-containing proteins are shown to catalyse the ADP-ribosylation of ATP and of ADP-ribose through ADPR polymerase-like and NADase activity, forming ADPr-ATP and di-ADPR respectively. The paper appeared in *Science*, 29 July 2022. An enzyme that makes two products from two substrates is an enzyme with a branched output, and the branch is what allows the two immune pathways to be tuned separately.

Binding of ADPr-ATP or di-ADPR allosterically promotes the interaction of the EDS1-SAG101 complex with the helper NLR NRG1A, in vitro and in planta. The product of the enzyme is therefore the signal, rather than a byproduct of the reaction. A product that binds a complex and changes its shape is an allosteric switch, and the switch is the step between the enzyme and the defence response.

A small molecule that is synthesized on demand inside the cell is a signal with no transport problem, and the compartments where the reaction happens are the compartments where the receptor sits. The two molecules diffuse within the same cell, which is the design that a plant immune system needs. A signal with no transport problem is a signal that can act where it is made, and the compartments of synthesis are the compartments where the receptor waits.

TIR domains had been known as a hydrolase of NAD, and the paper turns that into a transferase with two outputs. An enzyme that makes two products that both signal is an enzyme that can produce a graded response, and the two branches of immunity appear to be graded independently. Two branches of immunity that are graded independently is the arrangement that lets a plant spend on one defence without spending on the other.

Two companion papers in the same issue describe the receptor mechanism from the structural side, and the three together establish the signalling molecule and its target. A result that appears in three journals in one week is a result that the field had converged on. Three papers in one issue is the kind of coincidence that a field arranges, and the arrangement is what makes the mechanism credible rather than merely interesting.

Relevance to the clinic is indirect, because a crop that resists disease without a yield penalty is the long-term prize, and the pathway is a target that a breeder can reach. The paper is a mechanism, and a mechanism is the step before a modification. A breeder can reach a pathway that is already defined, and a defined pathway is the step before a modified crop.
