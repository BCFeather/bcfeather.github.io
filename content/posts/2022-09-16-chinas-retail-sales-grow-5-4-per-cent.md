Industrial output rose 4.2 per cent, and fixed asset investment rose 5.8 per cent in the first eight months. The National Bureau of Statistics published the monthly figures on 16 September, and retail sales rose 5.4 per cent in the year to August. The reading was up from 2.7 per cent in July and above the forecast of 3.5.

August 2021 had been affected by lockdowns in several provinces, and the comparison flattered the number. The retail figure was helped by a low base. On a two-year basis the growth was about 2 per cent, which is well below the trend of the previous decade. The statisticians publish both measures, and economists prefer the two-year one.

Car sales were the strongest category. Sales of passenger vehicles rose 30 per cent in August, and the government had halved the purchase tax for small-engine cars in June. Electric vehicles rose 100 per cent, and they were 28 per cent of new sales. The tax cut was due to expire at the end of the year and was extended in the summer.

Property was the weakest. Investment in real estate fell 7.4 per cent in the first eight months, and sales by floor area fell 23 per cent. New starts fell 45 per cent, which is the number that drives demand for steel and cement. The government had cut mortgage rates and relaxed purchase limits in more than 100 cities, and the effect had been small. Sales of home appliances rose 3 per cent after subsidies were reintroduced in July. Sales of home appliances rose 3 per cent after subsidies were reintroduced in July.

Infrastructure investment rose 10 per cent, and manufacturing investment rose 9, which offset part of the property decline. The government had front-loaded spending on projects approved in the previous year, and local governments had been told to finish them. That support was expected to fade in the fourth quarter as the backlog cleared. Household behaviour was the underlying problem. Surveys showed a preference for saving, and the household saving rate had risen from 30 per cent to 33. The zero-covid policy limited travel and entertainment, and services consumption grew more slowly than goods. Youth unemployment was 18.7 per cent in August, the highest on record. Exports rose 7 per cent in August, and the surplus widened despite weaker demand in Europe.

Economists said it would add 0.5 points to growth in the second half, which was not enough to reach the annual target. The stimulus announced in the summer was worth about 1 trillion yuan, and it was aimed at infrastructure and at small firms. The government did not change the target, and it said it would accept a lower number. Growth for the year came in at 3 per cent, the second lowest since 1990. Consumption contributed less than half of the total, and net exports contributed more than usual because imports fell faster than exports. The debate about how to raise household demand continued through the following year.
