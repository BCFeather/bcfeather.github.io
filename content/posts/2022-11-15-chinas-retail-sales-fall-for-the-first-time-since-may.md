---

On the month, sales fell 0.2 per cent, and the two-year comparison was 2.8 per cent. The National Bureau of Statistics published the figures on 15 November, and retail sales fell 0.5 per cent in the year to October. The forecast had been a rise of 1 per cent, and the reading was the first decline since May. Online sales of goods rose 3 per cent, and they were 26 per cent of the total.

Industrial output rose 5 per cent, above the forecast of 4.5, and it was supported by infrastructure spending and by exports. Fixed asset investment rose 5.8 per cent in the first ten months, and infrastructure investment rose 8.7. Property investment fell 8.8, unchanged from the previous month. The restrictions had returned in October. Several cities imposed lockdowns, and the government maintained the zero-covid policy through the month. Travel and entertainment spending fell, and restaurant revenue fell 8 per cent. Online sales rose 3 per cent, and they were 26 per cent of the total. Steel output fell 1 per cent, and cement output fell 3, which reflects the construction slump.

Car sales were the exception. They rose 12 per cent in October, and electric vehicles rose 60 per cent. The tax cut on small-engine cars had been extended to the end of the year, and buyers were bringing purchases forward. The subsidy for electric vehicles ended in December, and sales surged in the last month. The number of cities under full lockdown was 20 at the end of October, and 40 under partial rules.

Property was still the largest drag. Sales by floor area fell 23 per cent in the first ten months, and new starts fell 38 per cent. Developers had defaulted on 4 trillion yuan of debt, and the government had introduced measures to complete unfinished projects. The measures were funded by local governments and were slower than the problem. Property sales fell 23 per cent, and the decline was 30 per cent in the largest cities.

Youth unemployment was 17.9 per cent, down from 18.7 in August, the statisticians also reported. The rate had been suspended for two months in the summer, and the government had changed the definition. The new definition excluded students who were looking for work, and economists said the figure understated the problem.

Consumption recovered slowly after the reopening. The government announced a further 1 trillion yuan of infrastructure spending in the autumn, and it cut quarantine requirements in November. The reopening was announced in December, and the restrictions ended in January. The retail figures for December were the last published under the zero-covid policy. Retail sales rose 8 per cent in the first half of 2023, and services recovered faster than goods. The savings rate stayed high, and the government introduced measures to encourage spending in the summer. The property market did not recover, and investment fell again. The government's measures to finish projects were worth 2 trillion yuan, and they were funded by policy banks.
