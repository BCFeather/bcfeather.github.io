UN human rights office in Palestine said on Friday that repeated strikes on hospitals have pushed Gaza's health system close to collapse, three days after an attack on Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis killed six people and wounded about 30.

Strike hit a building housing the surgical department early on Thursday. Among the dead were a general surgeon, Rizk Abu Shaira, and a 13-year-old patient, according to Gaza's health ministry, and a 20-year-old man wounded in the attack died later, which raised the toll to seven. Volunteers and civil defence crews carried patients out through corridors filled with rubble. Atef al-Hout, who runs the complex, said the blast badly damaged intensive care units and surgical wards, and a British surgeon volunteering there said the strike came just after midnight and shook the whole building.

Its assessment said three missiles were fired and only one detonated, leaving unexploded ordnance beside wards that still hold patients. Nasser is one of the few hospitals in the territory still admitting casualties, and its surgery department stopped taking new cases after the strike, which left intensive care and several wards badly damaged. Health authorities described the attack as a serious assault on the health system and called on the international community and UN agencies to intervene, while families moved wounded relatives to other buildings on the same site.

Israel's military said it struck a senior Hamas commander and other fighters operating inside the hospital, and that Hamas had used the complex as a command centre for planning attacks, storing weapons and sheltering fighters. Reuters said it could not independently verify the allegations. The commander named in the statement was not among the dead. Hamas rejected the account, called the strike a war crime and said it remains committed to the ceasefire agreed in October 2025.

Health officials say 1,622 Palestinians have been killed in attacks since the truce took effect and 5,878 wounded, figures the ministry released on Wednesday. More than 74,000 people have been killed since October 2023, by the same count, and the UN estimates that rebuilding the territory will cost about 71 billion dollars. Aid officials say most of the enclave's hospitals are out of service, and they do not expect the recent Israeli election to change the course of the war.

Its statement recalled three strikes on Nasser in August 2025 that killed 22 people, five of them journalists, and said Israel had not provided enough information to verify claims that hospitals had lost their protected status. A 2024 report cited by the office found that most hospitals in the territory had already been destroyed by then. International humanitarian law obliges all parties to respect and protect medical personnel and facilities, the office said, and the World Health Organization has warned that each attack leaves less of Gaza's care system standing. Its officials have repeatedly asked both sides to protect health facilities.
