description: "In *Caenorhabditis elegans*, a single identified class of interneurons spans the temporal scales that a layered architecture is supposed to divide between separate levels."
---

Animal behaviour is organized into nested patterns that span several timescales, and the architecture that produces them is usually assumed to be layered. A paper in *PNAS* on 12 November 2024 reports that in *Caenorhabditis elegans* one class of interneurons carries the hierarchy on its own. A small nervous system is the reason the question can be settled at all, since a class of cells in a mammal cannot be silenced and watched over several timescales in the same preparation. Three hundred neurons is the scale at which a whole nervous system can be described, and description at that scale is what makes the claim about one class auditable.

The animal is a favourable subject because its nervous system is small and its behaviour is stereotyped. A cell class can be identified, silenced and observed, and the timing of the response can be measured directly. Optogenetic silencing is fast enough to interrupt a bout in progress, which is the manipulation that separates a cell that schedules behaviour from a cell that merely permits it.

One class of interneurons is shown to control behaviour at more than one timescale. Short bouts and longer sequences both change when the same cells are manipulated, which is the observation a layered model does not predict. Two timescales controlled by one class is a statement about fan-out, since the same cells must project to targets that operate on different clocks.

The manipulation is genetic and optogenetic rather than observational, so the claim is causal. A class that can be silenced, and whose silencing alters behaviour at both scales, is a class that organizes both. Causality of this kind is what a correlational account of hierarchy has always lacked, and the nematode supplies it more easily than any vertebrate.

Hierarchy in this animal may therefore be a property of connectivity rather than of layers. A single class with appropriate inputs and outputs can produce nested timing without occupying a distinct level. Connectivity producing hierarchy is a more economical explanation than layers producing it, and economy is an argument in a system with three hundred neurons. Economy of explanation is not proof, and a layered account could still be true if the class is a layer of one.

On the modelling side, the result does not abolish hierarchy, since the class still sits above the motor neurons it drives. What it removes is the assumption that each timescale needs its own tier of interneurons. What a layered architecture supplies in a larger brain is modularity, and modularity is what a single class may not be able to provide.

Whether the same compression exists in animals with larger nervous systems is the obvious question, and an answer would require a preparation in which a class can be manipulated as cleanly. Clean manipulation is the constraint that decides where the question is asked next, and few preparations in vertebrate neuroscience meet it.
