Meta reported first-quarter revenue of 36.46 billion dollars on 24 April, up 27 per cent from a year earlier, and net income of 12.37 billion, which was well above what analysts had modelled. The shares fell anyway, because the company raised its forecast for capital spending again. The shares had risen by more than a third during the year before the release, and the guidance removed the part of that gain that depended on spending discipline.

Advertising market is the reason for the revenue. Demand from retailers and from Chinese sellers of cheap goods recovered during the quarter, and the price of an advertisement rose as the company's recommendations improved the rate at which users respond. A better ranking system is a price rise that the advertiser accepts. The price of an advertisement rose for a fourth consecutive quarter, and the company attributed part of the gain to a ranking model that it rebuilt during the previous year.

Reality Labs remains the cost. The division that builds headsets and the software for them lost close to four billion dollars in the quarter, and it has lost money every quarter since it was separated out. A division that loses money is a division that the company has to justify with a horizon rather than with a number. The company has said the division's losses will narrow next year, and it has not said when the division will break even, which is the question the analysts keep asking.

Spending is the dispute. The company now expects capital expenditure of between 35 and 40 billion dollars this year, most of it for computing capacity to train and run models, and the executives argued that the spending is a response to demand they can already see. A cost that is guided upward twice in a year is a cost that the market stops treating as an investment. The spending is mostly for chips and for the buildings that house them, and the depreciation of that equipment will appear in the accounts for years after the demand that justified it has been tested.

User base is the platform. More than three billion people use one of the applications each month, and the company has begun to push its assistant into the messaging applications where the engagement is highest. A distribution network that is already built is the cheapest place to put a new product. The applications reach more people than any other private platform, and the company has begun to test its assistant in the markets where messaging is the primary way people use the internet.

What the quarter shows is that a company can grow quickly and still disappoint, when the market's question has moved from revenue to the size of the bill that the growth requires. A company that guides costs upward while revenue beats is a company that is choosing growth over the margin, and the market's reaction was a vote on that choice.
