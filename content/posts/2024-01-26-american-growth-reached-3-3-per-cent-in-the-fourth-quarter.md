American output grew at an annual rate of 3.3 per cent in the fourth quarter, according to the advance estimate published on 25 January, well above the 2 per cent that forecasters expected. Consumer spending rose at 2.8 per cent. The figure was the strongest for any quarter since the end of 2021, and the revision to the third quarter left the second half stronger than the first. The figure was stronger than the forecast, and the quarter was the second in a row above three per cent, a pace the economy has not sustained since 2021.

Composition is the reassuring part. The inventories contributed less than in the previous quarter and the trade contributed positively, which means the growth came from the demand rather than from the stock building. A quarter that is driven by the consumer is a quarter that the next one can repeat. The government spending added about half a point, and the state and local governments hired at a pace that the budgets had not planned for. The spending was financed by the savings that households accumulated during the pandemic, and the saving rate fell to 4.0 per cent.

Households had the income to spend. The wages rose faster than the prices, and the saving rate fell, which is the pattern of a household that is drawing on the cushion it built during the pandemic. A cushion that is drawn down is a support that weakens with use. The saving rate fell to about four per cent, which is below the level of 2019 and close to the lowest in the series.

Growth for the year was 2.5 per cent, faster than the forecasters expected at the start of it, and unemploymentyment rate ended the year near a five-decade low. A year that beats the forecast is a year that the models did not describe. The inflation measure in the report rose at an annual rate of about two per cent, which is the first quarter in a year that the number has been at the target.

Market reaction was modest. Yields fell slightly because the report showed the inflation in the quarter was lower than expected, and the probability of a March cut rose a little. A report that is strong on growth and soft on prices is the combination the committee wants. The economists who publish the forecasts have raised their estimates for this year twice since the autumn, and the range is now between one and two per cent.

What the quarter shows is that the economy ended the year with momentum, and the question for the spring is whether the consumer can keep spending without the savings that supported the autumn. A quarter that is strong because the household spends and the government hires is a quarter that depends on two things that can both change quickly.
