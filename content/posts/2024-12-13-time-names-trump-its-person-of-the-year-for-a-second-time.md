---Time named Donald Trump its person of the year on the twelfth of December, the second time it has given him the title, after 2016. Announcement was made by the magazine's editor and published with a profile built on interviews at Mar-a-Lago and on the campaign trail. Citation referred to his comeback after losing the presidency in 2020, to the political realignment that produced it and to the effect his return will have on American institutions. Ritual around the announcement included the president-elect ringing the opening bell at the New York Stock Exchange that morning.

Magazine has given the title to a person or a group every year since 1927, and the choice carries no endorsement of the person chosen. Editors have used it for figures they admired and for figures they considered dangerous, and the profile that accompanies it is often critical. This year's article described the campaign as one built on grievance and on a promise of disruption, and it described the movement that elected him as a coalition that now includes parts of the technology industry, parts of the labour movement and a large share of young men.

Significance of the award is mostly as a register of the year. 2024 was a year of elections in more than sixty countries, most of them decided against incumbent governments, and the magazine's choice reflects the largest of those reversals. Return of a figure who had left office after an attempt to overturn an election, who was then prosecuted, and who campaigned from a courtroom in the middle of a trial is the story that foreign editors found hardest to explain and that American voters found easiest to accept.

Reaction was predictable on both sides. Supporters treated the cover as confirmation that the country had made a decision the establishment had resisted, and critics treated it as the magazine rewarding the thing it had spent nine years warning about. Past recipients have included Hitler in 1938 and Stalin in 1939 and 1942, which the magazine has always defended as documentation rather than celebration, and editors cited that history in the accompanying letter.

The Business of the award is also a business. Cover sales rise in the week of the announcement, and the title has become a brand that the magazine markets through events and through licensing, which is one reason the choice is announced in December and staged in public. Time does publish other lists, and the most influential companies and the annual list of a hundred figures generate revenue across the year.

Political effect is small and the symbolic effect is not. Cover of a magazine does not move votes, and the year's outcome had already been decided five weeks earlier. What the award does is fix a version of the year that other people will argue with, and the argument is already running: whether 2024 should be remembered as the year of a comeback or as the year in which a political system discovered how much of its own legitimacy it had spent. Both readings will be in the history books, and the magazine has chosen one of them.
