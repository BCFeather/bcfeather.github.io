---

The earthquake struck at about one in the afternoon on Monday, and the epicentre was near the coast of Michoacán, in the west of the country. The magnitude was seven point seven by the estimate of the national service, and the depth was about fifteen kilometres, which is shallow enough to be felt across the plateau. Mexico City shook for more than a minute, and the alarms that the capital had tested that morning sounded for real in the afternoon. Two people died, one of them in a collapse in the state of Colima, and several were injured.

Date is the part that people noticed. The earthquake of 1985, which killed thousands in the capital, also happened on 19 September, and so did the one of 2017, which brought down buildings in the same districts. The coincidence has made the date a national rehearsal: the sirens are tested every year at eleven in the morning, and the schools run evacuation drills. This year the drill had finished two hours before the real thing, and the videos of children filing out of classrooms were followed by videos of adults standing in the street.

Damage was concentrated in the states along the coast, where the shaking was strongest. A hospital in Michoacán lost part of its facade, and the airport in the region closed while the runway was inspected. Power went out in parts of the capital, and the metro stopped for an hour. The tsunami warning centre issued an alert for the Pacific coast and withdrew it within two hours, and the ports remained open.

Mexico's building codes have been rewritten twice since 1985, and the enforcement has been uneven. The capital's older neighbourhoods have thousands of buildings put up before the rules existed, and the programme to strengthen them has moved slowly. Engineers say the soft soil of the old lake bed amplifies the shaking, which is why the same magnitude produces different results in different districts. The city's civil protection office has published a map of the risk, and the map has not changed the market for cheap flats.

The president said the federal government would send units to the coast and that the reconstruction would be paid for from the emergency fund. The army and the navy began to clear roads, and the state electricity company sent crews to the substations. Schools in the affected areas closed for two days, and the authorities asked people to check the gas pipes in their homes before they went back inside.

What the day showed is a country that has learned to run the drill and has not learned to fix the buildings. Two deaths in a shock of that size is a good result by the standards of the region, and the number is good because most of the structures held. The next one will arrive on a date nobody has chosen, and the drill will have been rehearsed, and the flats above the shops in the old districts will still be built of what they were built of.
