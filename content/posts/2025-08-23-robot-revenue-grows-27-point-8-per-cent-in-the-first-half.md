Half-year figures, published by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology at the World Robot Conference, are strong in a year when many manufacturing sectors are flat. Revenue for robot companies rose 27.8 per cent in the first six months, industrial robot output rose 35.6 per cent and service robot output 25.5 per cent.

China has been the largest industrial robot market for twelve consecutive years, with 302,000 units sold in 2024. Robot density in manufacturing now ranks third in the world, behind Korea and Singapore and ahead of Germany and Japan, a position reached in about a decade from a low base. Density is measured as robots per 10,000 manufacturing workers, and the Chinese figure has roughly doubled since 2019, mostly through small six-axis machines in electronics and automotive parts. Industrial robots are installed in 71 of the 92 industry categories and 236 sub-categories in the national accounts.

Service robots are where the growth is least visible and most commercially advanced. Chinese manufacturers supplied 84.7 per cent of the global commercial service robot market by shipments in 2024, according to IDC. Most of those machines are not humanoid: they are cleaning units in shopping centres, delivery robots in hotels, and inventory machines in warehouses, sold on three-year leases with a service contract. That model produces predictable revenue and hides the capital cost from the buyer, which is why the service segment grows faster in revenue than in unit shipments.

The humanoid segment is smaller and louder. Songyan Dynamics said it had taken more than 2,000 commercial orders in the first half and had added a second production site in Beijing to meet them. UBTECH plans to deliver 500 industrial humanoid units this year, and its Walker S2 is designed around a three-minute battery swap so that a machine can cover two shifts.

Beijing's own numbers are worth separating from the national ones. The city's robot industry revenue grew about 50 per cent in 2024, and it counts 57 specialized "little giant" firms and 30 humanoid integrators, both among the highest in the country, and its humanoid sector accounts for about a third of the national total. Yizhuang, the industrial zone that hosts the conference, has gathered more than 300 robot and intelligent manufacturing companies.

The question for the second half is whether the order books convert. A robot that costs 199,000 yuan and needs a technician on call is a different purchase from a machine tool, and buyers are asking about uptime, spare parts and who pays when a joint wears out. The industry's answer, for now, is a service network built around the same stores that sell the machines. That is a retail solution to an industrial problem, and it works only where the density of buyers is high enough to justify a workshop. Beijing has that density, and most provinces do not. The result is a market that looks national in the statistics and regional in the service bay.
