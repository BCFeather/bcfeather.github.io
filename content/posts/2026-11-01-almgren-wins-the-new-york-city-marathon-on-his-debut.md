Andreas Almgren won the New York City Marathon on Sunday in his first race over the distance, crossing the line in 2:06:01 after sitting in the lead group for most of the morning and breaking clear in the closing kilometres. The 31-year-old Swede had never run 42.195 kilometres in competition before he started from Staten Island, and the field he beat included men who had spent years learning how to close a marathon.

Yemaneberhan Crippa of Italy finished 13 seconds back in 2:06:14, and Kamohelo Mofolo of Lesotho took third in 2:06:26, one second clear of defending champion Benson Kipruto, who was fourth. Haftu Strintzos of Australia came home fifth in 2:06:34 and the American Zouhair Talbi sixth in 2:06:43, so the leading group held together far longer than the clock at the front suggested.

Victory made Almgren the first Swedish man to win in New York and the first Swede to win any of the six major marathons. He arrived with a strong road record but no marathon behind it: in September he won the world half marathon title in Copenhagen in 58:06, a European record, after a 10,000 metres bronze at the Tokyo world championships in 2025 and a European 10,000 metres title this summer. Stepping from the track to 42 kilometres usually takes several attempts, and the handful of runners who have managed it first time tend to be those with the least to unlearn.

Sheila Chepkirui of Kenya won the women's race in 2:24:36, her second New York title in three years after victory in 2024 and third place last year. Jess McClain of the United States took second in 2:24:42, six seconds adrift after passing Hellen Obiri in the closing metres, the first American podium place in the race since 2018 and a result that will be read as progress for a domestic development programme that has struggled at the distance.

Sunday's edition was the 55th running and marked 50 years of the five-borough course, which first linked Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Manhattan in 1976. Organisers altered part of the route for the first time in 15 years, and more than 60,000 runners from 140 countries were entered, most of them hours behind the leaders and running a different race altogether on the same streets.

Winning time was modest by the standards of the course, whose men's record of 2:05:06 has stood since 2011, and the pace at the front stayed cagey for long stretches. The climb up Fifth Avenue and the loop into Central Park have decided this race for decades, and they rewarded the runner who had spent least energy earlier. Almgren's patience suited a debutant with nothing to compare the distance against, and the last five kilometres made the difference.
