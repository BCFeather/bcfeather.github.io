HP announced the cuts on 22 November with its results for the quarter to October. The reduction of 6,000 jobs is about 12 per cent of the workforce, and it will be completed by 2025. The company said it would take a charge of 1 billion dollars, half of it in the current quarter. The company's fiscal year ended in October, and revenue was 63 billion dollars. The company had 58,000 employees at the end of the year, down from 65,000.

Revenue fell 11 per cent to 14.1 billion dollars, and net income was 3 billion for the year. The personal systems segment, which is the computer business, fell 13 per cent, and printing fell 6. The company said demand had weakened in Europe and in China, and it said it expected the market to shrink again next year. The company's margin was 8 per cent, and it had been 9 a year earlier. Its free cash flow was 3 billion dollars, and it returned 2 billion to shareholders.

Printing was the profit engine, producing 60 per cent of operating profit on 30 per cent of revenue, and the margin was 19 per cent. The company said the number of pages printed had fallen 5 per cent in the year, and it said it would sell more services to offices.

He said the company had too many models and that it would reduce the number by a third. The company had already cut 5,000 jobs in 2020. The new plan was the third restructuring in five years, and the chief executive said the company would simplify its product line. The merger with Xerox had been abandoned in 2020, and the company had been under pressure from an activist investor. The investor had argued that the printing business should be separated, and the board rejected the idea in 2021. The new plan did not include a separation. The chief executive had been in the job for two years, and he had cut 4,000 jobs in 2021.

Shares rose 4 per cent on the results and fell 2 per cent the next day. They had fallen 20 per cent in the year, and the company's value was 30 billion dollars. The dividend yield was 4 per cent, and the company said it would keep the payout.

Shipments fell 20 per cent in the third quarter, the largest decline on record, according to the research firms. The personal computer market was the problem. The pandemic had pulled forward demand, and the replacement cycle had ended. The company said it expected the market to return to growth in 2024. The cuts were completed faster than planned, and the company said in 2023 that it had reached the target. Revenue fell 15 per cent for the year, and the shares fell 10 per cent. The company raised its dividend in 2024, and the printing business continued to shrink.
