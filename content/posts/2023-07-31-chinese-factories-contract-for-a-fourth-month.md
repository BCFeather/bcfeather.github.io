China's official manufacturing purchasing managers' index rose to 49.3 in July from 48.8 in June, the fourth month below the 50 mark. The new orders measure was about 49.5, and the new export orders about 46.9. The production measure rose to about 50.2. The figure was below the forecast of about 49.5, and the Caixin index, published the next day, came in at 49.2. The new orders have been below 50 for four months, which is the longest run since the first half of 2022.

Demand is still the problem. The orders from the exporters have been falling since the spring, and the ones from the American and the European retailers have not returned. The survey's respondents said the customers were buying the smaller batches and asking for the longer credit. The backlog of the orders fell to about 45, and the one of the imports to about 46 as the domestic demand weakened. The property construction has been the largest drag, and the cement and the steel orders have fallen with it. The respondents said the orders for the Christmas season had been placed later than usual, and some had been cut by about 10 per cent.

Prices were weak. The output prices fell to about 48, and the input prices to about 45, as the commodity costs came down and the factories cut the prices to win the orders. The producer prices have fallen over the year for about eight months. The producer prices fell by about 5.4 per cent over the year in June, which is the steepest since 2015.

Services held. The non-manufacturing index fell to 51.5 from 53.2, and the construction to about 52. The travel and the restaurants were the strongest, and the respondents said the summer bookings had been better than the spring. The catering and the hotels were above 55, and the transport was the second strongest. The new export orders have been below 50 for five months, and the one of the imports for four as the domestic demand weakened.

Employment softened. The measure for the manufacturing fell to about 48, and the one for the services to about 47. The graduates entering the market have pushed the youth unemployment to about 21 per cent, and the government has promised the support. The government has announced the measures for the private firms and the platform companies, and the effect has been small so far. The services employment fell to about 47, which is the weakest since the spring of 2022.

This report is the first with the new premier's measures in it. A factory sector that has contracted for four months while the services expand is an economy that is leaning on the reopening, and the reopening has been losing the momentum. The economists expect the growth of about 4.5 per cent in the third quarter, which is below the target of the year.
