Samsung published its preliminary results on 7 October, and operating profit was 10.8 trillion won for the quarter to September. The figure was 32 per cent below the same quarter of 2021 and below the 12 trillion that analysts had expected. Revenue was 76 trillion won, roughly flat on the year. The guidance is a preliminary estimate, and the company publishes full accounts later in the month.

Memory chips were the reason. Prices for DRAM had fallen 20 per cent in the quarter, and prices for NAND had fallen 30, as data centre customers cut orders. Samsung is the largest maker of both, and its chip division had produced two thirds of group profit in the previous year. The division's margin fell from 35 per cent to 15 in six months. Server makers had bought two years of memory in 2021, and they were now working through that stock.

Demand had turned in the summer. Server customers had built inventory during the pandemic and were now using it, and personal computer sales fell 20 per cent in the quarter. Smartphone sales fell 8 per cent, and Samsung's own handset margin fell despite the launch of two folding models in August. The company said demand would not recover before the second half of 2023. Its handset business shipped 63 million phones, and the average price fell for the first time in three years.

Samsung did not cut production. It said it would keep investing in capacity, because it expected the downturn to be short and because it wanted market share when prices recovered. That decision was criticized by analysts, who said the industry would have to cut output to end the glut, and it was defended by the company as a long-term choice. Analysts said the decision had cost the industry two quarters of price recovery.

Rivals took the other route. Micron said in September that it would cut capital spending by 30 per cent, and SK Hynix cut its own by half in October. Both companies reported losses in the following quarter, and both said they would reduce wafer starts. Samsung's refusal was the reason prices stayed low into 2023. The won helped the reported numbers. The currency fell 15 per cent against the dollar in the quarter, and Samsung books most of its sales in dollars, so the revenue line was supported by the exchange rate. The operating profit line was not, because costs are mostly in won.

Samsung's chip division lost money in the first quarter of 2023, the first loss since 2008, and the company finally cut output in April. The shares rose 2 per cent on the day, because the market had feared worse. The company's shares had fallen 30 per cent in the year, and foreign investors had sold 10 trillion won of them. The full results, published at the end of October, confirmed the preliminary figures. The downturn lasted three quarters. Prices recovered in the summer, and the company returned to profit in the fourth quarter of 2023.
