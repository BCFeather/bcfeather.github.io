description: "The Las Vegas electronics show ran four days with more than 115,000 attendees and a programme weighted towards cars, health sensors and the software that sits between them."
---

Gadget shows are usually judged by the objects on the stands, and this year the stands had fewer of them. CES opened on 5 January in Las Vegas with about 115,000 registered attendees, up from a pandemic low but still short of the 170,000 who came in 2019. Organizers counted more than 3,200 exhibitors, and the floor was full enough that the walk from the north hall to the south took twenty minutes at noon. Organizers had budgeted for a smaller crowd, which made the turnout the first piece of news the industry had in a year.

Carmakers took the largest share of the new space. Mercedes, BMW, Stellantis and a handful of Chinese brands brought electric models and, more often than the cars themselves, the screens and driver systems inside them. Sony and Honda showed a joint vehicle under a new brand, and Qualcomm spent its press conference on the chips that would run the dashboard rather than the phone in a pocket. A few stands showed solid-state battery cells with charging claims that no independent laboratory had yet tested.

Health hardware followed close behind. Wearables that measure blood pressure without a cuff, rings that track temperature through the night, and a remote monitoring kit for nursing homes filled two halls. Companies in that corner of the show were careful about claims, because the regulatory path for a device that measures something new is long and expensive. Several wrist devices carried the same sensor package from two suppliers, which says something about where the components come from.

Software had the quieter presence and the louder argument. The Matter standard, which lets lights and locks from different makers speak to one another, was on more booths than any single product. Vendors that had spent years selling their own app were now selling compatibility, and several admitted that the app had become a cost rather than a feature. Interoperability is cheaper to promise than to certify, and the certification queue runs to months rather than weeks.

Absences shaped the week as much as exhibits did. Several large firms stayed home or sent a smaller team, and the conference programme leaned on executives from chip suppliers and component makers rather than the consumer brands that once filled the keynote slots. Attendance from China recovered, which mattered for the parts business that keeps the halls busy. One large exhibitor moved its stand to a hotel suite and counted meetings instead of footfall.

Numbers from the organizers suggest an industry that has stopped shrinking and has not started growing. Revenue from the show was reported as roughly in line with the previous year, hotel rates in the city were lower than in 2019, and the mood among exhibitors was that the next two years would be about shipping what had already been announced. Nobody in the halls expected a boom this year, and plenty of people said so on the record.
