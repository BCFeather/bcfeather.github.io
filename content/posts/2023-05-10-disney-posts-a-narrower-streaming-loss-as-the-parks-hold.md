description: "The quarter to March missed the estimate on the revenue, while the streaming loss fell and the parks earned more than the film studio."
---

Disney reported a revenue of 21.8 billion dollars for the quarter to March, up 13 per cent, and a profit of 1.49 billion. The earnings per share of 0.93 dollars missed the forecast of 1.03. The shares fell about 4 per cent after the close. The revenue was about 1 billion below the estimate, and the profit fell by about 25 per cent on the year. The company said it would take a charge of about 150 million dollars in the next quarter for the content it has removed from the services.

Streaming was the main story. The direct-to-consumer segment lost about 659 million dollars, which is about 400 million better than the forecast, and the losses have been narrowing for two quarters. The company said the target of the profit by the autumn of next year was still in reach. The company cut about 4,000 jobs in the quarter as part of a plan to remove 7,000, and the savings are expected to reach about 3 billion dollars a year. The streaming advertising tier had about 2 million subscribers, and the company said the average revenue per user on it was higher than on the ad-free one.

Disney+ lost subscribers in the quarter. The service ended the quarter with about 157.8 million subscribers, down about 4 million, and the company blamed the price rise in the United States and the end of the promotion in India. The average revenue per subscriber rose by about 4 per cent. The Hulu subscribers were flat at about 48 million, and the ESPN+ reached about 25 million.

Parks did the work on the profit. The experiences segment reported an operating income of about 2.2 billion dollars, up about 29 per cent, and the revenue rose as the visitors returned to the Asian parks. The cruise line was the strongest part, and the company said the demand had held after the price rises. The domestic park revenue rose by about 11 per cent, and the international one by about 30 as the Paris and the Tokyo parks recovered.

Its film studio was weaker. The revenue from the content sales fell by about 40 per cent, and the operating income fell by about 70 as the releases of the quarter were small. The company has about 20 films planned for the next two years, and the slate is the part the market is waiting for. The company said the next quarter would bring the release of the live-action remake and the animation, and the marketing costs would rise with it.

This quarter is the one in which the streaming turned. A company that loses 659 million dollars on the streaming while the parks earn 2.2 billion is a company that is funding the future from the present, and the present is the part that has to hold. The board has been under pressure from the activist investor, and the proxy fight was settled in the winter with the seats on the board.
