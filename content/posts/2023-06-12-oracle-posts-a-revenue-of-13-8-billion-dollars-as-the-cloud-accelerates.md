Oracle reported a revenue of 13.8 billion dollars for the quarter to May, up 17 per cent, and a net profit of 3.3 billion. The earnings per share of 1.67 dollars beat the forecast of 1.58. The shares rose about 3 per cent in the extended session, and the guidance for the year to May 2024 was above the estimate. The revenue was about 300 million above the estimate, and the constant currency growth was about 20 per cent. The company said the AI customers had signed about 30 contracts in the quarter, and the demand was the one it had not planned for.

Cloud did the work. The infrastructure revenue rose by about 55 per cent to 1.4 billion dollars, and the one of the applications by about 25. The total cloud revenue was about 4.4 billion, and the company said the remaining performance obligations were about 63 billion, up about 40 per cent. The infrastructure business added about 400 million dollars of the revenue on the quarter, which is the fastest growth in its history. The applications cloud grew by about 25 per cent, and the Fusion suite was the largest contributor.

Its database is the base. The company said the autonomous database had grown by about 60 per cent, and the migration of the large customers had been the part that the sales force had been built around. The support revenue fell by about 3 per cent as the customers moved to the cloud. The database licences fell by about 10 per cent, and the company said the decline was planned.

Expenses were controlled. The operating margin was about 35 per cent, and the company said the headcount had fallen by about 5 per cent over the year. The restructuring charge was about 300 million dollars, and the savings were about 1 billion on the annual basis. The company has cut about 3,000 jobs since the autumn, and the savings are being spent on the data centres.

Guidance was the news. The company expects the revenue to grow by about 8 per cent in the year and the earnings per share to be about 5.70 dollars, which is above the forecast of 5.50. The cloud infrastructure is expected to grow by about 70 per cent in the first quarter. The capital spending is expected to be about 8 billion dollars in the year, up from about 6, as the company builds the capacity.

This quarter is the one in which the AI demand started to show. A company that grows the infrastructure by 55 per cent while the legacy support falls is a company that has made the transition, and the transition is the one the customers pay for. The shares have risen by about 50 per cent this year, which is the best performance among the large software companies.
