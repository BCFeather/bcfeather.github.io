Payrolls rose by 261,000 in October, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 4 November. The forecast had been 205,000, and the figure was above it for the sixth time in seven months. The unemployment rate rose to 3.7 per cent from 3.5, and the participation rate fell to 62.2. Hiring had averaged 280,000 a month over the previous three months.

Weaker of the two surveys was the household one, which showed employment falling by 328,000, and the rise in the unemployment rate came from that measure rather than from layoffs. The two surveys had diverged all year, and the statisticians said the gap would be resolved in the annual revision.

Wages rose 0.4 per cent on the month and 4.7 per cent over the year, down from 5 per cent in September. The decline was the first in a year, and economists said it was consistent with inflation of about 4 per cent. The figure was still above the 3.5 per cent that the Fed regarded as consistent with its target. The employment cost index, a separate measure, rose 1.2 per cent in the quarter and 5 per cent over the year.

Hiring was concentrated in services. Leisure and hospitality added 72,000 jobs, and health care added 53,000. Manufacturing added 32,000, and construction 1,000. Retail lost 9,000, and transportation and warehousing lost 8,000, which were the sectors that had expanded during the pandemic. The report covered the month before the midterm elections, and the White House described it as evidence of a resilient economy. The opposition said the participation rate was still below its pre-pandemic level and that real wages had fallen for eighteen months. Both statements were accurate. Government added 36,000 jobs, and local education added 20,000.

Futures priced a half-point increase at 70 per cent, and the market's peak rate stayed near 5 per cent. The Fed had raised rates by 75 basis points two days earlier, and the report did not change the expectation for December. The S&P 500 rose 1.4 per cent on the day. The unemployment rate for black workers was 5.7 per cent, and for white workers 3.2.

August and September were revised up by 11,000 in total, and the three-month average was 289,000. The revisions were modest. The annual benchmark revision, published in February, raised the level of employment by 500,000, which is smaller than in previous years. Hiring slowed through 2023. Payroll growth averaged 250,000 in the first quarter and 150,000 in the second, and the unemployment rate stayed below 4 per cent. The labour market did not break, and the soft landing that the Fed had hoped for arrived in 2024. The report was the last before the election, and the president called it a sign of strength.
