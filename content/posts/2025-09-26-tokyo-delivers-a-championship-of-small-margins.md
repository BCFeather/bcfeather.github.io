Nine days at the Japan National Stadium, from 13 to 21 September, gave the World Athletics Championships a set of results that will be remembered for how narrow they were: heats in the morning, finals after dark, and margins of centimetres. Ryan Crouser won the shot put with 22.34 metres, his third consecutive world title, in a competition where silver and bronze were separated by three centimetres and the fourth-place throw was the same distance as the third.

Crouser's season had been almost empty. An elbow injury had kept him out of competition all year, and the qualifying round was his first hard throw since the previous September. He took the lead in round two with 21.99 and lost it briefly when Leonardo Fabbri of Italy and Tom Walsh of New Zealand both reached 21.94; his response in round five was the only throw of the night past 22 metres. Uziel Munoz of Mexico, fifth before the last round, produced 21.97 for silver, the first global throwing medal for his country.

Sprints produced the other close numbers. Noah Lyles won the 200 metres in 19.52, with Kenneth Bednarek second in 19.58 and Bryan Levell of Jamaica third in 19.64, four hundredths covering the minor medals, and a photo finish required for two of them. Rai Benjamin took the 400 metre hurdles in 46.52, with Alison dos Santos of Brazil 0.32 behind. The American men's 4x100 metre relay ran 37.29, the women's quartet 41.75, and the women's 4x400 metre relay broke a championship record that had stood since 1993 with 3:16.61.

Men's 4x400 metre relay went the other way. Rai Benjamin, the individual hurdles champion, took the baton in front for the United States and led for most of the last leg, until Collen Kebinatshipi of Botswana caught him at the line; Botswana took gold in 2:57.76, with the United States and South Africa both timed at 2:57.83. It was Botswana's first world relay title, and the sort of result that reorganizes a country's expectations of itself for a decade.

Two endings framed the week. Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce, in her final season, ran the third leg for Jamaica's bronze-medal 4x100 metre team and retired with a career tally that no sprinter has matched. Cole Hocker, disqualified from the 1500 metres semifinals six days earlier, won the 5000 metres in 12:58.30 with a closing lap that broke the field.

Quiet business that such meetings run on appeared too: a decathlon decided by a five-metre personal best in the javelin, a discus final delayed by rain and settled in the last round at ten in the evening, a high jump won in the rain on countback at two metres, and a women's 4x100 metre relay that turned on four hundredths of a second. Tokyo's stadium was full for the evenings and loud for the relays, which is the arrangement the sport depends on. Beijing hosts the next edition in 2027.
