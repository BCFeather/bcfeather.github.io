Alphabet published results for the third quarter on 25 October, and revenue rose 6 per cent to 69.1 billion dollars. The figure was the weakest growth since 2013 apart from the pandemic quarter of 2020, and it was below the 70.6 billion forecast. Net income fell 27 per cent to 13.9 billion. Traffic acquisition costs, which are the payments to partners, rose 3 per cent to 11.8 billion.

Advertising revenue was 54.5 billion, up 2.5 per cent, and that was better than the market had feared. Search grew 4 per cent, and YouTube advertising fell 2 per cent, the first decline since the company began reporting it separately. Network advertising, which is the part sold through other sites, fell 7 per cent. The company said the number of ads served had grown, and the price had fallen. The company said the number of paid clicks rose 8 per cent, and the cost per click fell 6.

Insurance and mortgage advertising had fallen sharply as rates rose, and cryptocurrency advertising had almost disappeared. The company blamed the strong dollar and the pullback by advertisers in some categories. Retail advertising held up, and travel advertising rose as restrictions ended. Costs were the issue investors raised. Headcount rose 28 per cent over the year to 187,000, and operating expenses rose 17 per cent. The company had hired aggressively during the pandemic, and it had not slowed in 2022. The chief executive said the company would be more disciplined and would prioritize. The finance chief said the pullback was broad and that it had begun in the summer.

Cloud revenue grew 38 per cent to 6.9 billion dollars, and its operating loss widened to 699 million. That unit had been loss-making since it began reporting separately, and the company said it would reach profitability in 2023. The loss was smaller than the market expected, and the shares rose in after-hours trading.

Regulation was in the background. The European Union had fined the company 4.1 billion euros in September for abusing its position in the mobile operating system, and it was appealing. The American Justice Department had filed a case against its advertising business in 2023, and the trial was set for 2025.

Revenue growth returned in the second quarter of 2023 as advertising recovered and as the dollar weakened. The company announced 12,000 job cuts in January 2023, about 6 per cent of the workforce. It said the cuts would save 2 billion dollars a year, and it took a charge of 2.5 billion in the first quarter. The shares rose 6 per cent on the day of the announcement. The company's cloud business reached profitability in the quarter, and its shares rose 50 per cent in the year. The advertising market had contracted for three quarters and recovered faster than expected. The company's other bets lost 1.6 billion dollars, and the unit that makes self-driving software lost 500 million.
