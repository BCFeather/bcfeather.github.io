---

American retail sales rose by 0.7 per cent in July, above the forecast of 0.4, and the June figure was revised to a gain of 0.3. The measure that excludes the cars, the petrol, the building materials and the food services rose by about 1 per cent, which is the strongest in six months. The figure was the fourth consecutive month of the gain, and the year-on-year growth was about 3.2 per cent. The revisions to June and May added about 0.2 percentage points to the level of the sales.

Online did the work. The non-store retailers, which are mostly the e-commerce, rose by about 1.9 per cent, and the restaurants by about 1.4. The two have been the strongest categories through the year, and the share of the online sales is about 16 per cent. The online sales are about 1.1 trillion dollars on the annual basis, and the growth has been about 8 per cent this year. The sales at the restaurants were about 87 billion dollars in July, which is a record in the nominal terms.

Cars were flat. The sales at the motor vehicle dealers rose by about 0.1 per cent as the inventories improved and the incentives returned, and the one at the petrol stations rose by about 0.3 as the prices rose. The average price of the new vehicle was about 48,500 dollars, which is about flat on the year.

Building materials rose by about 1.1 per cent after two months of decline, and the one of the furniture by about 2 as the housing completions picked up. The department stores fell by about 1.2, and the one of the clothing by about 0.5. The sales at the clothing stores fell by about 0.5, and the one at the sporting goods by about 0.7. The sales at the department stores were about 12 billion dollars in July, which is about half the level of 2019.

Households are still spending. The real spending has grown by about 2 per cent over the year, and the saving rate was about 4.4 per cent in June, which is well below the level of 2019. The credit card balances have risen by about 10 per cent, and the delinquencies have been edging up from the low levels. The credit card delinquencies were about 2.7 per cent in the second quarter, up from about 2.2 a year ago.

This report keeps the growth estimate of about 2 per cent for the quarter alive. A consumer that spends 1 per cent more on the control group is a consumer that has absorbed the rates, and the absorption is the part the recession forecasters have had to explain. The economists expect the consumption to grow at about a 2 per cent pace in the quarter, and the one for the year about 1.8.
