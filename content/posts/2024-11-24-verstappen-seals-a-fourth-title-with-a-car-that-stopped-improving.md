Max Verstappen finished fifth in Las Vegas on 23 November and with that result took a fourth consecutive drivers' title, a week before the final race of a season that had twenty-four rounds. He had won nine races, and the first half of the year was the part that made the championship possible. A season of twenty-four rounds is a season in which a lead built early has more races to survive. A title that is clinched with three races to spare is a title that was decided earlier than the calendar suggests.

Car stopped improving in the summer. Red Bull arrived at the first race with a design that was quicker than anything else, and by mid-season the rivals had closed the gap. A team that is defending a lead does not need the fastest car, and the engineering effort shifted from development to reliability and to strategy. A rival that brings an upgrade every race is a rival that forces the leader to spend its tokens on reliability.

Title was won in the first third of the season. Verstappen built a lead that was large enough to absorb a slower car, and the arithmetic of a points system that rewards consistency made the lead hard to erase. A rival that wins more races in the second half but finishes behind in the points is a rival that ran out of races. A points system that rewards finishing is a system that punishes a retirement more than it rewards a win.

Internal politics were part of the story. An investigation into the team principal ended with a decision that did not remove him, and the affair dominated the early coverage of a season that was otherwise about lap times. A team in a title fight is a team that cannot afford distraction, and the distraction was substantial. A controversy that dominates the winter is a controversy that a team carries into the next car.

Competitive picture changed the sport's economics. Four different teams won races during the year, and the field spread narrowed enough that qualifying became the decisive session on many weekends. A sport with more winners is a sport with more broadcast value, and the rule changes that produced it are due to be revisited. A rule change that narrows the field is a change that the regulator will cite when it writes the next one. A regulation that was written for closer racing produced a dominant car in its first year, which is the pattern every era follows.

What the season shows is that dominance has a half-life. A regulation that produces a dominant car produces it for two or three years, then the others catch up, and the champion's margin is decided before the catching up begins. A champion who wins with a slower car is a champion who has been tested on strategy rather than on pace.
