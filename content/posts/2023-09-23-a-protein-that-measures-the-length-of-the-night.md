title: "A protein that measures the length of the night"
date: "2023-09-23T09:05:00+08:00"

description: "A plant flowers when the dark period passes a threshold, and the protein that carries the signal is stabilised by darkness and destroyed by light."
---

A plant that flowers in spring and one that flowers in autumn are reading the same clock in opposite directions. The signal that starts the transition is a small protein made in the leaves and carried through the phloem to the shoot tip, and the amount of it that accumulates depends on how long the night is. A short night leaves little of the protein and a long night leaves enough to trigger the change, and the threshold is what makes the plant flower at a particular date.

The protein is stabilised by darkness. In light, a photoreceptor is active and marks the protein for destruction, so the protein is made and removed continuously while the day lasts. When the light goes off the destruction stops and the protein accumulates, and the concentration at dawn is a measure of the length of the night. A plant that is exposed to a flash of light in the middle of the night has its clock reset and does not flower, which is the experiment that established the mechanism.

Phytochromes do the marking, and their two forms interconvert with light. The form that absorbs red light is active and the form that absorbs far-red light is inactive, and the ratio of the two forms at the end of the day sets the sensitivity of the system. A plant in the shade of another plant sees a spectrum enriched in far-red light, and the change in the ratio is the signal that the plant is being crowded.

The evidence for the model is the behaviour of mutants. A plant that lacks the photoreceptor flowers as if the night were always long, and a plant that cannot destroy the protein flowers early regardless of the day length. The double mutant has the phenotype of the second, which places the destruction step downstream of the photoreceptor and upstream of everything else.

Practical use lies in the manipulation of flowering time. A variety that flowers too late for a short growing season can be given a version of the protein that is less sensitive to light, and a variety that flowers too early can be given one that is more sensitive. The same gene is the target in the breeding of soybean, wheat and rice, and the differences between varieties grown at different latitudes are often differences in this one gene.

What remains unresolved is how the shoot tip reads the concentration. The protein arrives from the leaves in a stream that varies with the day, and the meristem integrates the signal over several days before it commits. The integration is the part of the system that a model has to reproduce, and the measurements of concentration at the tip are still sparse. A meristem that has been exposed to a single long night does not flower, and one that has seen four does, which is why the decision takes a week rather than a day.
