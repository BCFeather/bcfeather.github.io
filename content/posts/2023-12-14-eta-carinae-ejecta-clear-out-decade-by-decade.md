description: "Three brightness and ionization events in Eta Carinae, in 1990, 2000 and 2018, are read as the dissipation of circumstellar material close to the binary rather than as changes in the star itself."
---

Eta Carinae is a massive binary inside a nebula it ejected, and its light has changed on human timescales. The brightness jump of the 1940s, accompanied by the appearance of narrow forbidden emission lines, is the best known event, and it has been explained in two ways: as evolution of the central binary, or as changes in the ejecta that screen the binary from view.

A preprint posted on 29 December 2023 continues a long monitoring campaign and identifies three more events. In the early 1990s the forbidden line emission rose without a corresponding increase in the brightness of the Homunculus, the bipolar nebula around the star. Around 2000 the central core brightened at a faster rate, again without a change in the nebula. By 2018, hundreds of narrow absorption lines from singly ionized metals had disappeared from the line of sight.

The three events point to one process operating at different radii. The 1990 change is attributed to a decrease in extinction along the line of sight from the binary to the Weigelt clumps, which sit close to the star: less dust in the way lets more line emission through without changing the nebula's own brightness. A screen thinning out is a change in the foreground, not in the source.

Brightening of the core in 2000, with no nebular counterpart, behaves differently. The cause is likely that the obscuring material moved or was destroyed closer in, where the radiation field is harder and the outflow faster. A structure that is being eroded from the inside would brighten the centre first and the outer nebula later, if at all.

The 2018 event is the clearest signal of ionization. Narrow absorption lines from singly ionized species require cool, partly neutral gas in front of the star, and their disappearance means that the gas along that line of sight has been ionized or removed. Either way, the column that produced the lines no longer exists.

Taken together, the sequence supports dissipation of circumstellar material inside the Homunculus, close to the binary, over a period of decades. The observations do not exclude slow evolution of the binary itself, and they make it unnecessary for the three events, which is the argument for preferring the simpler cause.

Limits are those of a single object and a single line of sight. The geometry of the ejecta is not resolved at the relevant radii, the extinction is inferred rather than measured, and the campaign depends on spectra taken with instruments that changed over thirty years. A second star with a similar nebula would test whether the sequence is generic. What the work supplies, beyond a reading of the past, is a prediction. If the screen is dissipating, the trend should continue, and future spectra should show further loss of narrow absorption together with a rise in the ionizing flux reaching the clumps. Monitoring that continues past 2018 will decide whether the interpretation holds.
