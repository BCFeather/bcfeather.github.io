Single-cell imaging has become cheap to acquire and expensive to analyse, and the expense is where a field's throughput goes to die. A paper in eLife on 18 April 2024 presents tools for the analysis step. Tools are the intervention that changes a pipeline, and a pipeline is what a microscopy project actually runs. Image processing is the bottleneck because cells vary and illumination drifts, and variation is what a fixed threshold cannot handle. Illumination drift is a slow change that a fixed threshold reads as a change in the biology, and the misreading is what an automated method corrects. A distributed tool is the form in which a method survives its authors, and survival is the test of a contribution.

Handling variation is what the tools automate, and automation is what makes a pipeline scale. Scale matters because a screen is a comparison across thousands of cells. A comparison needs consistent measurements, and consistency is what an algorithm supplies and what a person loses after the hundredth image. Correction is a modelling assumption, and the assumption has to be stated for a user to trust the output.

Loss of consistency is an error a manual pipeline cannot report. Reproducibility is the second argument, since a pipeline that depends on one analyst's judgement does not travel. Travel is what a method needs to be adopted, and adoption is how a toolkit changes a field. Trust in a pipeline comes from its reporting, and reporting is where method papers are weakest.

Adoption depends on accessibility, and accessibility means the tools run on ordinary hardware with documented inputs. Ordinary hardware is the constraint that separates a toolkit from a demonstration. Packaging is underestimated in method papers, and underestimation is why many pipelines are cited and few are used. Weak reporting is why many tools fail to reproduce, and reproduction is the standard a toolkit must meet.

Use is the measure of a method, and the measure is what the authors would want. Wanting a measure is a sign that the paper was written for users rather than for reviewers. Users need documentation, and documentation is what the toolkit supplies. Meeting a standard is the difference between a supplementary script and a distributed tool, and the difference is the paper's subject.

Supply is a form of generosity, and generosity is rare enough in this literature to be worth noting. Noting it is not the same as testing it, and testing is what a reader should still do. Do is the operative verb, and the paper invites it by publishing data.

Published data make the invitation concrete, and concrete invitations are what a field can act on. Acting on a toolkit means replacing an existing pipeline, and replacement has a cost. Cost is what adoption curves measure, and the curve is what future citations will trace.
