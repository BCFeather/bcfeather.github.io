Apple published results for the quarter to September on 27 October, and revenue rose 8 per cent to 90.1 billion dollars. Net income was 20.7 billion, and the gross margin was 42.3 per cent. The quarter was a record for the period, and it was the fourth consecutive record for any quarter. The figure compared with 83.4 billion a year earlier, and the growth came almost entirely from the iPhone.

Services were the growth engine. Revenue from the App Store, subscriptions, advertising and payments reached 19.2 billion dollars, up 5 per cent. That business carries a margin above 70 per cent, and it was the reason the group margin held up while hardware growth slowed. The company said it had 900 million paid subscriptions, up 150 million in a year. The App Store alone produced 7 billion, and subscriptions produced 8.

Supply had been constrained by the lockdowns in Zhengzhou, where the largest assembly plant is, and the company said it expected those problems to continue into the next quarter. The iPhone produced 42.6 billion dollars, up 10 per cent, and the company said demand for the Pro models had been strong. The company said the Pro models were 60 per cent of iPhone sales, up from 50.

Mac revenue rose 25 per cent to 11.5 billion, and the company had refreshed the line-up in the summer. iPad revenue fell 1 per cent, and the wearables business rose 10. The company said the personal computer market had contracted, and it had gained share in it. The guidance for the holiday quarter was the problem. The company said growth would be below the 8 per cent of the previous year, and it cited the strong dollar and the supply constraints. Analysts had expected 6 per cent, and the shares fell 5 per cent the next day. The services margin was 70 per cent, and the hardware margin was 34.

Prices went up in several markets in October, and the company said the dollar's rise had forced the move. The euro had fallen below parity, and the company's prices in Europe were set in euros, so the local revenue fell when translated. The company said it hedged part of the exposure. The company had 48.7 billion dollars of cash and marketable securities, and it returned 28 billion to shareholders in the quarter. It had bought back 400 billion dollars of stock since 2012, and it said it would continue. Its net cash position was neutral, which was a target it had set in 2018.
