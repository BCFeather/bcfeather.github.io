title: "British inflation stays above 10 per cent"
date: "2023-03-22T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [economy]
description: "The February reading was 10.4 per cent, up from 10.1, and food prices rose at the fastest rate since 1977."
---

Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, rose to 6.2 per cent from 5.8. The Office for National Statistics said on 22 March that consumer prices rose 10.4 per cent in the year to February. The figure was above the 9.9 per cent forecast and up from 10.1 in January. The monthly increase was 1.1 per cent, the largest for a February since records began in 1997. The figure was the highest in the group of seven economies and above the eurozone's 8.5 per cent.

Food and drink prices rose 18 per cent over the year, the fastest since 1977, and the monthly rise was 2.2 per cent. Vegetables were up 17 per cent and sugar 27 per cent, and the shortages of salad crops in February were cited by the statisticians. Bread and cereals rose 20 per cent, and the annual rate for that category had doubled in six months. The shortages were caused by cold weather in Spain and North Africa, which cut the salad harvest.

Restaurants and hotels raised prices by 12 per cent, and the category was the largest contributor to the monthly change. The surprise was in services, where inflation rose to 6.6 per cent from 6.0. The statisticians said the timing of half-term holidays had pushed up the accommodation component, and that the effect would reverse in March. Wages in the private sector rose 7 per cent, and the bank said that was feeding into service prices.

Energy was the only large category that fell, and the annual rate for gas was 40 per cent, down from 60 in the autumn. The energy guarantee had been extended to the summer, and the cap was set to rise to 3,000 pounds from July. Petrol was 2 per cent cheaper than a year earlier, and diesel was 5 per cent cheaper. The guarantee had cost the government 40 billion pounds, and the extension cost another 5 billion.

Markets had priced a quarter-point increase at 60 per cent before the release and at 90 per cent after it. The report was published a day before the central bank's meeting, and the committee raised rates the next day. The pound rose 0.4 per cent against the dollar, and the two-year gilt yield rose 0.1 points. The committee had raised rates by 3.5 points since December 2021, and the vote was 7 to 2. Economists said the measures would add about 0.3 points to inflation in the year. The figures were the last before the budget, which was delivered on 15 March, and the chancellor had said inflation was the priority. The budget extended the energy support for three months and raised the cap on childcare costs. The budget also cut the tax on business investment, which the chancellor said would raise growth by 0.2 points.

Food inflation had been expected to fall from the spring, because the wholesale prices of wheat and oil had come down. Supermarkets said they had cut the price of milk and butter in February, and the statisticians noted that the falls would appear in the April figures. The next release was due on 19 April. The April reading came in at 8.7 per cent, and the June reading at 7.9, which was slower than expected. Two per cent is the central bank's target, and the governor said in a letter to the chancellor that inflation would fall sharply from the summer. The letter is required when inflation is more than a point above the target, and this was the eighth in a row. The bank's forecast in February had inflation at 4 per cent by the end of the year.
