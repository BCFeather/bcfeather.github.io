Apple reported revenue of 90.75 billion dollars for the quarter to the end of March, four per cent below a year earlier, and iPhone revenue of 45.96 billion dollars, down 10.5 per cent, which was worse than any forecast the company's own guidance implied. Sales in Greater China fell eight per cent. The company's guidance for the current quarter was above the consensus, and the executives argued that the services line and the wearables would carry the growth the phones no longer deliver.

Comparison is part of the explanation. The quarter a year earlier had been lifted by the release of a phone that arrived late in the cycle, and the current model had been on sale for six months when the period began. A quarter that follows an unusual one is a quarter that a year-on-year figure flatters or punishes. The average selling price rose, which means the customers who did buy chose the more expensive models, and a smaller number of richer buyers is a mix that protects revenue and reduces the base.

Replacement cycle is the deeper cause. The phones in use are lasting longer, the software updates keep them useful, and the trade-in programmes have made the upgrade cheaper without making it more frequent. A product that lasts four years is a product that a company sells to the same customer once every four years. The company has been selling refurbished devices and financing them over longer terms, which spreads the cost of an upgrade without reducing the number of years between upgrades.

Services continued to grow and reached a record, which is the part of the business that benefits from the installed base rather than from new sales, and the executives pointed to it as the evidence that the customers are not leaving. A base that is loyal is a base that converts a hardware slump into a services annuity. Services revenue rose to a record on the strength of the commission the company takes from applications and of the subscriptions it sells itself, which is a margin far above the hardware.

China is where the arithmetic is hardest. The local brands have taken share at the price points where volume lives, and the company's answer, discounts through its own channels, has cost margin without restoring the shipment numbers. A discount in a market where the competitor is cheaper is a discount that closes part of a gap rather than all of it. The Chinese market is the one where the price gap matters most, and the company has been running promotions through its own stores rather than through the carriers it uses elsewhere.

What the quarter shows is that a company can post a weak hardware number and still be valued on its services, and the question is how long the base keeps paying when the device that anchors it is no longer the one people want. A hardware company that becomes a services company is a company that trades a cyclical revenue for an annuity, and the transition is only as durable as the devices that carry it.
