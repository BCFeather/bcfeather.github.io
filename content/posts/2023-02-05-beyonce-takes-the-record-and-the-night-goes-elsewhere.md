Beyonce won four awards at the ceremony on 5 February and took her total to thirty-two, one more than the record held since 1997 by the conductor Georg Solti. She won best dance and electronic album for Renaissance and best traditional R and B performance, and she did not perform, because the tour had not yet begun. The record was reported as the night's headline even though the awards that carried it were handed out before the television broadcast.

The three biggest prizes went to three different records. Harry Styles took album of the year for Harry's House, Lizzo took record of the year for About Damn Time, and Bonnie Raitt took song of the year for Just Like That, a win that the audience greeted with the kind of surprise that the category has specialized in. The spread was read as a reaction to the previous year, when the main prizes had gone to a single artist and the ceremony had been criticized for it.

Television built the evening around the anniversary rather than the competition. It was the sixty-fifth edition, and the producers had added a segment for every decade of the prize, with performances that were curated rather than competitive. The host, Trevor Noah, made jokes about the length of the ceremony and about the recording academy's history of ignoring Black artists in the main categories, which was a subject the evening kept returning to without resolving.

Two wins were read as milestones. Kim Petras, who won best pop duo or group performance with Sam Smith for Unholy, became the first openly transgender performer to win in a major televised category, and the speech she gave was cut short by the ceremony's timing rules. Viola Davis completed the set of competitive awards needed for an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar and a Tony, a distinction that fewer than twenty people hold. Both speeches were shorter than the producers had planned, which was a reminder that the ceremony's clock had become as political as its voting.

The business behind the ceremony was visible in the numbers. Ratings had fallen for three years and recovered slightly for this edition, and the academy had spent the year trying to broaden the voting body after an outcry about its membership. The changes showed up in categories that reward records with small audiences, and the main prizes stayed with artists who sell tickets, which is the compromise the institution has settled into.

What the evening demonstrated was how a prize system behaves when the field has no centre. The most awarded artist did not appear, the album of the year went to a British pop singer, and the song of the year went to a blues record that had been released without promotion. A ceremony that once ratified a mainstream was now a map of several, and the record it produced was one more item in that map. The institution had spent a decade trying to describe a field that no longer had a centre, and the record it produced was one more attempt.
