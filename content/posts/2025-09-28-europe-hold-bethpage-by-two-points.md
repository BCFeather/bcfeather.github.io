Bethpage Black is a public course on Long Island with rough that punishes a miss and greens that hold a well-struck iron. The 2025 Ryder Cup was played there in front of a home crowd, and the course setup gave the United States the conditions its captain had asked for, which is the usual arrangement for a home match. The course is long for a public facility and it plays to a power game that both teams can supply. Its par of 70 leaves little room for recovery.

Europe led 11 and a half to 4 and a half after two days, the largest lead the team has ever carried into the singles in the history of the contest. A four-point cushion is not decisive in a format that awards 12 points on Sunday, and the American team had won a singles session by a wide margin before. The morning foursomes had gone Europe's way, and the afternoon fourballs added to the total instead of halting it.

Sunday went to the United States by 8 and a half to 3 and a half, which brought the match to 15-13. The margin is the smallest since Medinah in 2012, when Europe won 14 and a half to 13 and a half, and it is Europe's first away win in 13 years, a run that had covered four matches on American soil. Medinah in 2012 is the reference point because Europe came from a similar deficit there and won by a single point.

Lowry halved with Henley after holing a six-foot birdie putt on the last, which kept a point that the Americans had looked likely to take, and Hatton halved with Morikawa to reach the 15th point that made the result safe. Hatton finished the week unbeaten in four matches, a return that a captain rarely gets from a player picked for temperament. Morikawa had chances on the last two greens, and the half reflected a match in which neither player led by more than one hole.

Eight of the twelve singles went the full 18 holes, which is a measure of how little separated the two teams on the day. A match that ends on the last green is a match in which a single putt decides a point, and the difference between 15-13 and 14-14 was a handful of those. The American points came from the middle of the order, where matches were decided by two holes or fewer.

The result says something about the two captains' selections and about the value of experience on a course that demands accuracy. It says nothing durable about the strength of either tour, since the venue changes every two years and the format compresses four days of golf into a score that a single session can swing. The next match is in Europe, where the setup and the crowd reverse, and history suggests the home team gains more than the course alone explains.
