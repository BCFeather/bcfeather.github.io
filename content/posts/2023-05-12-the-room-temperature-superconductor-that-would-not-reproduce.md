---

A claim of superconductivity at room temperature and at modest pressure would be a discovery of the century. The claim was made in March 2023 for a lutetium-hydrogen-nitrogen compound, and the field's response has been the response that such claims always attract: attempts to repeat it.

The rebuttal in *Nature*, 11 May 2023 reports one. A group that specializes in high-pressure synthesis made more than thirty samples following the original recipe and found no superconductivity. The samples' colours changed with pressure, which the original paper had cited as evidence, and the change was not accompanied by the electrical signature.

The signature is the test. Superconductivity means zero resistance and expelled magnetic field, and a claim without those measurements is a claim about a colour rather than about a state.

The pressure is the second problem. The original claim required about ten kilobars, a pressure far below that of the diamond-anvil experiments that dominate the field, and a measurement at that pressure is easier to make and easier to check.

The replication's failure is not proof of fraud. A synthesis that is sensitive to trace impurities can produce a superconducting phase in one laboratory and not in another, and the honest conclusion is that the claim is unreproduced.

The field's history is the context. A series of high-pressure superconductivity claims have collapsed under scrutiny, and the pattern has made journals and referees more cautious about resistance data.

The incentives are the underlying problem. A room-temperature superconductor would transform power transmission, and the prospect creates pressure to publish before the evidence is complete.

The lesson for readers is about the state of a claim. A single paper with an extraordinary result is a report, and a result that other groups reproduce is a fact.

What the rebuttal does not do is close the question. A different composition or a different synthesis route might still produce the phase, and the search continues with the original claim as one hypothesis among several.

For materials science, the value is a demonstration of how the field polices itself. A replication that fails is a result, and publishing it is what keeps an extraordinary claim from becoming an accepted one.

The model's fit is its argument. A single origin fails to reproduce the diversity of ancient genomes, and a weakly structured stem reproduces it without positing a migration that the fossil record does not support.

The date matters for archaeology. A split around 120,000 years ago is consistent with fossils of that age, and a model that fits both the genes and the stones is stronger than one that fits only the genes.
The data's presentation is the crux of the dispute. A resistance drop can come from a percolating metallic path, and a measurement that does not separate the sample's bulk from its surface is a measurement that can mislead. The rebuttal's electrical data are the point of its argument.
The preprint culture is part of the story. A claim that circulates before review is a claim that the public encounters without the field's filter, and the gap between the two is where the reputation of the field is made or lost.
