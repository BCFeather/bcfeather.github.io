Adobe published results for the quarter to December on 15 December, and revenue rose 10 per cent to 4.52 billion dollars. Net income was 1.18 billion, and the figure was above the 4.45 billion forecast. Revenue for the year was 17.6 billion, up 11 per cent, which is the slowest growth since 2013. The subscription business was the core. Digital media annualized recurring revenue reached 13.7 billion, up 2 per cent in the quarter, and the company said it had added 400 million dollars of net new ARR. The document cloud business grew 20 per cent, and it was the fastest growing part. The quarter was the first in three years in which revenue growth fell below 10 per cent.

Guidance was raised. The company said revenue for the year to December 2023 would be 19.1 to 19.3 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 19.0. It said earnings per share would be 15.30 to 15.60. The shares rose 6 per cent the next day, and they had fallen 45 per cent in the year.

Questions on the call were about the pending acquisition of Figma. The company said the deal was on track and that it expected to close in 2023, and it said the review by the Department of Justice was proceeding. It declined to comment on the timing, and the shares gave up some of the gain in January.

Costs were controlled. The company had frozen hiring in the autumn and had cut some roles, and its headcount was 26,000, up 5 per cent. The operating margin was 36 per cent, and the company said it would be 37 in the next year. It also said it would spend 1 billion dollars on buybacks. The company said it had 26,000 employees, and it would add 5 per cent in the next year.

Its business had been slower to feel the advertising downturn than its rivals, because most of its revenue comes from subscriptions rather than from ads. Its customers are professionals who pay monthly, and the churn rate had risen only slightly. The finance chief said the company had seen weaker demand from small businesses. The shares had fallen 45 per cent in the year, and they were 25 per cent below their peak. The company's value was 180 billion dollars, and it was the fifth largest software company. The dividend was unchanged, and the company had 6 billion dollars of cash.

Adobe paid a break fee of 1 billion dollars. The Figma deal was abandoned in December 2023 after the regulators in Britain and the European Union objected. Its revenue grew 10 per cent in 2023, and it raised prices for its creative suite in the autumn, which produced the strongest quarter of the year in the fourth. The company's shares had risen 30 per cent in 2021, and they gave back all of that in 2022.
