Antibody testing after vaccination became a popular pastime, and the assumption behind it was that a high titre meant protection. The assumption is reasonable in immunology and was unquantified in epidemiology, because the studies that followed vaccinated people rarely measured both titres and infections in the same population. Serology was used during the pandemic to decide who needed a booster, and the decisions were made on titre thresholds that varied between countries.

A British programme did both. Anti-spike IgG was measured in 222,493 people after second doses of ChAdOx1 or BNT162b2, and the titres were related to subsequent infection. The analysis appeared in *Nature Medicine*, 14 February 2022, and covered all ages and several dosing intervals, including the three-week interval used for BNT162b2.
