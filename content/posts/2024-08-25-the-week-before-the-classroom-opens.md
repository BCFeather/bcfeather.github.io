Last week of August is when a household buys what a school year needs. A list that the school sends in July becomes a bag of pens, a uniform, two pairs of shoes and a set of exercise books. The stationery shop works a season of ten days. A shop that sells paper and pens all year earns a third of its revenue in the week before term. Owners order the stock in June to be sure of it.

Longest queue belongs to the uniform. A shop that embroiders the school badge on a shirt needs three days for an order. A parent who arrives on the thirtieth is told to come back after the first day of term. The price rises with the queue. A set of exercise books that costs eight yuan in July costs ten in the last week of August. Shop does not describe that as a rise. It describes it as a different edition.

Book list has shrunk and the bag has not. A policy that limits the printed material that a school can require has removed some of the weight. Tablet and charger have replaced it. The boarding schools are the larger purchase. A family that sends a child to a school with dormitories buys a quilt, a mattress, a bucket and a thermos. Whole set is sold as a package by the shop outside the gate.

Second-hand trade has grown. A parent group that trades uniforms and textbooks has a thousand members in a district. Transaction is a bag on a doorstep rather than a shop. The tutors are taking bookings for the term. A class that meets on Saturday morning is sold out by the middle of August. Teacher who runs it has a waiting list that is longer than the class.

Anxiety is what the shops sell alongside the goods. A parent who buys the most expensive pen is buying a feeling as much as an object. Shopkeeper knows it. Parcels arrive at the same time as the shoppers. A courier who delivers a schoolbag ordered in July is carrying a box that the shop sold without a queue, and the corridor of a residential block is full of them on the last weekend.

Factory works the season on a shift. A workshop that makes shirts for three schools in a district cuts and sews in August what it sells in ten days, and the fabric was ordered in April. Lunch is the item the school supplies and the family argues about. A canteen contract is signed by a district office and a parent who wants a different menu is told that the contract runs for a year. The term opens on the first of September. The bags are packed on the night before, the photographs are taken at the gate in the morning, and the shops that were full for ten days are quiet by the afternoon. By the second week of September the uniforms are washed for the first time, the list of what was still missing has been written down, and the family knows what the year will cost.
