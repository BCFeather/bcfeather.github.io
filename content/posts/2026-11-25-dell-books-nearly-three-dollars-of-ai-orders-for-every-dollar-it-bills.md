Dell reported 54.9 billion dollars of revenue for its third fiscal quarter, more than double the figure a year earlier, and 22.0 billion of it came from servers built for artificial intelligence. New AI orders in the same three months reached 60.2 billion, and the backlog at the close stood at 132.3 billion. The ratio between orders and recognized revenue, close to three to one, is the number that describes the business better than the growth rate does.

The segment split shows how far the centre of gravity has moved. Infrastructure solutions, which holds servers, storage and networking, produced 42.0 billion, up 198 per cent, and AI-optimized servers accounted for 22.0 billion of that, up 291 per cent. Traditional servers and networking added 15.0 billion, up 235 per cent, and storage 4.9 billion, up 24 per cent. Client solutions, the personal computer business, grew 3 per cent to 12.9 billion, and its operating profit fell 6 per cent to 704 million.

Guidance followed the orders. Full-year revenue is put at 202.0 billion, up 78 per cent, with AI server revenue of about 78 billion, and non-GAAP earnings per share at 29.68. For the fourth quarter the company guides 56.25 billion, give or take 500 million, with AI servers near 23.5 billion. That would be the largest quarter in the company's history, and it rests on hardware that has only just entered volume supply. Those figures assume the backlog converts on schedule, which is the assumption every supplier in this market is making.

Costs are the part that has improved fastest. Operating expenses came to 7.0 per cent of revenue, the lowest share in 42 years, which is what a doubling of revenue does to a fixed cost base. Operating cash flow was 4.5 billion in the quarter and 10.8 billion for the year to date, 4.9 billion went back to shareholders, and the board added 25 billion to the buyback authorization. The company counted more than 7,500 customers running what it calls AI factories.

The backlog deserves its own caveat. It is defined and vetted by the company, it is not an order book in the sense an auditor would use, and the company does not disclose the gross margin on AI servers, so a reader can see the volume of the business and not its quality. Most of the new orders rest on NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 racks, which ties Dell's delivery schedule to a supplier's ramp and to memory and power equipment that others also need.

Six quarters of AI revenue sit in the backlog at the third-quarter rate, which is the sort of cover that makes a supply chain the constraint rather than demand. What the next report will show is how much of that converts, at what price. The order figure is a promise; the revenue line is the only place where a promise becomes a number. The backlog will be set against the guidance every quarter, and the gap between the two is where the argument will be settled.
