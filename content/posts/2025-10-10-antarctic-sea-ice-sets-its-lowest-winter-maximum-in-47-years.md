description: "Sea ice around Antarctica reached its annual maximum in September at 17.0 million square kilometres, the lowest in the 47-year satellite record, and the deficit is concentrated in one sector whose behavior has changed since 2016."
---

Antarctic sea ice grows through the southern winter and reaches a maximum in September, and the satellite record since 1979 has shown a slow increase for most of that period. The maximum reported this month is 17.0 million square kilometres, 1.4 million below the 1981 to 2010 average and the lowest in 47 years.

The anomaly is regional rather than uniform. Three sectors are close to their long-term average and one, the Ross Sea's eastern half, is 900,000 square kilometres below it. That concentration is what makes the event a scientific question rather than a statistic.

Wind is the candidate mechanism. A persistent low-pressure pattern over the Amundsen Sea pushed ice northward and opened a polynya, and the exposed ocean absorbed heat through the summer. The reanalysis shows that pattern was unusually persistent for six months.

Ocean heat is the slower factor. The upper 200 metres of the Southern Ocean have warmed 0.2 degrees in two decades, and a warmer mixed layer delays the autumn refreeze. The delay is small per year and cumulative over a decade.

The 2016 step change is the context. Before 2016, the record showed a slight increase; since then, three of the nine years have set record lows. A regime shift is a strong reading of four events, and the paper that reports the maximum makes the claim carefully, as a change in the distribution of anomalies rather than in a trend.

Consequences are local and specific. A smaller maximum exposes coastal ice shelves to wave action in spring, and two shelves in the sector have shown increased calving. It also changes the light field under the ice, which affects a phytoplankton bloom that the food web depends on.

Extent and thickness are different measurements. A satellite's passive microwave sensor counts the area with at least 15 per cent ice cover, and that number can stay high while the ice thins. A laser altimeter measures freeboard, and the two records have diverged in the past decade.

Wind explains part of the anomaly. A persistent pattern of offshore winds pushes ice away from the coast and exposes water that refreezes thin, and the winter's circumpolar wind index was in its lowest decile. A year with the opposite pattern can recover several hundred thousand square kilometres of extent without any change in ocean heat.

Sea ice's role in the climate system is reflective and structural. A bright surface returns sunlight that an ocean would absorb, and a coast without ice exposes water to wind that would otherwise be damped. A winter maximum is a seasonal statistic and its record is a statement about a season, not about a trend.

Sea ice is a thin, mobile skin over the ocean, and a season's record tests when that motion stops being temporary. A change in wind can move a million square kilometres in a season, and the record's value is that it shows when the wind's effect stops being temporary.
