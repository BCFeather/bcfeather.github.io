Ministers met on 5 September by video conference and agreed to reduce the October quota by 100,000 barrels a day. The cut was about a tenth of a per cent of world supply, and it reversed a similar increase agreed in August for September. The group described the decision as a response to a weakening outlook rather than to the current price.

Brent had fallen from 120 dollars in June to 92 dollars in the last week of August, and the group wanted to put a floor under it. The meeting took place a day after the G7 announced a price cap on Russian crude, and ministers said they would watch how that worked before deciding on volumes. Two members had argued for a larger cut and were talked down by the larger producers.

Compliance had been poor through the year. The group's own figures showed output 3.5 million barrels a day below its quotas, mostly because of members that could not pump what they had promised. That gap meant the nominal cut changed little in practice, because the barrels being removed were never there. The decision was read as a signal about the months ahead. The group had been increasing quotas through the summer while the price fell, and the reversal suggested it would defend 90 dollars. The next full meeting was scheduled for 5 October, and ministers said then that they would consider a substantial cut. Compliance had improved in August, and the group said the gap was narrowing as maintenance ended.

Officials said they would release more if the price rose, and the capacity to do so was limited. The Americans had been asking for more supply since the spring, and the White House said the decision was short-sighted. The strategic reserve had been drawn down by 180 million barrels over six months, and it stood at its lowest level since 1984.

Analysts noted that the group was setting quotas while the market was pricing a recession. Demand forecasts had been cut for the fourth month, and the outlook for Chinese consumption was the weakest part of the picture. The International Energy Agency said in its September report that demand growth would slow to 1.5 million barrels a day, half the pace of the previous year.

Brent rose 10 per cent in the week after that decision, and the argument with the White House became a public one. The cut was reversed within a month. The group met on 5 October and agreed to remove 2 million barrels a day from November, which was the largest reduction since 2020. Prices ended the year lower than they began it. The group's cuts held through the winter, and a new round of reductions followed in April 2023. The quota system was renegotiated at the end of 2023, when several members were given higher baselines for 2024. The group's next technical meeting was set for 28 September, and it was cancelled at short notice.
