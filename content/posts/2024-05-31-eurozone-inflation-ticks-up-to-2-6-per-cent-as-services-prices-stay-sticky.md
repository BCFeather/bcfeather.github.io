Eurozone inflation rose to 2.6 per cent in May from 2.4 per cent in April, according to the estimate published on 31 May, the first increase this year. Core inflation, which excludes energy and food, held at 2.9 per cent. The figure was in line with what the economists expected, and the details showed that the rise came almost entirely from the energy comparison rather than from new pressure.

Base effect is the explanation. Energy prices had fallen sharply in May of last year and the comparison is now against a lower base, which means the same price this year shows up as a larger increase. An effect that is arithmetic is an effect that the central bank can look through. The council has said it will look through the base effects, and the members have been careful to describe the monthly numbers as noisy rather than as signals.

Services are the sticky part. Prices in the service sector rose 4.1 per cent, and the measure has moved little for six months, which the council has said reflects wages rather than imported costs. A number that is flat at a high level is a number that keeps the last mile long. The wages in the service sector rose at more than five per cent in the first quarter, and the contracts that were signed then will keep the price pressure in place for the rest of the year.

National figures diverge. The economies that rely on tourism have seen prices rise faster as the season begins, and the industrial ones have seen them fall, which means the average is a compromise between different inflations. A single rate for nineteen economies is a rate that describes none of them exactly. The country that has seen the fastest price growth is the one where the tourism season starts earliest, and the country with the slowest is the one whose industry is still in recession.

Decision in June is now the focus. The council has signalled a cut, and the tick up in the headline is not expected to stop it, because the members have said they are looking at the direction of the core measure rather than at the monthly headline. A cut that is justified by the core is a cut that the headline cannot derail. The council meets on 6 June, and the market has priced a cut with a probability that the members have done nothing to discourage.

What the month shows is that the last two points of disinflation are the slowest, and the arithmetic of the base effects will make the headline noisy even as the underlying trend improves. A headline that rises while the core holds is a headline that the central bank has to explain, and the explanation is the part of the meeting that the market will read most closely.
