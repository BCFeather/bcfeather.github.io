Monkeypox was expected to spread slowly because it is a double-stranded DNA virus with a proofreading polymerase, and the 2022 outbreak spread through dozens of countries in weeks. The genomes explain part of the discrepancy. A virus that was assumed to be too slow to track by sequencing has now been tracked by sequencing, and the assumption is the part that the outbreak disproved.

Sequencing and phylogenomic characterization covered isolates from the multi-country outbreak and compared them with genomes from earlier cases, including the 2018 and 2019 infections linked to an endemic country. The paper appeared in *Nature Medicine*, 24 June 2022. A single origin is the conclusion that the phylogeny supports, and the divergent branch is the evidence that the origin was not a single introduction from an endemic country.

All outbreak genomes belong to clade 3, and the outbreak most likely has a single origin. The 2022 lineage B.1 clusters with the earlier cases but segregates on a divergent branch, which the authors read as continuous accelerated evolution.
