Ageing is heterogeneous at the level of a single cell and stereotyped at the level of a tissue, and the two observations have been difficult to reconcile. A paper in *eLife* on 15 November 2024 locates the reconciliation in the luminal epithelium of the mammary gland. Reconciling variation with regularity is the central problem of tissue ageing, and the mammary gland is a convenient place to pose it.

The mammary epithelium is a bilayer of two lineages, luminal epithelial cells and myoepithelial cells, which differ in phenotype and in function. The luminal cells are also the putative cells of origin for breast cancer, which is why their ageing matters. A bilayer with two lineages allows the experiment to ask which layer carries the stereotyped response, and that is the design the paper used.

Single-cell measurements show that individual luminal cells age differently from one another. Some accumulate one signature and some another, and the distribution widens with age rather than shifting as a whole. Widening of a distribution is a different phenomenon from a shift in its mean, and the distinction is what makes heterogeneity measurable. Widening with age is the observation that makes heterogeneity a phenomenon of ageing rather than a fact of measurement.

Tissue-level measurements show something else. The organ acquires a stereotyped set of changes, and those changes are reproducible across animals in a way that the single-cell variation is not. Reproducibility across animals is the property that makes a tissue-level change a phenotype rather than an artefact of sampling.

Integration is the mechanism proposed. The luminal layer averages its heterogeneous responses into a small number of outcomes, so that variation at one level produces regularity at the next. Averaging can be mechanical as well as regulatory, since cells that lose adhesion or divide at different rates can produce a uniform surface. Whether averaging is regulatory or mechanical decides what an intervention should target, and the paper leaves the question open.

The implication for cancer risk is direct, because a cell of origin that ages in a stereotyped way is a cell whose transformation can be studied in a defined background. Heterogeneity that is averaged away is heterogeneity a model of risk can ignore. A defined background is what an experiment on transformation needs, and a tissue that ages predictably supplies one. A predictable tissue is also a tissue in which an intervention can be tested against a stable baseline, which is what a prevention trial needs.

On the modelling side, the averaging mechanism is not identified, and whether it is transcriptional, adhesive or a matter of selection among cells is the question the next study would take up. Transcriptional, adhesive and selective explanations are not exclusive, and a single study can rarely separate three mechanisms at once. Separating three mechanisms would need lineage tracing as well as transcriptomics, and lineage tracing in the mammary gland is well established.
