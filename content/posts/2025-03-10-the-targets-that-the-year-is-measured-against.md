The work report read out in the capital on the fifth of March set the year's growth target at around five per cent, the same figure as the two years before, and raised the deficit ratio to four per cent of GDP, which is the highest it has been in decades. The consumer price target was lowered to around two per cent, from three, which is the more interesting of the two revisions because it is an admission about the present rather than a hope about the future.

The growth figure is a promise as much as a forecast. It is disaggregated into provincial targets within weeks, and the provincial targets are then disaggregated into municipal ones, and a municipal official knows by April what number his or her year will be judged against. The mechanism is why the figure has been met so consistently in the past, and it is also why the figure is treated with caution by analysts, who look at the components, the electricity consumption, the freight volume, the retail sales, to see what the number is made of.

The deficit at four per cent is a decision about who borrows. The central government borrows more and the local governments borrow through special bonds with a quota that is set in the same document, and the shift of the borrowing to the centre is a response to the state of local finances, which have been strained since the land sales that funded them fell. The money is directed at infrastructure that the centre has chosen, and the choice is one of the things that the report is actually about.

The consumer prices target is the one that describes the problem the year is trying to solve. An economy with growth around five per cent and inflation near zero is an economy in which demand is weak relative to supply, and the report's answer is to subsidize the purchase of things, cars, appliances, home renovation materials, through a trade-in programme whose budget was doubled this year to three hundred billion yuan. A subsidy for buying a washing machine is a demand policy, and it is also a policy with a sunset, because the households that replaced the machine this year will not replace it next year.

The report also contains the parts that do not have numbers attached, and those are the parts that a specialist reads. The language on the private sector, on the treatment of entrepreneurs, on the enforcement of regulations against arbitrary fines, is a message to investors that is repeated in slightly different words every year and is watched for the changes in those words. A single phrase removed or added in that section moves more capital than the growth target.

What the document does, in the end, is fix the year. The months that follow will be reported against it, the provinces will be ranked by it, and the analysts will spend the year arguing about whether the figure is the one that the statistics describe. The argument is the normal state of the system, and it starts on the day the target is read aloud.
