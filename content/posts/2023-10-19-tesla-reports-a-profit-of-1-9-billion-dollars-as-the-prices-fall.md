Tesla reported a profit of 1.85 billion dollars for the third quarter on 18 October, down 44 per cent from a year earlier, on the revenue of 23.4 billion dollars. The operating margin fell to 7.6 per cent from 17.2. The revenue rose by about 9 per cent, and the net profit was below the 2.2 billion that the analysts had expected, which is the sixth quarter in which the margin fell. The revenue was the highest of the year, and the deliveries were about 6 per cent below the second quarter as the price cuts failed to lift the demand in China.

Price cuts are the reason. The company has cut the prices of its cars by about 25 per cent since the start of the year in America, and the average selling price fell by about 1,000 dollars a quarter. A price that is cut is a margin that is spent to keep the volume. The incentives have been repeated in China and Europe, and the company has been offering the insurance and the charging credits to the buyers who collect in the quarter.

Volume did not follow. The deliveries fell to 435,000 in the quarter as the factories were retooled for the new version of the Model 3, and the company said the output would be lower again in the fourth quarter. A line that is stopped is a delivery that does not happen. The retooling at the plants in Shanghai and Fremont cost about 2 billion dollars in the quarter, and the company said the upgrades would raise the output per line.

Energy was the bright spot. The storage business deployed about 4 gigawatt hours of the batteries, and the revenue from the energy generation rose by about 40 per cent, which is the part the company has said will grow faster than the cars. A battery that is sold is a grid that is stabilized. The energy business made a gross margin of about 24 per cent, and the company said the Megapack factory in Shanghai will start the production next year.

Cybertruck is the question. The company said the first deliveries will be on 30 November, and the analysts expect the volumes to be small in the first year. A truck that arrives four years late is a truck that the rivals have already matched. The truck has about 1.5 million reservations, and the company has said the price will start at about 60,000 dollars, above the 40,000 promised in 2019.

What the quarter shows is that Tesla has become a carmaker with a carmaker's margin, and the margin is the part the valuation has not yet accepted. A maker that cuts the prices to hold the volume is a maker that has run out of the room, and the room is what the next model must create.
