description: "When ribosome-associated quality control fails, the protein TTC3 accumulates and cognitive function declines, a link between a basic quality-control pathway and neurological disease."
---

A ribosome that stalls on a messenger RNA leaves a half-made protein, and a cell has a pathway that removes it. The ribosome-associated quality control machinery splits the stalled ribosome and tags the incomplete chain for degradation, and the pathway's failure is a source of protein aggregation.

In *PNAS* on 21 March 2023, that failure is traced to a phenotype. The authors show that dysregulation of the quality-control pathway leads to overaccumulation of TTC3, a protein the pathway would normally keep in check, and that the accumulation is accompanied by cognitive impairment.

Feedback supplies the mechanism. TTC3 is an E3 ligase whose substrates include proteins the quality-control pathway depends on, so accumulation of TTC3 feeds on the machinery that should restrain it, and the loop is what turns a modest defect into a persistent one. Two steps of that machinery can fail separately, since a splitting factor releases the ribosome's subunits while a tagging factor marks the nascent chain, and a defect in either step leaves a different intermediate.

Because an incomplete chain can be degraded, sequestered, or allowed to interfere with a complex it resembles, the route taken decides the consequence, and the data point to the third, which makes the effect a gain of function rather than a loss. Clearance supplies a second variable, since a protein that rises may be removed by a chaperone, and the balance between production and removal sets the steady state a therapy would target.

Model systems are the evidence, and the behavioural readouts in the animals are what connect a molecular accumulation to a cognitive outcome. Several neurodegenerative conditions involve stalled translation and aggregated protein, and a pathway common to them is a target one therapy could address, so the direction proposed is to lower TTC3 and test whether that alone restores function.

Distance from a patient sets the caveat, because a mouse with an engineered defect is not a person with a sporadic disease, and evidence that TTC3 accumulates in patients is the bridge the discussion has to build. Cognition itself is measured coarsely, since a behavioural battery scores performance rather than the decline a patient reports.

The threshold stays unestablished, because a protein that accumulates slightly may be harmless and the level at which function declines is a number a therapy would need. Neurobiology gains a connection between two fields, since ribosome quality control is a cell-biology topic and cognition a clinical one, and the chain of evidence here runs from a stalled ribosome to a behavioural deficit. Quality control becomes a disease axis of its own, because a cell that cannot dispose of its mistakes accumulates them, and that accumulation is a mechanism separate from whatever caused the mistake. TTC3 is also a candidate marker, since a protein that rises before symptoms could be measured in fluid from a patient, and a marker is what a trial of an early intervention needs.
