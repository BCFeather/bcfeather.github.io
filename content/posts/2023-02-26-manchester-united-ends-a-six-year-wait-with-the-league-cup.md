Wembley hosted the final on 26 February in front of 87,000 people. Manchester United beat Newcastle United 2-0, with Casemiro heading in from a free kick on 33 minutes and Marcus Rashford scoring from close range six minutes later. Newcastle had the better of the opening half hour and hit the post through a deflection. Attendance was the largest for a League Cup final since the stadium was rebuilt, and the ticket allocation was split evenly.

It was United's first trophy since the Europa League of 2017, and the club had changed managers three times in the interval. Erik ten Hag, appointed in the summer, had also taken the team to the final of the FA Cup and the quarter-final of the Europa League. He said afterwards that the win would not change the standards at the club, which is the sort of remark managers make when they know it might. Ten Hag had won nothing in his first six months, and the pressure on him had been reported as serious in January.

Newcastle were playing in their first final since 1999, and their manager admitted the occasion had affected the players. The club had spent heavily since the takeover of 2021, and this was the first season in which the spending showed on the pitch. Their league position was fourth, and they had conceded fewer goals than any other team in the division. Newcastle had not won a domestic trophy since 1955, and their supporters filled the west end of the ground.

A sponsor's name was added in 2017, and the competition had been low on the priorities of the largest clubs for a decade. United fielded a strong side in every round, which was read as a sign that the new manager wanted a trophy early. Attendance across the competition rose 30 per cent on the previous season, and the final was the most watched League Cup match since 2010. The competition pays 100,000 pounds to the winner, which is small next to the Premier League's payments.

Both clubs had reasons to treat the win as a beginning rather than an end. United's ownership was in the middle of a sale process, and the bidders had asked about the squad's value. Newcastle's owners had said they would invest in the training ground before the stadium, and the plans were published in March. Two bidders for the club had visited the training ground, and both had asked about the age of the squad. The sale process was run by a bank, and the deadline for final offers was set for April.

By the time of the final the Premier League title was already decided, and the two clubs met again in April. United finished third and Newcastle fourth, which put both in the Champions League for the following season. The manager said the trophy had done more for the dressing room than any result in his first months. United's league finish was their highest since 2018, and the club conceded 43 goals, fewer than the season before. Newcastle's defensive record was the best in the division, and their manager was named manager of the month twice.
