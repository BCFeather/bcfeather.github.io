A man opened fire at Michigan State University on the evening of 13 February, killing three students and wounding five, two of them critically. The shooting began at Berkey Hall, where a class was in session, and continued at the student union a short walk away. The university ordered students to shelter in place, and the order stayed in force for more than four hours while police searched the campus.

Three students died, and two of them came from the same suburb of Detroit. The gunman, a forty-three-year-old man with no connection to the university, was found dead from a self-inflicted wound after a confrontation with police in a car park. Investigators said he had a note in his pocket and a history of mental health treatment, and they said they had not established a motive.

The response followed a pattern that American campuses had rehearsed. Students described barricading doors with desks, turning off lights and texting their parents from inside classrooms, and the videos they posted were the first record of the event. Police from the city, the county and the state arrived within minutes, and the university's alert system sent messages that students said arrived after they had already heard the shots. Students in the union described hiding behind a counter for forty minutes while the shooter walked past the window.

The political response was exhausted and immediate. The state's Democratic governor called for the legislature to act on the gun bills that had been stalled for a year, and the Republican majority in the state senate said the problem was mental health rather than firearms. Michigan had no red flag law at the time of the shooting, and the university had no policy on the storage of weapons in dormitories, which were the two specifics that the debate kept returning to. The legislature had been negotiating a package of background checks and safe storage rules, and the shooting moved the file from committee to the floor within a month.

Accounting began at the university the next morning. Classes were suspended for two days, the campus was closed to visitors, and counselling was offered in the union where the second attack had happened. Students who had been in the building described the difficulty of returning to a room that had been photographed by every news organization in the country, and the university announced that Berkey Hall would be renovated rather than reopened as it was.

By the time the funerals were held, the country had moved on to the next item, as it always does. The shooting was the forty-eighth on a campus or in a school in the previous twelve months according to one tally, and it was the sixth in Michigan in eighteen months. Each of those events had produced the same statements, and the statements had produced nothing that the following month could measure. The statements that followed were the same statements that had followed the previous forty-seven, and the month that came after them produced nothing measurable.
