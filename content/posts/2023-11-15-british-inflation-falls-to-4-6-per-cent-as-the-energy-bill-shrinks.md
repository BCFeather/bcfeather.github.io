British consumer prices rose 4.6 per cent in the year to October, the statistics office said on 15 November, down from 6.7 per cent in September and below the forecast of 4.8. The figure is the lowest since the autumn of 2021, and the measure has now fallen for four months in a row. The figure was the lowest since the autumn of 2021, and the economists said the fall was helped by the base effect of the energy prices last year.

A lower energy price cap did the work. The regulator cut the ceiling on household bills by about 7 per cent at the start of October, and gas prices are now about a third below the peak. A cap that is lowered is an inflation that is removed by the regulator. The cap is set every three months, and the next change in January is expected to raise the bill by about 1 per cent. The food inflation has fallen for six months, and the prices of the vegetables are below the level of last year. Services stayed firm. The services inflation was about 6.6 per cent, and the wages have been rising by about 7.8 per cent in the private sector. A wage that is high is a service that stays expensive. The bank has said it needs the services to fall to about 5 per cent before it cuts, and the gap is about 1.6 points.

Core inflation fell to 5.7 per cent from 6.1, and the economists said the fall was faster than the bank had expected in its forecast two weeks earlier. A fall that is faster is a cut that arrives sooner. The traders now price the first cut for the spring, and the two-year yield fell to the lowest since the summer. The bank had forecast 4.8 per cent in its report, and the governor said the surprise was welcome but not enough.
