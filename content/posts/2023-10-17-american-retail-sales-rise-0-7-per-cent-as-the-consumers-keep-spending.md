American retail sales rose 0.7 per cent in September from the previous month, the Census Bureau said on 17 October, more than the 0.3 per cent that the economists had expected. The control group rose 0.6 per cent. The figure was the sixth monthly gain of the year, and the sales are about 3 per cent above their level a year earlier in nominal terms. The figure was the strongest since the spring, and the sales have now grown for six months, which is the longest run since 2021.

Motor vehicles led the gain. The dealers sold about 15.5 million vehicles at an annual rate, the fastest since the spring, and the prices were little changed after a year of increases. A car that is bought is a big ticket that the rate cuts had been expected to stop. The average price of a new vehicle is about 48,000 dollars, and the dealers are offering the incentives that the manufacturers have been paying for. The vehicle sales were the highest since the spring of 2021, and the dealers have been rebuilding the inventories that the chip shortage had emptied.

Restaurants and bars rose by about 1 per cent, and grocery stores by about 0.4 as food prices settled. A meal that is eaten out is a household that is not cooking, which is the pattern of a confident consumer. The bars and the restaurants have been adding jobs faster than any other sector, and the spending there is now above its level before the pandemic in real terms. The grocery spending rose by about 4 per cent in nominal terms over the year, and the volumes have been flat as the shoppers traded down to the cheaper brands.

Online sellers did well. The non-store retailers rose by about 1.1 per cent, and the department stores fell as the shoppers shifted the channels. A channel that shifts is a retailer that must change its leases. The online share of the sales is about 16 per cent, and the warehouse operators have been adding the capacity for the holiday season. The sporting goods and the hobby stores fell by about 1.5 per cent, and the clothing was little changed as the autumn ranges arrived.

Revisions raised the summer. The August gain was revised up to 0.8 per cent from 0.6, and the July to 0.5, which means the consumer did more in the third quarter than the data first showed. A revision that is upward is a growth that is stronger. The econometricians note that the retail data cover about a third of the consumer spending, and the services are the part that the report does not see. The saving rate is about 3.4 per cent, well below the 5 per cent of 2019, which the economists say is the sign that the households are drawing on the reserves.

What the month shows is that the consumer has kept spending through the rate rises, and the spending is the reason the recession that the surveys predicted has not arrived. A consumer who spends more when the borrowing costs more is a consumer who believes the job is safe, and the belief is what the Fed has been trying to test. A spending that holds up at this level of the rates is a spending that the Fed will have to slow with the rates alone, and the alone is the worry.
