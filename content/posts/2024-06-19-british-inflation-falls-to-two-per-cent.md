British consumer prices rose 2.0 per cent in the year to May, the first time the rate has reached the target since 2021, according to the report published on 19 June. Services inflation was 5.7 per cent and the core measure 3.5 per cent. The figure was exactly what the economists expected, and the reaction came from the services number, which was higher than the consensus. The report is the last before the election that the committee will see, and the members have said they will not respond to it in public.

Headline numbers are comparisons, and the energy line is the one that did the work. The regulator's cap is lower than it was a year ago, and the contribution of electricity and gas is now negative, which is a rate that has arrived on the arithmetic rather than on the underlying pressure. The regulator lowered the cap twice this year, and the effect on the index is the largest single reason the headline has fallen.

Services are what the committee watches. The measure has fallen from a peak above seven per cent and remains well above the level consistent with the target, and the members have said it reflects the wage settlements signed last year. A price set by a wage moves when the contract ends. Wages in the service sector rose by about six per cent in the year, and the committee has said it wants the settlements to moderate before it cuts.

Traders read the report as a reason to expect a cut in August rather than in June. The probability of a reduction at the meeting the next day fell and the pound rose, which is the reaction of a market that had positioned for a softer number. A report that meets the target and disappoints shows how much the detail matters. The committee meets on 20 June, and the vote was expected to be a hold with the minutes describing the decision as finely balanced.

Election timing is unavoidable. The vote is two weeks away and the government has described the figure as proof that its plan worked, while the opposition pointed to the level of prices rather than to their rate of change. A target met in an election month is a target both sides claim. The two campaigns have used the same number to make opposite arguments, which is the normal condition of an election in which the level of prices is the issue.

What the month shows is that the headline can reach the target while the measure the committee uses stays high, and the gap between the two is the whole of the argument about when to cut. A target reached in the headline is a target that has to be held in the services, and holding it is the work that remains. The August meeting is the next opportunity, and the committee has said the decision will depend on the settlements that are reported in July.
