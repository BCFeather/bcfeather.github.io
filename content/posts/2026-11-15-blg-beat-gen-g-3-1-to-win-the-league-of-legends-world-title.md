BLG won the League of Legends world championship in New York on Saturday, beating Gen.G 3-1 after losing the opening game of the best-of-five final. The Chinese club, owned by the video platform bilibili, lifted the Summoner's Cup for the first time, and the win arrived shortly after 03:00 on Sunday in Beijing, when a large part of its support was still awake.

LPL now holds a fourth world title, after Invictus Gaming in 2018, FunPlus Phoenix in 2019 and EDward Gaming in 2021. The league had waited five years for another, a stretch in which Korean sides won every edition and Chinese rosters were repeatedly rebuilt around imported players from Seoul. BLG itself was founded in December 2017, when bilibili bought a slot in the LPL, and the club had spent nine seasons trying to turn domestic form into a world title, with several roster overhauls along the way.

Knight, the BLG mid laner whose real name is Zhuo Ding, was named the most valuable player of the final. He said few observers had expected him to outplay Chovy, the Gen.G mid laner whose real name is Jeong Ji-hoon, or for his team to beat the side that had looked the strongest through the group phase. 'I have proved that I am better than him, and that BLG is the best,' he said afterwards.

Its path to the trophy was uneven. BLG entered as the LPL's second seed, drifted to 2-2 in the Swiss stage and had to beat HLE 2-0 in a deciding series to reach the knockout rounds. Once there, it swept DK 3-0 in the quarter-final, beat defending champions T1 3-1 in the semi-final and closed out Gen.G in the final. DK had arrived unbeaten in the Swiss stage and had won the Esports World Cup in the summer.

Victory over T1 ended a run of 13 straight best-of-five wins for the Korean organization against LPL opponents at world championships, and it meant BLG had eliminated all four LCK teams in a single tournament, a first for a Chinese club. T1, led by the veteran mid laner Faker, had won the previous three titles and six in total, and had been the heaviest favourite in the bracket's bottom half.

Gen.G had reached the final unbeaten, with 3-0 wins over LYON and Invictus Gaming in the knockout rounds, and was widely priced as the strongest team of the tournament. More than 270,000 posts about the tournament had been published on bilibili before the final, according to the site, which said more than 20 million people watched the deciding series, with some 3.3 million comments posted during the broadcast. The site also handed out free premium memberships to users from noon on Sunday. Fans on Weibo called the run a one-through-four, and the club's own post afterwards read: 'The wind rises this morning, the champion is BLG.'
