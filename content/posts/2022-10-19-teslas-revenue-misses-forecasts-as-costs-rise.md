Tesla published results on 19 October, and revenue was 21.45 billion dollars, up 56 per cent on the year but below the 21.98 billion that analysts had forecast. Earnings per share were 1.05, above the 1.01 expected. The company delivered 343,830 vehicles in the quarter, a record, and the shortfall was in price rather than in volume. The average selling price was 53,000 dollars, down from 56,000 a year earlier.

Prices had been cut in China in the quarter to defend market share, and the average selling price fell 3 per cent. The automotive gross margin was 27.9 per cent, down from 30.5 a year earlier and below the 28.4 that analysts expected. The company blamed higher costs for batteries and for the ramp of two new factories in Berlin and Texas. The Berlin plant built 2,000 cars a week, and the Texas plant built 3,000.

Production exceeded deliveries by about 22,000 vehicles, which is unusual for a company that had been supply constrained. The company said the gap was because cars in transit to customers had not been counted by the end of the quarter, and it said the backlog was growing. The energy business was the bright spot. Storage deployments reached 2.1 gigawatt hours, a record, and revenue from that segment rose 40 per cent. The services business also grew, and the company said the charging network had sold more energy than in any previous quarter.

Meeting its target of 50 per cent growth in deliveries for the year was ruled out by the company. It had said in January that growth would be 50 per cent or more, and the chief executive repeated that in July. The fourth quarter would need to grow 40 per cent on the third to reach the target, and the company said supply chains made that unlikely.

Investors said the distraction was visible in the results, and the board said it was not. The stock fell 6 per cent the next day and ended the year 65 per cent lower. The company had lost more than half its value since January, and the chief executive's purchase of Twitter in October added to the pressure. The company said it would build a new factory in Mexico, and the announcement came in March.

Tesla delivered 405,000 vehicles, and the full-year total was 1.31 million, up 40 per cent. The fourth quarter did reach a record. The company cut prices again in January 2023 in China and in the United States, and the margin fell to 19 per cent in the first quarter. The results marked the end of the growth-at-any-price phase. Analysts said the company would have to compete on price against established manufacturers that were entering the electric market, and the margin it had earned in 2021 and 2022 was unlikely to return. The shares recovered in 2023 as the price cuts produced volume growth.
