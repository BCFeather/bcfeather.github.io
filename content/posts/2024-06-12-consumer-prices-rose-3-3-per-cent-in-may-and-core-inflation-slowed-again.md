Consumer prices rose 3.3 per cent in the year to May, a tenth less than the month before, and the core measure fell to 3.4 per cent. The report was published on 12 June and was the first in five months to come in below what the forecasters expected. The figures were close to the consensus, and the detail that the analysts emphasized was the three-month annualized rate, which fell below three per cent for the first time this year. The three-month annualized rate that the committee prefers fell to 2.7 per cent, which is the number the analysts quoted afterwards rather than the headline.

Shelter is the slow part. Rents for new tenants have been rising more slowly than the figure in the index, and the lag between the two is about a year, which means the index will keep falling even if the market does not change. A statistic that lags is a statistic that is already telling the future. The rental vacancy rate has been rising in the southern cities, and the builders who finished apartments in the last two years are now competing with the landlords who bought them.

Goods prices fell for a third month, and the discounting in the shops has been more aggressive than usual as the retailers work through the stock they bought in the winter. A price that falls is a price that the central bank does not have to fight. The inventory that the retailers carry is higher than they planned, and the markdowns that follow are a form of disinflation that the index records with a lag.

Air fares and insurance were the components that kept the core high, and both are the ones that the economists describe as noisy, because they are set by a few firms and move for reasons that the models do not capture. A component that is volatile is a component that a single month cannot settle. The insurance component has risen faster than any other for a year, and the regulators in several states have begun to approve the increases that the insurers requested.

Market moved quickly. Yields fell and the probability of a cut by September rose above the level of the previous week, which is the reaction of a market that had been worried about the opposite surprise. A report that beats expectations is a report that the positioning has to be adjusted for. The committee meets on the same day as the report, and the decision was expected to be a hold with a projection of one cut before the end of the year. The two-year yield fell by ten basis points, and the dollar weakened against the currencies of the countries whose central banks have already cut.

What the month shows is that disinflation is now driven by the parts of the basket that are set by contracts rather than by markets, and those parts move slowly in both directions. A trend that is confirmed by three months is the one the committee has said it needs, and the report is the second of the three.
