Two events recur in Chinese secondary schools every term, neither rare enough to be filed as bad luck. A boy who has not run a kilometre since primary school covers a thousand metres for a grade, in heat, on a field without a defibrillator, and goes down in the last bend. A girl stops eating and sleeps four hours a night, and by spring her classmates are told, in a sentence that never uses the word, that she is gone.

Each scene is answered in one institutional voice: the matter is being handled, the family is being cared for, and little beyond that is released. What joins the two cases is arithmetic. A school counts what it is scored on, and a body's tolerance and a mind's load sit off the sheet until they fail loudly.

Follow the time and the pattern becomes visible. Hours are allocated by what an examination will eventually weigh, so sport lived outside the timetable for two decades, its weekly lessons converted into study halls whenever a test approached, until a 2021 notice fixed the weekly hours and required thirty minutes of daily exercise, and the same year's Provisions on the Protection of Minors in Schools forbade restraints on leaving the classroom during breaks. Sport then re-entered the ledger as a score: Yunnan raised physical education from 50 points to 100 in 2020, Henan from 70 to 100, Beijing from 30 to 50. A subject that cannot be scored loses its hours, and the reform did buy hours; it also created an examination sat by cohorts whose conditioning had been deferred for years, one event on one date for trained and untrained pupils alike.

Physiology does not negotiate with timetables. Sudden cardiac death in young athletes runs at 0.4 to 0.6 per 100,000 athlete-years, male cases outnumbering female by nine to one, and most deaths fall during or just after exertion. Italy's figure is the useful one: 3.6 per 100,000 before systematic screening, 0.4 after, and survival falls by roughly a tenth for each minute a fibrillating heart waits for a shock. Campus equipment remains thin. Defibrillators were urged by a 2021 circular rather than required, and about 4,127 schools held pilot status by July 2025. A resting electrocardiogram is still outside the school check, so the commonest cause is identified at autopsy or never.

The mental half of the ledger follows the same accounting. The 2022 blue book put 14.8 per cent of adolescents at some risk of depression and 4.0 per cent at severe risk, with rural pupils at 20.0 per cent. A 2023 plan across seventeen departments promised a mental-health teacher in 95 per cent of schools by 2025, annual screening and one file per pupil. The target measures staffing, not capacity: one counsellor for two thousand pupils can triage and cannot treat, and a register without a service converts need into surveillance.

Responsibility is where the incentive turns perverse. The Civil Code presumes a school at fault over a pupil under eight, and asks of an older one only whether a reasonable school would have acted otherwise. A 2019 opinion from five departments defined eight categories of protest at school gates and told schools to call the police, so the dispute ends in mediation, where quiet is bought with a settlement and a confidentiality clause. What would change the arithmetic is cheap: annual counts of serious incidents with causes from medical records, a defibrillator within three minutes of the far touchline, an electrocardiogram where a trained reader exists, and claims paid without silence clauses. A school that counted properly would know how many pupils ran a kilometre without a medical event, how many saw a counsellor, and how long each waited. At present the only quantity it reliably produces is the score.
