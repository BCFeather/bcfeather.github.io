Two kinds of event recur in Chinese secondary schools every term, and neither is rare enough to be filed as bad luck. Both are worth picturing plainly. In the first, a boy who has not run a kilometre since primary school covers 1,000 metres for a grade, in heat, on a field without a defibrillator, and goes down in the last bend; the notice that follows offers the family condolences and records that he felt unwell. In the second, a girl stops eating and sleeps four hours a night, and by the spring term her classmates are told, in a sentence that never uses the word, that she is gone.

Neither scene belongs to any one school, and both recur often enough that the details blur between them. Between them sits the ordinary afternoon, a fall, a sprain, a bill settled at a clinic counter. The institution answers in one voice: the matter is being handled, the family is being cared for, and little beyond that is released. What joins all three is arithmetic. A school counts what it is scored on, and a body's tolerance and a mind's load sit off the sheet until they fail loudly.

To see how that happens, follow the time. Hours in a Chinese secondary school are allocated by what an examination will eventually weigh, which is why the timetable is a faithful map of the assessment regime. Sport lived outside that map for two decades: the weekly hours existed on paper, physical education teachers were said to fall ill whenever a mathematics test approached, and the last period of the day was routinely converted into a study hall. Two regulations had to be issued to reverse the drift. A 2021 notice on pupil fitness set the weekly hours explicitly, four for the first two primary years, three for the upper primary and lower secondary years, two for the senior years, and required thirty minutes of collective exercise each day. The same year, the Provisions on the Protection of Minors in Schools forbade unnecessary restraints on talking, playing and leaving the classroom during breaks. A legal prohibition on restricting recess is a precise measurement of how much recess had been restricted.

Sport then re-entered the ledger, this time as a score. Yunnan moved physical education from 50 points to 100 in 2020, putting it level with Chinese, mathematics and the foreign language. Henan went from 70 to 100, Beijing from 30 to 50 with a stated path to 70 by 2029, Jiangxi from 10 to 60 over a decade. The intention was defensible, since a subject that cannot be scored loses its hours, and the reform did buy hours. What it also bought was a new examination, sat by cohorts whose conditioning had been deferred for years. A pupil who has trained twice a week for a term is prepared for a maximal effort to a degree that a pupil who has never trained is not, and the test admits no distinction between them. Term-end testing converts both into the same event: an unacclimatized body asked for its best number on a fixed date, often in the first warm week of the year.

Physiology does not negotiate with timetables. Sudden cardiac death in young athletes runs at roughly 0.4 to 0.6 per 100,000 athlete-years in the registries that count it, with male cases outnumbering female by something like nine to one, and more than nine in ten occurring during or immediately after exertion. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy accounts for about a third of American cases, arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy for the largest share of Italian cases, while congenital coronary anomalies, channelopathies such as long QT syndrome and blunt-impact commotio cordis account for a large share of the rest. Italian surveillance also supplies the number that matters most: incidence of about 3.6 per 100,000 before systematic screening and 0.4 after it was introduced. Many of these deaths are survivable if a heart in ventricular fibrillation is shocked within a few minutes, since survival falls by roughly a tenth for every minute of delay.

Most of what a field does to a body is milder than either extreme, and the habit is formed there. A pupil trips at the far end of the track, or turns an ankle on the hurdle run, or lands on a wrist in a basketball game played on a surface last relaid before the building went up. A teacher walks the child to the clinic, a parent is telephoned, the hospital bill is settled, and nothing is written down; the episode survives in three memories and in no register. The accounting is the same as in the fatal case, at a price small enough to hide, and a school that keeps no record of a sprain will keep none of a fracture and none of a heart, because counting begins with the belief that a line in a book changes what happens next. The mild events are also the useful ones, since a torn ligament, a broken wrist over the kerb of a long-jump pit and a scalp cut from a goalpost are readable warnings about surface, equipment and supervision, and each of them has a cheap remedy. Lose the warning and only the tail of the distribution stays visible.

Against that, the equipment on campus is thin. A 2015 measure on sports risk required health records for every pupil, exemptions for those unfit to take part, an emergency plan, and a first-aid point at large events. Defibrillators arrived later and weaker: a 2021 circular with the Red Cross urged schools to install them, a pilot covered 150 senior schools and universities, and by July 2025 about 4,127 schools held pilot status. Urging is not requiring. A resting twelve-lead electrocardiogram is still not part of the routine school health check, which means the commonest cause is usually identified at autopsy or never. Italy's ninefold fall was bought with exactly that test, and the cost per athlete is a fraction of what one settled claim costs.

The mental half of the ledger appears the same way. The 2022 blue book on national mental health put 14.8 per cent of adolescents at some risk of depression, 4.0 per cent at severe risk, with rural secondary pupils at 20.0 per cent and scores rising with grade. A 2023 plan signed by seventeen departments set a target of 95 per cent of schools with a full or part-time mental-health teacher by 2025, an annual screening from the fifth grade upward, and one file per pupil. The target measures staffing, not capacity. One counsellor for two thousand pupils can triage and cannot treat; referral depends on child psychiatry that most counties do not have; and a screening instrument that produces a register without a service converts need into surveillance. Counselling rooms are also, in the ordinary running of a school, the place a pupil is sent after a crisis rather than before one.

Responsibility is where the two halves meet, and where the incentive turns perverse. Under the Civil Code, a school is presumed at fault when a pupil under eight is injured on its watch, and is liable for an older pupil only where it failed its duty of care, which makes the operative question whether the school did what a reasonable school would have done. A 2019 opinion from five departments then defined eight categories of protest at school gates, from banners and wreaths to blocking gates and leaving a body on campus, and instructed schools to call the police. The effect is a standoff with one exit. A grieving family's only lever is visibility; a school's cheapest response is closure; and the dispute ends in mediation, where the price of quiet is a settlement and, frequently, a confidentiality clause.

Once we understand what schools stand to gain or lose, the behaviour might stop looking like cowardice. A headteacher is judged on examination results, and recorded safety incidents count against the school in its annual assessment. A death that is admitted enters the record as a negative; a death framed as an unforeseeable medical event leaves nothing measurable behind. The same reflex operates earlier and cheaper: an injury entered in the annual assessment is a deduction, so a school that keeps a sprain off the sheet is rehearsing the answer it will need for a heart. Liability insurance pays a claim without producing a finding about cause. An autopsy requires a family's consent, and families decline for reasons that are entirely human, while no institution has an interest in pressing the point. The result is a death with no cause, a cause with no prevention, and a file that closes. Prevention needs a diagnosis, and the system is organized to avoid one.

Ironically, what would change the arithmetic is unglamorous and mostly cheap. Schools could publish annual counts of serious incidents with the cause drawn from medical records, so that a pattern becomes visible before it becomes a scandal. Every sports field could hold a defibrillator within three minutes of the far touchline, with staff drilled on the route and the pads. Pre-participation screening could ask the family history and the symptoms, and add an electrocardiogram where a trained reader is available. Conditioning could run across the year, and no pupil could be asked for a maximal test without it. Heat rules could exist. Reviews could sit outside the school's chain of command and hold a right to the records. Settlements could be paid without silence clauses.

On the other side, counsellor ratios could be set at a caseload a person can carry. Counselling time could be protected from timetable raids. The stakes of one examination could be lowered through multiple sittings and banded results, since pressure of that kind is structural and does not yield to encouragement. Waiting time is the number that turns a pledge into a service, and districts rarely publish it. A pupil seen after three weeks has been let down by a schedule.

A school that counted properly could answer questions it currently cannot. It would know how many of its pupils can run a kilometre without a medical event, and how many did so this year. It would know how many saw a counsellor last term, how many were referred onward, and how long each waited for an appointment. It would know how many of its own sports injuries were treated on site and how many in hospital. And when a pupil died, it would state what happened, in public, as institutions that keep records are able to do, instead of concealing everything because 'the CCTV camera was not working'. The alternative is the present arrangement, in which the only quantity a school reliably produces is the score, and the only lesson a death teaches is how to manage the story.
