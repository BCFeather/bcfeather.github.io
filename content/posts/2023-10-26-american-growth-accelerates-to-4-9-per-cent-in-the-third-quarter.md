American output grew at an annual rate of 4.9 per cent in the third quarter, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said on 26 October, up from 2.1 per cent in the second and the fastest since the end of 2021. The figure was above the forecast of 4.5 per cent, and the growth is now above the pace that the economists consider the trend, which they put at about 2 per cent.

Consumption did most of it. The household spending rose at about 4 per cent, and the services and the cars led, which shows the consumer has kept spending despite the rates. A consumer that spends at this pace is a demand that the tightening has not yet reached. The spending on the restaurants and the travel rose by about 5 per cent, and the saving rate fell to about 3.4 per cent from 5 in the spring. The spending on the goods rose by about 5 per cent, and the cars and the parts were the strongest as the dealers rebuilt the stock.

Inventories added to the total. The firms built the stocks by about 80 billion dollars, and that contribution is a one-off because the stocks cannot keep growing faster than the sales. A warehouse that is filled is a growth that will not repeat. The inventory contribution was about 1.3 points of the growth, and the final sales to the domestic buyers rose at about 3.5 per cent. The ratio of the inventories to the sales rose to about 1.4, which is close to the level that the retailers consider normal and above the 1.3 of last year.

Government spending added too. The federal spending rose at about 4 per cent as the infrastructure projects and the defense orders were paid, and the state and local spending followed. A spending that is fiscal is a demand that the rates cannot stop. The defense spending rose by about 8 per cent, and the analysts said the aid to Ukraine is part of the line in the accounts.

Business investment was soft. The spending on the equipment fell and the structures rose, and the residential investment fell for a ninth quarter as the mortgages reached 8 per cent. A house that is not built is a growth that is missing. The residential investment has fallen in every quarter since the start of last year, and the sales of the new homes have begun to recover as the builders cut the prices. The commercial construction rose for a third quarter as the factories for the chips were built, and the offices were the weakest part of the category.

What the quarter shows is that the economy has absorbed the rate rises so far, and the absorption is the reason the Fed has said it will keep the rates high for longer. A quarter at 4.9 per cent is a quarter the Fed will cite as evidence that it can wait, and the waiting is the policy.
