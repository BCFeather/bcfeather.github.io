Instant retail, the business of delivering a purchased item within half an hour, began as a grocery service and has spread to categories that nobody expected: cosmetics, phone chargers, medicine, pet food, and in some cities, a single bottle of wine at eleven at night. The apps promise thirty minutes and mostly deliver in twenty-five. In a district where twenty orders an hour leave a single store, a rider can chain four drops into a loop; in a suburb where the same store sends three, the fee cannot cover the trip.

The economics are unusual. A conventional e-commerce order travels from a regional warehouse through a sorting centre and a delivery station, and the cost is spread across a parcel that may travel a thousand kilometres.
