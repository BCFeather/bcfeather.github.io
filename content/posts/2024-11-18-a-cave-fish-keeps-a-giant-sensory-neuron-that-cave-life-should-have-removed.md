The Mauthner neuron is a giant multisensory cell that triggers the rapid escape response of a fish, and a cave fish that never needs to escape ought to lose it. A paper in *PNAS* on 18 November 2024 finds that the cave form keeps it, and keeps it under stabilizing selection. A neuron that no longer serves its function but remains under selection is a case that tests how tightly development constrains evolution.

*Astyanax mexicanus* exists as surface populations with predators and cave populations without them. The comparison is a natural experiment, and the absence of predation is a strong prediction about which neurons should atrophy. Surface and cave forms diverged recently enough that the comparison is between populations rather than between species, which sharpens the inference.

The prediction is that the Mauthner neuron should shrink, lose sensitivity or disappear. Cave fish do show reduced escape behaviour, and that behavioural difference is the phenotype a reductionist account would attribute to the cell. Reduced escape behaviour with an intact cell separates the function from the structure, and that separation is the paper's central observation. Behaviour and anatomy are measured in the same animals in this study, which is what allows the two to be compared directly.

What the anatomy shows is a cell that persists, with a morphology and a projection that vary far less than the surface form's. Constraint rather than decay is the pattern the measurements describe. Low variance in a population that has lost the function is the signature of constraint, since decay would widen the distribution rather than narrow it. Persistence of a cell is evidence that removal is not the default response to disuse, and the default matters for how loss is modelled elsewhere.

Stabilizing selection is the interpretation. Variation is present in the population, and the variants that depart from the typical cell leave fewer descendants, which is what selection against deviation means. Selection against deviation is a different force from selection for a function, and the distinction matters for how the result is read. Constraint on morphology is visible in the variance, and variance is a quantity that a small sample can estimate poorly.

Why a cell without a behavioural function remains under selection is the puzzle. One answer is that the neuron keeps a sensory role the cave environment still rewards, and another is that its development is entangled with structures that are still needed. Entanglement with development is the explanation that would make the cell's persistence a by-product rather than an adaptation.

On the modelling side, the paper supplies the pattern and not the mechanism, and the mechanism would require an experiment that manipulates the cell in cave embryos. Manipulation in cave embryos is technically demanding, and the demand is the reason the mechanism remains open. A mechanism is what would turn an interesting persistence into a case study of developmental constraint, and the case study is what the field lacks.
