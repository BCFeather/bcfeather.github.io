Samsung held its Unpacked event in San Francisco on 1 February and announced three phones for the year. The S23 and S23 Plus keep the design of the previous generation, and the Ultra changes its shape at the corners. Prices were set at 799, 999 and 1,199 dollars, the same as last year in the United States and higher in Europe. The event was streamed to 190 countries, and the company said 40 million people watched it live or within a day.

Its main sensor is 200 megapixels, which bins down to 12 by default and can shoot full resolution on demand. The company says the larger sensor gathers more light, which matters more than the pixel count for photographs taken indoors. Two telephoto lenses remain, one at 3 times and one at 10 times, and the periscope design is unchanged. Sensor size is the part that matters, and the main camera is now 1.4 times larger than the one it replaces. Night photographs are where the difference shows, and the company demonstrated them in a dark room at the venue.

Qualcomm supplies the chip everywhere this year, and that is a change. In previous generations the company used its own Exynos silicon in Europe and the Snapdragon in the United States, and reviewers complained about the difference. The Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 is made on a 4 nanometre process, and Samsung says the vapour chamber inside is 1.6 times larger than before. Thermal management has been the weak point of the range for three years, and the larger chamber is the response to that.

Battery capacity rose to 5,000 milliamp hours on the Ultra, and the screen brightness reached 1,750 nits in a measured test. The company also promised four generations of operating system updates, which is longer than most rivals offer. Those commitments matter more to buyers than another camera feature, according to surveys the company published alongside the launch. Samsung also kept the price of the S23 in Korea flat, which is unusual in a year when the currency fell.

Pre-orders in Korea were reported at 1.1 million units in the first week, a record for the range. European carriers said the upgrade offer, which doubled the storage for the same price, drove most of the interest. Analysts noted that the premium segment had held up better than the mid-range during the slowdown, which is why the launch concentrated there. Carriers in Britain and Germany said trade-in offers accounted for half of the early orders.

What the launch did not settle was the price of the foldables, which the company sells at twice the level of the Ultra. The event closed with a short film shot on the new sensor, and the phones went on sale on 17 February. Samsung shipped 260 million phones in 2022, and it told analysts it expected a similar number this year. Shipments of the Ultra were expected to be about 40 per cent of the range, up from 30 per cent last year. Memory prices fell through the year, and analysts said the bill of materials was lower than in 2022.
