Baidu showed a chatbot answering questions about a Chinese poem, solving a mathematics problem and writing a short story about the company's founder. The presentation was streamed on 16 March and lasted an hour. The demonstrations used questions that had been prepared in advance, and no live audience questions were taken. The shares fell 10 per cent during the presentation and closed 6 per cent lower. The presentation was held at the company's headquarters in Beijing and lasted 60 minutes.

Announced in February with a promise to launch in March, the product drew attention mainly because of the date. The company said the model had 260 billion parameters, trained on a mix of web data and its own corpus. It said the model could answer in Chinese better than the models trained mostly on English, which is the claim that matters in the domestic market. The training corpus was reported to be 4 trillion tokens, which is larger than the overseas models disclosed.

Two of the answers were shorter than the questions, and one referred to a source that did not exist. The demonstrations were weaker than expected, and the writing sample had a factual error about the founder's birthplace. The company's chief executive said afterwards that the version shown was not the final one and that the model would improve with use. The chief executive said the model would be retrained every quarter, and that the next version was due in May.

Reviewers noted that the Chinese product had not been tested on open benchmarks, and the company said it would publish scores in April. The comparison with the models released overseas was immediate and unfavourable. The scores it published then put the model below the leading systems on reasoning tasks and above them on Chinese-language tasks. The benchmarks it published in April covered 20 tasks and put the model in the middle of the field.

Access was opened to a limited group of users in April, and the company said it had 20,000 applicants in the first week. The interface was text-only at first, and the image features came in the summer. Enterprise customers were offered a private deployment, which is the product the company expected to sell. The private deployment cost 1 million yuan a year for the smallest configuration.

Analysts said the path from a demonstration to revenue was the same one the overseas firms were taking, and that it would take a year. The launch mattered commercially because search advertising had been flat for two years. The company said the chatbot would be integrated into its search product, and that it would not charge consumers for the basic version. Search revenue had grown 1 per cent in 2022, and the company's advertising share had fallen for three years. Competitors in the domestic market released their own models within a month, and two of them published better benchmark scores. The company's shares recovered in the summer when the paid version was launched, and the product reached 100 million users by the autumn. The episode is remembered for the fall rather than for the product.
