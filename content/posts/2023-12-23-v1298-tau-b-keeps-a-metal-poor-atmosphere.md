Almost everything known about exoplanet atmospheres comes from planets that are billions of years old, by which time the gas has settled, the interior has cooled and the primordial envelope has been partly rewritten by escape and mixing. The first hundred million years are the interval in which a planet still carries what it formed with, and that interval has been largely inaccessible because young systems are noisy and their planets are faint.

V1298 Tau b offers a look at that interval. The planet orbits a pre-main-sequence star, is about 23 million years old, and is the size of a warm Jupiter, which makes it a transiting target whose atmosphere can be sampled while the envelope is still fresh. A preprint posted to arXiv on 28 December 2023 reports a transmission spectrum taken across several wavelengths and finds absorption from water vapour at \(5\sigma\), together with an unusually large atmospheric scale height that the signal depends on. The star's youth helps in one respect and hinders in another: the planet is warm and puffed up, and the same activity that inflates it adds noise to the stellar spectrum that has to be removed before the planetary lines can be read.

Mass and density are quoted as upper limits: \(24\pm5\,M_{\oplus}\) for the mass at the level the data allow, and \(0.12\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}\) for the density, which places the planet among the least dense objects yet found. A body that large and that tenuous has an envelope that is still extended, and the scale height that makes the water detection possible is a consequence of the same inflation.

The retrieved metallicity runs below solar, at \(\log Z = -0.1\) with an interval from \(-0.72\) to \(+0.66\) in solar units, and that is the result with the widest consequences. Core accretion predicts that a giant planet born from solids should be enriched relative to its star, and a sub-solar value in a young Jupiter argues either that the planet formed by accreting pebbles in situ or that the enrichment expected from theory is not what young envelopes show.

Methane is absent from the spectrum, which points to an interior warmer than the formation entropy alone would produce. Warm interiors suppress methane in favour of carbon monoxide, so the non-detection is evidence about the planet's thermal state as much as about its chemistry, and it sits awkwardly with a planet that is supposed to be cooling from a recent birth.

Caveats are plentiful. The mass and density are limits rather than measurements, the metallicity depends on the retrieval model and its priors, and one planet is not a population. What the object does supply is a target that will change: as it contracts it should become a Neptune or sub-Neptune, and a second epoch of observation, decades from now in the planet's own time, would catch it mid-transition and test whether the envelope keeps its low metal content.
