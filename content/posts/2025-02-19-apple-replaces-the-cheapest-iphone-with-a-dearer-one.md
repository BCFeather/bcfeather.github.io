description: "Apple announced the iPhone 16e on 19 February at 599 dollars, ending the SE line and raising the entry price by 170 dollars while adding the chip, the camera and the battery that the old model lacked."
---

iPhone SE was the phone that Apple sold to people who wanted the smallest and cheapest device in the range, and it kept a home button and a small screen long after the rest of the line had moved on. The 16e replaces it with a 6.1-inch screen, a notch, Face ID and the A18 chip, which is the same silicon that the iPhone 16 uses.

Price is 599 dollars for 128 gigabytes, with 256 and 512 gigabyte versions above it, and the phone comes in black and white. That is 170 dollars more than the SE it replaces, and it sits 200 dollars below the iPhone 16, which leaves the range with a cheaper tier that is no longer cheap.

Apple's own modem is the detail that the company did not put in the headline. The 16e is the first iPhone with a cellular modem designed in house, which the company calls the C1, and the claim is that it is the most power-efficient modem in any iPhone. A modem that draws less power is a modem that lets a smaller battery last a day, and the vertical integration is the strategic point.

Camera is a single 48 megapixel sensor with a two-times crop that produces a 12 megapixel image, which is the arrangement that Apple used on the iPhone 16 and not the two-lens system of the Pro. The phone lacks the ultra-wide camera, the Dynamic Island and the magnetic ring that the rest of the line carries, and the omissions are the way Apple separates a cheaper tier from the standard one.

Timing is a bet on the middle of the market. Apple's revenue from the iPhone had been flat for a year, and the growth that the company reported came from services; a cheaper phone that reaches a buyer who would otherwise keep a four-year-old device is the way to add units without discounting the flagship. The 16e was available for pre-order in China at 4,499 yuan from 21 February, which is a price that puts it against the domestic flagships.

Analysts read the price as a margin decision. A phone that starts at 599 dollars with a chip from the flagship and a modem of the company's own design can be made at a cost that leaves room for the services revenue that follows the sale, and the services attach rate on a new device is higher than on an old one. The trade is that the entry price is now the price of a mid-range Android flagship.

What the model does to the line is to remove the last phone that a buyer on a budget could afford without a carrier subsidy. A 599 dollar phone is a phone that costs 25 dollars a month for two years, which is the financing model that the carriers run, and the question of whether the market will pay it was answered by the first weekend of orders.
