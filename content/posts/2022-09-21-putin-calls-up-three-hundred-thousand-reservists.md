President Vladimir Putin signed a decree on Wednesday morning ordering a partial mobilization, and he said in a televised address that the army needed three hundred thousand reservists. It is the first call-up of its kind since the second world war, and the decree was approved by the parliament in the afternoon. The defence minister said the pool from which the men would be drawn numbered about twenty-five million, and that students and conscripts would be exempt. The announcement came hours after the authorities in the occupied regions said they would hold referendums on joining Russia.

The decree is vague in the way that matters. It does not say which categories of reservists are included, and it leaves the quotas to the regional governors, who have been told to fill them within weeks. A second law passed the same day raised the penalties for desertion and for surrender, and it created new offences for refusing to serve. Lawyers in Moscow said the combination gives the recruiting offices discretion that the courts have not been asked to review.

Reaction inside the country was the one the decree was designed to prevent. Protests were reported in more than thirty cities, and the monitoring group that counts arrests put the number in the thousands within a day. Flights from Moscow to Istanbul, Yerevan and Tbilisi were sold out by the afternoon, and the prices on the remaining seats rose tenfold. Queues formed at the land borders with Georgia and Kazakhstan, and the authorities in those countries said they would not close them.

Militarily the purpose is to hold a front that has been stretched by the counteroffensive in the north-east. Russia's army has been short of infantry since the spring, and the units in the Donbas have been filled by recruits from the occupied areas and by volunteers on short contracts. Analysts said the new men would arrive without training and without equipment, and that the value of the decree is political as much as it is military. It moves the war from an operation to a national effort, which is a different kind of commitment.

Putin also repeated a warning about nuclear weapons, and he said that the countries that had threatened Russia should understand that the response would be serious. The American and European governments said they had heard the warning before and that it changed nothing in their planning. The referendums are expected to be held in the last week of September, and the argument in Moscow is that a vote would allow the government to describe an attack on those regions as an attack on Russia.

What the mobilization does to the country is the part that will be measured over months. The men who leave are the ones who build houses and drive trains, and the regions that supply most of them are the poorest. The recruiting offices have been told to deliver a number, and the polls show that the war has supporters and that the call-up has far fewer. The winter will arrive with the front longer than it was in the summer and with the trains fuller than they were in the spring.
