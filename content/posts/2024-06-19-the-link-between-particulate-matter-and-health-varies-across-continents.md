Particulate matter kills through mechanisms that differ by region, and a model that assumes one global relationship is a model that will misattribute risk. A paper in Nature Communications on 19 June 2024 tests that assumption across continents. Testing is what a global model needs before it is used for local policy. Oxidative potential is the property that the paper links to health, and the property describes what an aerosol does chemically rather than how much of it there is. A coefficient that varies is a coefficient that a global model should not use, and using it is what the paper warns against. A coefficient is a compressed statement about a population and a place, and compressing is what makes it portable and dangerous.

Chemistry is what mass alone cannot capture, and capture is the problem with a globalized coefficient. Inter-continental variability is the result, and variability means that a coefficient fitted in one place does not transfer to another. Transfer is what a global model does by construction, and construction is where the error enters. Sources differ between continents, and source is what sets the chemistry of a particle. Warning is a modest act with a large effect, since mortality estimates travel through global models into national numbers.

A particle from combustion differs from one from dust, and difference is the variability the model reports. Reporting variability is what a user needs to decide whether a local study is required. Local studies are expensive, and expense is why global models exist. Existing is not the same as being correct, and correctness is the claim being tested. National numbers are what a health ministry publishes, and publishing an estimate built on a wrong coefficient is a cost.

Testing a coefficient is a modest contribution with a large consequence, since a coefficient is what a health estimate rests on. Estimates of mortality are built from coefficients, and coefficients vary, so estimates vary. Varying estimates are what a policy has to handle, and handling them means reporting uncertainty. Uncertainty reported honestly is the contribution a global study can make. Cost is borne by the people the estimate describes, and describing them accurately is the point.

A global study cannot replace a local measurement, and the paper says so. Saying so is what keeps the work from being overread, and overreading is common in this literature. Common errors are the ones a careful paper explicitly refuses.

Refusing to generalize is a form of rigour, and rigour is what makes a result useful to a modeller. Modellers need to know where a coefficient applies, and where is a question of geography. Geography is finer than a continent in some cases, and the paper flags that limit.

Limits flagged in advance are limits a user can plan around. Planning around uncertainty is what an air quality programme does, and doing it well requires knowing which parameter carries the risk. Risk carried by one coefficient is what this paper locates.
