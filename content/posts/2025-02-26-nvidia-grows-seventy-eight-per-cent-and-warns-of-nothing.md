---

Revenue for the quarter ending 26 January was 39.31 billion dollars, against guidance of about 37.5 billion and 22.1 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. The company's fiscal year closed at 130.5 billion, more than double the previous one, and the growth has been concentrated in a single product line and in a handful of customers.

Data centre revenue was 35.6 billion dollars, up 93 per cent from a year earlier and 16 per cent from the previous quarter. The segment now provides nine tenths of the company's revenue, and the customers who buy the systems are the cloud providers, the model developers and a growing list of enterprises that are building inference capacity of their own.

Gross margin was 73.0 per cent on a GAAP basis, down 1.6 points from the previous quarter and 3.0 points from a year earlier. The company attributed the decline to the ramp of a new platform, whose manufacturing yields are lower at the start of a cycle, and to the mix of systems that customers ordered, with more of the complete rack-scale products that carry a lower margin than a bare board.

Diluted earnings per share were 0.89 dollars on a GAAP basis, up 14 per cent from the previous quarter, and the company returned cash through a buyback and a dividend that it has raised every year since it began paying one. The balance sheet carries more inventory than it did a year ago, which the company described as a deliberate position ahead of a product transition.

Guidance for the next quarter was about 43 billion dollars, which would be another sequential increase of roughly ten per cent. That number is the one that the market trades on, and it implies that the company sees no pause in the build-out, that the customers who placed orders have the power and the buildings to install the systems, and that the transition to the next architecture does not leave a gap.

Risk that the company did not discuss is concentration. A small number of customers account for a large share of revenue, and the disclosure that the company makes about them is limited to a note in the filing, which names no customer and gives no share. A customer that pauses its build-out, for reasons of capital discipline or of its own demand forecast, removes a visible block of revenue in a single quarter.

What the quarter shows about the cycle is that the demand has not yet met a constraint that the company cannot supply against. The constraints that the industry has described are power, packaging capacity and memory, and the company's guidance implies that it has secured enough of each to grow another ten per cent. Next report will be the test of whether the customers still have the appetite and the power.
