---

Eight days from 28 January to 4 February produced 9.51 billion yuan in ticket sales and 187 million admissions, both records for the lunar new year holiday. One animated sequel accounted for 4.84 billion of that, more than half the total, and the second-placed film took 2.28 billion. The distribution was extreme even by the standards of a market that concentrates on holidays, and it says as much about the structure of Chinese film financing as about the film itself.

Animation is the genre that benefits most from a holiday. A family ticket is bought for three or four people, the screenings that suit families are the ones a holiday schedule has to fill, and a well-known character removes the risk that a parent takes when choosing a film. The 2019 original earned five billion yuan and established the character, and the sequel arrived with an audience that had been waiting six years. Six years is a long gap for a franchise, and the sequel was delayed several times by production problems that the studio has not detailed.

Production is where the money went. The film was made over five years by a group of studios that coordinated shot by shot, with a large effects budget and a production line that resembles an industrial process more than a workshop. Chinese animation has built that capacity gradually, through television series and through service work for foreign productions, and the sequel is the first project that used it at feature scale. Labour is the constraint, because a shot that takes a week at the wrong stage can hold up a sequence for a month.

The revenue mechanics favour the leader. Exhibitors allocate screens on advance sales, and a film that fills them keeps them, so a strong opening compounds. The sequel's share rose every day of the holiday, which means the films around it lost screens as the week went on, and a film that opened to a good number on day one could still finish far below its budget. Minimum ticket prices rose in the same period, which means part of the revenue growth is price rather than attendance.

What the record does not show is a healthy market. Total 2024 box office was about 42.5 billion yuan, well below the 54.9 billion of 2023, and the holiday's record sits inside a shrinking year. A market where one week sets a record and the other fifty-one decline is a market that has concentrated its demand rather than grown it. Cinemas in smaller cities closed at a faster rate than in the largest ones, and the screens that remain are in malls that also sell the merchandise.

Export is the question the industry now asks. Animation travels better than comedy because character design and action carry across languages, and the sequel has been released in a number of overseas markets with subtitles and dubbing. Whether a Chinese mythology can sell tickets in a market that does not know the story is the test that the next film will answer.
