Vladimir Putin signed decrees on the evening of 21 February recognizing the independence of the two separatist republics in eastern Ukraine, the Donetsk and Luhansk people's republics, and he announced that Russian troops would enter the territory as peacekeepers. In a televised address that lasted nearly an hour he said Ukraine had never had a real state and that the country's government was a Western creation. The two republics had declared independence in 2014, and no other government had recognized them.

Troops moved the same night, according to the Russian parliament's own account, and the upper house voted on 22 February to permit the use of the armed forces abroad, which it did within an hour of the request. The decree was ratified within an hour, and the defence ministry published pictures of vehicles crossing into the republics, which it described as a peacekeeping operation at the request of the local authorities. The Ukrainian government called the move an act of aggression and said the line of contact had been shelled along its whole length.

The diplomatic response came quickly. Germany suspended the certification of the Nord Stream 2 pipeline, which had been completed but never opened, and the chancellor said the project could not be approved in the circumstances. Britain sanctioned five Russian banks and three individuals, the European Union agreed a package of sanctions on the finance sector and on the deputies who had voted for recognition, and the United States imposed measures on the two republics and on the banks that served them. The announcements came one after another through the afternoon of 22 February, coordinated in advance and timed for the European markets.

Legally, the argument turned on the Minsk agreements, the 2015 protocol that had set out how the separatist areas would return to Ukraine with a special status, and on the question of who was entitled to interpret them. Russia had signed it and had spent seven years insisting that Ukraine negotiate with the republics, and on 21 February the president said the agreements no longer existed. The German and French governments, which had brokered the protocol, said the recognition had destroyed the only framework that had kept the conflict from becoming a war.

Oil and gas prices rose on the news, and the Russian currency weakened. The Ukrainian government asked for an emergency meeting of the Security Council, where a draft resolution condemning the recognition was vetoed by Russia, and the secretary-general said the decision was a violation of the territorial integrity of Ukraine. China's representative abstained and called for restraint, and the Indian representative did the same.

Six weeks of diplomacy ended that night in a speech about history, delivered to an audience of officials and broadcast without delay. The troops that crossed the border on 21 February were described as peacekeepers and were few, and they gave the Russian government a legal claim that the republics were under its protection. The larger attack began three days later, from the same direction, and the recognition provided the argument for it.
