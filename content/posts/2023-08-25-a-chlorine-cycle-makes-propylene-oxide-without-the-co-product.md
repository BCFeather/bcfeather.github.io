Propylene oxide is a chemical intermediate for polyurethane and for glycols, and its industrial production makes a co-product for every molecule of the target. A route that has no co-product would change the process's economics.

The route in *Science*, August 2023 does that. The authors use a chlorine-mediated cycle that oxidizes propylene and regenerates the oxidant, so that the only net product is the epoxide.

The chemistry is a two-step loop. A chlorine species oxidizes the propylene, and an electrochemical or a chemical step returns the chlorine to its active form, and the loop's net reaction is propylene plus oxygen.

The selectivity is the advantage. A direct oxidation tends to over-oxidize, and a mediated route that stops at the epoxide avoids the combustion that costs yield.

The co-product's absence is the economic point. A process that makes styrene or a tert-butanol alongside the epoxide is tied to those markets, and a process without a co-product is one that can scale independently.

The energy is the second metric. The regeneration step has a cost, and the authors' comparison of the loop's energy with the incumbent's is the claim.

The stability is the caveat. A chlorine species corrodes equipment, and a process that uses one needs materials that the incumbent does not.

The scale is the limitation. A laboratory reactor is small, and an electrochemical cell at industrial scale is an engineering problem.

What the paper does not establish is the economics. A route's viability depends on the price of electricity and on the capital cost, and the paper's analysis is a laboratory estimate.

For chemical engineering, the value is a process designed around a constraint. The co-product is the incumbent's weakness, and a mediated loop is a way to remove it.

The chlorine's role is the mediator. A species that is regenerated is one that is not consumed, and the loop's stoichiometry is the process's advantage.

The epoxidation's selectivity is the kinetic question. A chlorine species that attacks the double bond forms the epoxide, and one that attacks the epoxide's ring opens it.

The electrochemical step is the regeneration. An anode that oxidizes chloride returns the active species, and the cell's voltage is the energy cost.

The comparison with the hydrogen peroxide route is useful. A peroxide route has water as its co-product, and a mediated route with electricity has none.

The separation is the downstream. A product that is the only organic in the stream needs less distillation, and the separation's cost is part of the process's advantage.

The safety is the chlorine's handling. A corrosive oxidant at scale is a hazard that the design must contain, and the containment is a capital cost.
The oxidant's choice is the process's constraint. A reagent that is cheap and regenerable is one that the economics can tolerate, and chlorine is cheap because it is made electrochemically.
The reactor's materials are the engineering. A chlorinated stream at temperature attacks stainless steel, and a lined reactor or a titanium one is the cost that the process must carry.
