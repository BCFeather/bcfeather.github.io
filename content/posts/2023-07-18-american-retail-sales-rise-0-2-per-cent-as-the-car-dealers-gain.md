American retail sales rose by 0.2 per cent in June, below the forecast of 0.5, and the May figure was revised up to a gain of 0.5. The measure that excludes the cars, the petrol, the building materials and the food services rose by about 0.6, which is the one the economists use for the consumption in the growth numbers. The figure was the third consecutive month of the gain, and the year-on-year growth was about 1.6 per cent. The revisions to May and April added about 0.3 percentage points to the level of the sales.

Cars did the work. The sales at the motor vehicle dealers rose by about 1.6 per cent as the incentives returned and the inventories improved. The dealerships have been the strongest part of the retail for three months, and the prices have been flat as the supply normalized. The average price of the new vehicle was about 48,000 dollars, which is about the same as a year ago after the two years of the gains. The inventory of the cars on the lots was about 1.8 million, up from about 1 million at the worst of the shortage.

Petrol stations fell. The sales dropped by about 1.4 per cent as the prices at the pump fell by about 1 per cent, and the volume was about flat. The spending at the grocery stores rose by about 0.4, and the one at the restaurants by about 0.9. The petrol was about 3.50 dollars a gallon at the end of June, down about 25 per cent from the level of a year ago.

Online sales were flat. The non-store retailers, which are mostly the e-commerce, rose by about 0.1 after the strong spring, and the department stores fell by about 1.4. The furniture rose by about 1.4 as the housing completions picked up. The sales at the building material stores rose by about 0.4, and the one at the garden centres by about 1.2.

One subgroup is the signal. A rise of 0.6 per cent is about the pace of the income growth, which suggests the households are spending what they earn and not more. The saving rate has fallen to about 3.9 per cent, which is well below the level of 2019. The real spending has grown by about 1.5 per cent over the year, and the nominal by about 4.

This report is the one that keeps the growth forecast alive. A consumer that spends 0.6 per cent more on the control group is a consumer that has not stopped, and the growth of about 2 per cent in the quarter rests on it. The economists expect the consumption to grow at about a 1.5 per cent pace in the third quarter, which is slower than the spring.
