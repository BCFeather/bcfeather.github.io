Ground-floor objections are concrete. A lift shaft built onto the front of the building takes the light from two windows and the space where bicycles are parked. The owners there would pay a share of the cost for a service they do not need, and the compensation they have asked for is a reduction in their share, which the other floors resist because the total cost does not change. A compromise that has worked elsewhere is a payment from the upper floors to the ground floor, structured as a one-off rather than as a discount.

Money is the second problem. A shaft, a machine room, a car and the electrical work come to something between 400,000 and 700,000 yuan depending on the design, and the subsidy from the district covers perhaps a third. The rest is apportioned by floor, with a formula that gives the sixth floor the largest share and the second floor the smallest. The survey alone costs several thousand yuan, and it is paid before anyone knows whether the project will proceed.
