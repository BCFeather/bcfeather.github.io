An infant under six months cannot be vaccinated, and the only antibody that child has is the antibody that crossed the placenta. Whether that transfer is enough to prevent hospitalization is a question that a test-negative design can answer. A test-negative design compares the vaccination status of infants who test positive with that of infants who test negative for the same reason, and it therefore controls for the propensity to be tested.

Enrolment covered 537 infants hospitalized with COVID and 512 controls without it at 30 hospitals in 22 states between July 2021 and March 2022, and compared the odds of full maternal vaccination during pregnancy. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 22 June 2022. Thirty hospitals in 22 states is a sample that covers both the Delta and the Omicron periods, and the split between them is the comparison that the paper was designed to make.

Effectiveness against hospitalization was 52 per cent overall, with an interval from 33 to 65, 80 per cent during the Delta period and 38 per cent during the Omicron period. Among the case infants, 21 per cent received intensive care, and two died, neither of whose mothers had been vaccinated. Two deaths in a cohort of 537 cases is a small number and a meaningful one, and the fact that neither mother had been vaccinated is the kind of detail that a case series contributes.
