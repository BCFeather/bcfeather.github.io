Target published results for the quarter to April on 18 May, and revenue rose 4 per cent to 25.2 billion dollars. Net income fell 52 per cent to 1 billion, and earnings per share were 2.19 against the 3.07 forecast. The shares fell 25 per cent, which was the largest one-day fall since 1987. Its gross margin was 25.7 per cent, and it had been 30 a year earlier.

Inventory was the reason. Stock rose 43 per cent, and the company said most of the increase was in bulky goods such as furniture, televisions and outdoor equipment. It had ordered those goods in 2021 when demand was strong, and the demand shifted to travel and to services in 2022. The company said it would cut prices and cancel orders. Bulky goods such as furniture and televisions accounted for most of the increase.

Sales of clothing and of home goods fell, and sales of food and of essentials rose. The margin fell to 25.7 per cent from 30. The company said it would have to discount 1 billion dollars of stock, and that freight costs had risen 1 billion. It cut its operating margin guidance to 6 per cent from 8, and it withdrew its forecast for the year. The mix had shifted. The company said its customers were spending on travel and on restaurants, and that they were trading down within its stores. Its digital sales grew 3 per cent, which was slower than in 2021. Its food and essentials business grew 10 per cent, and its apparel fell 10.

Supply chain investment had cost 4 billion dollars over two years, and it said those costs would fall in 2023. It also said it would hold more inventory of essentials and less of discretionary goods, and that it had learned from the quarter.

Its chief executive said the company had not seen the shift coming. He said the pandemic had trained the company to order early, and that the habit had left it with the wrong stock. The chairman said the company would be more flexible, and that it would use its stores as warehouses. The shares had risen 10 per cent in the year before the results, and they fell 25 per cent after them. The company's value was 60 billion dollars, down from 100 billion in 2021. It cut its capital spending plan by 500 million and suspended buybacks. He said the company would cut 500 million dollars of costs in the year.

Profit recovered in the second half. It cleared the excess stock by June, and its margin rose to 6 per cent in the third quarter. Its shares ended the year 40 per cent lower, and its revenue for the year was 109 billion dollars, up 2.8 per cent. It reported a 40 per cent fall in profit for the year.
