NVIDIA published results for the quarter to April on 25 May, and revenue rose 46 per cent to 8.29 billion dollars. The figure was a record and it was above the 8.1 billion forecast. Data centre revenue was 3.75 billion, up 83 per cent, and gaming was 3.62 billion, up 31. Net income was 1.6 billion. Its gross margin was 65.5 per cent, and it had been 66.2 a year earlier.

Guidance was the problem. The company said revenue for the July quarter would be 8.1 billion dollars, and analysts had expected 8.4. It said Russia and China would take 500 million dollars off the quarter, and that it had stopped selling to Russia. It also said it would take a charge of 140 million for inventory. It said the charge of 140 million was for inventory it had bought for Russia.

Gaming had been the strongest part of the business for two years. The company said demand for its cards was still above supply, and that it had sold 3.5 million units in the quarter. It said the lockdowns in China had reduced sales by 10 per cent, and that the crypto mining demand had begun to fall. Its automotive business was 138 million dollars, and it had doubled in a year. Its share of the gaming market was 80 per cent, and it had raised prices twice in the year.

Data centre had become the largest business. Its growth came from the cloud providers and from enterprises that were building artificial intelligence systems, and the company said its share of the market was 80 per cent. It said it had 30,000 customers for its artificial intelligence software. The margin was 65.5 per cent, and it had been 66.2 a year earlier. The company said the cost of components had risen, and that it had paid more for wafers. It said it expected the margin to recover in the second half, and it did not.

Analysts said the inventory charge and the Russia exit were the reasons, and that the gaming business had peaked. The company had announced a four-for-one stock split, and it took effect in July. It also said it would buy back 15 billion dollars of stock, and it raised its dividend by 4 cents. Its cash flow was 2.4 billion dollars in the quarter. The shares fell 5 per cent after the results and 50 per cent in the year. The company's value was 400 billion dollars, down from 800 billion in 2021. It held 21 billion dollars of cash, and its debt was 11 billion.

Gaming's decline was confirmed in August. The company reported a 33 per cent fall in gaming revenue and a 19 per cent fall in total revenue, and it said it had cut prices. Its data centre business kept growing, and it reached 15 billion dollars in the year to January 2023.
