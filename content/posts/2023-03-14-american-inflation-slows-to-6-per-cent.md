Food at home rose 10.2 per cent over the year, the first double-digit reading since 1979. The Labour Department said on 14 March that consumer prices rose 6.0 per cent in the year to February. The figure was in line with the forecast and down from 6.4 per cent in January. The monthly increase was 0.4 per cent, slower than the 0.5 of the previous month. It was the eighth consecutive decline from the peak of 9.1 per cent in June. The index is based on a basket of goods and services that is revised every two years.

Shelter remained the largest contributor, rising 0.8 per cent on the month and 8.1 per cent over the year. Rent and owners' equivalent rent both rose, and the annual rate for that category is now higher than at any point since 1982. Because leases turn over slowly, the shelter index lags market rents by about nine months, and the market rents had been falling since the summer. New leases had been falling since the autumn, according to the private rent indices.

Energy fell 0.6 per cent over the month and 1.9 per cent over the year, and gasoline was 1 per cent cheaper than in January. Food rose 0.4 per cent, the smallest monthly increase since 2021, and eggs fell 6.7 per cent after peaking in January. Used cars fell 2.1 per cent, and apparel rose 1.1 per cent. Electricity rose 12.9 per cent over the year, and natural gas rose 5 per cent. Core inflation was 5.5 per cent over the year and 0.5 per cent on the month, slightly above the forecast. Services excluding shelter rose 0.5 per cent, and the annual rate for that category stayed above 6 per cent. Goods prices were flat, and the disinflation that had driven the headline lower since the summer had stalled. Core services excluding shelter was the category officials said they watched most closely.

Yields fell on the day, because the banking stress was expected to do some of the tightening. The report was published between the collapse of two banks, and the market read it differently from the way it would have a week earlier. Futures priced a quarter-point increase at the March meeting with a probability of 60 per cent, down from 70. Two banks had failed in the previous three days, and the deposit insurer had taken over both.

Officials have said they watch both, and the gap between them has been about a point for a year. The central bank's preferred measure, the personal consumption index, was published at the end of the month and showed 5 per cent. That measure weights shelter less than the consumer price index, which is why it has been lower through the cycle. The personal consumption index had been below the consumer price index for eight months.

Whether that happens depends on the rental market, which had been cooling since the autumn as new leases were signed at lower rates. The shelter component was expected to turn down in the summer, and forecasts for the June reading ranged from 3.5 to 4.5 per cent. The next release was due on 12 April. Shelter is weighted at about a third of the index, which is why it dominates the annual rate.
