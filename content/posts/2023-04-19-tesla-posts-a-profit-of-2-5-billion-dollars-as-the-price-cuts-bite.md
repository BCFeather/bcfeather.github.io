Tesla reported a revenue of 23.3 billion dollars for the first quarter, up 24 per cent, and a profit of 2.5 billion, which was 24 per cent lower than a year earlier. The earnings per share of 0.85 dollars missed the forecast of 0.86. The shares fell about 6 per cent after the close. The revenue was about 1 billion below the estimate, and the energy unit was the only line that beat. The free cash flow fell to about 0.4 billion from 2.2, and the cash balance was about 22 billion.

Margins did the damage. The automotive gross margin fell to about 19.3 per cent from 29.1 a year earlier, as the company cut the prices in January and again in April. The average selling price fell by about 12 per cent, and the cost of the materials did not fall as fast. The operating margin fell to about 11.4 per cent from 19.2, and the cost per vehicle fell by about 3 per cent. The battery costs rose as the price of the lithium stayed high, and the company said the new contracts would lower them in the second half.

Deliveries were 422,875, a record for the quarter. The company said the production was about 440,000, and the difference is the stock in transit to the customers in Europe and Asia. The demand has held in China, and the price cuts there were the deepest. The Model Y was the best-selling vehicle in the world in the quarter, and the company said the order backlog was the highest since the summer. The inventory rose to about 15 days of the sales, and the company said the stock in transit to the export markets explains most of it.

Energy storage grew. The storage deployments reached 3.9 gigawatt hours, and the revenue from the unit rose by about 148 per cent. The solar installations fell, and the company said the margin on the storage was the best in the group. The gross margin on the storage was about 11 per cent, and the deployments are expected to grow faster than the cars this year. The solar installations fell to about 67 megawatts, and the company said the business was being folded into the storage unit.

Capital spending rose to about 2.5 billion for the quarter. The company has been building the factories in Texas and Germany, and the next model is the pickup that is due later this year. The finance chief said the margin would stay above 20 per cent through the year. The company said the factory in Mexico would be announced later in the year, and the next model is expected to cost about half of the Model 3. The production of the pickup has started in Texas, and the first deliveries are due in the third quarter.

This quarter is the one the investors have been waiting for. A company that cuts the prices by 12 per cent and still earns 2.5 billion dollars is a company that can defend the volume, and the volume is the strategy. The shares have fallen about 50 per cent since the peak of 2021, and the debate is whether the cuts are a strategy or a retreat. The market has been raising the estimates for the deliveries of the year, and the price is the part that the analysts cannot model.
