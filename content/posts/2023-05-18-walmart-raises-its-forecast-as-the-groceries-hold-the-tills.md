description: "The first quarter beat the estimates on the profit, with the grocery sales strong and the general merchandise weaker as the shoppers traded down."
---

Walmart reported a revenue of 152.3 billion dollars for the quarter to April, up 7.6 per cent, and a profit of 1.67 billion. The earnings per share of 1.47 dollars beat the forecast of 1.32. The shares rose about 1 per cent after the report, and the company raised the forecast for the year. The revenue was about 1 billion above the estimate, and the operating income rose by about 7 per cent. The company said the inflation in the food had moderated, and the prices in the aisles were about 5 per cent higher than a year ago.

Groceries did the work on the sales. The comparable sales in the United States rose by about 3.8 per cent, and the grocery was the largest contributor as the shoppers bought more of the food at home. The fresh produce and the private label grew faster than the national brands, which is the sign of the trading down. The grocery now accounts for about 60 per cent of the American sales, and the company said the share had risen through the quarter. The average basket grew by about 2 per cent, and the number of the transactions by about 1.5 as the traffic held.

General merchandise was weaker. The apparel and the home goods fell by the high single digits, and the company said the customers were delaying the discretionary purchases. The electronics were the weakest line, and the toys and the seasonal goods were cleared at the discounts. The company said the inventory was about 2 per cent lower than a year ago, which is the first decline since 2021.

E-commerce grew by about 27 per cent. The pickup and the delivery have become the default for the grocery, and the company said the advertising business had grown by about 30 per cent. The marketplace of the third-party sellers was the part that the margins come from. The advertising business earned about 0.6 billion dollars in the quarter, and the company said the margins were about three times the retail ones.

Its international segment grew by about 12 per cent. The sales in China rose by about 25 per cent on the constant currency, and the one in Mexico by about 10. The Flipkart in India was the largest contributor to the growth, and the company said the market was the one it would invest in. The international sales were about 28 billion dollars, and the company said the currency effects had subtracted about 3 percentage points.

Its forecast for the year was raised. A retailer that grows the grocery while the apparel falls is a retailer that is winning the traffic, and the traffic is the part the margins follow. The guidance for the earnings per share was raised to between 6.10 and 6.20 dollars from 5.90 and 6.05.
