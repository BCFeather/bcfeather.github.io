Numbers published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on 12 March showed a headline rate that had fallen from 3.0 per cent in January, and a monthly increase that had slowed from 0.5 per cent to 0.2. Forecasters had expected 0.3 per cent for the month and 2.9 per cent for the year, so the report beat expectations on both measures and gave the equity market a reason to rally.

Energy did the work. Petrol prices fell during the month, and the energy index declined after a January increase of 1.1 per cent, which removed the component that had driven the previous report. Shelter rose 0.3 per cent, roughly half the January pace, and it accounted for nearly half of the monthly increase, which is the pattern that has held for two years.

Food was the exception, and eggs were the extreme. The egg index rose 10.4 per cent in the month and 58.8 per cent over the year, a consequence of an outbreak of avian influenza that removed millions of laying hens from production. A carton that cost two dollars in 2023 cost more than six in some markets, and the shortage had become a political topic before it became a statistical one.

Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.2 per cent in the month and 3.1 per cent over the year, the smallest annual increase in almost four years. A core rate that is still above 3 per cent, with an unemployment rate near 4 per cent, describes an economy in which demand is strong enough that service prices keep rising at a pace the committee does not like.

The reaction was relief rather than celebration, because the report described February and the tariff measures had arrived in March. Importers had been building inventory ahead of the duties, and the question that the next two reports would answer is how much of the tariff cost reaches the consumer and how quickly. A goods price that rises in April is a goods price that the February index cannot see.

Markets moved accordingly. Equity indices recovered the losses of the previous week, the two-year Treasury yield fell, and the probability of a cut at the March meeting stayed near zero, which is where it had been since January and where it would stay through the spring. A report that beats expectations without changing the policy path is a report that removes a tail risk without opening a door.

What the year would show is that the February improvement did not last. Inflation stayed near 3 per cent through the spring, the tariff measures added to goods prices, and the committee held rates at every meeting after January. The February report is the last clean reading before the trade policy arrived, and that is why it is worth remembering.
