Saudi Arabia said on 5 September that it would extend a voluntary cut of a million barrels a day until the end of December, and Russia said it would keep its own export reductions in place. Brent crude rose above 90 dollars a barrel for the first time since November. The voluntary cut was first announced in July and has been rolled over month by month, and the extension to December removes the uncertainty that had hung over the autumn. The ministry said the cut would be reviewed monthly and could be extended again, which is the language it has used since July to keep the market guessing.

Cutting output is a policy of price rather than of volume. The kingdom needs a price above 80 dollars to balance its budget, and it has been producing about nine million barrels a day, the lowest in years. A producer that sells less to earn more is a producer that has made a choice. The budget requires about 80 dollars a barrel, and the finance ministry has already projected a deficit this year for the first time in a decade.

Traders had expected the extension. The analysts had written it into their forecasts, and the rise on the day was modest, which suggests the decision was priced. A decision that is expected is a decision that moves the price less. The rise of about 30 per cent since June has lifted the price to a level that the producers say covers their investment, and the volatility has fallen with it. The forward curves for the delivery in six months are trading below the spot price, which the traders call backwardation and read as a sign that the shortage is now.

Demand is the other half. Chinese imports have been strong and the American driving season has ended, and the forecasters still expect the demand to grow by about two million barrels a day this year. A cut that meets a growing demand is a balance that tightens from both ends. The inventories in the rich economies are about five per cent below their five-year average, which is the buffer that the cuts have removed.

Politics of the price is delicate. American petrol prices have been rising, and the White House has said it is watching, and the kingdom has been keen to keep the relationship smooth. A price that helps the producer is a price that hurts the consumer. The administration has released a quarter of its strategic reserve this year, and the reserve is at the lowest level since 1983.

Another extension shows a group that has chosen to defend the price rather than the market share, and the share will be the question when demand turns. The share will be tested when the demand peaks, and the producers have said they will restore the barrels gradually rather than all at once. A barrel at 90 dollars is a barrel that pays for the budget, and the budget is what the extension is meant to protect.
