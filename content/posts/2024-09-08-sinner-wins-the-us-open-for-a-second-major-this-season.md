Jannik Sinner won the US Open on 8 September, beating Taylor Fritz in straight sets in the final, and took his second major title of the year four months after winning the Australian Open. He is the first Italian man to win the tournament. The final was his second of the year in a major, and the first that he had reached without a five-set match in the fortnight.

Match was decided by the return. Sinner broke serve early in each of the first two sets and held his own service games without facing a break point until the third, which is the pattern of a player who is stronger at the end of a rally than at the start. A final that is decided by length is a final that rewards fitness. His second serve was the statistic that decided the match, because he won more than half of the points that began with it, which is a number that most players do not reach.

Crowd was against him and it did not matter. A home player in a home final brings an audience that cheers every point of his, and Sinner's response was to slow the pace between points and to play the big points with the safer pattern. A player who wins in that noise is a player who has learned to treat the crowd as weather. He said afterwards that he had tried to keep the rallies long, because a longer rally gives the better mover the advantage, and the crowd's noise does not last that long.

Season has a complication. A doping case involving a prohibited substance was disclosed in August and an independent tribunal accepted his explanation that the substance entered through a massage, a decision that some players have criticized. A title won in that atmosphere is a title that will be discussed for longer than most. The case has been handled by an independent tribunal rather than by the tour, and the tour has said that it will not comment on a decision it did not make.

Rankings are now his. He leads the tour by a margin that would survive a missed tournament, and the player who had dominated the previous two years has played a reduced schedule. A ranking that changes hands is a ranking that reflects a change in who is available. The player who had held the top ranking for most of the past two years has played fewer tournaments because of injury and has said he will return in the new year.

What the win shows is that a season can produce a dominant player without producing a dominant personality. Sinner's game is quiet and repetitive, and the repetition is the thing that wins five-set matches in the second week of a Grand Slam. The season ends with two majors for two players and a third who won the other two, which is a distribution that the sport has not seen for two decades.
