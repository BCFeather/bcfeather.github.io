Prices for American consumers rose 6.5 per cent in the year to December, down from 7.1 per cent in November and the sixth straight month of slower annual inflation. The monthly figure was a fall of 0.1 per cent, the first decline since the spring of 2020, when the first lockdowns pushed petrol and travel prices down. Food prices rose 10.4 per cent over the year, the largest increase since the 1970s, though they slowed on the month.

Energy did most of the work. Petrol was 1.5 per cent cheaper than a year earlier, and fuel oil and electricity both fell on the month as global crude prices came down from their summer peak. Used cars and trucks dropped for a sixth month, and apparel and furniture also eased as retailers worked through stock built up during the shortages. Airfares fell 8 per cent in December, and hotel rooms were 1.5 per cent cheaper than a year before.

Shelter remains the stubborn part. Rents and owners' equivalent rent rose 0.8 per cent on the month and 7.5 per cent over the year, and because the measure lags actual leases by six to twelve months, it is expected to keep headline inflation elevated well into the spring. Services excluding housing, watched closely because it tracks wages, rose 0.3 per cent. Medical care services, which had lagged the rest of the index, began to move up in the autumn and rose 0.1 per cent in December.

Markets read the report as evidence that the tightening cycle was near its end. Treasury yields fell, the dollar weakened against the euro and the yen, and futures priced a quarter-point rise at the February meeting with a pause after that. Core inflation at 5.7 per cent was still more than double the central bank target, which kept officials cautious in public. Two regional bank surveys published the same week showed firms expecting to raise prices by about 3 per cent over the coming year.

Wages give the other side of the argument. Average hourly earnings rose 4.6 per cent over the year, and the ratio of vacancies to unemployed workers, though down from its peak, stayed above one for a nineteenth month. Firms that had raised prices to cover pay were not yet reporting that the pressure had gone. Quits fell for a fifth month, and the share of firms reporting difficulty hiring eased from its 2022 peak.

Fed officials had lifted the benchmark rate by 425 basis points across 2022, to a range of 4.25 to 4.5 per cent, the fastest run since the early 1980s. December was the first month in which the goods side of the index behaved like a normal year and the services side did not, and the gap between them is the number that matters for the spring. Officials have said the terminal rate would probably stay above 5 per cent through the year, higher than the market had priced in December.
