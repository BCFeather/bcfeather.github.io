description: "Amazon introduced Alexa+ on 26 February, a generative assistant that can hold a conversation, act on services and run on the company's models, priced at 19.99 dollars a month and free for Prime members."
---

Original Alexa was a voice interface to a set of skills, and each skill was a script that a developer wrote for a specific request. That design produced a device that could set a timer and play a song and could not follow a conversation, and the company has spent several years saying that the next version would change that.

Alexa+ is the version that changes it. The assistant runs on a large language model, it can take several steps to a goal, and it can call the services that a household uses, which in the demonstration included booking a table, ordering a car and reading a security camera feed to see whether the dog had been walked. It also lives outside the speaker, on a website and in a phone app, where a user can upload a document and ask for a summary.

Price is 19.99 dollars a month, and Prime members get it without paying extra. The company framed that as a benefit of the membership rather than as a price cut, and the arithmetic is the one that Amazon has used before: a service that is free for a Prime member makes the membership more valuable, and the membership is the subscription that the retail business depends on.

Access starts in the United States in April, in English only, and the first group is the owners of the newest Echo Show devices. That staged rollout is the pattern that the company used for the first Alexa, and it is the pattern that a service with a large model behind it needs, because the cost of an inference is real and the company has to learn what people ask before it commits to a general release.

Capability that the demonstrations emphasized is memory. The assistant remembers what a household told it, which is the feature that makes a conversation feel like one, and it is the feature that carries the privacy question. A device that remembers a family's preferences is a device that holds a record of them, and the company's answer is that the record sits in an account and can be deleted.

Competition is the context. The assistants that other companies sell are free at the point of use and are paid for by advertising or by a subscription to a bundle, and Amazon is asking a household to pay a monthly fee for a device that it already bought. The company's argument is that the fee buys a service that the others do not provide, which is the control of a home and the purchase of things.

What the launch tests is whether a household will pay for a conversation. The first Alexa was free because it sold hardware; the second one is a subscription because the model costs money to run, and the answer will decide whether the assistant market is a hardware market or a services market.
