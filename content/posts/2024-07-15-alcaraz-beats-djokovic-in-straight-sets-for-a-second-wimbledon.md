Carlos Alcaraz beat Novak Djokovic in the Wimbledon final on 14 July, 6-2, 6-2, 7-6, in a match that lasted a little over two hours and never looked like a contest. The victory was his second at the tournament and his fourth at a major, at an age when most players are still learning the surface. A player who has won four majors before his twenty-second birthday has won them on three surfaces and in three different conditions. He has now won on clay, grass and hard court, which is the test that the great players of the previous era also passed.

The third set was the only passage in which the older player was ahead. Djokovic led in the tiebreak and lost it, and the points that decided it were the ones where he was drawn forward and passed. A match that is lost at the net is a match that a younger player has decided to win at the net. A tiebreak that turns on two passing shots is a tiebreak that a younger player wins because he is willing to run for them.

Comparison with the previous generation is the theme of the season. Two players born in the 1980s had won almost every major for twenty years, and the men who are beating them now are a decade younger and play a different game. A change of guard that happens in public is a change that arrives with scores rather than with announcements. A decade of dominance by two men is a decade in which an entire generation of players never won a major.

The grass is the surface that rewards the change. A surface that is faster than clay and lower than hard court is a surface where a serve and a forehand decide more points than a defensive rally, and the new generation serves bigger. A tournament that was once a baseline contest is now a serving contest. A serve that is unreturnable is a shot that shortens the rally, and short rallies favour the player with the bigger weapon.

Tour's economics are the backdrop. A sport that depends on three names for its television audience is a sport that has to sell a new set of names, and the tours have begun to schedule events around the players who can fill a stadium. A transition that the marketing department did not plan is a transition that it now has to endorse. A tour that schedules its biggest events around a few players is a tour that takes a commercial risk every time one of them retires.

What the final shows is that dominance is now shared. A player who wins four majors by twenty-one is a player who will be measured against the two he replaced, and the measurement will be made over a decade rather than a season. A decade is the right measure for a career and the wrong one for a rivalry that has just begun.
