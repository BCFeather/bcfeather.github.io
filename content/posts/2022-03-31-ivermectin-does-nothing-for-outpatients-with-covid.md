---

Ivermectin became a political object before it became an empirical one. Laboratory studies showed antiviral activity at concentrations far above those achieved in plasma, small trials reported benefits, and meta-analyses that pooled them produced a positive result that regulators declined to act on. The drug is cheap, widely available and off-patent, and those properties made it attractive in countries that could not buy antivirals and in movements that distrusted the agencies recommending them.

A platform trial in Brazil tested the drug properly. Symptomatic adults with a confirmed infection, up to seven days of symptoms and at least one risk factor were randomized to ivermectin at 400 micrograms per kilogram daily for three days or placebo, across 12 public health clinics. The paper appeared in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, 30 March 2022.

Of 3,515 randomized patients, 679 received ivermectin and 679 placebo, with the rest assigned to other interventions in the platform. The primary composite outcome was hospitalization within 28 days or an emergency visit for worsening illness that required more than six hours of observation. A platform trial randomizes within a single protocol and can add or drop arms, which is the design that a pandemic requires when the standard of care changes every few months.

That outcome occurred in 100 patients in the ivermectin group, or 14.7 per cent, and in 111 in the placebo group, or 16.3 per cent, a relative risk of 0.90 with a Bayesian credible interval from 0.70 to 1.16. Of the 211 events, 171 were hospital admissions.

An interval that crosses one, so the trial did not show a benefit, and the estimate is precise enough to exclude the large effects that the earlier meta-analyses had reported. A drug that reduced hospitalization by half would have produced a visible separation in a trial of this size. The Bayesian framework was chosen in advance, and a credible interval that includes one is a statement that the data are compatible with no effect.

This population was unvaccinated and enrolled in 2020 and 2021, which is the group and the period in which an antiviral would have had the most scope to work. If the drug had activity, this is the trial design in which it should have appeared. A trial of 1,358 patients is small by the standards of cardiovascular medicine and large by the standards of the ivermectin literature.

Its lesson is about how evidence accumulates. Small trials with subjective endpoints and heterogeneous dosing produced a signal that dissolved when a single well-powered trial with a objective composite was run, and the policy that had been built on the signal took months to unwind. A trial of this size also excludes a moderate benefit, which is the claim that the earlier meta-analyses had made, and the difference between the two literatures is the difference between pooling small studies and running a large one.
