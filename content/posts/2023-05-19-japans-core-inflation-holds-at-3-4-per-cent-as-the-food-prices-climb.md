Japan's core consumer prices, which exclude the fresh food, rose by 3.4 per cent over the year to April, up from 3.1 per cent in March. The headline measure rose by 3.5 per cent, and the measure that also strips the energy rose by 4.1, which is the fastest since 1981. The figure was in line with the forecast, and it is the sixteenth month above the 2 per cent target. The measure for the whole country followed the Tokyo figure that had been published two weeks earlier, which had also shown the food leading.

Food did the work on the index. The prices of the food rose by about 9 per cent, and the one of the eggs by about 20 as the bird flu cut the supply. The restaurant prices rose by about 4, which is the line that the tighter labor market shows in. The rice prices rose by about 5 per cent, and the one of the vegetables fell by about 3 as the spring harvest arrived. The food inflation has been above 5 per cent for a year, and the government has been releasing the stock of the rice and the wheat.

Services were softer. The prices of the services rose by about 1.8 per cent, and the one of the transport by about 1.5 as the government subsidies held the rail fares. The bank has said the services are the part that has to rise for the inflation to be sustainable. The rents rose by about 2 per cent in the largest cities, and the one of the hotels by about 8 as the inbound travel recovered.

Energy was a drag on the index. The electricity fell by about 17 per cent over the year, and the gas by about 10, as the subsidies of the government were paid through the utilities. The effect is scheduled to fade in the autumn, when the support is reviewed. The petrol rose by about 1 per cent on the month as the crude rebounded, and the kerosene fell as the heating season ended.

Its currency was the background. The currency has fallen by about 10 per cent against the dollar since the start of the year, and the imported prices have been rising. The pass-through to the consumer prices has been slower than in the past, and the bank cites it as the reason the inflation is not higher. The import prices rose by about 4 per cent over the year, and the one of the food and the energy by about 8.

This report keeps the pressure on the bank. A core at 3.4 per cent with the services at 1.8 is the mix that the governor has described as the one he wants to see change, and the meeting in June is the next test. The governor has said the bank would look at the wages and the services before it moved, and the spring wage talks have delivered about 3.6 per cent at the large firms.
