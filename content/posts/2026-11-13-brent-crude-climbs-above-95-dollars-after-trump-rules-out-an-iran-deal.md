Brent crude climbed above 95 dollars a barrel on Thursday and shares on Wall Street closed lower, after the American president said his country no longer needed an agreement with Iran. Speaking at the White House, he argued that the conflict with Iran had lifted fuel costs and that he would take the same decisions again, a remark traders read as a signal that sanctions and military pressure will stay in place for now. It also removed the main reason investors had been willing to sell crude this month.

Brent gained 3.22 per cent to 95.75 dollars, while West Texas Intermediate rose 2.55 per cent to 88.74 dollars. European natural gas jumped 9.79 per cent to 95.14 euros a megawatt-hour, and American gas added 4.68 per cent to 3.37 dollars per million British thermal units. Gas markets in Europe react sharply to any supply hint once winter demand sets in.

Equity indices drifted lower rather than collapsing. Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 189.40 points, or 0.36 per cent, to 52,297.09. S&P 500 fell 0.35 per cent, or 26.98 points, to 7,592.40, Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.39 per cent to 25,366.09 and Nasdaq 100 gave up 0.30 per cent to 28,289.19. Small caps did worse, with Russell 2000 down 1.66 per cent at 2,799.06, and Cboe volatility index rose 7.26 per cent to 18.76.

Precious metals took the hardest knock. Spot gold dropped 2.78 per cent to 3,877.12 dollars an ounce, its lowest since October 2025, and silver fell 3.22 per cent to 50 dollars. Traders treated dearer energy and firm bond yields as a reason to hold fewer defensive assets, and the two moves reinforced each other through the session. Funds that had built defensive positions in bullion trimmed them as the dollar firmed against most Asian currencies.

Labour market data added to the mix. Initial claims for unemployment benefits rose to 200,000 in the week to 7 November, above the 195,000 economists had expected, a reminder that the jobs market is cooling only slowly. Technology shares stayed under pressure, and NVIDIA fell again as investors weighed the resignation of Jensen Huang as chief executive three days after the board ordered an independent review.

European shares finished mixed. Stoxx Europe 600 added 0.14 per cent to 664.01 and FTSE 100 rose 0.25 per cent to 10,863.09. Dearer crude feeds straight into headline inflation, and several strategists said the jump makes the December meeting of the Federal Reserve harder to read, with traders still debating whether policy tightens again before the year ends. Energy importers in Asia face the same arithmetic, and the move will show up in their trade balances within a month or two.
