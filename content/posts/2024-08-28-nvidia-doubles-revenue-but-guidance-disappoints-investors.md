---

NVIDIA reported revenue of 30.04 billion dollars for the quarter to the end of July, up 122 per cent from a year earlier and 15 per cent from the previous quarter, with the data-centre business contributing 26.3 billion. The shares fell after the release because the guidance for the next quarter, about 32.5 billion, was close to rather than above the most optimistic forecast. A year earlier the same quarter produced 13.5 billion, so the company added more revenue in twelve months than most chipmakers earn in a decade.

Concentration is the story. A company that earns most of its revenue from a handful of customers is a company whose growth depends on their capital budgets, and the buyers are building clusters that are measured in tens of thousands of accelerators. A customer list with four names is a customer list that can change a quarter's results with one decision. Four customers account for a large share of sales, and a cluster that costs a billion dollars is a purchase that one board can postpone.

Product transition is the risk that the market was pricing. The next generation of accelerators was being sampled to customers and was not yet in volume production, and a manufacturing problem would delay the revenue that the guidance assumes. A ramp that has slipped once is a ramp that investors discount. A delay in one rack-level system held back some shipments in the quarter, and the company said the problem had been resolved before the end.

Margin is the other number. Gross margin reached about 75 per cent, which is a level that no comparable semiconductor business has sustained, and it depends on pricing power that lasts only while supply is short. A shortage that ends is a margin that falls. The Chinese market has become a small share of revenue because of export controls, and the forecast assumes nothing from it.

Networking business is the part that is often ignored. A cluster is limited by the fabric that connects the accelerators, and the company sells the switches and the optical components as well as the chips, which means it captures the cost of the whole system. A supplier that sells the compute and the network is a supplier that sets the price of a cluster. Customers are designing their own accelerators, and a customer that designs its own chip is a customer that buys fewer of someone else's.

What the quarter shows is that the growth is real and the expectations have outrun it. A company that doubles its revenue and falls on the guidance is a company whose valuation depends on the next year rather than on the last quarter. The shares had doubled during the year before the release, and a stock that has doubled needs a surprise rather than a result. Analysts spent the call asking about supply rather than about demand, which is the question that a company in a shortage gets.
