A sequence database grows faster than a structure database, and the gap is the reason that most proteins are annotated by inference. A prediction that is accurate enough turns the gap into a census.

The census in *Nature*, 13 September 2023 builds it. The authors predict structures for a large fraction of the sequences in the major databases, cluster them, and report the families and the taxonomic distribution.

The scale is the achievement. Hundreds of millions of models is a computation measured in weeks of a large cluster, and the result is a resource that a single lab could not produce.

The clustering is the analysis. A space of models is reduced to a few hundred thousand clusters, and each cluster is a family with a representative structure.

The taxonomic distribution is the finding. A family's spread across the tree of life tells whether it is ancient or recent, and the authors' mapping is what makes the census a biological statement.

The unknown fraction is the honest part. A prediction's confidence falls with distance from a known structure, and the authors report the fraction that is reliable.

The comparison with the previous census is the frame. An earlier effort covered a smaller set with a less accurate method, and the growth in coverage is the paper's measure of progress.

The limitation is the model's bias. A network trained on known structures reproduces their biases, and a family that resembles nothing in the training set is the one most likely to be wrong.

What the paper does not provide is a function. A census of shapes is a scaffold for annotation, and the assignment is a separate effort.

For bioinformatics, the value is an infrastructure. The databases' annotation has been a bottleneck, and a structure for every sequence is a bottleneck removed.

The representative's choice is the clustering's detail. A cluster's centroid is a structure that stands for the family, and the choice of centroid affects the comparison.

The confidence is a per-residue quantity. A model's core is reliable and its loops are not, and the authors' reporting of the confidence map is what a user needs.

The taxonomic mapping is the biological value. A family that appears in bacteria and in eukaryotes is one that predates their split, and a family confined to one group is one that arose later.

The computation's cost is the barrier. A prediction for a long sequence takes minutes of a graphics processor, and the total is a resource that only a large institution can supply.

The database's growth is the maintenance problem. A census is a snapshot, and a resource that is not updated becomes a historical record.

The use in annotation is the immediate payoff. A new genome's proteins can be assigned to families by structure, and the assignment is faster than an experiment.
The family's size is a distribution. A cluster with many members is a common fold and one with few is a rarity, and the shape of the distribution is a biological result.
