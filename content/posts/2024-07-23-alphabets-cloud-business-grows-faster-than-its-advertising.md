Alphabet reported second-quarter revenue of about 85 billion dollars on 23 July, up 14 per cent from a year earlier, with the cloud division growing by nearly thirty per cent and the advertising business growing at a slower pace. The mix is the part that investors watched. A quarter that beats expectations while the mix shifts is a quarter that investors treat as a transition rather than a result.

Cloud is the smaller number and the faster grower. A business that sells computing and models to other companies is a business that benefits from the same demand that is lifting the chipmakers, and the customers are the ones building their own models. A supplier to the builders is a supplier whose revenue is a function of their capital spending. A customer that builds a model needs storage, networking and accelerators, and the supplier sells all three.

Search is the larger number and the political risk. A business that earns from advertising against a query is a business that a regulator can examine for how it arranges the answers, and the remedies being discussed include separating the distribution deals that make the default position. A default position is an asset that a court can take away. A regulator that is considering remedies is a regulator that has already decided the conduct is a problem. The remedies under discussion would end the payments that make the search engine the default on phones and browsers.

The capital spending is the tell. The company said it would spend more on data centres and chips in the second half, and the guidance was raised twice during the call. A company that raises capital spending while its largest business grows slowly is a company that is buying a position in the next platform. A raised guidance is a commitment that a finance director has to defend at the next call. The spending is on accelerators and on buildings, and the depreciation arrives before the revenue that the capacity is meant to serve.

The competition is now vertical. A search engine that is integrated with a model is a search engine that answers rather than links, and the companies that own the models are also the companies that own the assistants on the phones. A market that was about links is now about answers. An answer that is generated is an answer that removes the click that the advertising business sells.

What the quarter shows is that the advertising business funds a transition that it cannot lead. A company with a cash machine and a new business is a company that can afford to be patient, and patience is the advantage that a smaller competitor does not have. A cash machine that funds a transition is an advantage that lasts as long as the transition does. The search business still pays for the model research, the data centres and the lawyers, and that arrangement is what the remedies could end.
