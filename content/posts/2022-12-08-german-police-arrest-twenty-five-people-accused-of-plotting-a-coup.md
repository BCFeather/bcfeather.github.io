title: "German police arrest twenty-five people accused of plotting to storm the parliament"

description: "Raids across eleven states break up a group that wanted to install a prince as head of state, and the prosecutor calls it a coup."
---

Police and commandos raided homes across eleven states at dawn on Wednesday and arrested twenty-five people who the federal prosecutor says were part of a group planning to storm the parliament and take power. The group called itself the Patriotic Union, and its members include a man who describes himself as a prince of a former German dynasty, a former judge and former soldiers. Prosecutors said the plan involved an armed entry into the building in Berlin and the installation of a provisional government. One person was killed in a shootout in Bavaria, and two were arrested abroad.

Heinrich XIII, a seventy-one-year-old from the House of Reuss, is described by prosecutors as the leader, and he has no claim to any throne and had been trying to buy weapons through a network of contacts. The group is part of a movement that refuses to accept the legitimacy of the Federal Republic and calls it a company rather than a state. Several of its members had tried to obtain weapons permits and had been refused, which is what put them on the list of the domestic intelligence service. The service had been watching them for months, and the raids were timed for the morning.

About three thousand officers took part, and the searches went on through the day. Prosecutors said they had found rifles, ammunition, a stock of food, and documents describing a plan to form a government with ministries and a foreign policy. The group's ideas about foreign affairs were said to include contacts with Russia, and the prosecutor's statement mentioned a Russian woman among the detainees. Investigators described the preparation as serious and the capacity as limited.

The politics of it are delicate. The group is associated with the movement that protested against the pandemic rules, and with parts of the far right that had been active in the east of the country. Ministers said the arrests showed that the state could defend itself, and the justice minister warned against treating the affair as a joke. The interior minister said the network was larger than the arrests of one morning could suggest, and that the investigation would continue.

Parliament was guarded on Wednesday and held its scheduled session. The president cancelled an engagement and praised the security services, and the leaders of the parties in the coalition made statements that sounded strikingly alike. The far-right party in the parliament said it had no connection to the group and condemned the plot, which its opponents noted came after years of rhetoric about the illegitimacy of the state.

What the case shows is how far a shared refusal of the political order can travel. The people arrested belong to no militia with a training camp, and several of them were old enough to have been adults when the wall came down. The prosecutor's charge is that they formed a terrorist group, and the test in court will be whether a collection of grievances and a few rifles amounts to a plan. The interior minister's answer is that in a country with a history like this one, the state cannot wait to find out.
