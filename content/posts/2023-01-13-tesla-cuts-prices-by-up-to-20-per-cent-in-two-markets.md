title: "Tesla cuts prices by up to 20 per cent in two markets"
date: "2023-01-13T09:20:00+08:00"
categories: [business]
description: "The company reduced sticker prices on its two volume models in the United States and Europe, hours after reporting deliveries that fell short of its own growth target."
---

Tesla cut the prices of its two volume cars in the United States and several European markets on 13 January, with reductions of up to 20 per cent. The Model Y long-range version, which had cost 65,990 dollars in the middle of 2022, was listed at 52,990 dollars, and the base Model 3 fell to 43,990 dollars before any tax credit was applied. The reductions came in the same week as cuts in Europe, where the Model Y fell by about 10 per cent in Germany.

Timing followed the delivery report. The company had said it delivered 1.31 million vehicles in 2022, a record but short of the 50 per cent growth it had promised at the start of the year. The shortfall meant inventory, and the fall in Chinese sales in December meant discounts that had already been offered through insurance and financing rather than price. Deliveries in the quarter had fallen 12 per cent from the previous three months, the first sequential decline in two years.

Cutting prices contradicted the language used by the chief executive in the previous year, when he said demand was so far ahead of supply that prices could rise. It also reversed six rounds of increases during 2022, most of them in the first half, when the order book was long and raw material costs were climbing. The chief executive had said in October that demand was strong, and the cut is the clearest evidence that it was not.

Owners who had bought in December were unhappy. The resale value of a two-year-old Model Y fell within a week on used-car sites in several countries, and the company's own trade-in offers moved with the sticker, so people who had financed at the old price owed more than the car was worth. Used prices for the Model 3 fell 20 per cent over the quarter, and dealers began refusing them in part exchange.

Competition on the low end of the market shaped the decision. Chinese makers had pushed price cuts of their own through the autumn, and the American tax credit of 7,500 dollars, restored in January, applied to vehicles below a price cap that the Model Y had only just met before the reduction. The credit of 7,500 dollars applied only to vehicles under 55,000 dollars, a cap the Model Y had been above all year.

Margins were the question investors asked. Tesla had reported an operating margin near 17 per cent in the third quarter, the highest among large carmakers, and analysts estimated that a 20 per cent price cut on the Model Y removed most of the gross profit per vehicle at the old cost base. The shares fell 4 per cent over the following week while volume expectations rose. Gross margin fell to 19 per cent in the first quarter from 29 a year earlier, and the shares recovered only in the summer.
