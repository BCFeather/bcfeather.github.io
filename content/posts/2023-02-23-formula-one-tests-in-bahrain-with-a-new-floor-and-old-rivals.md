Testing ran from 23 to 25 February at the Sakhir circuit, and each team had three days and one car. The rules had changed again, with the floor edges raised by 15 millimetres to stop the bouncing that had plagued the previous season. Cars also carried a larger fuel allowance for the tests, which let teams run longer stints. The circuit is 5.4 kilometres and the tests were run in daylight, which is unusual because the race is at night.

Red Bull set the fastest time of the week, with Sergio Perez on the second day and Max Verstappen close behind. The team completed 400 laps across the three days, more than any other, and the long-run pace looked strong. Ferrari was second on the timesheets and had a more consistent car than the year before, according to its drivers. Verstappen's best lap was 0.3 seconds quicker than the Ferrari, and the times were set on different tyres. Red Bull's race pace was 0.4 seconds a lap better than anyone else on the long runs, which is the number that matters.

Mercedes arrived with a revised bodywork and admitted it had misunderstood the regulations in 2022. The car was faster in a straight line and slower in the corners, and the team said it would bring a new floor to the third race. Lewis Hamilton said the car was not yet good enough to win, which was the most quoted remark of the week. Mercedes brought a new front wing on the last day, and the team said it had found 0.2 seconds.

Midfield teams were closer together than in previous years. Aston Martin, with a new factory and a former champion as a driver, was within a tenth of the leaders on one lap. McLaren and Alfa Romeo were separated by two tenths, and the smallest gap between any two cars was 0.05 seconds. Aston Martin's improvement was the surprise of the winter, and its driver finished second in the first race.

Reliability was the issue for two teams. AlphaTauri lost a day to a gearbox problem, and Williams changed an engine overnight. The cost cap had made teams cautious about spare parts, and one team principal said the limit was the reason for shorter programmes rather than for slower cars. The cost cap was set at 135 million dollars for the season, and it had been reduced from 145. Three teams admitted they had exceeded the cap in 2021 and agreed to penalties in the autumn. Two teams missed the first day because their cars had not passed the crash tests.

Racing began at the same circuit a week later, and the results matched the test closely. Red Bull won the first race with a comfortable margin, and the field behind was tighter than it had been for a decade. The next test was scheduled for 2024, at the same venue, because the weather there is predictable. The first race was won from pole, and the fastest lap went to a car that finished seventh. Red Bull won 21 of the 22 races that season, and the title was settled in September.
