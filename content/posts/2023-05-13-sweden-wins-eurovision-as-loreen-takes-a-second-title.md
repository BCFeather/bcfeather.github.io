Sweden won the Eurovision Song Contest in Liverpool on 13 May, with Loreen taking 583 points for the song Tattoo. Finland finished second with 526 points, and Israel third with 362. The margin was the narrowest since the current voting system began in 2016. The singer had won the contest in 2012 with Euphoria, and the second title puts her among the few who have won twice. The show was held in the arena of the Liverpool football club, and the stage was built in about six weeks. The contest drew the largest audience since 2016 in the countries that broadcast it, and the online views of the winning performance passed 100 million in a week.

Juries did the work. Loreen led the jury vote with 340 points, and Finland was fourth with 150. The public vote went to Finland with 376 points, which brought the contest close. The combined result held the lead for Sweden by 57 points. The juries from the 37 countries voted on the night, and the public vote was weighted equally in the total. The voting system was changed in 2016 to separate the jury and the public scores, and the change has produced the closer results.

Staging was the argument. The staging used a moving platform and an LED box that rose above the singer, and the effect was the most discussed of the night. The song had been the favourite of the bookmakers since the national final, and the odds were about 1 to 2 on the night. The bookmakers had made Sweden the favourite after the semi-final, and the price shortened through the week. The Swedish broadcaster had staged the show in a smaller arena in 2016, and the production this year was the largest the country has mounted.

Finland's entry was the crowd favourite. The song was a rap number with the choreography in green, and the arena sang along through the performance. The Finnish broadcaster said the result was the best in the country's history. The Finnish entry finished first in the public vote in about 18 countries, and the singer became the most searched act of the night.

Liverpool hosted the contest because the winner of 2022 could not. The British city spent about 20 million pounds on the production, and the tickets sold out in minutes. About 160 million people watched the final across the continent. The organizers said the security costs had doubled since the contest in Turin, and the budget was covered by the broadcaster and the city.

A seventh win ties Sweden with Ireland for the most. A contest that is decided by 57 points is a contest where the staging and the diaspora vote both matter, and the margin is the part the losing countries will argue about. The winner hosts the contest the next year, and the Swedish broadcaster has said the event will be held in Stockholm or in Gothenburg.
