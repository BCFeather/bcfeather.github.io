Max Verstappen won the Monaco Grand Prix on 28 May, starting from the pole and leading every lap of the race. Fernando Alonso finished second for Aston Martin, about 28 seconds behind, and Esteban Ocon took third for Alpine. The win was the fourth of the season for the champion. The team had brought a new floor to the car, and the upgrade was the part the rivals had feared. The margin over the second place in the qualifying was about a tenth of a second, which is the largest at Monaco this year.

Qualifying decided the race. Verstappen took the pole with a lap about 0.1 seconds faster than Alonso, and the narrow streets of the principality do not allow the overtaking that the gap would suggest. The grid position is the race at Monaco, and the teams know it. Alonso had been quickest in the second practice, and the Aston Martin has been the surprise of the season. The pole at Monaco has produced the winner in about 70 per cent of the races since 2000.

Rain changed the plan. A shower fell about 20 minutes before the start, and the teams had to choose between the intermediates and the slicks. The leaders started on the intermediates, and the track dried quickly, which forced the early stops and the order changes in the midfield. The teams had about 10 minutes of the dry running before the shower, and the data was thin.

Strategy was the story. The leaders pitted for the slicks at about the same time, and the undercut did not work because the pit lane at Monaco costs about 20 seconds. The race settled into the procession that the circuit produces, and the gap at the front grew by about 0.5 seconds a lap. The pit lane at Monaco is the shortest in the calendar in the distance and the longest in the time, and the teams plan the stops around it. The team had told him to stay out for two more laps, and the call cost him about four seconds and the place behind Alonso.

Ferrari was the disappointment. Leclerc started third and finished sixth after the team called him in for the slicks a lap too early. The stewards gave him a five-second penalty for the unsafe release, and the home race ended in the midfield again. The stewards reviewed the incident after the race and gave the penalty, which dropped him from the fifth to the sixth. The stewards also investigated the incident at the first corner, and they decided that no further action was needed.

Both titles are now the champion's to lose. A driver who wins from the pole at Monaco has done the hard part on Saturday, and the season now has the Red Bull ahead in both titles. The champion leads the standings by about 40 points, and the next race is in Spain in June.
