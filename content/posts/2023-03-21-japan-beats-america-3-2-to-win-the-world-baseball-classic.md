Japan beat the United States 3-2, and the deciding runs came on a double by Munetaka Murakami in the fourth inning. The final was played on 21 March in Miami in front of 36,000 people. The team had won the tournament in 2006 and 2009 and had finished third in 2015. It was the first title for the manager in his second tournament. The manager had also led the team in 2009, and he retired from the role after the final.

America had won the 2017 edition and had the stronger lineup on paper. Its only runs came on a home run by Trea Turner in the second inning and a single in the eighth. It left eight runners on base and struck out 13 times, six of them against Japan's bullpen. The American lineup had hit 12 home runs in the tournament, and it hit one in the final.

Japan's starter pitched 4 and two-thirds innings and allowed one run. The bullpen allowed one run over the last four innings, and the closer struck out the side in the ninth. The team had lost one game in the tournament, to Mexico in the group stage, when the tie-break rules put it in second place. The closer had saved three games in the tournament, and he had not allowed a run.

Spectators numbered 1.3 million across the tournament, a record, and the television audience in Japan was the largest for a baseball game since 2015. The event had been expanded to 20 teams and played across three countries. The final was the most watched game in the competition's history in the United States, with 5.2 million viewers. The group stage was played in Tokyo, Taichung and Phoenix, and the final round in Miami.

Japan's league had released its players for the tournament with an agreement that covered insurance and playing time. The country's baseball federation had spent 4 million dollars on the programme over four years. The manager said the win would help the domestic league, which had lost audience to football over the previous decade. The domestic league had averaged 20,000 spectators per game, and the figure was falling.

America was missing several pitchers who had declined to take part, and its manager said the timing of the event, before the season, limited who was available. The competition was scheduled again for 2026, and the organisers said they would consider moving it later in the year. The next edition was set for March 2026, and the organisers said 20 teams would take part again. Japan returned home to a parade in Tokyo and a reception with the prime minister. The winning pitcher signed with a major league club in the following winter, and the double by Murakami was voted the play of the tournament. The next edition was set for March 2026 in Tokyo, Miami and San Juan.
