American employers added 339,000 jobs in May, far above the forecast of about 190,000 and the strongest month since January. The unemployment rate rose to 3.7 per cent from 3.4, and the participation held at 62.6. The two numbers moved in opposite directions, which is the pattern of a labour market that is drawing people in. The figure was the fourteenth consecutive month of the job growth above 200,000, which is the longest run since 1990. The unemployment rate has been between 3.4 and 3.7 per cent for seventeen months, which is the longest stretch at that level since the 1960s.

A different survey explains the rise in the rate. The number of the unemployed rose by about 250,000 as more people started looking for work, and the measure counts only the ones who are searching. The payroll survey and the household survey have diverged by about 2 million jobs over the year, and the gap is the part the economists argue about. The participation of the prime-age workers was about 83.4 per cent, the highest since 2008.

Hiring was broad across the sectors. The health care added about 52,000 jobs, the leisure about 42,000, and the professional services about 64,000. The construction added about 25,000, and the government about 47,000 as the local schools filled the vacancies. The temporary help fell for a fourth month. The retail added about 3,000 jobs after the losses of the spring, and the transport and the warehousing shed about 15,000.

Wage growth slowed. Average hourly earnings rose by 0.3 per cent on the month and by 4.3 per cent over the year, down from 4.4. The measure for the production workers was about 4.6, and the gap with the salaried staff has been narrowing since the winter. The wage growth for the leisure was about 5.5 per cent, and the one for the information sector about 2.5.

Revisions were small. The two previous months were cut by about 21,000, and the three-month average is about 283,000. The pace is about double the level the Fed considers consistent with the stable prices, and the committee has been watching it. The average weekly hours were about 34.3, which is slightly below the level of the winter.

Markets read the report as mixed. The traders cut the odds of a June increase to about 30 per cent before the meeting, and the two-year yield fell by about 10 basis points. A job market that adds 339,000 jobs while the wage growth slows is the soft landing that the Fed has been trying to engineer, and the engineering is not finished. The committee meets on 13 and 14 June, and the market expects a pause with the door left open for July.
