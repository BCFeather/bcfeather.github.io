Eurozone consumer prices rose 2.4 per cent in the year to November, the flash estimate said on 30 November, down from 2.9 per cent in October and below the forecast of 2.7. The figure was the lowest since the summer of 2021, and the measure has fallen in seven of the last nine months. The figure was the lowest since the summer of 2021, and the measure has fallen in six of the last eight months.

An energy base effect did most of it. The prices fell by about 12 per cent over the year, and the comparison is with the month when the gas contracts for the winter were signed. A base that passes is a headline that drops. The electricity prices are about 20 per cent below the level of last year, and the gas is about 35 below. The energy prices are now about a third below the peak of the autumn of last year, and the effect will fade out of the index by the spring.

Food cooled further. The food, alcohol and tobacco rose by about 6.5 per cent, down from 7.4, and the fresh vegetables have fallen for four months. A basket that stops rising is a shopper who notices. The prices of the oils and the cereals have fallen, and the one of the sugar is still about 40 per cent above last year. The food prices are about twice the target, and the analysts expect the line to keep falling as the input costs of the farmers ease.

Services were sticky. The prices rose by about 4 per cent, and the one on the holidays and the restaurants was above 5 as the wages rose. A service that holds is a wage that is still being passed on. The services measure has been above 4 per cent for a year, and the bank has said it needs it below 3.5 before it cuts. The wages have been rising by about 5 per cent under the agreements, and the bank expects the pace to slow next year.

Core inflation fell to 3.6 per cent from 4.2, and the economists said the fall was helped by the base of the package holidays, which was unusually high last year. A core that falls is a bank that can talk about the cuts. The figures come two weeks before the bank's next meeting, and the traders have priced the first cut for the spring. The core was helped by the base of the package holidays, and the economists said the underlying pace is now about 3 per cent.

What the month shows is that the headline is near the target and the services are the last mile, and the last mile is the one that the bank has said will be slow. A headline at 2.4 per cent is a headline that the bank cannot ignore, and the ignoring is over.
