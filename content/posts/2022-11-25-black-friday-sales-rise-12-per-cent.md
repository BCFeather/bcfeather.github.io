Adobe Analytics reported the figures on 25 November, and online spending on Black Friday reached 9.1 billion dollars. The figure was 12 per cent above the previous year and 2.8 per cent above 2019. The company tracks 80 per cent of online transactions in the United States, and it is the standard measure of the day. The figure was the highest for the day since the company began tracking it in 2014.

Discounts were the reason. The average discount was 25 per cent, up from 20 a year earlier, and the deepest were in electronics and in toys. Retailers had ordered too much stock in 2021 and were clearing it, and the discounts began in October rather than in November. The number of transactions rose 8 per cent, and the average basket fell 3 per cent. Consumers bought more items at lower prices, which is consistent with the shift towards essentials. Categories that grew were groceries, apparel and health products, and categories that fell were furniture and sporting goods. Electronics were discounted 30 per cent, and toys 35.

Retailers said the queues were shorter because more people used pickup. The physical shops were busier than expected. The National Retail Federation said 115 million people shopped in stores over the weekend, up 17 per cent. The traffic was helped by the weather, and by the fact that the holiday season had begun earlier. Groceries grew 20 per cent, and they were the category with the largest increase.

Online accounted for 78 per cent of sales in the week to Black Friday, according to one survey. That figure had been rising for a decade, and it accelerated during the pandemic. The share of sales made on a mobile phone was 45 per cent, and it had been 40 a year earlier. The federation surveyed 5,000 shoppers, and it said the average spend was 320 dollars.

Retailers extended the discounts into Cyber Monday, and Adobe reported 11.3 billion dollars of spending on that day. The promotions continued after the weekend. The total for the five days was 35 billion, up 8 per cent. The federation said the season would grow 6 to 8 per cent. Inventories had been the problem all year, and the discounts were the solution. Nike had reported inventory up 44 per cent in September, and Target and Walmart had both warned in the autumn. The discounts cleared the stock, and the margin pressure showed in the fourth quarter results. The share of sales made on a phone had risen every year since 2015.

Sales for the whole season ended at a record. The Census Bureau reported retail sales in November rose 0.6 per cent, and the holiday total was 960 billion dollars, up 7 per cent. The share of sales made online passed 20 per cent for the first time, and the federation said it would stay there.
