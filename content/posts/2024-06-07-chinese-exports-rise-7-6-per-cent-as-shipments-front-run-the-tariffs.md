Chinese exports rose 7.6 per cent in May from a year earlier, the strongest gain in a year, while imports rose 1.8 per cent. The trade surplus widened to 82.6 billion dollars. The figures were published on 7 June. The customs data are reported in dollars and the comparison is with a month in which the ports were still recovering, which adds to the apparent gain. The customs administration attributed the gain to the base and to the price of electronics, and it did not describe the orders as a rush.

Timing explains much of the gain. Importers in the United States and in Europe placed orders ahead of the duties that were announced in May, and the shipments that left the ports in June were booked in the spring. A shipment that is brought forward is a shipment that is taken from the next quarter rather than added to this one. The electronics and the electric vehicles were the categories that grew fastest, and both are the ones that the American duties now cover.

Destinations are the detail. Exports to South-East Asia grew faster than exports to the United States, and the analysts who follow the trade data say that part of that flow is goods that will be assembled and then shipped onward. A route that is lengthened is a route that the statistics record twice. The re-export route is the one that the tariff authorities have been investigating, and the inquiries have not yet produced a duty that closes it.

Imports are the weaker number. The demand for machinery and for components has not recovered with the exports, which suggests that the factories are using the capacity they already have rather than adding to it. An import figure that lags is a figure that says the investment has not arrived. The property sector is the reason investment is weak, and the builders that have stopped work are not ordering the equipment that the import figures would record.

Prices are falling at the same time. The export prices have declined for a year, and the volume gain is partly a gain of cheaper goods, which is the pattern that the trading partners describe as a subsidy. A price that falls while volume rises is a price that the importers notice. The producers have been cutting prices to hold market share abroad, and the margin that they give up is the cost of keeping the factories running.

What the month shows is that a tariff announced in advance produces a rush before the deadline, and the test of the policy will be the quarter after the front-running has ended. A trade figure that is flattered by timing is a figure that the next two months will contradict, and the contradiction is the part that matters for the year. The share of the exports that go to the countries with new duties is about a fifth, and the front-running was concentrated there.
