Eurostat published the final figures on 18 November, and consumer prices rose 10.6 per cent in the year to October. The flash estimate had been 10.7, and the revision was small. It was the highest rate in the series, and the sixth record in eight months. Energy rose 41.5 per cent, and the contribution was 4.4 points. Food rose 13.1, and services 5.6. Core inflation was 5 per cent, and it had risen every month since January. The statisticians said the revision was because of small changes in the German and Italian data. The revision was the first downward one in six months, and it came from the German states.

Germany reported 11.6 per cent, France 7.1, Italy 12.6 and Spain 7.3. The differences between countries were large. The Baltic states were above 20, and the Netherlands was 16.8. The commission said the spread reflected national support schemes and the weight of energy in each country's consumption basket. Energy contributed 4.4 points, and food 2.6.

Rates went up by 75 basis points on 27 October, and again in December. The deposit rate was 1.5 per cent in November, and the real rate was minus 9. The bank's president said in a speech that the bank would keep raising until the rate was in restrictive territory, and she said that point had not been reached.

Industrial production fell 1.9 per cent in September, and retail sales fell 1.8. The economy was contracting. The purchasing managers' index was 47.1 in November, which is below the 50 that separates growth from contraction. The commission forecast a recession of 0.5 per cent for 2023. Wages were rising. Negotiated pay rose 4.4 per cent in the third quarter, and the bank's tracker showed 5 per cent for the settlements signed in the autumn. The bank said unit labour costs were rising at 5 per cent, and it said that was the reason core inflation was not falling. The index had been above 50 for 15 months, and it fell below in July.

December brought new projections, with growth cut to 3.4 per cent for 2022 and 0.5 for 2023. It raised the inflation forecast for 2023 to 6.3 per cent and for 2024 to 3.4. It said the peak had been reached in the fourth quarter. The commission's forecast was published on 11 November, and it was the fourth cut of the year.

Inflation fell to 10.1 in November and 9.2 in December as the base effects from energy arrived. Core inflation kept rising until March 2023 and then fell, and the bank paused in September at 4 per cent. The recession was mild, and output fell 0.1 per cent in the first quarter of 2023. Energy futures had fallen 25 per cent since August, and the effect would show in the winter data.
