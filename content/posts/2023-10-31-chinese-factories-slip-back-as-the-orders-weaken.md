title: "Chinese factories slip back as the orders weaken"
date: "2023-10-31T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The official manufacturing index fell below the line in October after a month above it, and the new export orders were the weakest component."
---

Official manufacturing activity measured 49.5 in October on the purchasing managers' index, down from 50.2 in September, the statistics bureau said on 31 October, back below the line that separates expansion from contraction. The figure was below the forecast of 50.1, and the index has now been above the line only once in the last six months. The reading was the first decline in three months, and the sub-index for the production fell to about 50.6 while the one for the new orders fell to 49.8. The figures were published on the last day of the month, and the market had expected a small gain after the September reading.

Falling export orders led the decline. The new export orders fell to about 46, the lowest since the start of the year, as the demand from Europe and America weakened and the trade with the belt and road countries slowed. A container that is not booked is an order that does not reach the factory. The export orders have been below the line for eight months, and the shipments to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations fell for a second month. The volume of the exports fell by about 6 per cent in September, and the shipments to the European Union by about 15.

Domestic orders were firmer. The new orders index fell but stayed near the line, and the infrastructure spending has kept the demand for the materials. A site that is built is an order that the fiscal policy generates. The special bonds issued by the local governments reached about 3.5 trillion yuan, and the quota for the year is nearly exhausted. The infrastructure investment has been growing by about 6 per cent, and the spending on the railways and the power grids was the strongest.

Services were the bright spot. The non-manufacturing index fell to 50.6 from 51.7, and the construction held above 50 as the projects continued. A service sector above the line is an economy that is not contracting. The services index has been above the line for ten months, and the travel and the restaurants were the strongest over the holidays.

Prices are still falling. The output price index was about 47, and the input price about 49, which is the deflation in the factory gate that the producer price data have been showing. A price that falls is a profit that shrinks. The producer prices fell 2.5 per cent in September, and the profits of the industrial firms fell by about 9 per cent over the year. The prices of the steel and the cement are below the cost of the production for about a third of the mills.

What the month shows is that the stimulus has kept the domestic demand near the line while the exports weaken, and the near is not enough to reach the growth target. A reading below the line in October is a reading that the stimulus must answer, and the answer will come in the winter. The government has said it will meet the target of about 5 per cent, and the economists expect more spending in the winter.
