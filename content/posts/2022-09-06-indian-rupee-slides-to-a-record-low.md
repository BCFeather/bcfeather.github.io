title: "Indian rupee slides to a record low"
date: "2022-09-06T09:20:00+08:00"

description: "The currency passed 79.9 to the dollar on 6 September, and the central bank's reserves fell by 8 billion dollars in a month."
---

Traders said the central bank had sold dollars at 79.8 to slow the move and then stepped back. The rupee opened at 79.6 and weakened through the session to 79.9, the lowest level since the currency was floated in the 1990s. It had fallen 7 per cent against the dollar since January, and 4 per cent since the war in Ukraine began.

Reserves told the same story. Foreign exchange holdings fell to 561 billion dollars at the end of August from 604 billion in April, a drop of 43 billion in four months. Part of that was valuation, because the dollar rose against most currencies in the same period. The remainder was intervention, and economists estimated that the bank had spent 20 billion dollars defending the level.

Services exports and remittances were strong and covered about half of it. The pressure came from the current account. The trade deficit widened to 28 billion dollars in the June quarter, the largest on record, as oil and coal import bills doubled. The government raised import duties on gold in July, which cut that flow by a third but did not close the gap.

Capital flows turned. Foreign investors sold 30 billion dollars of Indian equities in the first nine months of the year, and the bond market saw outflows for six straight months. The dollar was rising because the Federal Reserve was raising rates faster than anyone else, which drew money out of emerging markets and into American assets. The government response was fiscal rather than monetary. It cut the excise duty on petrol and diesel in May and again in July, and it raised the subsidy on cooking gas. The central bank raised its policy rate from 4 per cent in May to 5.9 per cent in September, faster than it had moved in a decade.

Companies began to hedge. Importers that had waited for the rupee to recover bought forward cover in August, and the forward premia widened. Exporters held their dollars longer, which added to the spot pressure. The bank's governor said the currency would find its own level and that reserves were for smoothing rather than for targets. The rupee crossed 80 in the last week of September and 82 in October, and it ended the year near 82.7. The current account deficit reached 3.7 per cent of output for the year, and the finance ministry said it would be funded. The central bank's reserves recovered to 580 billion by March as the dollar fell.

For economists the episode became a test of how much intervention a large emerging market can afford. The answer, in this case, was that reserves could slow a slide without reversing it. The bank's own review, published in 2023, said the operations had been aimed at volatility and that the level had not been defended. Forward premia for one year widened to 3 per cent, up from 2.5 in July.
