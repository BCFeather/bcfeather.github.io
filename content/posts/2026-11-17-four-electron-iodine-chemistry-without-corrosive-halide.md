Aqueous zinc-iodine cells hold appeal for grid storage: iodine is abundant, the electrolyte is water, and the positive electrode stores charge by changing oxidation state instead of hosting ions in a lattice. Nearly all of that promise has been realized on the two-electron couple, which offers a theoretical capacity of \(211\ \mathrm{mA h\,g^{-1}}\). Extending the chemistry to iodine(+1) doubles the figure to \(422\ \mathrm{mA h\,g^{-1}}\) and adds a plateau near 1.07 V against the standard hydrogen electrode.

Reaching that second plateau has meant dissolving large amounts of halide in the electrolyte, so that the reactive iodine(+1) cation is held as an interhalogen complex. Concentrations in the literature run to tens of moles per litre, and the brine attacks current collectors. Chen, Dong and He report an alternative in *Nature*, 11 November 2026: a framework built from kosmotropic anions, polar anions and a nucleophilic species, with no halide in it. The pouch electrolyte, for instance, was 1.5 M zinc sulfate, 0.5 M zinc acetate and 0.4 M nicotinamide.

Cycling held up better than the halide route might suggest. Zinc-iodine cells kept 96 per cent of capacity after 1,000 cycles at \(1\ \mathrm{A\,g^{-1}}\) and 94 per cent after 2,000 cycles at \(2\ \mathrm{A\,g^{-1}}\), and four-electron conversion survived areal loadings up to \(12.2\ \mathrm{mg\,cm^{-2}}\). The authors also ran the framework in a 1.1 Ah pouch cell and in aluminium-iodine cells, where the same electrolyte supported the same conversion.

Two interfaces do the work at once. Polar anions crowd the electrode surface and displace water, producing a water-poor, anion-rich layer in which iodine chemistry proceeds. Kosmotropic anions act in the bulk, breaking the hydrogen bonds that would otherwise keep the nucleophile tied to its hydration shell, so that the nucleophile is free to form Nuc-I(+1)-Nuc halogen bonds. Raman and X-ray photoelectron spectra of cycled electrodes support that picture, and a transparent series of acetate, sulfate and perchlorate behaves as the mechanism predicts, including at matched ionic strength.

Retention figures describe the shape of a fade without giving its cause. Coulombic efficiency stayed low enough that the authors trace capacity loss to two coupled drains on the iodine inventory, one reversible and one not, with the irreversible share growing as areal loading rises. The cells that carry the cycling claim are coin cells at \(2.2\ \mathrm{mg\,cm^{-2}}\), the aluminium work is also at coin format, and the pouch is a single unit. The cost argument rests on a techno-economic model, with no bill of materials for a built stack behind it.

Three tests would decide whether the framework leaves the laboratory. Independent groups would need to reproduce 2,000 cycles at matched loading against an interhalogen electrolyte, so that the comparison is like for like. A costed stack would show whether removing halide and costly collectors lowers the price per kilowatt-hour. Long cycling at high areal loading would show whether the drain in iodine inventory can be managed, not merely measured.
