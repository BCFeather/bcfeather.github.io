Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing reported a profit of 206.9 billion Taiwan dollars for the first quarter, down about 15 per cent from a year earlier, on a revenue of 508.6 billion. The revenue fell by about 5 per cent and was at the top of the company's own guidance. The shares rose about 2 per cent on the results. The profit was about 2 per cent above the estimate, and the gross margin held at about 56 per cent. The revenue in the dollars inched up by about 1 per cent as the weaker currency helped the translation.

Consumer chips were the weak part. The revenue from the smartphones fell by about 27 per cent over the quarter, and the one from the computers by about 20. The customers had built the stock in 2022 and were working it down, and the orders for the mature nodes were cut the most. The revenue from the automotive rose by about 5 per cent, and the one from the industrial and the consumer fell by about 30. The customers in the consumer electronics had been the first to cut, and the panel said the phones would be the last to recover.

Advanced nodes held. The revenue from the 5-nanometre and the 7-nanometre processes was about half of the total, and the demand from the AI accelerators was the bright spot. The company said the AI revenue would grow by about 50 per cent this year and would double over the next five. The company said it would keep the price of the advanced wafers, and the customers had accepted the increase in the winter. The capacity for the advanced packaging is being doubled, and the AI chips need it as much as the logic.

Gross margin fell to about 56 per cent from 60. The utilization of the older lines was lower, and the company said the price of the wafers had been raised for the advanced nodes to offset the mix. The capital spending for the year was cut to about 32 billion dollars from 36. The depreciation is rising as the factories in Arizona and Japan come on line, and the company said the margin would stay above 50 per cent. The capital spending of about 32 billion dollars is about 10 per cent below the plan of the autumn, and most of it goes to the advanced nodes.

Second quarter guidance was cautious. The company expects the revenue to fall by about 5 per cent again, and the recovery is not expected before the second half. The inventory of the industry is still above the level of the long-run average, and the correction is not finished. The utilization of the 3-nanometre line is about 50 per cent, and the company said it would reach the full capacity in the second half. The mature nodes are running at about 70 per cent, which is the lowest since 2019.

These results are the cleanest read on the electronics cycle. A foundry that earns 207 billion Taiwan dollars while the smartphones fall by 27 per cent is a foundry that is carried by the data center. The shares are up about 15 per cent this year, and the AI orders are the reason the market has forgiven the quarter. The company is the largest maker of the advanced chips, and its guidance is the closest thing the industry has to a forecast.
