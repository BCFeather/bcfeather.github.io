Disney published results for the quarter to September on 8 November, and revenue rose 9 per cent to 20.2 billion dollars. Net income fell to 162 million from 1.6 billion, and the direct-to-consumer unit lost 1.47 billion. The shares fell 13 per cent the next day, their worst day since 2001. Disney+ added 12 million subscribers in the quarter, more than expected, and the total reached 164 million. The growth came from Asia and from Europe, and the average revenue per user fell 5 per cent because the new subscribers took the cheaper plans. The service had launched in 2019 and had reached 100 million in two years. The quarter was the fourth in the company's fiscal year, and revenue for the year rose 23 per cent.

Revenue rose 36 per cent to 7.4 billion dollars, and the operating margin was 30 per cent. The parks business was the profit engine. Attendance was above pre-pandemic levels, and the company said it had raised prices and had introduced a paid reservation system. The cruise line was profitable for the first time since 2019. Domestic parks were the strongest, and international parks were still below their 2019 level.

Profitability by 2024 was the target the company had set in 2020. It moved that target to 2024 at the earliest, and the chief executive said the company would cut costs by 3 billion dollars. It also raised the price of Disney+ by 38 per cent in December, and it introduced an advertising tier.

Content spending was the reason. The company spent 30 billion dollars on it in the year, and it had been spending to fill the service with series. It said it would spend less in 2023 and would make fewer, larger shows. It also said it would take a write-down of 1 billion dollars on content it had decided not to release.

Reinstating the chief executive in November, two weeks after removing him, was the board's decision. The previous chief executive had been in the job for two years and had been dismissed over the cost of the streaming strategy. The reinstated one said he would keep the strategy and change the spending. The company had 200,000 employees, and it said the cuts would fall in film and in streaming.

Disney+ reached profitability in the quarter to September 2023, a year ahead of the revised target, and it lost subscribers in the following quarter. The company had also lost the rights to some sports in the quarter, and it said the loss would reduce revenue by 1 billion dollars a year. It had bid for the Indian Premier League and had lost to a rival, and the cricket rights were the largest single content deal in the market. The shares ended the year 45 per cent lower. The company cut 7,000 jobs in 2023, and it restructured into three divisions.
