Ninth China International Import Expo opened in Shanghai on Thursday, with 4,142 overseas exhibitors and more than 440,000 square metres of exhibition space, the largest in the nine-year history of the event. Organizers said 162 countries, regions and international organizations are taking part in the six-day show, which runs to 10 November at the national exhibition centre in the city's west.

More than 300 Fortune Global 500 companies and industry leaders have stands, the first time the figure has passed 300, and nearly 200 firms have attended all nine editions. Companies appearing for the first time number more than 2,000, and the count of global, Asian or China debuts has reached 480, also a record for the fair, which has built its reputation on launches rather than on order books.

American exhibitors hold more than 50,000 square metres, the eighth year in a row that the United States has been the largest national presence, with German, British and French companies close behind. That ranking has survived several years of tariff disputes, and organizers cite it whenever they are asked whether foreign firms are losing interest. Organizers have stressed that the show is about imports rather than exports, and that overseas firms account for most of the floor space, a framing that matters when trade talks turn to market access and when foreign delegations want a photograph of themselves inside the hall.

Beyond the six established exhibition areas, an innovation incubation section houses 633 technology projects from nearly 40 countries, almost a fifth more than at the last edition. Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang gave the opening address, and the parallel Hongqiao International Economic Forum used the first day to release its annual report on world openness, a document that has become a fixture of the autumn calendar, and delegations from several countries used the margins of the opening to hold bilateral meetings of their own.

Exhibition space is divided into six areas: intelligent industry and information technology, consumer goods, food and agricultural products, automobiles and smart mobility, medical devices and healthcare, and trade in services. The expo was first held in 2018 and is co-organized with the World Trade Organization, the United Nations Development Programme, UNCTAD, the Food and Agriculture Organization, UNIDO and the International Trade Centre, a list that explains why trade ministers treat it as a diplomatic occasion as much as a commercial one.

Logistics around the site have been expanded for the crowds. Seven self-service tax refund machines across six service points handle rebates in 13 languages and promise completion in three minutes, badge holders can take 12 city bus routes free of charge and enter the maritime museum without a ticket, and organizers have deployed 100 humanoid exoskeletons to help staff move freight between halls. Smaller conveniences, but they are the part exhibitors remember when they decide whether to come back next year, and the organizers know it.
