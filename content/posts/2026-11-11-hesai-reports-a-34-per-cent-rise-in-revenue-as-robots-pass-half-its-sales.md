Hesai reported third-quarter revenue of 1.07 billion yuan on 10 November, 34.3 per cent more than a year earlier, and a net profit of 80.8 million. It was the sixth consecutive quarter of profit on the American accounting standard. Deliveries of lidar reached 729,700 units, 65.3 per cent more than a year earlier, and for the first time the robotics business supplied more than half of revenue. Revenue beat the guidance the company had given in August.

Change came from the products rather than the cars. The finance chief, Fan Peng, said Hesai had never seen itself as an auto parts supplier, and that lidar had dominated revenue only because electric cars and driver assistance grew quickly. Consumer robots, industrial logistics and commercial machines have since opened other uses, and the modules that move a robot's joints have become a business of their own. Lidar for robots sells at a higher margin than lidar for cars, the company said.

A new strategic growth segment reported 90.5 million yuan of revenue, 101.4 per cent more than in the previous quarter, and shipped 23,405 power modules, 96.8 per cent more than in the second quarter. Cumulative shipments reached 35,295 by the end of September, and monthly capacity has risen to 10,000 sets. The company kept its guidance of 200 to 300 million yuan of revenue from the segment this year, and expects shipments of power modules to reach six figures in 2027. Full-body joint modules have entered mass production.

Operating profit in the core lidar business was 145 million yuan, 88 per cent more than a year earlier and the fastest growth in the company's history. Margins held and costs were flat, so most of the extra gross profit reached the operating line. That operating leverage is what the finance chief asked investors to notice. Gross margin in the segment was broadly stable year on year.

Robotics has begun to reach the reference designs that matter. A dexterous hand built with Hesai modules, the Sharpa W01 with 22 active degrees of freedom, has entered NVIDIA's Isaac GR00T humanoid reference. In October the company also released Kosmo, an engine that turns real space into three-dimensional training data, which it argues is the missing input for physical artificial intelligence. Kosmo is aimed at the data problem that limits humanoid training. The engine was shown at a conference in Shanghai.

Cars remain the larger market and the weaker one. Chinese demand has come in below the expectations set at the start of the year, and some customers have cut their sales plans, which reaches the suppliers. The robot business covered that shortfall this quarter. Whether it can keep covering it depends on how quickly humanoid makers move from prototypes to volume. Car sales in China have grown more slowly than the industry planned. Analysts expect the segment to carry the growth into next year.
