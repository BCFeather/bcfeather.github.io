Twitter filed a lawsuit against Elon Musk on Tuesday in the court of chancery in Delaware, and it asked the judge to order him to complete the purchase that he agreed in April. The complaint says that he breached the merger agreement when he announced on Friday that he was terminating it, and that his stated reasons are excuses for a decision he had already made. The company's shares closed at about thirty-four dollars, which is well below the fifty-four that he had offered. The trial has been requested for September.

The contract is lopsided in ways that a buyer usually avoids. It contains a commitment to complete the deal and a fee of one billion dollars if either side walks away, and it does not contain a general right to withdraw because of the condition of the business. The buyer's case is that the company misrepresented the number of accounts that are run by bots, and that the misrepresentation is a material breach. The company's case is that the buyer was given the data and that he waived the right to ask for more.

The quarrel has been public since the spring, and it has been conducted on the platform itself. Musk agreed to buy the company in April without doing the diligence that such deals usually involve, and he said at the time that he wanted to remove the spam accounts and to make the platform a place for free speech. He then began to complain that the company would not tell him how many of the accounts were fake, and he sold shares in his car company to raise the money. The board accepted his offer after a process that had lasted two weeks.

What the contract means now belongs to a judge in Wilmington, not to the parties' press releases. Delaware law allows a buyer to walk away only if the target has suffered a material adverse effect, which is a high bar, and the courts have rarely found one in a company that is still operating. The remedy that the company is asking for is specific performance, which would force the purchase, and the alternative is damages. The buyer's lawyers have said that specific performance would be inappropriate because the financing is no longer in place.

Traders have priced the argument, and they have priced it low. The shares of the company have fallen by a third since the offer, and the arbitrage spread suggests that the traders expect a settlement rather than a completion. The banks that agreed to finance the deal have been trying to sell the debt to investors and they have had to offer discounts. The employees have been told that the company will continue to operate normally, and several of them have left.

Delaware will decide what a signed promise costs. A man with a large stake in a public company offered to buy it, signed a contract and then changed his mind, and the contract says what happens next. The court will read the words and the words were written by lawyers who were paid to make them precise. Whatever the judge decides, the case will be a lesson for the next buyer who wants to announce a deal before the diligence is done.
