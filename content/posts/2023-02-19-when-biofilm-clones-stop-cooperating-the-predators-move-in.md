description: "A study of multispecies biofilms finds that the spatial architecture of clonal cooperation breaks down under predation, changing the ecology of the community and the way its members compete. It appears in *PNAS* on 7 February 2023."
---

A biofilm is a community with a structure. Bacteria attach to a surface, secrete a matrix, and divide in place, so that the descendants of a founder stay together. That spatial arrangement is what makes cooperation possible, because a cell that pays a cost can benefit its relatives.

The study in *PNAS*, 7 February 2023 examines what happens when the arrangement is disturbed. The authors build multispecies biofilms, map their spatial structure as clones grow, and introduce a predator that consumes bacteria, then observe the community's ecology.

The result is a breakdown. Predation disrupts the clonal architecture, mixing cells that were relatives with cells that were not, and the cooperation that depends on relatedness weakens. The community becomes less productive and more heterogeneous.

The mechanism is spatial. A predator that moves through a biofilm opens channels and displaces clusters, and a displaced cluster loses the local density that lets secreted products be shared. The physical disturbance is the ecological disturbance.

The finding matters for how biofilms are understood. A biofilm's properties, such as its resistance to antibiotics, are often attributed to the matrix or to the cells' physiology, and the spatial structure is a third factor that the paper shows can be altered from outside.

The predator's own ecology is the other half. A predator that feeds on a structured community has access to prey that an unstructured one would hide, and the predator's growth is a function of the architecture it destroys.

The experimental design is the strength. A defined community, a defined predator and a spatial readout let the authors separate the effect of predation from the effect of competition, which a natural sample would confound.

The limitation is the number of species. A three-species biofilm is a model of a community, and a real one has hundreds. The principles may scale and the parameters will differ.

What the paper does not measure is the evolutionary response. A community under predation selects for cells that resist being eaten, and a resistance trait may change the architecture in turn.

For medicine, the implication is about chronic infections. A biofilm in a wound or on a catheter is a structured community, and a treatment that disrupts its architecture may make it more vulnerable rather than less, which is the opposite of what antibiotics alone do.

The matrix is part of the story. Extracellular polymers hold the community together and slow a predator's movement, and a matrix that is degraded by the predator's enzymes is a matrix that no longer protects.

Quorum sensing is the third factor. A biofilm's cells coordinate through diffusible signals, and a signal's concentration depends on local density. Break the clusters and the signals fall below threshold, which is another route by which architecture becomes behaviour.

The ecological lesson is general. Competition, cooperation and predation are usually modelled as interactions between populations, and the paper shows that geometry sets their strength. A community's shape is a parameter of its ecology.
