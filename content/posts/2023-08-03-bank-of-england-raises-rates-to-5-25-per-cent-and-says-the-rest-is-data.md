Britain's central bank raised its policy rate to 5.25 per cent on 3 August, a quarter-point increase and the fourteenth in the cycle. The vote was 8 to 1, with one member preferring to hold. The statement said the policy would stay restrictive for long enough to bring the inflation to the target. The rate has risen by about 5.15 percentage points since the end of 2021, which is the fastest cycle in the history of the bank. The dissenter wanted to hold, and the argument was that the effects of the earlier rises had not reached the borrowers.

Inflation has been falling. The consumer prices rose by 7.9 per cent in June, down from 8.7, and the bank expects the figure to fall to about 5 by the autumn. The core at 6.9 has been the part that has not moved, and the wages are the reason. The food inflation was about 14.8 per cent, and the services about 7.4, which are the two lines the bank watches. The bank said the wage growth in the private sector was about 7.8 per cent, which is well above the level consistent with the target.

Forecasts changed. The bank cut the growth forecast for the year to about 0.5 per cent, and the one for the next year to about 0.5. The unemployment is expected to rise to about 5 per cent by 2025, which is higher than the current 4 per cent. The bank said the mortgage rates would peak at about 6.5 per cent, and about 1 million households would refinance in the next year.

Markets read it as the peak. The traders cut the odds of another rise in September to about 40 per cent, and the peak of the rate to about 5.5. The pound fell about 0.5 per cent against the dollar, and the two-year gilt yield dropped about 15 basis points. The market now expects the first cut in the summer of next year, which is later than it had expected in the spring.

Its governor was careful. He said the committee would not cut until the inflation was on a path to 2, and he declined to say when that would be. He also said the wage growth of about 7.8 per cent was still too high for the target. He said the bank would watch the wage settlements of the autumn, and the next meeting is on 21 September. He said the bank had been criticized for the late start, and the criticism was the one he accepted.

A decision of this kind ends the aggressive phase. A rate of 5.25 per cent with the inflation at 7.9 is still a negative real rate, and the gap is the part the committee is waiting to close. A decision of this kind ends the aggressive phase.
