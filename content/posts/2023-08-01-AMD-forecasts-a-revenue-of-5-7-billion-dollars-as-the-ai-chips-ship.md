description: "The quarter to June fell by 18 per cent on the weak personal computer and the data centre demand, while the guidance for the autumn beat the estimate."
---

AMD reported a revenue of 5.4 billion dollars for the quarter to June, down about 18 per cent, and a net profit of 27 million. The earnings per share of 0.58 dollars beat the forecast of 0.57. The shares rose about 5 per cent as the guidance for the current quarter came in above the estimate. The revenue was about 100 million above the estimate, and the gross margin was about 50 per cent. The company said the restructuring had cut about 5 per cent of the staff, and the savings were about 300 million on the annual basis. The company said the free cash flow was about 1 billion dollars, and the one for the year would be about 3.

Its client business fell. The revenue from the personal computers was about 1 billion dollars, down about 54 per cent, as the inventory at the customers stayed high. The company said the market had bottomed in the second quarter, and the orders had improved in July. The average selling price of the personal computer chips rose by about 10 per cent, and the volume fell by about 55.

Data centre was weaker. The revenue was about 1.3 billion dollars, down about 11 per cent, as the enterprise customers cut the spending. The company said the new Genoa chips had been shipping, and the AI accelerator would arrive in the fourth quarter. The company said the MI300 accelerator would be the largest part of the AI revenue next year, and the customers had been sampling it.

Its embedded business held. The revenue from the chips for the industrial and the communications was about 1.5 billion dollars, up about 16 per cent, and the one from the gaming about 1.6 billion, down about 4. The gaming revenue fell as the console cycle aged, and the company said the next consoles would arrive in 2027.

Guidance was the news. The company expects the revenue of about 5.7 billion dollars in the current quarter, above the estimate of 5.4, and the data centre would grow by about 20 per cent as the AI chips ship. The gross margin would be about 51 per cent. The company said the capital spending would be about 2 billion dollars this year, and the one for the AI is about a third. The company has about 5.8 billion dollars of the cash, and the debt is about 2.5 billion.

This quarter is the one in which the AI order book starts to matter. A company that guides to 5.7 billion dollars while the personal computers shrink is a company whose next leg is the accelerator, and the accelerator is the part the market is pricing. The shares are up about 70 per cent this year, and the company is the second best performer in the large chip makers after NVIDIA.
