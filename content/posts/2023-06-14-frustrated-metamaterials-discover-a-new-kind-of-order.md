description: "A mechanical metamaterial whose elements cannot all satisfy their preferred orientation develops a non-orientable order, and its response depends on the order of operations, according to work in *Nature* on 14 June 2023."
---

Frustration is a familiar idea in magnetism, where spins that cannot all align produce a state with no simple pattern. A mechanical analogue of the idea has been built, and its behaviour is more peculiar than the magnetic version.

The metamaterial in *Nature*, 14 June 2023 is that analogue. The authors construct a lattice of hinged units whose orientations are coupled, so that a unit's preferred angle conflicts with its neighbours', and the conflict cannot be resolved everywhere at once.

The order that emerges is non-orientable. A path that carries a local frame around a closed loop returns it rotated, which means that the lattice has no globally consistent assignment of orientations, and the authors demonstrate that by experiment and by simulation.

The consequence is a response that depends on history. A sequence of deformations does not commute with another sequence, and the material's final shape records the path that produced it rather than only the final load.

The measurement is the demonstration. The authors track markers on the lattice through a cycle, and the rotated frame is visible in the reconstructed positions of the units.

The theory is a topological classification. A lattice's frustration can be described by how frames transform around loops, and the classification predicts which configurations are possible.

The design is a platform. The unit's geometry can be changed, and a family of lattices with different loop rotations follows from the same principle.

The application is mechanical memory. A material that records the order of its deformations is a material that stores a sequence in its shape, which is a form of memory that does not need electronics.

The limitation is the scale. The lattice is a tabletop object made by printing or by assembly, and a material that could be manufactured in bulk would need a different process.

What the paper does not establish is robustness. A lattice with defects may lose the non-orientability that the ideal structure has, and a real material always has defects.

For mechanics, the value is a concept borrowed and returned. Frustration came from condensed matter, and the metamaterial shows that the idea has a mechanical expression with its own phenomena.
The hinge is the design's essential part. A joint that resists rotation stores energy, and a joint that allows it transfers the frustration to its neighbours. The authors' choice of a compliant hinge is what lets the lattice relax without breaking.
The experiment's readout is optical. A camera records the markers, and a reconstruction gives each unit's orientation at every step. A lattice that could not be observed would be a model without a test.
The comparison with origami is the useful one. An origami pattern folds into a shape that is determined by the creases, and the metamaterial's shape is determined by the path. The second is a looser relation between structure and form.
The energy landscape is the underlying picture. A frustrated lattice has many configurations of similar energy, and a path between them crosses a barrier that the hinges' compliance sets. A material with a shallow landscape is a material that can be cycled without fatigue.
