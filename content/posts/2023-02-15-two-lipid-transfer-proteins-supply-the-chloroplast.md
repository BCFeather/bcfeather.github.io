The chloroplast's thylakoid membrane is a specialized lipid environment, and the lipids are not made there. They are synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum, and they have to cross the cytosol and the chloroplast envelope to reach the thylakoids.

In *PNAS* on 7 February 2023, two proteins that carry one of those lipids are identified. The authors show that Arabidopsis Sec14 proteins *SFH5* and *SFH7* mediate the interorganelle transport of phosphatidic acid, and that their loss changes chloroplast development.

Canonical Sec14 proteins are lipid-binding modules that present a lipid to an enzyme. Moving a lipid between membranes is a different function, and the evidence is that the proteins' location and their loss-of-function phenotypes fit a transport role, with localization at the envelope being the observation that makes the claim plausible.

Because phosphatidic acid is made in the endoplasmic reticulum and converted into phosphatidylglycerol and the galactolipids that dominate the thylakoid, a step that moves the precursor sets the supply, and the envelope's two membranes demand a carrier or a contact site, so these proteins are candidates for the carrier. Specificity supports the argument, since a Sec14 domain that binds the precursor and not its derivatives is a domain evolved for that job, and specificity is what separates a transport protein from a general lipid chaperone.

Development shows the consequence. Plants without the proteins have chloroplasts that develop abnormally, and the abnormality sits in the thylakoid's structure rather than in its protein content, which is consistent with a lipid supply defect. Contact sites offer an alternative route, because the chloroplast touches the endoplasmic reticulum at several points and lipids can move through a contact without a carrier, so the proteins may act where contacts are absent, which would explain the phenotype. Yeast supplies a comparison, since its Sec14 proteins regulate lipid metabolism and the plant ones are relatives, and transport may be a plant-specific elaboration of a conserved scaffold.

Direction is what the paper does not show, because a carrier that binds phosphatidic acid could move it outward or inward, and the experiments establish the requirement rather than the flux. Composition ties the result to stress, since cold and drought change the ratio of saturated to unsaturated thylakoid lipids and a plant that cannot move the precursor may remodel more slowly, which connects a transport protein to yield. Agriculture follows indirectly, as thylakoid lipids affect photosynthetic efficiency under stress and a protein that sets the supply is a breeding candidate, though a field trial is a long way from an Arabidopsis phenotype. Cell biology gains a case for a short list, because lipid traffic between organelles runs through contact sites, shuttling proteins and vesicular traffic, and the Sec14 proteins are a shuttling example in a system that has few.

Which contact sites the proteins serve is the next question, and a plant lacking both a carrier and a contact would show whether either route can substitute for the other.
