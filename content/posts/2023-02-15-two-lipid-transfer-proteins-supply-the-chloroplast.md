---

The chloroplast's thylakoid membrane is a specialized lipid environment, and the lipids are not made there. They are synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum, and they have to cross the cytosol and the chloroplast envelope to reach the thylakoids.

The study in *PNAS*, 7 February 2023 identifies two proteins that carry one of those lipids. The authors show that Arabidopsis Sec14 proteins SFH5 and SFH7 mediate the interorganelle transport of phosphatidic acid and that their loss changes chloroplast development.

Sec14 proteins are lipid-binding modules, and their canonical role is to present a lipid to an enzyme. A Sec14 domain that moves a lipid between membranes is a different function, and the paper's evidence is that the proteins' location and their loss-of-function phenotypes match a transport role.

Phosphatidic acid is the precursor of the thylakoid lipids. It is made in the endoplasmic reticulum and converted to phosphatidylglycerol and to the galactolipids that dominate the thylakoid, and a step that moves the precursor is a step that sets the supply.

The chloroplast's envelope is the barrier. A lipid that must cross two membranes needs a carrier or a contact site, and the paper's proteins are candidates for the carrier. Their localization at the envelope is the observation that makes the claim plausible.

The phenotype is developmental. Plants without the proteins have chloroplasts that develop abnormally, and the abnormality is in the thylakoid's structure rather than in its protein content, which is consistent with a lipid supply defect.

The parallel with yeast is instructive. Yeast has Sec14 proteins that regulate lipid metabolism, and the plant proteins are relatives. The transport function may be a plant-specific elaboration of a conserved lipid-binding scaffold.

What the paper does not show is the direction of transfer. A carrier that binds phosphatidic acid could move it outward or inward, and the experiments establish the requirement rather than the flux.

The agricultural angle is indirect. Thylakoid lipids affect photosynthetic efficiency under stress, and a protein that sets the supply is a candidate for breeding. A field trial is a long way from an Arabidopsis phenotype.

For cell biology, the result adds a case to a small list. Lipid transport between organelles is mediated by contact sites, by shuttling proteins and by vesicular traffic, and the Sec14 proteins are a shuttling example in a system that has few.

The proteins' lipid specificity is the argument's support. A Sec14 domain that binds phosphatidic acid and not its derivatives is a domain evolved for the precursor, and the specificity is what distinguishes a transport protein from a general lipid chaperone.
The envelope's contact sites are the alternative route. The chloroplast touches the endoplasmic reticulum at several points, and lipids can move through a contact without a carrier. The paper's proteins may act where contacts are absent, which would explain why their loss produces a phenotype.
Thylakoid lipid composition is a stress trait. Cold and drought change the ratio of saturated to unsaturated lipids, and a plant that cannot move the precursor may be less able to remodel. That connects a transport protein to yield under stress.
