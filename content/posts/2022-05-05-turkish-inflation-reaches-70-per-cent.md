Producer prices rose 122 per cent, which is the fastest in the series. The statistics office published the figures on 5 May, and consumer prices rose 69.97 per cent in the year to April. The reading was the highest since 2002 and it was above the 68 per cent forecast. Food prices rose 89 per cent, and the price of sunflower oil doubled after the invasion. The index covers 400 goods, and the statisticians changed the weights in January.

Imported energy and food were priced in dollars, and the pass-through was immediate. The cause was the currency. The lira had fallen 44 per cent against the dollar in the year, and it had lost 80 per cent since 2021. The transport index rose 105 per cent, and food 89.

Inflation was 70 per cent and the policy rate was 14, so the real rate was minus 56 per cent. The policy was unusual. The central bank had cut its policy rate from 19 per cent in September to 14 per cent in December, and it held it there. The president had said that higher rates cause inflation, and he had replaced three governors in two years who disagreed. The result was that real rates were deeply negative. Subsidies rather than rates were the government's response. It raised the minimum wage 50 per cent in January and again 30 per cent in July, and it capped electricity prices for households. The measures supported consumption and added to the inflation. The current account was in deficit of 7 per cent of output, and the central bank used reserves to defend the lira. Its net reserves fell below zero in the summer, and it introduced a scheme that paid lira holders a premium if the currency fell further. It also required exporters to convert 40 per cent of their foreign currency earnings into lira.

Economists said the growth was borrowed from the future. The economy grew 5.6 per cent in the year, which was faster than most. The growth came from consumption and from exports, and the government said the model would produce a surplus in 2023.

Inflation peaked at 85.5 per cent in October and fell to 64 per cent in December as the base effect worked. The central bank reversed course after the election in May 2023, and it raised the policy rate to 40 per cent by November. Inflation was 62 per cent at the end of 2023. The lira was 18.6 to the dollar at the end of the year, and it had been 8.5 in 2021.
