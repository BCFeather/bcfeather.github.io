description: "A hydrometallurgical plant has reached 96 per cent lithium recovery from shredded cells, and the number that decides its economics is the purity of the output rather than the recovery rate."

Recycling a battery is a sequence of separations, and lithium is the element that the sequence has historically lost. A plant commissioned this year reports 96 per cent recovery of lithium from shredded cells, against 70 for the process it replaced, and the report's own framing puts purity ahead of recovery.

The process shreds cells under water, separates the casing and the copper by density, and leaches the black mass with sulphuric acid and hydrogen peroxide. Lithium is then precipitated as a carbonate after a solvent extraction step.

Purity is 99.5 per cent, which is the grade a cell manufacturer requires for a new cathode. A recovery rate of 96 per cent that produces an off-grade carbonate is worth less than 80 per cent at battery grade, because the output would go to a lower-value use.

Energy and reagents are the cost. The plant consumes 4 kilowatt-hours per kilogram of feed and 2.4 kilograms of sulphuric acid, and its operating cost is 1,900 dollars per tonne of black mass against a metal value of 4,100 at current prices.

Feedstock is the constraint. A plant needs 20,000 tonnes of cells a year, and the region's collection system delivers 12,000. The plant runs at 60 per cent capacity, which doubles its unit cost.

Pyrometallurgy is the trade this plant declined. A smelter recovers cobalt, nickel and copper and burns the lithium into a slag, and its capital cost is lower and its feedstock tolerance higher. A hydrometallurgical plant earns more per tonne and needs cleaner feed.

Regulation is setting the target. A battery regulation requires 50 per cent lithium recovery by 2027 and 80 by 2031, and the plant's 96 per cent is above both, which makes the permit's conditions the easy part of the business.

A battery's lithium is 3 per cent of its mass and 20 per cent of its value, and a process that recovers the cathode metals and loses the lithium is a process that has recovered the bulk. Lithium's price fell 70 per cent in two years, which is why the recovery rate's value depends on when the plant was financed.

Feedstock quality is the constraint on purity. A mixed feed of consumer electronics and vehicle packs carries manganese and aluminium that a battery-grade carbonate cannot contain, and a plant that sorts its feed spends 120 dollars a tonne on sorting to earn 400 on purity.

A recycling plant's siting is decided by transport. A battery pack is a hazardous material for shipping, and a plant more than 500 kilometres from its feedstock pays a freight cost that a hydrometallurgical margin cannot absorb. That is why plants cluster near vehicle assembly.

One number is not enough here: recovery needs purity beside it. A recovery rate describes what leaves the plant and a purity describes what it is worth, and a plant that recovers everything into a worthless product has recycled nothing.
