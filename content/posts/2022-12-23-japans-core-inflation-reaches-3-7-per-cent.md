Headline inflation was 3.8 per cent, and the two measures had converged for the first time in eight years. The statistics bureau published the figures on 23 December, and core consumer prices rose 3.7 per cent in the year to November. The reading was the highest since 1981 and it was above the 3.6 forecast. The figure had been 3.6 per cent in October, and it had doubled since January.

Energy was the largest contributor. Electricity prices rose 20 per cent and gas 15, and the government's subsidy scheme had kept the figures below what they would otherwise have been. Food rose 6 per cent, and the prices of processed food had risen for 15 months. Durable goods rose 9 per cent because of the weak yen.

A narrower measure that excludes energy as well rose 2.8 per cent, and it was above the bank's target for the sixth month. The core measure excludes fresh food and includes energy, which is the standard for the country. The bank had said it expected the narrow measure to fall back in the spring. Wages had begun to rise. Base pay grew 1.5 per cent in the autumn, and the spring negotiations were expected to produce 3 per cent. The bank said it needed to see wage growth of 3 per cent for inflation to be sustainable, and the 2023 round produced 3.6 per cent. The bank's target is 2 per cent, and the governor said the overshoot was acceptable.

On 20 December the central bank widened its cap on the ten-year yield from 0.25 to 0.5 per cent. It was a technical change rather than a rate increase, and the governor said it was not the start of tightening. The market took it as a signal, and the yen rose 3 per cent on the day. The yen had fallen 20 per cent in the year, and the cap change reversed 5 per cent of that.

He held rates at minus 0.1 per cent and kept the yield cap, and he said the bank would not raise until wages had risen. The bank's governor had said in December that the wage picture would determine policy. The market tested the cap for three months, and the bank bought 10 trillion yen of bonds. The bank bought 10 trillion yen of bonds in January, more than in any month of the year.

Food prices were the visible part of the inflation. A survey of 1,000 households found that 60 per cent expected prices to rise further, and the government said it would subsidize electricity bills from January. The cost of the subsidy was 2 trillion yen. Inflation stayed above 3 per cent until the summer of 2023 and then fell to 2.8 in September. The bank raised rates in March 2024, and it ended the negative rate policy and the yield cap. Wage growth in the 2024 round was 5.1 per cent, the highest in 33 years.
