Raising the bank rate by 50 basis points on 15 December, the Monetary Policy Committee took it to 3.5 per cent. It was the ninth increase of the year and the highest rate since 2008. The committee voted 6 to 3, with two members preferring to hold and one preferring 75 points. The statement said the labour market was still tight and that inflation was too high. It said the bank would respond forcefully if the outlook suggested more persistent pressure. The governor said in the press conference that the bank had not finished and that the peak would be lower than the market expected. The rate had been 3 per cent after the November meeting, and it was 0.1 per cent a year earlier.

Inflation was 10.7 per cent in November, down from 11.1, and the bank said it expected it to fall sharply in the second half of 2023. Its forecast had inflation at 5 per cent by the end of the year, and the actual figure was 4. The bank said the fall would come from energy and from goods. The governor said the peak would be 4.5 per cent, and the market expected 4.

Output had fallen 0.3 per cent in the third quarter, and the bank said it expected a further fall in the fourth. The economy was in recession by the bank's definition. The committee's projection showed eight quarters of contraction, and the actual outcome was one quarter of decline and then stagnation. The budget had changed the arithmetic. The autumn statement in November had tightened fiscal policy by 55 billion pounds, and the bank said that would reduce demand and would allow lower rates. The bank's forecast incorporated the measures, and it said the effect would be 0.4 points off growth. The bank's projections showed inflation at 5 per cent by the end of 2023, and the actual figure was 4.

Mortgage rates had begun to fall from their autumn peak. The average two-year fix was 6.1 per cent, down from 6.6 in October, and the number of products had recovered. The bank said the effect of higher rates would still be felt by 1.8 million households refinancing in the next two years. Output fell 0.3 per cent in the third quarter, and the bank expected a further fall in the fourth.

Its next meeting was on 2 February, at which the committee raised by 50 points to 4 per cent. It then raised by 25 points in March and in May, and it paused in September at 5.25. The bank said in August 2023 that it expected inflation to fall to 5 per cent by the end of the year, and that forecast was met.

Two members wanted to stop in February, and one wanted 75 points in March. The split on the committee widened in 2023. The governor said the disagreements were about the pace and not the direction, and he said the committee would keep watching the labour market.
