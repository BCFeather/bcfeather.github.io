---

Alphabet reported a revenue of 74.6 billion dollars for the quarter to June, up 7 per cent, and a net profit of 18.4 billion. The earnings per share of 1.44 dollars beat the forecast of 1.34. The shares rose about 6 per cent after the report, and the cloud unit produced an operating profit. The revenue was about 600 million above the estimate, and the operating margin was about 29 per cent, up from 24 a year ago. The company said the currency effects had subtracted about 2 percentage points from the revenue.

Advertising was steady. The search revenue rose by about 3 per cent to 42.6 billion, and the one of YouTube by about 4 to 7.6. The network revenue fell by about 5 as the small advertisers cut the spending, and the company said the retail and the travel were the strongest categories. The paid clicks rose by about 4 per cent, and the cost per click fell by about 4 as the mix shifted to the cheaper formats. The other revenue, which includes the hardware and the app store, fell by about 2 per cent.

Its cloud turned a profit. The revenue rose by about 28 per cent to 8.03 billion dollars, and the operating profit was about 395 million after the losses of the previous quarters. The company said the AI customers had been the part that drove the demand, and the margins would improve as the utilization rose. The company said the backlog of the cloud contracts was about 60 billion dollars, and the AI workloads were about 10 per cent of it.

Costs came down. The total expenses rose by about 5 per cent, and the headcount was about 180,000, down about 10,000 from the end of last year. The company said the hiring would be slow for the rest of the year, and the capital spending would be about 28 billion. The restructuring charge was about 500 million dollars in the quarter, and the total for the programme is expected to be about 2 billion.

Buybacks continued. The company repurchased about 15 billion dollars of the stock in the quarter, and the cash was about 118 billion. The dividend remains off the table, and the management said the priority was the investment in the AI. The company has bought back about 55 billion dollars of the stock this year, and the shares are up about 45 per cent.

This quarter is the one the cost cuts show in. A company that grows by 7 per cent while the cloud turns profitable is a company that has stopped paying for the growth twice, and the second payment is the one the investors have been waiting for. The other two large platforms reported the slower advertising in the same week, and the comparison was the one the analysts drew.
