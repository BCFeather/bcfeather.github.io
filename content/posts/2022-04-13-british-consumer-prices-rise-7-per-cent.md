Energy carried the rise. The office said the gas had risen 50 per cent, and that the electricity had risen 30. Food rose 6 per cent, and the office said the bread had risen 10. The office said the fuel had risen 30 per cent, and that the used cars had risen 25. Prices were 7 per cent higher in the year, the fastest since March 1992, above the 6.7 forecast. The monthly gain was 1.1 per cent, and the core rate 5.7. Food and energy did most of the damage.

It said the energy cap had risen 54 per cent, and that the households would pay 700 pounds more. The bank raised rates on 17 March, and it said the inflation would reach 9 per cent in April. The office said the real earnings had fallen 3 per cent, and that they had fallen for 13 months. Gas rose 50 per cent, electricity 30, food 6, bread 10 and fuel 30. Used cars rose 25 per cent in the year. The office said the food prices had risen fastest since 2008.

War pushed prices up. The office said the wheat had risen 40 per cent, and that the oil had risen 70. The office said the shops had raised prices 6 per cent, and that the restaurants had raised 9. The energy cap was to rise 54 per cent, adding 700 pounds to bills. Real earnings fell 3 per cent, the thirteenth monthly fall. Households faced the worst squeeze since records began.

A peak of 9 per cent came in April, and 9.4 in June. The office said the energy had risen 80 per cent, and that the food had risen 10. The bank raised to 1.75 per cent in June, and to 2.25 in September. Wheat rose 40 per cent, and oil 70. Shops raised prices 6 per cent, and restaurants 9. The office said the war had cut wheat supplies from Ukraine.

A fall to 10.1 per cent in September, and to 10.5 in October. The office said the energy cap had risen 27 per cent in October, and that the bank had raised to 3 per cent. Energy rose 80 per cent, and food 10, by June. Rates rose to 1.75 per cent in June and 2.25 in September.

A fall to 3.9 per cent in December 2023, and to 2.5 in December 2024. The office said the energy had fallen 20 per cent, and that the food had fallen 1 per cent. The office said the services had risen 5 per cent in 2024, and that the bank had cut to 4.75 per cent. The energy cap rose 27 per cent in October, and rates reached 3 per cent. Energy fell 20 per cent, and food 1 per cent, in 2024. The bank raised seven times in 2022. The bank cut to 4.75 per cent in November 2024.
