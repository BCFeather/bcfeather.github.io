The August box office closed at 5.985 billion yuan, according to the ticketing platforms, against 4.07 billion in July and 1.91 billion in June. The pattern is unusual: in most recent years July has been the stronger month of the summer holiday, and this year the second half carried the season.

The summer total, from 1 June to 31 August, was 11.966 billion yuan, up 2.76 per cent on last year, with 321 million admissions, up 12.75 per cent. The average ticket price fell 8.9 per cent to 37.2 yuan, which explains how admissions can rise while revenue barely does. Cinemas sold more seats at a lower price, and premium-format screens took about 8.3 per cent of the total, the highest share in five years.

Three films did most of the work. *Nanjing Photo Studio*, a historical drama built around the photographs that documented the Nanjing massacre, took 2.89 billion yuan and became the highest-grossing film of the summer and the highest-grossing historical film in the market's history. *Wandering Mountain*, an animated feature derived from an anthology series, took 1.455 billion and became the largest two-dimensional animated film in Chinese cinema. *Shadow Pursuit*, an action film released in mid-August, took 879 million after a slow start.

The rest of the top ten was thinner: a costume comedy at 678 million, a dinosaur sequel at 567 million, a domestic animated sequel at 492 million, a motor-racing film at 429 million, a stage-derived drama at 403 million, a detective anime at 398 million and a war film at 384 million. Domestic titles took 76.21 per cent of the total, roughly unchanged from last year, and nineteen films passed 100 million.

The audience changed more than the films did. Buyers aged 40 and over passed 20 per cent of the total for the first time, at 22.7 per cent, and new buyers in that age group rose sharply, pulled in by the historical drama and by the stage-derived film. The under-29 share continued its long decline, which the platforms attribute to competition from games and short video rather than to price.

For the industry, the lesson is about release strategy. The three leaders were not predicted to lead, and each built its audience through previews and word of mouth rather than through an opening weekend. A market where the biggest film of the season opens quietly and finishes first is a market where reviews matter, which is a healthier arrangement than one where the schedule decides. The second lesson is about price. A 37.2-yuan average ticket is low enough that a family of four can decide on a Saturday afternoon, and the trade-in of admissions for revenue worked: the season sold 12.75 per cent more seats for 2.76 per cent more money. Whether that is a strategy or a squeeze on margins is the question cinema chains will be answering in the fourth quarter.
