Israel's president, Isaac Herzog, told Gadi Eisenkot on Saturday evening that he intended to give him the task of forming the next government, and the ceremony that makes the task formal was set for five o'clock on Sunday afternoon at the President's Residence. The announcement closed eleven days of counting, consultation and arithmetic that followed the election of 27 October. Eisenkot, who founded the Yashar party in September last year, answered on X the same evening: 'This is the time to establish a national emergency government. I will do anything possible to prove us worthy.'

Arithmetic explains how fragile the thing is. Sixty-seven members of the 120-seat Knesset recommended Eisenkot to the president, the 48 of four opposition parties together with the 19 held by Arab-led parties. Forty-six recommended Benjamin Netanyahu, and the seven of United Torah Judaism recommended nobody at all. The recommendations are published, while the coalitions they imply stay private. A government needs 61 votes to be sworn in, which means the six votes by which his recommendations exceed that threshold are the six he cannot count on.

The election was the one the calendar required: the 25th Knesset was dispersed in July, and the vote was left on the last date the law allowed. The result, certified on 4 November, gave Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats, the opposition bloc 48, and the Arab-led parties a record 19. Yashar was standing in its first election, and its leader came out of the count with more recommendations than any other candidate in the country. That is how a party founded fourteen months ago reached the door of the prime minister's office.

Under the basic law the task carries 28 days, and the president may add 14 more if the work needs them. If Eisenkot fails, Herzog can hand the mandate to another member of the Knesset, or 61 members can ask him to give it to a candidate of their choosing. Failing that, the Knesset dissolves and the country votes again. The clock is running on a parliament that has produced deadlock before.

The obstacles are specific, and arithmetic is the least of them. Two of the four parties that recommended him have argued in public about whether sitting with Arab-led parties is acceptable, and those parties have conditions that go beyond portfolios. The two ultra-Orthodox parties hold 13 seats between them, and the seven who declined to recommend anybody belong to one of them. Netanyahu used the evening to call for a national unity government, to warn that a coalition resting on Arab support would damage the country, and to accuse those parties of seeking Israel's destruction.

Nothing in the mandate settles what a government would do; it settles only who must try first. Whoever ends up inside it will inherit the same file, in a country that has now voted twice in four years without either camp winning a majority. What Herzog handed over on Sunday afternoon was the right to attempt an answer, with a deadline attached and a chamber that can end the attempt whenever 61 of its members prefer another candidate.
