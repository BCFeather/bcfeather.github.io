ASML sells the machines that print the finest features on a chip, and its extreme ultraviolet tools are the only ones that can do the smallest layers at volume. That position makes the order book a leading indicator for the whole industry: chipmakers commit to a tool years before it produces revenue, so bookings in one quarter describe capacity that will arrive later. The company reported its third quarter on 15 October 2025. A tool costing hundreds of millions is ordered years ahead, and the buyer's factory must be ready before it arrives.

Net sales came to 7.5 billion euros, with a gross margin of 51.6 per cent and net income of 2.1 billion. Net bookings were 5.4 billion euros, of which 3.6 billion was extreme ultraviolet. The company sold 66 new lithography systems and 6 used ones, and installed base management, which is service and field options, contributed 1,962 million euros. The bookings mix is the figure to hold on to: two thirds of it was extreme ultraviolet, the product line that carries the highest price and the longest lead time.

Against the second quarter the direction is mixed. Sales fell from 7,692 million to 7,516 million, the gross margin from 53.7 to 51.6 per cent, net income from 2,290 million to 2,125 million, and earnings per share from 5.90 to 5.49 euros. Bookings dipped from 5,541 million to 5,399 million, which is a small move for a line that swings by billions between quarters. A 2.1 point fall in gross margin is large for a company whose process is stable, and the summary does not separate mix from pricing.

Guidance for the fourth quarter is 9.2 to 9.8 billion euros of net sales at a gross margin of 51 to 53 per cent. For the full year the company expects growth of about 15 per cent with a margin near 52 per cent, and it does not expect 2026 to come in below 2025. China is the exception: demand there is expected to fall significantly in 2026 after two strong years. The fourth quarter guidance brackets a step up of about a third over the third quarter, which is the pattern the company has described in earlier cycles.

The quarter also carried two product and capital notes. ASML shipped its first advanced packaging product, the TWINSCAN XT:260, an i-line scanner with up to four times the productivity of existing tools, which addresses the 3D integration market rather than the leading-edge logic one. It paid an interim dividend of 1.60 euros per share on 6 November and bought back about 148 million euros of stock, taking the programme to 9.0 million shares for 5.9 billion euros, short of the 12 billion euro target within the 2022 to 2025 window.

One quarter of bookings says little on its own, since a single order for a large system moves the total, and the guidance is a statement of expectations rather than a forecast. The China decline is given qualitatively, without a figure, and the mix between logic and memory is not broken out in the release. Details of the 2026 outlook and a new buyback programme are promised for January. Memory customers are absent from the bookings detail, and that segment has been the swing factor in previous downturns.
