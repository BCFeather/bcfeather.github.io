Tidal turbines convert a flow that reverses twice a day and cannot be turned off, and the engineering problem is fatigue rather than peak load. A 1.5-megawatt device has operated in a tidal stream for four years and came through a storm with a 12-metre sea state, and the operator's report is the first long-duration record for the technology.

The device is a three-bladed horizontal-axis turbine on a gravity base, with a pitch system that feathers the blades when the flow exceeds the design current. The storm's surface waves did not reach the rotor at 30 metres depth, and the load came from the current's surge.

Damage amounted to one blade's leading edge, replaced during a scheduled maintenance window. Inspection found no crack in the hub or the gearbox, and the generator's insulation resistance was within specification.

Capacity factor is the number that decides the economics. The device ran at 38 per cent of its rating over four years, which is higher than an offshore wind farm's 45 per cent only if one ignores that tidal power is predictable to the minute.

Predictability is the value proposition. A tidal array's output can be forecast a year ahead from astronomy, and a grid operator pays for that in a way it does not pay for wind. The operator's contract includes a capacity payment for the firm component.

Cost is where the wind turbine comparison lands. A tidal turbine's capital cost is 3.5 times a wind turbine's per megawatt, and its maintenance is more expensive because every intervention needs a vessel and a dive team. The technology's future depends on arrays rather than on single devices.

Environmental monitoring ran alongside. The operator's sonar surveys found no change in the fish population near the device, and the turbine's blade speed was limited to below the threshold that damages a passing fish.

A tidal site's energy is predictable in a way a wind site's is not. A strait's current reverses twice a day with a timing set by the moon, and a grid operator can schedule a tidal farm's output a year ahead. The value of that predictability is what a capacity payment buys.

Biofouling is the maintenance item. A blade in a tidal stream collects barnacles within a season, and a rough surface loses 6 per cent of its efficiency. The device's scheduled stops include a cleaning, and the interval between them is the operator's main cost decision.

A tidal array's grid connection is the project's largest line item. A single device at 1 megawatt justifies a cable and a substation; a farm of ten shares them, and the farm's economics depend on a site with a strait wide enough for ten devices and a current fast enough for each.

Certainty, not price, is what a tidal farm sells. Tidal output is small and known, and a grid with a large share of variable renewables will pay for known.
